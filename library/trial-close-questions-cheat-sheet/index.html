Trial Close Questions Cheat Sheet

40 low-pressure trial-close questions, grouped by purpose, that surface real objections early — before they surface as a stalled deal at the finish line.

What's inside

  • 6 directional questions checking overall fit
  • 6 feature/fit-specific questions
  • 6 timeline questions
  • 6 budget/value questions
  • 6 stakeholder buy-in questions
  • 4 competitive questions
  • 6 risk and concern-surfacing questions for late-stage deals
  • Usage rules: when to ask, how often, and what to do with the answer

40 questions to surface real objections before they become deal-killers

Usage rule: a trial close is a question, not a pitch. Ask it, then stop talking. Whatever hedge, pause, or qualifier comes back is the real objection — write it down and address it before moving on. Sprinkle these throughout the whole cycle; don't save them all for the end.

Directional (overall fit)

  1. "On a scale of 1–10, how well does this solve the problem we talked about — and what would make it a 10?"
  2. "If nothing changed between now and next month, would this still be a priority for your team?"
  3. "Walking through this today, is there anything that surprised you — good or bad?"
  4. "How does this compare to what you pictured when we first talked?"
  5. "If you had to explain this to your boss in one sentence, what would you say?"
  6. "What's your gut reaction right now, before we get into details?"

Feature / fit specific

  1. "Which of the things we just covered matters most for your day-to-day?"
  2. "Was there a capability here you didn't expect, that changes how you're thinking about this?"
  3. "Was there anything in that demo that felt like it was missing?"
  4. "If we could only keep three of the features we showed, which three would you keep?"
  5. "How would this fit into the workflow your team already uses?"
  6. "Is there a use case you have in mind that we haven't covered yet?"

Timeline

  1. "If we moved forward, when would you actually want this live?"
  2. "What's driving that timeline — is there a date something else depends on?"
  3. "What would need to happen internally between now and then?"
  4. "If this took longer than expected to implement, what's the impact on your side?"
  5. "Is there a reason to move faster than we've been discussing, or is this the right pace?"
  6. "What does the next 30 days look like for your team, realistically?"

Budget / value

  1. "How does this compare to what you budgeted for solving this problem?"
  2. "Whose budget does this come out of, and have they seen the numbers yet?"
  3. "If the ROI plays out like we modeled, what does that mean for your team next year?"
  4. "Is price the main thing standing between us and moving forward, or is it something else?"
  5. "How is this usually funded — one-time budget, or does it need a renewal cycle?"
  6. "What's the cost of NOT solving this in the next two quarters?"

Stakeholder buy-in

  1. "Who else needs to feel good about this before it's a done deal?"
  2. "If I talked to [stakeholder] today, what do you think their biggest question would be?"
  3. "Has anyone on your team seen something like this before and had a strong opinion, good or bad?"
  4. "Who's most likely to push back internally, and what would they push back on?"
  5. "If your team championed this internally, what would they need from me to make that case?"
  6. "Is there anyone who hasn't been part of these conversations who should be?"

Competitive

  1. "Are you comparing this to anything else right now?"
  2. "What would make you choose an alternative over this, if you were being totally honest?"
  3. "Is there something a competitor does that you wish we did too?"
  4. "If price and features were identical everywhere, what would actually decide this for you?"

Risk / concern surfacing (finish-line specific)

  1. "What's the one thing that could still derail this, in your mind?"
  2. "If this doesn't move forward, what do you think the reason will end up being?"
  3. "Is there anything you're not telling me because it feels awkward to bring up?"
  4. "On a scale of 1–10, how confident are you this gets approved internally?"
  5. "What would you need to see to move from 'interested' to 'ready to sign'?"
  6. "If I disappeared tomorrow, would this deal still move forward?"

What to do with the answer

  • A hedge ("probably," "I think so," "should be fine") is not a yes — ask a follow-up before moving on.
  • Log every answer under 7/10 or every hedge in your CRM notes — these become your objection-handling prep for the next call.
  • If three or more finish-line questions (35–40) come back weak, stop pushing toward close and go back to discovery on that specific gap.

How to use it

Pick 2–3 questions relevant to the stage of the deal you're in and drop them naturally into the conversation — never fire off a block of them like an interrogation.
