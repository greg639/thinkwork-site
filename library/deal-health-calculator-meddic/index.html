Deal Health Calculator (MEDDIC-Weighted)

A weighted scoring worksheet that turns what you know about Metrics, Economic Buyer, Decision Criteria, Decision Process, Pain, and Champion into a single win-probability percentage — plus a formula that names the exact gap to close first.

What's inside

  • The 6-element MEDDIC weighting model (weights sum to 100%)
  • A 0–5 scoring rubric with concrete anchor descriptions for every element
  • The weighted-score formula and total win-probability calculation
  • The 'single gap to close first' formula, based on weighted point loss, not raw score
  • A fully worked example with real numbers
  • Interpretation bands for what the final percentage should mean for your forecast
  • A tie-break rule for when two elements show the same weighted gap
  • A blank fill-in table to run on your own open deal

Score what you actually know — not what you hope is true — across all six MEDDIC elements, apply the weights below, and you get two things: a real win-probability percentage, and the single highest-value gap to go close next.

Step 1 — The weights

Not all six letters carry equal risk. Economic Buyer and Decision Process kill more deals late than any other letter, so they're weighted heaviest.

ElementWeight
Metrics15%
Economic Buyer20%
Decision Criteria15%
Decision Process20%
Identify Pain15%
Champion15%
Total100%

Step 2 — Score each element 0–5

Metrics 0 = No quantified metrics discussed at all 1 = You have a hunch about impact but no numbers from the buyer 2 = Buyer has mentioned a metric informally, unconfirmed 3 = Buyer has confirmed a specific metric and rough current value 4 = Buyer has confirmed metric + target/gap value tied to your solution 5 = Buyer has put the number in writing and will defend it internally

Economic Buyer 0 = You don't know who the economic buyer is 1 = You know their name/title but have never spoken with them 2 = You've heard about them only secondhand, through your champion 3 = You've had a direct call with the EB 4 = EB has personally confirmed priority/budget in a conversation with you 5 = EB is actively engaged — asking questions, pushing back, or opening doors for you

Decision Criteria 0 = Unknown how they'll evaluate or choose 1 = Informal sense of criteria, not confirmed by the buyer 2 = Buyer has listed criteria verbally, unranked 3 = Buyer has confirmed criteria and roughly ranked them 4 = Criteria formally documented (RFP/scorecard) and you've influenced it 5 = You co-authored or heavily shaped the criteria with the buyer

Decision Process 0 = No idea how a decision gets made or who signs 1 = Vague sense that "there's an approval process" 2 = Buyer has named the steps, not the people or dates 3 = Buyer has confirmed steps, approvers, and rough dates 4 = You have a mutually agreed close plan with dates and named owners 5 = Close plan is signed off by buyer and EB, dates tracking on schedule

Identify Pain 0 = No confirmed pain — this is a "nice to have" 1 = Pain implied but not stated by the buyer 2 = Buyer has named a pain point informally 3 = Buyer has confirmed pain and its business impact 4 = Buyer has connected pain to a specific initiative or budget priority 5 = Buyer has stated the cost of inaction in numbers, unprompted

Champion 0 = No internal champion identified 1 = A friendly contact, with no proof they'll act on your behalf 2 = Contact has advocated once (shared info, made an intro) 3 = Contact has taken a real internal risk for you (booked EB meeting, pushed timeline) 4 = Contact coaches you proactively on internal politics 5 = Contact has a personal stake in this deal's success beyond your product

Step 3 — The formula

``` Weighted Score (per element) = (Raw Score ÷ 5) × Weight Total Win Probability = sum of all 6 weighted scores

Weighted Gap (per element) = Weight − Weighted Score → The element with the LARGEST Weighted Gap is your single priority. ```

Win probability: 47%. Biggest weighted gaps: Economic Buyer and Decision Process, tied at 16.0.

Tie-break rule: when Economic Buyer and Decision Process tie for the largest gap, fix Economic Buyer first — you generally can't get real Decision Process detail (approvers, dates, sign-off) without direct EB access. Every other letter gets easier to move once these two are solid.

How to use it

Score each of the six elements 0–5 based on hard evidence (not optimism), apply the weights, and total the percentage before every forecast call; whichever element shows the largest weighted gap is the one action item you take into next week, not a general to-do list.
