Objection Handling Role-Play Script Bank for Team Training

Fifteen scored role-play scenarios across five objection categories, built to run cold in a 30-minute weekly team training session, with a shared scoring rubric and a monthly rotation plan.

What's inside

  • 30-minute session run-of-show (warm-up, 2 paired rounds, manager-led round, pattern debrief)
  • Scoring rubric: Acknowledge/Diagnose/Respond/Confirm, 1-5 each, 20 pts max
  • Category 1: Price — 3 scenarios with persona and escalation line
  • Category 2: Timing/Status Quo — 3 scenarios
  • Category 3: Trust/Past Experience — 3 scenarios
  • Category 4: Competitor — 3 scenarios
  • Category 5: Authority/Budget — 3 scenarios
  • 4-week monthly rotation plan covering all 15 scenarios
  • Facilitator notes: escalation-line rule, group scoring, role-swapping

15 scored scenarios for a 30-minute weekly session

Session Format (30 minutes, run cold, no prep needed)

TimeActivity
0:00–0:03Manager picks 2 scenarios for the week (rotate categories so all 5 get covered across a month)
0:03–0:05Quick reminder of the scoring rubric (below) — same one every week so reps calibrate over time
0:05–0:13Round 1: Pair up, one plays prospect (using the scenario card), one plays rep. 3 min role-play, 1 min self-score, 3 min group feedback
0:13–0:21Round 2: Swap roles, new scenario. Same structure
0:21–0:27Round 3: Manager plays the toughest prospect persona live against a volunteer, group scores together
0:27–0:30Manager names the ONE pattern seen across the session (not every mistake — one theme) and assigns it as the following week's focus

Scoring Rubric (use for every scenario, 1–5 each, 20 max)

Dimension1 (Poor)3 (Adequate)5 (Excellent)
AcknowledgeArgued or ignored the objectionGeneric "I understand"Named the specific concern back accurately
DiagnoseResponded to the surface objection onlyOne diagnostic question askedDug at least two layers to the real root cause
RespondGeneric pitch/reassuranceRelevant but generic proofSpecific, proportionate response tied to the diagnosed cause
ConfirmMoved on without checkingAsked "does that make sense?"Explicit check-back + concrete next step with date/owner

Category 1 — Price

1. "It's just too expensive." Prospect persona: Practical, budget-conscious mid-level manager, not hostile, just needs justification. Escalation if handled well: "Okay, but even if I agreed, I still need to justify it to my boss — what do I tell her?"

2. "Your competitor quoted us 30% less for basically the same thing." Prospect persona: Has a specific competing quote in hand, slightly testing to see if you'll panic-discount. Escalation if handled well: "So convince me the extra 30% is actually worth it — specifically."

3. "We'd need this to cost half as much to make sense for us." Prospect persona: States a specific number as if it's non-negotiable, watching to see if you fold immediately. Escalation if handled well: "What could you actually do at that number, then?"

Category 2 — Timing / Status Quo

4. "This isn't a priority for us right now." Prospect persona: Polite, non-committal, genuinely juggling other fires. Escalation if handled well: "Okay, say I bought into the urgency — what would actually need to happen in the next 30 days to make this real?"

5. "Let's revisit this next quarter." Prospect persona: Friendly stall, vague on why, avoiding a real answer. Escalation if handled well: "Fine — what specifically changes between now and next quarter that makes this a better time?"

6. "We're happy with what we're currently using." Prospect persona: Genuinely content, slightly defensive of their own past decision to buy the incumbent. Escalation if handled well: "Alright, tell me one thing that would have to break before you'd even consider switching."

Category 3 — Trust / Past Experience

7. "We tried something like this before and it was a disaster." Prospect persona: Visibly still frustrated, guarded, testing whether you'll dismiss the history or engage with it. Escalation if handled well: "Okay, but how do I know this won't be the exact same story?"

8. "I've never heard of your company." Prospect persona: Mildly skeptical, size/credibility concern, not hostile. Escalation if handled well: "Fine, but why would I bet on a smaller player over someone established?"

9. "Our last vendor overpromised and underdelivered." Prospect persona: Guarded, specifically worried about being burned again, watching your language for over-promising. Escalation if handled well: "Every vendor says that though — what's actually different?"

Category 4 — Competitor

10. "We're already in talks with [Competitor]." Prospect persona: Further down a parallel path, testing whether you'll badmouth the competitor. Escalation if handled well: "So what would you say is actually different, without just trashing them?"

11. "[Competitor] has a feature you don't." Prospect persona: Specific, detail-oriented, has clearly done homework. Escalation if handled well: "Okay, so how do you make up for not having that?"

12. "Everyone in our industry uses [Competitor]." Prospect persona: Social-proof-driven, slightly worried about being the outlier choice. Escalation if handled well: "So why would we be the exception?"

Category 5 — Authority / Budget

13. "I'm not the one who makes this decision." Prospect persona: Genuinely mid-level, cooperative but limited authority. Escalation if handled well: "Even if I liked it, my boss is going to ask me hard questions — what do I tell her?"

14. "We don't have budget allocated for this." Prospect persona: States it flatly, slightly closed off, testing whether you'll just walk away. Escalation if handled well: "Even if that were true, walk me through why I should go find budget for this specifically."

15. "I'd need sign-off from finance/legal/procurement before anything moves." Prospect persona: Process-oriented, not objecting to value, objecting to friction. Escalation if handled well: "Fine, but that process alone could take three months — is it really worth starting?"

Monthly Rotation Plan (so all 15 get covered)

  • Week 1: Price (1, 2, 3)
  • Week 2: Timing/Status Quo (4, 5, 6)
  • Week 3: Trust/Past Experience (7, 8, 9)
  • Week 4: Competitor (10, 11, 12) — Authority/Budget (13, 14, 15) folded into the Round 3 "manager plays toughest persona" slot across the month

Facilitator Notes

  • Always deliver the escalation line if the rep handles the first objection well — real objections rarely resolve in one exchange, and reps need reps at handling the follow-up push, not just the opener.
  • Score out loud as a group in the feedback minutes — calibration across the team matters more than any single score.
  • Rotate who plays the prospect — reps learn as much from playing a skeptical buyer as from playing themselves.

How to use it

Run the 30-minute format weekly, pulling 2 scenarios per session per the rotation plan, always pushing the escalation line when a rep handles the opener well, and score every round live against the same 4-dimension rubric so scores are comparable week over week.
