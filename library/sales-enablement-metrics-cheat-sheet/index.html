Sales Enablement Metrics Cheat Sheet

The one-pager of KPIs that actually predict revenue impact — ramp time, certification pass rate, skill retention, win-rate lift — set against the vanity metrics to stop reporting.

What's inside

  • 8 metrics that matter, each with its formula
  • 5 vanity metrics to stop reporting, with why they mislead
  • The 'so what' test for deciding whether a metric belongs on a dashboard
  • Ramp time, certification pass rate, and skill retention formulas
  • Coaching cadence adherence and win-rate lift formulas
  • Guidance on pairing every leading metric with a lagging one

Metrics that matter

MetricFormulaWhy it matters
Ramp time to full productivityDate of first full-quota-attainment month − start dateDirectly measures whether onboarding/enablement compresses time-to-value
Certification pass rateReps passing on first attempt ÷ total reps attemptingA low first-attempt rate means the certification bar or the training that precedes it is miscalibrated
Skill retention (90-day)Assessment score at 90 days post-training ÷ score immediately post-trainingTraining that doesn't stick is a cost, not an investment — this is the number that tells you
Coaching cadence adherenceScheduled 1:1 coaching sessions actually held ÷ scheduledCoaching that doesn't happen on cadence doesn't compound — this is a leading indicator for everything else
% of reps at Proficient+ per competencyReps scoring Proficient or above (e.g., 20+/32 on a skill module) ÷ total repsTells you where the org-wide skill gaps actually are, competency by competency
Win-rate lift(Post-intervention win rate − pre-intervention win rate) ÷ pre-intervention win rateIsolates the effect of an enablement intervention on the metric that ultimately matters
Time-to-first-dealDate of first closed-won ÷ start dateA ramp sub-metric that catches problems earlier than full-quota-attainment does
Content/practice utilization rateReps actively using an enablement asset in the 30 days before a stage change ÷ total reps in that stageDistinguishes assets that are actually used from ones that are just published

Vanity metrics — stop leading with these

MetricWhy it misleads
Training completion / attendance rateMeasures presence, not skill change. A 100% completion rate with 0% behavior change is a failed program reported as a success.
# of content pieces publishedVolume isn't value. Ties enablement's worth to output, not outcome.
Single-session NPS/satisfaction scoreReps can enjoy a session that changes nothing about how they sell. Satisfaction ≠ skill transfer.
Total LMS loginsA vanity proxy for engagement that says nothing about whether anyone learned or applied anything.
# of certifications issuedMeaningless without the pass-rate context (see above) — issuing certifications isn't the same as raising the bar.

The "so what" test

Before any metric goes on your dashboard, ask: "If this moved 10%, would revenue, retention, or ramp speed move with it?"

  • If yes — keep it, and pair it with the lagging metric it's supposed to influence (e.g., pair coaching cadence adherence with win-rate lift).
  • If no — cut it. It's decoration, not a dashboard.

How to use it

Use this to build or prune your enablement dashboard — every metric on it should pass the 'so what' test at the bottom, and every leading metric should be paired with the lagging metric it's supposed to move.
