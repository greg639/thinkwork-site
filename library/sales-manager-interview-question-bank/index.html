Sales Manager Interview Question Bank (120 Questions)

120 interview questions for hiring sales managers, organized by leadership, coaching, pipeline/forecast management, performance accountability, hiring/team-building, and cross-functional strategy — so panels stop improvising.

What's inside

  • 20 Leadership & Vision questions
  • 20 Coaching & Talent Development questions
  • 20 Pipeline & Forecast Management questions
  • 20 Performance Management & Accountability questions
  • 20 Hiring & Team-Building questions
  • 20 Cross-Functional & Strategic questions
  • A stage-mapping guide for which categories to run at which interview round
  • 3 annotated examples of strong vs weak answers

Assign each interviewer 1–2 categories rather than letting everyone ask a bit of everything — this stops the pattern where a candidate gets asked "tell me about a time you coached someone" six times and nothing else.

Category 1 — Leadership & Vision

  1. What does "sales leadership" mean to you, in one sentence?
  2. Describe the leadership style you default to under pressure — and one that's the opposite.
  3. Tell me about a decision you made that was unpopular with your team but right for the business.
  4. How do you build trust with a team you've just inherited?
  5. What's a leadership belief you held early in your career that you no longer hold?
  6. How do you decide when to be directive versus when to let a rep find their own way?
  7. Describe how you'd handle a top performer who's openly critical of a strategic decision you didn't make but have to enforce.
  8. What's your approach to communicating a target/quota increase the team will hate?
  9. Tell me about a time you had to lead through a layoff, restructure, or territory realignment.
  10. How do you keep morale up during a quarter you know the team is going to miss?
  11. What's the difference between managing a team and leading one, in your own words?
  12. Describe a time your vision for the team conflicted with your own manager's direction. What did you do?
  13. How do you decide what to escalate versus what to shield your team from?
  14. Tell me about a time you had to deliver a message you didn't personally agree with.
  15. What does "psychological safety" look like in practice on a sales floor, not just in theory?
  16. How has your definition of a "good week" as a leader changed over your career?
  17. Describe how you set the tone in your first 30 days with a new team.
  18. What's one thing you'd change about how sales leaders are typically evaluated?
  19. Tell me about the leader who influenced you most, and the specific thing you took from them.
  20. How do you know when it's time to walk away from a role or a company as a leader?

Category 2 — Coaching & Talent Development

  1. Walk me through your standard 1:1 structure with a rep.
  2. Tell me about a rep you turned around from underperforming to hitting number. What specifically did you do?
  3. Describe your call-review process — how often, what format, what you're listening for.
  4. Tell me about a rep you coached hard on and it didn't work. What did you learn?
  5. How do you tailor coaching for a rep who's defensive versus one who's coachable?
  6. What's the hardest piece of feedback you've ever given a direct report?
  7. How do you coach a rep who's hitting quota but you know their fundamentals are weak?
  8. Describe how you identify what a specific rep needs to work on next.
  9. Tell me about a time a rep pushed back on your coaching. How did you handle it?
  10. How do you balance coaching your weakest reps against developing your strongest ones?
  11. What does a great discovery-call coaching conversation sound like, specifically?
  12. How do you know when a rep has actually internalized feedback versus just nodding along?
  13. Tell me about a skill gap you noticed across your whole team, not just one rep, and what you did about it.
  14. How do you use call recordings or CRM data to make coaching concrete rather than vague?
  15. Describe your approach to onboarding and ramping a new rep in their first 90 days.
  16. Tell me about a rep you promoted or helped get promoted. What did that development path look like?
  17. How do you coach a veteran rep who's plateaued after years of solid performance?
  18. What's your view on role-play as a coaching tool — when does it work and when doesn't it?
  19. Tell me about a time you had to have a coaching conversation about attitude/culture fit, not skill.
  20. How do you measure whether your coaching is actually working?

Category 3 — Pipeline & Forecast Management

  1. Walk me through how you run a weekly pipeline review.
  2. Tell me about a quarter where your forecast was significantly wrong. What happened?
  3. What's your personal forecast methodology — commit/best-case/pipeline categories, weighted stages, or something else?
  4. How do you handle a rep who consistently sandbags their forecast?
  5. How do you handle a rep who consistently overcalls their forecast?
  6. Describe how you'd triage a pipeline that looks healthy in dollar terms but is stalled in stage progression.
  7. What CRM hygiene standards do you enforce, and how do you enforce them without becoming the "CRM police"?
  8. Tell me about a time you had to walk a bad forecast number up the chain. How did you handle that conversation?
  9. How early in a quarter do you know whether the team is going to make number?
  10. What's your view on pipeline coverage ratios — what's healthy for this kind of sales motion?
  11. How do you handle deals that have been stuck in the same stage for multiple review cycles?
  12. Describe how you'd rebuild a pipeline for a rep whose territory or book was just realigned.
  13. Tell me about the tools/reports you rely on most to manage pipeline, beyond gut feel.
  14. How do you balance chasing this quarter's number against building next quarter's pipeline?
  15. What's a forecasting mistake you made early in your management career that you don't make anymore?
  16. How do you handle a mega-deal that's propping up an otherwise weak forecast?
  17. Describe how you'd approach a territory or account-list redesign for your book of business.
  18. How do you decide which deals need your direct involvement versus staying hands-off?
  19. Tell me about how you use win/loss data to change team behavior, not just report on it.
  20. What's your approach to end-of-quarter deal review calls with reps who are behind?

Category 4 — Performance Management & Accountability

  1. Walk me through how you'd handle a rep who's missed quota for two consecutive quarters.
  2. Tell me about a time you had to put someone on a performance improvement plan. How did that conversation go, and what was the outcome?
  3. Describe a time you had to terminate an underperforming rep. What led up to that decision?
  4. How do you distinguish between a skill problem, a will problem, and an environment/territory problem when someone's missing number?
  5. Tell me about the hardest accountability conversation you've had with a top performer, not an underperformer.
  6. How do you hold a rep accountable without becoming a micromanager?
  7. What metrics do you use to catch a performance problem early, before it shows up in a missed quarter?
  8. Describe how you'd handle a rep who blames the product, marketing, or pricing for every loss.
  9. Tell me about a time you had to manage out someone who was well-liked on the team.
  10. How do you set expectations differently for a rep in month 2 of ramp versus a 2-year veteran?
  11. What's your view on stack-ranking or forced distribution for performance reviews?
  12. How do you handle a rep who's technically hitting activity metrics but not producing results?
  13. Describe your documentation practice when performance issues start — what do you write down, and when?
  14. Tell me about a time HR or legal got involved in a performance situation you were managing. What did you learn?
  15. How do you keep a high performer from coasting once they've already hit quota for the period?
  16. What's the earliest-stage warning sign you look for that a rep is going to miss number?
  17. How do you handle performance conversations with a rep going through something personal (health, family) that's affecting their numbers?
  18. Describe how you recalibrate quota or targets fairly when circumstances outside a rep's control change mid-year.
  19. Tell me about a time you disagreed with your own manager about whether someone should be let go.
  20. How do you make sure accountability standards feel consistent across your whole team, not selectively enforced?

Category 5 — Hiring & Team-Building

  1. Walk me through your interview process when you're hiring a rep.
  2. Tell me about the best hire you ever made. What made you confident in that call?
  3. Tell me about a hiring mistake you made. What did you miss, and what do you do differently now?
  4. What do you look for in a resume/background that predicts success in this specific sales motion?
  5. How do you evaluate coachability in an interview, specifically — what questions or exercises do you use?
  6. Describe how you build a diverse pipeline of sales candidates, not just the obvious network hires.
  7. What's your view on hiring reps from competitors versus adjacent industries versus first-time sales reps?
  8. How do you structure a role-play or case study in your interview loop, and what are you scoring for?
  9. Tell me about how you use reference checks — what do you actually ask, and what have references told you that changed a decision?
  10. How do you calibrate interviewer scores across a panel so the loudest opinion doesn't win the debrief?
  11. Describe how you build team cohesion when hiring several people in a short window (fast scale-up).
  12. What ratio of experienced hires to first-time reps do you aim for on a team, and why?
  13. How do you decide comp/offer negotiation limits before you're in the room with a candidate?
  14. Tell me about a time you had to convince a hesitant candidate to accept an offer. What worked?
  15. What onboarding elements do you consider non-negotiable in a new rep's first two weeks?
  16. How do you handle a candidate who interviews well but references come back lukewarm?
  17. Describe your approach to building bench strength/succession for your own role.
  18. What's a red flag in a sales interview that most panels miss?
  19. How do you keep your best reps engaged so they're not a flight risk during a hiring push?
  20. Tell me about how you've used data (not gut feel) to improve your hiring hit rate over time.

Category 6 — Cross-Functional & Strategic

  1. How do you work with marketing to align on lead quality and volume?
  2. Describe a time sales and product disagreed on priorities. How did you resolve it?
  3. How do you partner with Customer Success/Account Management on renewal and expansion risk?
  4. Tell me about a time you had to push back on a pricing or packaging decision from above.
  5. How do you use competitive intelligence in how you coach your team?
  6. Describe how you'd build the annual/quarterly plan for your segment from scratch.
  7. How do you decide territory or segment boundaries when the business is growing quickly?
  8. Tell me about a time finance/RevOps flagged a problem with your forecast methodology. What happened?
  9. How do you influence roadmap or product decisions when you don't have direct authority over product?
  10. Describe your relationship with your own manager — how do you manage up effectively?
  11. How do you think about the trade-off between short-term number and long-term pipeline health?
  12. Tell me about a cross-functional project you led that wasn't strictly a "sales" initiative.
  13. How do you handle a situation where marketing's messaging doesn't match what's actually working in the field?
  14. Describe how you'd evaluate whether your team needs a new tool (CRM feature, dialer, enablement platform).
  15. How do you think about compensation design — have you ever proposed changes to a comp plan, and why?
  16. Tell me about how you use win/loss and competitive data to shape go-to-market strategy, not just your own team's tactics.
  17. How would you approach entering a new market or segment with your current team?
  18. Describe a time you had to say no to a request from another department that would have hurt your team's number.
  19. What's your view on the sales leader's role in company culture beyond their own team?
  20. Where do you want this function/team to be in two years, and what's the first thing you'd change to get there?

Which categories map to which interview stage

  • Recruiter/hiring-manager screen: 3–4 questions from Category 1 (Leadership) + 2 from Category 5 (Hiring)
  • Panel round 1 (peer/skip-level): Category 2 (Coaching) + Category 4 (Performance Management)
  • Panel round 2 (cross-functional partners): Category 6 (Cross-Functional) + Category 3 (Pipeline/Forecast)
  • Final round (your manager/exec): Category 1 (Leadership) revisited at depth + 2–3 wildcard questions from any category based on gaps in earlier rounds

What a strong vs weak answer sounds like

Q62 — "Tell me about a time you had to put someone on a PIP": Weak: "I don't really believe in PIPs, I just have honest conversations." (Avoids the actual question, no evidence of a real process.) Strong: Names the specific situation (anonymized), the exact metrics that triggered it, the documented plan with dates, the check-in cadence, and the actual outcome — including if it ended in termination — plus what they'd do differently next time.

Q84 — "What do you look for in a resume that predicts success": Weak: "I look for hustle and a track record of success." (No specifics, no way to verify or replicate.) Strong: Names concrete signals — comparable deal size/cycle, quota attainment numbers with context, tenure patterns matching the company's stage — and specifically what they verify via reference rather than resume claims.

Q111 — "Short-term number vs long-term pipeline health": Weak: "Both matter equally." (Avoids the actual trade-off the question is testing for.) Strong: Gives a specific real scenario where they had to choose — e.g., pushing a rep to close a bad-fit deal to hit a number vs protecting churn risk — and explains the actual decision, the reasoning, and the downstream result.

How to use it

Assign 1–2 categories per interviewer before the loop starts so questions don't overlap, use the stage-mapping guide to structure the loop, and use the annotated examples to calibrate what a "strong" answer sounds like across the whole panel before interviews begin.
