Sales Org Chart Templates by Growth Stage

Three ready-to-adapt org charts for seed, Series A, and Series B+ go-to-market teams — with reporting lines, headcount ratios, and the specific trigger that tells you it's time to hire the next role.

What's inside

  • Seed-stage chart: founder-led, full-cycle AEs, when to add the first SDR
  • Series A chart: first management layer, SDR/AE split, first RevOps and CSM hires
  • Series B+ chart: segmented AE pods (SMB/Mid-Market/Enterprise), dedicated Enablement and RevOps functions
  • Management-ratio guidance per stage and per segment
  • A quick-reference table of hiring triggers (signal to next hire)
  • Span-of-control warning for scaling managers

Three ready-to-adapt org charts for go-to-market teams at seed, Series A, and Series B+. Copy the chart into your own doc, relabel names, and use the ratio/trigger notes to decide your next hire rather than your whole structure at once. Don't skip a stage's structure just because you can afford Series B+ titles — title inflation without the underlying management layer is how seed-stage sales orgs quietly become unmanageable.

1. Seed Stage (Pre-PMF to ~$1–2M ARR, 1–8 sales headcount)

Principle: founder-led sales, minimal layers, everyone touches the full cycle.

`` Founder / CEO (acting Head of Sales) │ ├── Founding AE #1 (full-cycle: source → close) ├── Founding AE #2 (full-cycle: source → close) └── SDR / BDR (optional — only once outbound volume justifies it) ``

  • Ratio: founder manages up to 4–5 reps directly; beyond that, hire a manager before adding more reps.
  • Notes: no dedicated sales manager yet — the founder is the sales manager and should personally run deal reviews. Every AE should be full-cycle; role specialization comes later. First SDR hire is triggered by outbound volume, not by "we should have one."
  • Trigger to move to Series A structure: founder can no longer sit in on every deal review, or you've hired past headcount 6–8 without a dedicated manager.

2. Series A (Repeatable Motion, ~$2–10M ARR, 8–30 sales headcount)

Principle: first layer of management, functional specialization begins, RevOps emerges.

`` VP Sales / Head of Sales │ ├── Sales Manager (AE team) │ └── AE ×6–8 │ ├── SDR Manager │ └── SDR/BDR ×6–8 │ ├── Sales Enablement / Ops lead (1st dedicated hire, often part-time/shared) │ └── Customer Success Manager (1st CSM — retention starts mattering) ``

  • Ratio: 1 manager per 6–8 ICs. Beyond 8 direct reports, a manager can't run weekly 1:1s, deal reviews, and live call coaching — use a lower ratio for complex/enterprise ACV.
  • Notes: replace "founder gut feel" hiring with a documented rubric (see the Sales Hiring Cheat Sheet) — you're hiring a team now, not one-off. First RevOps hire usually arrives after the second sales manager; the trigger is "nobody can tell me accurate pipeline coverage." CSM function typically starts as one person wearing onboarding + support + renewal hats.
  • Trigger to move to Series B+ structure: more than one clearly different AE segment (e.g., SMB vs. Mid-Market), or a single VP Sales can no longer manage both new-business and expansion revenue.

3. Series B+ (Multi-Segment / Multi-Motion, $10M+ ARR, 30+ sales headcount)

Principle: segmentation by deal size/motion, dedicated enablement and ops functions, CS becomes its own org.

`` CRO │ ├── VP Sales, New Business │ ├── Sr. Sales Manager — SMB │ │ └── AE ×6–8 (per pod) │ ├── Sr. Sales Manager — Mid-Market │ │ └── AE ×6–8 (per pod) │ └── Sr. Sales Manager — Enterprise │ └── AE ×4–6 (lower ratio — longer cycles) │ ├── VP Sales Development │ └── SDR Managers ×2+ (segmented to match AE segments) │ └── SDR/BDR ×6–8 per pod │ ├── VP Customer Success / Retention │ ├── CSM Managers │ │ └── CSM ×8–12 per pod (ratio depends on ACV/touch model) │ └── Renewals/Expansion specialists │ ├── Head of Sales Enablement (dedicated, full-time) │ └── Enablement Manager(s) — content, onboarding/ramp, certification │ ├── Head of RevOps │ └── Sales Ops, Forecasting/Analytics, Tooling/CRM admin │ └── Sales Engineering Lead (if technical sell) └── Solutions Engineers, aligned to AE segments ``

  • Ratio: 6–8:1 for SMB/transactional, 4–6:1 for Mid-Market, 3–4:1 for Enterprise. CSM ratios vary hugely by ACV — high-touch enterprise can be 1:10 accounts; PLG/self-serve can be 1:200+.
  • Notes: Enablement becomes a named function with its own budget — usually the highest-leverage hire at this stage, and the one companies delay too long. RevOps becomes the source of truth for forecasting and comp. Segmentation should be reflected in comp plans, ramp time, and enablement content — not just the org chart.
  • Watch for: span-of-control creep. Letting a strong manager keep 10–12 reps "because they're good at it" is the #1 reason coaching quality silently degrades at scale.

Quick Reference: When to Hire the Next Layer

SignalHire
Founder can't sit in every deal reviewFirst Sales Manager
Reps manually reconciling data across toolsFirst RevOps/Sales Ops hire
More than one clearly different buyer motionSegment the AE org
A manager has 9+ direct reportsSplit the pod / add a manager
New reps take >2 quarters to hit full productivityDedicated Sales Enablement hire
Renewal/expansion revenue > 25% of totalSplit CSM into onboarding vs. renewal/expansion roles

How to use it

Identify your current ARR/headcount band, copy that chart into your own org-planning doc, and use the ratio and trigger rows to decide your next single hire rather than restructuring everything at once.
