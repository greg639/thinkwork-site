Mutual Action Plan (MAP) Template

A shared, dated roadmap you build WITH your buyer, not for them, that turns a vague 'let me think about it' into a jointly-owned path to signature with a named owner on every step.

How to use it

Fill in the header and milestone table together with your champion on a live call within 48 hours of confirming mutual interest, then review and update it together, out loud, at the end of every call until signature.

What's inside

  • The exact words to introduce the MAP so it lands as mutual, not as a sales tactic
  • Deal header block capturing every stakeholder you need to track
  • Pre-built 13-step milestone library from discovery to kickoff, with owner and status columns
  • A section where the buyer defines 'done' in their own words
  • Mutual commitments table, what each side gives up to keep the deal moving
  • Risk and blocker log with an escalation trail
  • Executive sponsor alignment check
  • Revision log and a weekly review cadence rule that keeps the plan honest

What this is and when to use it

Introduce this the moment a prospect says something like "this looks like a fit" or "let's figure out next steps." The MAP only works if you build it together, live, on a call: a MAP only your side has filled in is just a project plan with a buyer's logo on it, and it won't survive the first internal priority shift.

The exact words to introduce it

"Given what you've told me about [reason for buying / internal deadline], most teams that get this live by [target date] end up building a simple shared plan: one page, both teams keep it updated, so nothing falls through the cracks on either side. Can we spend 15 minutes right now blocking out the milestones together?"

Do not say: "Here's our MAP for you to review." That reframes it as your document and you lose the mutual ownership that makes it work.

Cadence rules (what keeps a MAP from becoming fiction)

  1. Review it together, live, screen-shared, at the end of every call. Never let it sit unopened more than 7 days.
  2. If a date slips, say so out loud: "I see [milestone] moved, what changed on your end?" Silent date-slips are the #1 way MAPs stop meaning anything.
  3. Every milestone gets exactly one owner. If a row shows "Both," split it into two rows.
  4. Send the updated MAP as your post-call follow-up email, it replaces a generic recap and keeps both sides honest between calls.
