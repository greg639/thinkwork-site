Paper Process & Decision Criteria Checklist

The MEDDIC step most reps skip until it's too late: a checklist for surfacing legal, security, procurement, and signature-authority requirements early enough to protect your close date.

What's inside

  • Legal review requirements: MSA ownership, redline practice, DPA/GDPR needs
  • Security/InfoSec checklist: questionnaires, certifications, review owner and SLA
  • Procurement checklist: thresholds, RFP vs. sole-source, PO requirements, vendor onboarding
  • Signature and budget authority checklist by deal size threshold
  • A timeline-mapping step that backdates every paper-process step from your target close date
  • Suggested questions to ask the buyer for each checklist item, not just what to track internally

Most reps run a clean Metrics-Champion-Pain conversation and then get blindsided in the final two weeks by legal, security, or procurement steps nobody mapped. Run this checklist by the middle of your sales cycle — not the week you expect to close.

Legal

  • Confirmed whether the prospect requires their own MSA/paper, or will sign yours
  • Identified who reviews contracts — in-house counsel or an outside firm — and their typical turnaround time
  • Asked whether redlines are standard practice for this buyer or rare
  • Confirmed data processing agreement (DPA) / GDPR (or equivalent regional) addendum requirements, if applicable
  • Asked: "Last time you signed with a vendor our size, how long did legal review actually take?"

Security / InfoSec

  • Asked whether a security questionnaire or vendor risk assessment is required
  • Confirmed whether SOC 2 / ISO 27001 / equivalent certification will be requested, and whether you have it ready to send
  • Identified the security review owner by name and their typical SLA
  • Asked whether security review runs in parallel with legal, or only starts after commercial terms are agreed
  • Asked: "Is there a security review for every vendor, or only above a certain contract size?"

Procurement

  • Confirmed whether procurement is involved at all, and at what dollar threshold they get pulled in
  • Identified the procurement contact and their process — competitive bid, RFP, or sole-source justification
  • Asked whether a signed PO is required before invoicing or kickoff
  • Confirmed vendor onboarding requirements: W9/tax forms, banking details, certificates of insurance
  • Asked: "Once we agree on terms, what does procurement need from us before a PO gets cut?"

Signature & Budget Authority

  • Identified who has actual signature authority at this deal's dollar threshold (not just who's been in the room)
  • Confirmed whether multi-year or above-threshold deals require additional sign-off (board, finance committee)
  • Asked whether e-signature (DocuSign or equivalent) is accepted, or wet-signature/notarization is required
  • Asked: "At this contract value, whose signature actually finalizes it?"

Timeline

  • Mapped every paper-process step above onto a calendar, working backward from the target close date
  • Confirmed with the buyer or champion who owns each step, and what happens if a step slips
  • Built the paper process into your mutual close plan as named line items — not a single "contracting" bucket at the end

When to run this

Run the full checklist the moment Decision Criteria and Economic Buyer are confirmed — do not wait until you're asking for a signature. If your champion can't answer more than half of these questions, that's a sign you need a direct conversation with someone in legal, security, or procurement before you forecast this deal to close.

How to use it

Work through each section with your champion or economic buyer as soon as the deal is qualified, log the owner and date for every checked item into your close plan, and treat any blank row as an open risk to your forecast date.
