Demotivated Rep Coaching Guide

A word-for-word conversation framework for diagnosing whether a slump is a skill gap, a will gap, or a fixable circumstance, with a 3-condition test for whether you actually have grounds for a PIP.

How to use it

Use this as your script for the next 1:1 with a rep whose numbers have slipped: read the three diagnostic questions verbatim if you need to, then route using the table before deciding on any performance action.

What's inside

  • Pre-conversation homework checklist (4 items) before you sit down
  • Word-for-word conversation opener that avoids leading with the dashboard
  • The 3 diagnostic questions (skill / will / circumstance) with what each answer reveals
  • A routing table mapping what you heard to the next coaching action
  • The two-commitment close (one from you, one from them) with a scheduled follow-up
  • The 3-condition PIP test, only move to a formal PIP if all three are true
  • 3 explicit "what not to do" traps managers fall into

Diagnosing skill, will, or circumstance: before you write a PIP

Why this matters: Most managers respond to a slump with either a pep talk (works for about a week) or a PIP (works for compliance, not for recovery). Both skip the diagnostic step. A slump has exactly three possible root causes: skill, will, or circumstance, and each one requires a completely different conversation. Guessing wrong wastes a quarter and a rep you might have kept.

Before you sit down: do your homework (15 minutes)

Do not walk in cold. Pull:

  • Last 90 days of activity metrics (calls, meetings booked, pipeline created): not just closed-won
  • Last 90 days of conversation/call quality if you have it (call recordings, coaching notes)
  • Anything that changed in the last quarter: comp plan, territory, manager, team, product, personal life you're aware of
  • Your own honest answer: "Have I actually coached this person in the last 30 days, or just reviewed their numbers?"

Step 1: Open without the number (2 minutes)

Do not open with the dashboard. Open with the person.

"I want to check in on you, not just your pipeline. I've noticed [specific, neutral observation, e.g., 'you've had fewer meetings on the calendar the last few weeks'] and I want to understand what's going on before we assume anything. This isn't a performance conversation, it's a 'let's figure this out together' conversation."

Then stop talking. The most common mistake here is filling the silence with your own theory before they've answered.

Step 2: Ask the three diagnostic questions

Ask all three, in this order, even if you think you already know the answer. Take notes on how they answer, not just what they say.

1. The skill question:

"Walk me through your last discovery call, step by step. What did you ask, and what did you do with what they told you?" Listen for: can they describe a structure at all? Do they know what "good" looks like even if they didn't execute it? If they can't describe the mechanics of the skill, that's a skill gap, not a motivation problem.

2. The will question:

"When you sit down to prospect or make that next call, what's actually going through your head?" Listen for: dread, resentment, "what's the point," comparison to a better quarter. If they can describe the skill correctly but don't want to do it, that's a will gap: and your next question is why the will dropped (burnout, comp change, lost belief in the product, feeling overlooked, a personal event).

3. The circumstance question:

"Is there anything happening: on your patch, in the product, at home: that's making this quarter harder than it should be, that I might not know about?" Listen for: territory changes, a bad batch of leads, a product gap they keep hitting, a personal situation. If yes, you have a circumstance problem layered on top of whatever else you find: fix this first, because no amount of skill or will coaching survives a genuinely broken circumstance.

Step 3: Route based on what you heard

What you heardLikely root causeWhere to go next
Can't describe the skill mechanicsSkill gapTraining plan + shadowing + role-play reps (see Skill/Will framework)
Describes skill correctly, doesn't want to do itWill gapMotivation conversation: dig into why, don't just cheerlead
Something changed that isn't their faultCircumstanceFix the circumstance first; re-diagnose in 2 weeks if the slump continues
All three show up togetherCompound / at-riskDon't try to fix everything at once: pick the one causing the most damage right now and start there

Step 4: Close the conversation with one commitment each

  • You commit to one specific thing you'll do differently (fix the circumstance, add a coaching session, change nothing)
  • They commit to one specific, observable action for the next 2 weeks (not "try harder": something you can both check: "3 role-played cold opens with a peer before Friday")
  • Put a date on the calendar right now for the follow-up: don't leave it to "let's check in soon"

The PIP test

Only move toward a formal PIP if, after this conversation and one real coaching cycle (2–4 weeks) with a genuine attempt at the fix:

  • The circumstance has been addressed and performance still hasn't moved, and
  • The skill has been coached and practiced and still isn't landing on real calls, and
  • The will conversation happened honestly and the rep still isn't re-engaging

If you can't check all three, you don't have evidence for a PIP yet, you have evidence you skipped a step.

What not to do

  • Don't diagnose from the dashboard alone: activity metrics tell you that something's wrong, never why.
  • Don't lead with the PIP as a threat to "motivate" them: it collapses the will category into fear, which looks like compliance for two weeks and then relapses.
  • Don't fix the circumstance and skip the will conversation, a fixed territory doesn't repair a rep who's stopped believing they can win.
