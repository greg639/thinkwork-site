A score is only worth having if it isn't one person's opinion. Your marking is done by a calibrated panel of assessors; one call in five is independently marked a second time by another of them, working blind. This statement reports how closely those pairs agreed — the measure of whether the marking is consistent, and something you can place in front of your own clients.

What was measured

Each double-marked call is scored by two assessors independently; the second never sees the first's marks, the AI's score, the source report, or the participant's real name. Two figures are reported, and both are published rather than the flattering one alone:

Exact agreement
The two assessors recorded the identical 0–4 score on that criterion.
Within one band
Their scores differed by no more than a single point — the same judgement, one gradation apart.

What is excluded, and why

Where a selected second-marking pass yields no comparable score — for example a not-applicable pass, or a recording too compromised to score fairly — it is left out of the figures above rather than counted as disagreement, and noted here, so that a data-quality finding is never misreported as an assessor one.
