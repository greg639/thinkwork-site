Mindset 1:1 Coaching Script

The exact open-ended questions, in sequence, that surface a rep's real mental blocker in a 1:1, replacing the reflexive "how's pipeline" with questions that actually get an honest answer.

How to use it

Run the sequence in order during your next 1:1, spend 5-8 minutes on Layer 2 before touching numbers, and use the listening guide to catch avoidance language you'd otherwise talk past.

What's inside

  • A non-pipeline opening line that disarms the default status-update mode
  • A 3-layer question sequence: surface check, diagnostic dig, reframe + commitment
  • A listening guide, 6 language patterns that signal the real blocker underneath
  • Branch questions for when a rep deflects or minimizes
  • A closing accountability loop with an exact commitment question
  • A follow-up script for the next 1:1 that closes the loop

The problem this fixes

"How's pipeline?" trains reps to give you a status update, not the truth. By the time a mental blocker shows up in the numbers, it's been running unaddressed for weeks. This script starts everywhere except the numbers, and only gets to the pipeline once the real blocker is on the table.

Layer 1: Open (60-90 seconds, before anything work-related)

"Before we get into anything: how are you doing, actually? Not the pipeline version, the real version."

Wait. Let the silence sit. If they answer with a work update anyway, redirect once:

"That's helpful, but I meant you specifically, what's been sitting with you this week?"

Layer 2: Diagnostic Dig (5-8 minutes, this is the core of the script)

Ask these in order. Don't rush past a short answer: a one-word response is usually a door, not a closed one.

  1. "What's one deal or call this week that you've been avoiding thinking about?"
  2. "What's the story you're telling yourself about why that one's stuck?"
  3. "If I asked [a peer they respect] to guess what's really going on with that deal, what would they say that's different from what you just told me?"
  4. "When you think about picking that back up today, what's the first feeling that shows up, before you even think about strategy?"
  5. "Has this feeling shown up before this deal? When was the last time?"
  6. "What would you have to believe about yourself for this to not feel hard?"

Listening Guide: What to Listen For

While they answer, listen for these six patterns. Each one points to a different underlying blocker:

  • Externalizing blame ("the client's just slow," "marketing sent bad leads"), often masks fear of a direct ask.
  • Catastrophizing ("if I lose this one, the quarter's dead"), often masks a scarcity/money-mindset belief.
  • Vague deflection ("it's fine, just busy") on a question that should have a specific answer, usually means the real answer feels unsafe to say out loud.
  • Over-preparation talk ("I just need to research them more") on a deal that's been researched for weeks, usually masks call reluctance.
  • Comparison language ("everyone else seems to close these faster"): points to a comparison-trap belief, not a skill gap.
  • Flat affect on a win (describes a good result with no energy): an early burnout or disengagement signal, not modesty.

Layer 3: Reframe + Commitment

Once you've named what you're hearing, don't fix it for them. Ask:

  1. "If that belief wasn't true, what would you do differently on that deal today?"
  2. "What's the smallest version of that action you could do in the next 24 hours?"
  3. "What do you need from me: not more leads or more time, but from me specifically, to make that easier?"

If They Deflect (branch questions)

If the answer to any Layer 2 question is "I don't know" or a shrug, don't move on: go sideways instead of forward:

  • "That's fair, take your time: if you did know, what's the first guess?"
  • "What would you tell a teammate if they described this exact situation to you?"
  • "Is this more about the deal, or about something happening outside work that's showing up here?"

Closing: Accountability Loop

End every mindset 1:1 with this exact question, said out loud, not just implied:

"So the one thing you're committing to before we talk again is ___. Say it back to me so I've got it right."

Write down their exact words. Do not paraphrase it into your own language in your notes, use theirs.

Next 1:1, Closing the Loop

Open the following 1:1 with:

"Last time you said you'd [their exact commitment]. What happened?"

If it didn't happen, don't lead with disappointment: go back to Layer 2, question 1, applied to the commitment itself: "What's the story you're telling yourself about why that didn't happen?"
