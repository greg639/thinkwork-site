Cold Call & Discovery Roleplay Interview Script

A structured, 15-minute live roleplay hiring managers can run word-for-word to see real selling behavior under pressure instead of a rehearsed interview answer.

What's inside

  • Interviewer setup and prep instructions
  • The buyer persona brief to prepare before the interview
  • Minute-by-minute run of show across the full 15 minutes
  • Word-for-word objection lines to deploy at specific minutes
  • Escalating difficulty structure (cold open, mid-call resistance, discovery deepen)
  • Debrief question set for the final 2 minutes
  • Scoring notes mapped back to the 7-Competency Hiring Rubric

15 minutes, live, structured — built to surface real selling behavior

This roleplay replaces "tell me about a time you handled an objection" with an actual objection, live. Give the candidate the scenario setup only — never the persona brief or the objection lines below.

Interviewer Prep (do this before the candidate arrives)

  • Read and internalize the Buyer Persona Brief below — you are playing this person, not yourself.
  • Decide in advance which 2 of the 3 objection lines you'll use (don't use all of them on every candidate — pick based on role seniority).
  • Have a timer visible to you (not to the candidate).
  • Brief a second interviewer, if present, to take notes only — one person plays the prospect, the other scores.

Buyer Persona Brief (interviewer's eyes only)

You are: Jordan Reyes, VP of Operations at a 300-person logistics company. Surface demeanor: Busy, mildly annoyed at the interruption, gives short answers. Hidden reality: You do have a real, unaddressed problem — your team is manually reconciling data across three systems and it cost you a $40K reporting error last quarter — but you will not volunteer this. It only surfaces if the candidate asks a real diagnostic question about how your team currently handles the process this call is about. What you're testing: Whether the candidate gets past your surface resistance to find the real problem, or gives up / pitches features at your polite brush-off.

Minute-by-Minute Run of Show

0:00–1:00 — Frame the Scenario (interviewer, out of character)

"I'm going to play a prospect named Jordan Reyes, VP of Operations. You're calling me cold — I don't know you, I didn't take a meeting, I picked up because I happened to be at my desk. Your goal is to get 15 minutes on my calendar next week. Take 30 seconds to think, then call whenever you're ready. Go ahead."

1:00–1:30 — Candidate Prep (silent, candidate-only)

1:30–8:00 — Cold Call Roleplay (in character, ~6.5 minutes) Open flat and busy: "Jordan speaking." Give short, mildly resistant answers. Deploy objections at these checkpoints:

  • ~Minute 2 (early brush-off): "I've got about 90 seconds — what's this about?"

(Watch for: does the candidate ask a real question, or launch into a pitch under time pressure?)

  • ~Minute 4 (deflection): "Yeah, we've already got a process for this. It's not really a priority right now."

(Watch for: does the candidate accept this at face value and move to close, or probe what "the process" actually looks like?)

  • ~Minute 6 (stall): "Look, just send me something over email and I'll take a look when I get a chance."

(Watch for: does the candidate fold and agree to "send an email," or hold the line for a specific next step?)

If the candidate asks a genuine, specific diagnostic question about how the team currently handles the relevant process (not "how's business"), reveal a partial version of the hidden reality: "Honestly, it's a bit of a mess — we're pulling data from a few different systems by hand." Do not volunteer the $40K error unless they dig further.

8:00–9:00 — Transition (interviewer, out of character)

"Good stop there. Let's say that call actually landed a meeting. Fast-forward — I'm now sitting across from you for a discovery call. I've got real time now. Go ahead and pick up the conversation."

9:00–13:00 — Discovery Deepen (in character, ~4 minutes) Be more open now, but still don't volunteer the full picture unprompted. Reward specific questions with specific answers. If asked about business impact, reveal the $40K reporting error only if they ask directly what happens when the manual process goes wrong.

13:00–15:00 — Debrief (interviewer, out of character) Ask the candidate directly:

  1. "Walk me through what you think Jordan's real problem is, based on that call."
  2. "What's one thing you'd do differently if you ran that call again?"
  3. "Which moment in that call did you feel least confident in?"

Scoring Notes — Map to the 7-Competency Rubric

What you observedCompetency
Asked specific, layered questions instead of accepting "we have a process" at face valueDiscovery & Diagnostic Questioning
Responded to the actual words in the stall/deflection rather than a canned lineActive Listening & Objection Navigation
Connected the manual-process problem to cost/risk once uncoveredValue Articulation & Business Case Building
Held a clear, specific next step instead of accepting "send an email"Process Discipline & Pipeline Management
Response to debrief question 2 and 3 — specific, non-defensive self-assessmentCoachability & Resilience
Tone, pacing, and structure across the full 15 minutes, especially under the early time-pressure objectionCommunication & Executive Presence
Persistence past the first and second brush-off without becoming pushyDrive & Self-Generation

Score each competency 1–5 immediately after the debrief using the definitions in the 7-Competency Hiring Rubric, before discussing with any other interviewer.

How to use it

Print the Buyer Persona Brief for yourself only, run the 15 minutes on a visible timer without showing the candidate the objection checkpoints in advance, and score the 7-competency mapping table immediately afterward while the specifics are still fresh.
