Qualification Framework Comparison Guide (BANT vs. MEDDIC vs. CHAMP)

A side-by-side breakdown of BANT, MEDDIC, and CHAMP so sales leaders can pick the right qualification model for their motion, deal size, and cycle length — instead of defaulting to whichever one they learned first.

What's inside

  • One-line definition and origin of each framework
  • Comparison table: focus, pillar count, best deal size, best cycle length, biggest weakness
  • Decision tree: which framework fits your motion (transactional / mid-market / enterprise / champion-led)
  • When to blend frameworks (e.g., CHAMP for early discovery, MEDDIC for late-stage forecast)
  • Rollout guidance: what to change in CRM fields and call scoring per framework
  • Common leader mistake: mandating one framework across a mixed-motion team

The three frameworks in one line each

  • BANT (Budget, Authority, Need, Timeline) — IBM, 1950s. A fast go/no-go filter.
  • MEDDIC/MEDDPICC (Metrics, Economic Buyer, Decision Criteria, Decision Process, [Paper Process,] Identify Pain, Champion, [Competition]) — 1990s, PTC. Built for complex, multi-stakeholder enterprise sales.
  • CHAMP (Challenges, Authority, Money, Prioritization) — 2010s. Leads with the prospect's problem before money or authority.

Decision tree

  1. Deal size under $10k, cycle under 30 days, single decision-maker? → BANT.
  2. Deal size $50k+, multiple stakeholders, formal procurement? → MEDDPICC.
  3. Consultative motion where leading with pain builds trust before money comes up? → CHAMP.
  4. Mixed motion (SMB + enterprise in one team)? → Blend: CHAMP or BANT for early discovery, escalate to MEDDPICC fields once a deal crosses your enterprise threshold.

Blending in practice

Many high-performing teams run CHAMP's pain-first opening, then map straight into MEDDPICC fields once Authority and Need are confirmed — this avoids the 'budget-first' feel of BANT while still getting forecast-grade rigor for bigger deals.

The leader mistake to avoid

Mandating one framework CRM-wide regardless of deal size forces reps to either skip fields that don't apply (SMB reps ignoring Paper Process) or under-qualify complex deals (enterprise reps stopping at BANT's four pillars). Segment your CRM qualification fields by deal-size tier, not by team preference.

How to use it

Use the decision tree to assign a default framework per segment (SMB / mid-market / enterprise), then configure your CRM's qualification fields to match — don't force one model across every deal size.
