Negotiation Style Quiz

Every rep negotiates on instinct under pressure, and that instinct usually defaults to one of four patterns: competitive, collaborative, accommodating, or avoidant. None of them is "correct" — each wins in some situations and quietly costs you in others. Answer all 12 scenarios below the way you'd actually react (not the textbook answer) to see your default style, its real strengths and blind spots, and how to handle a counterpart who negotiates the opposite way you do.

Your numbers

Pick the instinctive first move, not the "correct" one.

Method & assumptions

  • Built on the two dimensions behind most negotiation-style models — how hard you push for your own interests, and how much you protect the other side's — collapsed here into four styles for clarity rather than the five-style academic version.
  • Each of the 12 scenarios scores to one style; your result is the distribution across all twelve answers, not a single fixed label.
  • This is a self-report reflection tool, not a validated psychometric instrument — use it to prompt self-awareness before a negotiation, not as a fixed identity.
  • A blended result (no style much above 40-50%) is common — most people's default shifts with deal size, stakes, and who's across the table.

How to use it

Work through all 12 scenarios picking the option closest to your real instinct under pressure, not the textbook-correct move — the quiz is only useful if it reflects how you actually negotiate. Your result updates live as you change answers. Read the strengths and blind spots for your default style honestly (every style has both), then use the "countering the opposite style" list before your next negotiation against a counterpart who reads differently from you. If you land on a blended result, that's normal — treat both listed styles as live patterns you flex between rather than picking one to identify with.
