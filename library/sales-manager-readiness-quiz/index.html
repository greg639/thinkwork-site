Am I Ready to Be a Sales Manager? Readiness Quiz

A 10-minute, 20-question self-assessment scored across five real first-time-manager competencies — coaching, letting go of your own bag, accountability conversations, operational rigor, and self-regulation — so you find your gaps before the promotion, not during it.

What's inside

  • 20 scored questions across 5 core management competencies (coaching, letting go of the bag, accountability, process rigor, self-awareness)
  • 1–5 scoring scale with clear behavioral anchors
  • Section-by-section and total scoring worksheet
  • 4 score bands from 'Stay in the seat longer' to 'Ready now'
  • A 'build it in your seat' table mapping each low-scoring section to a concrete pre-promotion action
  • A 'what actually changes on day one' reality check

Time to complete: ~10 minutes Who it's for: Individual contributors being considered for, or considering, a first-time sales management role.

How to use this quiz

Answer all 20 questions honestly — score yourself as you actually behave today, not as you hope you'd behave once you have the title. Use the 1–5 scale below for every question, then add up your total and read the scoring guide.

Scoring scale

  • 1 = Rarely / Not true of me
  • 2 = Occasionally
  • 3 = About half the time
  • 4 = Most of the time
  • 5 = Consistently / Always true of me

Section A — Coaching & Developing Others (not doing the deal yourself)

  1. When I see a teammate handling a call badly, my instinct is to ask them questions that help them find the fix — not to jump in and tell them what to say.
  2. I've spent real time (not just a Slack message) helping a peer improve a specific skill in the last 3 months, without being asked.
  3. I can describe what "good" looks like for a discovery call, a negotiation, and an objection-handling moment — in specific, teachable language, not just "read the room."
  4. I get genuine satisfaction from someone else's win, even when I had no hand in closing it myself.

Section A score: ___ / 20

Section B — Letting Go of Your Own Bag

  1. I could stop carrying a personal quota tomorrow and still feel like my work has value.
  2. When a deal I used to run stalls, my first thought is "what does my rep need to learn here," not "let me take this call."
  3. I'm comfortable being measured on my team's number instead of my own number.
  4. I don't feel the urge to jump on my best rep's biggest deal "just to make sure it closes."

Section B score: ___ / 20

Section C — Accountability & Difficult Conversations

  1. I've told a peer, directly and without softening it into mush, that their behavior or performance wasn't good enough — and stayed in the discomfort of that conversation rather than rushing to end it.
  2. I can give critical feedback without either sugar-coating it into meaninglessness or delivering it in a way that shuts the other person down.
  3. I hold people to a deadline or a commitment even when it's uncomfortable to follow up.
  4. When someone underperforms, I look first at what I could change (coaching, clarity, resourcing) before deciding the person is the problem.

Section C score: ___ / 20

Section D — Process, Forecasting & Operational Rigor

  1. I keep my own pipeline / CRM hygiene clean without being chased — accurate stages, next steps, close dates.
  2. I understand how a sales forecast is built (weighted pipeline, stage conversion, historical accuracy) well enough to explain it to someone else.
  3. I can read a set of numbers (conversion rates, activity metrics, win rates) and turn them into a specific action, not just a status update.
  4. I've run a meeting, a project, or a process for other people before (formally or informally) and it didn't fall apart without me micromanaging it.

Section D score: ___ / 20

Section E — Self-Awareness & Emotional Regulation

  1. When I'm frustrated with someone's performance, I can wait until I've cooled down before addressing it — I don't fire off the message in the heat of the moment.
  2. I actively ask people who report to me (or would report to me) how I'm doing, and I mean it — not as a rhetorical courtesy.
  3. I recognize when my own mood (a bad month, a rejection, a personal issue) is bleeding into how I treat other people, and I catch it.
  4. I'm as comfortable being wrong in front of the team as I am being right.

Section E score: ___ / 20

TOTAL SCORE: ___ / 100

Scoring Guide

85–100 — Ready Now You show the core behaviors of a manager already, informally. The title would mostly formalize what you're doing. Focus area: scale — the habits that work for 2–3 people need systems to work for 8–10.

65–84 — Ready With Support You have real strengths but at least one section is a genuine gap. Don't take the role blind — name the gap out loud to whoever is promoting you and ask for a specific 90-day support plan (a mentor manager, a coaching-skills course, shadowing 1:1s) targeted at your lowest-scoring section.

45–64 — Not Yet — Build These Skills First You're probably being considered because you're a great rep, not because you're demonstrating manager behaviors yet. That's normal — most first-time managers are in this range. Before you take the role: pick your single lowest-scoring section and spend 60–90 days deliberately practicing it in your current seat.

Below 45 — Stay In the Seat Longer This isn't a verdict on your ceiling — it's a timing signal. Taking a management role from this baseline is how good reps become resentful, ineffective managers who quietly want their old job back within a year. Revisit this quiz in 6 months.

Build It in Your Current Seat (no title required)

Lowest sectionDo this before you get the title
A — CoachingVolunteer to onboard the next new hire. Practice asking questions instead of giving answers.
B — Letting go of the bagMentor one peer on one specific skill for one full quarter and track their improvement, not your assist.
C — AccountabilityGive one piece of unsolicited, direct, kind feedback per week to a peer for a month.
D — Process/forecastingAsk your current manager to walk you through how the team forecast is actually built. Shadow one forecast call.
E — Self-awarenessAsk three people who work alongside you: "What's one thing I do that makes collaboration harder?" Then just listen.

What Actually Changes on Day One (read this before you say yes)

  • Your calendar becomes other people's calendars first.
  • Your win is no longer "I closed X" — it's "my team closed X, and three of them got better at something along the way."
  • You will have to have a hard conversation with someone you like, this month, probably about something small that's actually about something bigger.
  • Nobody will tell you when you're doing it wrong. You have to go looking for that feedback.

How to use it

Score yourself section by section, total the five scores, then read the matching band — if any section scores below 12/20, treat it as your pre-promotion development priority rather than something to figure out on the job.
