5-Touch Cold Email Sequence Template

A ready-to-send five-email cold sequence with subject lines, timing gaps, and a break-up email, each touch built around a different angle instead of repeating the same pitch.

How to use it

Load the five touches into your sequencing tool on the day gaps specified (Day 1, 3, 7, 12, 19) and pull any prospect who replies at any point, even with a "not interested."

What's inside

  • Touch 1 (Day 1), pain-led direct email with A/B subject lines
  • Touch 2 (Day 3), proof / social-proof email
  • Touch 3 (Day 7), no-ask question or resource email
  • Touch 4 (Day 12), trigger-event / urgency email
  • Touch 5 (Day 19), break-up email
  • Timing overview table (Day 1 / 3 / 7 / 12 / 19)
  • A/B subject line pairs for every touch
  • Sequence rules: 100-word cap, pull on any reply, subject line tone

Five emails, five angles, built-in timing gaps, and a break-up email that does double duty, it closes the loop and often gets the best reply rate of the whole sequence. Fill in the brackets once per persona and reuse.

How to use this: Load these into your sequencing tool (or send manually) exactly on the day gaps below. Don't send all five with the same angle: each touch is a different reason to reply, not a repeat of touch one.

Touch 1: Day 1: Pain-Led Direct

Subject lines (A/B):

  • "[First Name], quick question about [specific pain]"
  • "ramp time at [Company]?"

Body:

Hi [First Name], I'll keep this short. I work with [role/title] at [industry/company type] companies, and the pattern I keep seeing is [specific pain point: one sentence, concrete]. Is that showing up at [Company], or have you already got it handled? [Your Name]

Touch 2: Day 3: Proof / Social Proof

Subject lines (A/B):

  • "re: [specific pain]"
  • "how [similar company/category] handled this"

Body:

Hi [First Name], Following up in case my last note got buried. [Similar company/type of company] had the same issue: [one-sentence result, with a real number if you have one]. Took about [timeframe] to see it move. Worth 15 minutes to see if the same applies at [Company]? [Your Name]

Touch 3: Day 7: New Angle: Question or Resource, No Ask

Subject lines (A/B):

  • "one question"
  • "not selling anything in this one"

Body:

Hi [First Name], No pitch in this one: just curious: when [specific pain] shows up for your team, who ends up owning fixing it? You, or someone else? Asking because it changes how I'd even approach a conversation, if one made sense. [Your Name]

Optional resource variant: replace the question with a one-line link to a genuinely useful asset (checklist, benchmark, short guide) and no CTA beyond "thought this might be useful given [specific pain]."

Touch 4, Day 12: Trigger Event / Urgency

Subject lines (A/B):

  • "saw [trigger event], timing question"
  • "does [trigger event] change the calculus?"

Body:

Hi [First Name], Saw [trigger event: funding, new hire, expansion, job posting, etc.]. That's usually the point where [specific pain] either gets fixed or gets worse before anyone has time to look at it. If it's the latter right now, happy to grab 20 minutes: if it's already being handled, no worries at all. [Your Name]

If no trigger event is available, substitute a hard deadline/urgency angle: "Wrapping up outreach on this for [quarter], wanted to give you one more shot before I stop following up."

Touch 5, Day 19: The Break-Up Email

Subject lines (A/B):

  • "should I stop reaching out?"
  • "closing the loop"

Body:

Hi [First Name], I've reached out a few times about [specific pain] and haven't heard back: totally understand, inboxes are brutal. I'll take that as "not a priority right now" and stop following up. If that changes, or if I've got the wrong person entirely, just reply and let me know, happy to pick this back up whenever it's relevant. All the best, [Your Name]

Sequence Rules

  • Never send touch 2–5 to someone who replied: pull them from the sequence immediately, even a "not interested" reply.
  • Each subject line should look like it came from a person, not a marketing tool: lowercase, short, specific.
  • Keep every email under 100 words. If you can't say it in 100 words, it's not a cold email, it's an essay.
  • The break-up email consistently outperforms touches 2–4 on reply rate, don't skip it to "save" a prospect from feeling pressured. It's the opposite: it removes pressure.
  • If a prospect opens every email but never replies, that's a signal for a phone touch, not a sixth email.
