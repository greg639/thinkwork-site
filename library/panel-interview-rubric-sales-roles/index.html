Panel Interview Rubric for Sales Roles

A shared 1–5 scoring rubric across eight sales-specific competencies with behavioral anchors, so every interviewer scores independently before the debrief and 'I liked them' never gets to masquerade as a hiring criterion.

How to use it

Print one scorecard per interviewer per candidate, score independently during or immediately after the interview, and run the 15-minute debrief agenda exactly as ordered, lowest score talks first.

What's inside

  • 8 scored competencies with 1/3/5 behavioral anchors
  • Role-based weighting guidance (AE vs. SDR vs. senior)
  • Individual scorecard template for each interviewer
  • Red-flag list that overrides a high score
  • 4-step panel calibration protocol (silent scoring, lowest-scorer-first, tie-break)
  • 15-minute structured debrief agenda

How to run this: every interviewer scores independently, in writing, before anyone talks in the debrief. No score gets changed after group discussion starts unless new factual information (not opinion) comes up.

The 8 scored competencies

Score each 1, 3, or 5 (use 2/4 for judgment calls between anchors).

1. Discovery & Questioning Skill (Weight: AE/closing roles 20%, SDR roles 15%)

  • 1: Asks generic, surface questions or launches straight into pitching
  • 3: Asks relevant questions but doesn't follow the thread; misses obvious follow-ups
  • 5: Asks a question, listens to the actual answer, and asks a specific follow-up that goes one layer deeper based on what they just heard

2. Objection Handling & Composure Under Pressure (Weight: 15%)

  • 1: Gets defensive, argues, or folds immediately when pushed back on
  • 3: Handles it adequately but sounds scripted/rehearsed
  • 5: Stays calm, asks a clarifying question before responding, and reframes without being combative

3. Communication Clarity & Presence (Weight: 15%)

  • 1: Rambles, buries the point, needs multiple attempts to answer a direct question
  • 3: Clear but long-winded; gets there eventually
  • 5: Answers land in under 30 seconds, structured, no filler; visibly adjusts style for different panelists

4. Coachability (Weight: 15%: test live: give feedback mid-interview and ask them to redo a moment)

  • 1: Defends the original approach, doesn't visibly adjust, or agrees verbally but repeats the same behavior
  • 3, Adjusts the specific thing you flagged but nothing more
  • 5, Adjusts the flagged behavior AND applies the underlying principle to a different part of the exercise unprompted

5. Resilience / Grit (Weight: 10%)

  • 1: Describes failure only in terms of external factors
  • 3: Acknowledges some personal contribution to a past failure but the story is vague
  • 5: Gives a specific, owned account of a real failure, what they did differently afterward, and a measurable result

6. Process Discipline (Weight: 10% IC / 15% roles with forecasting responsibility)

  • 1: Can't describe how they track pipeline/activity beyond "I just remember"
  • 3: Has a system but it's informal/self-built
  • 5: Describes a specific, disciplined process unprompted and can explain why each step matters

7. Track Record Credibility (Weight: 10%)

  • 1: Numbers are vague, shift when questioned, or can't be tied to quota/context
  • 3: Numbers check out but candidate can't explain the "why" behind good or bad quarters
  • 5: Numbers are specific, consistent under questioning, and candidate can explain quota context, ramp, and market conditions

8. Values / Culture Add (Weight: 10%: score using the companion Culture-Fit Interview Guide's rubric)

  • 1: Value stories are generic/rehearsed, or conflict with a stated company value with no self-awareness
  • 3: Reasonable alignment, nothing notable either way
  • 5: Gives a specific, credible example of living the value under real pressure, including a time it cost them something

Red flags that override a high score

  • Speaks about a former manager, team, or company with contempt or blame, unprompted
  • Cannot name one specific thing they were bad at or got wrong in their last role
  • Inflates or can't reconcile numbers when asked a direct follow-up
  • Treats front-desk/EA/coordinator staff poorly before or after the interview, always ask
  • Talks over or dismisses a panelist based on seniority or role

Panel calibration protocol

  1. Silent scoring first. Every interviewer submits their scorecard before the debrief starts, no comparing notes beforehand.
  2. Debrief order: lowest scorer speaks first. Stops the most senior or most enthusiastic interviewer from anchoring the room.
  3. State the number before the narrative. Each person gives their total score before explaining why, surfaces disagreement immediately.
  4. Tie-break on disagreement >20 points between any two interviewers: re-interview the specific competency that diverged (20-30 min focused follow-up) rather than averaging out the disagreement.
  5. No score changes after discussion starts unless a panelist shares a specific fact the others didn't have (not a feeling).

15-minute debrief agenda

  • Min 0–3: Silent scorecards submitted, totals read aloud in order (lowest first)
  • Min 3–8: Each interviewer gives their single strongest reason for their score (30 sec each)
  • Min 8–12: Discuss any competency with >20-point spread only
  • Min 12–14: Check the red-flag list as a group
  • Min 14–15: Decision: advance / reject / additional interview, recorded with the reason
