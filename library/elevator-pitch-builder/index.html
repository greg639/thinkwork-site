Elevator Pitch Builder

Five prompts about your audience and product, one assembly formula, and two worked examples — enough to walk away with a practiced 30-second pitch in one sitting.

What's inside

  • 5 build prompts (Who / Problem / What you do / Outcome / Differentiator)
  • Assembly formula sentence template
  • Worked example — B2B SaaS (mid-market ops software)
  • Worked example — field sales (industrial equipment)
  • 5-item delivery checklist
  • 3 stress-test questions to check real differentiation

Answer the 5 prompts honestly and specifically (avoid jargon), then run your answers through the assembly formula below to get your 30-second pitch.

Step 1: Answer the 5 prompts

Prompt 1 — WHO do you help? Be specific — not "businesses," but the actual role/company type/segment. Your answer: _______________________

Prompt 2 — What PROBLEM are they dealing with? State it the way THEY would say it, not how your product brochure says it. Your answer: _______________________

Prompt 3 — What do you actually DO (in plain terms)? One sentence, no acronyms, no internal product names unless well known. Your answer: _______________________

Prompt 4 — What OUTCOME do they get? Ideally quantified: time saved, revenue gained, risk removed, cost cut. Your answer: _______________________

Prompt 5 — What makes you DIFFERENT? Compared to doing nothing, a competitor, or an internal workaround. Your answer: _______________________

Step 2: Assemble it with the formula

"I help [WHO] who are dealing with [PROBLEM] to [OUTCOME] by [WHAT YOU DO] — unlike [ALTERNATIVE], we [DIFFERENTIATOR]."

Target length: 65–85 words (≈30 seconds spoken at a natural pace).

Step 3: Two worked examples

B2B SaaS (mid-market ops software): "I help operations leaders at 200–2,000 person companies who are drowning in manual approval workflows get decisions made in hours instead of days, by automating routing and sign-off in the tools they already use. Unlike generic workflow builders, we ship with the approval logic for finance, procurement and HR already built in — so you're live in a week, not a quarter."

Field sales (industrial equipment): "I help plant managers who are losing production time to unplanned downtime keep their lines running longer between failures, by putting predictive sensors on the equipment that fails most often. Unlike a scheduled-maintenance calendar, we tell you which machine to check this week — not just when the calendar says to."

Step 4: Delivery checklist

  • Read it aloud — if you run out of breath before the end, cut a clause
  • Time it — if it's over 30 seconds, remove qualifiers and adjectives first
  • Remove every acronym a stranger at a bus stop wouldn't know
  • Say it to a colleague outside sales — if they can repeat the outcome back, it works
  • Practice 3 versions: formal (exec), casual (peer/networking), written (LinkedIn bio)

Step 5: Stress-test questions

Ask yourself — if you can't answer these, revisit Prompt 5:

  • Could a competitor say this exact pitch word-for-word? If yes, it's not differentiated enough.
  • Does the outcome contain a number? If not, add one, even an estimate.
  • Would your WHO immediately recognize themselves in the first clause?

How to use it

Answer the 5 prompts in writing first, drop your answers into the formula sentence, then read it aloud against the delivery checklist and time it until it lands under 30 seconds.
