Sales Competency Framework Builder Toolkit

Worksheets, a taxonomy starter, and rubric templates for building a defensible, role-specific competency framework from scratch — validated with your own top performers, not copied from a generic list.

What's inside

  • Step 1: role-family and level definition worksheet
  • Step 2: a 10-competency taxonomy starter list with definitions
  • Step 3: a behavioral-anchor worksheet with one fully worked example (Negotiation)
  • Step 4: a full validation-workshop script to pressure-test the taxonomy with top performers
  • Step 5: a role-based competency weighting worksheet
  • Step 6: a rollout and calibration checklist

Purpose

Worksheets and a starter taxonomy for building a competency framework that's specific to your roles, your market, and your sales motion — defensible enough to base hiring, coaching, and promotion decisions on, not a generic list copied from a blog post.

Step 1: Define Role Families & Levels

Before you write a single competency, define what you're building the framework for. Fill in:

Rule: a competency framework tries to serve one role family well. If you're building for AEs and SDRs simultaneously, expect roughly 60% shared competencies and 40% role-specific ones — don't force a single taxonomy across meaningfully different jobs.

Step 3: Behavioral Anchor Worksheet

For every competency you keep, write what 1 through 5 actually looks like — in your context, using your deals. Do not skip this step; a competency without behavioral anchors can't be scored consistently by two different managers.

Worked example — Negotiation:

LevelBehavioral Anchor
1Concedes on price or terms immediately when pushed, without asking for anything in return
2Holds position briefly but concedes without a clear trade; doesn't have a pre-planned walk-away point
3Consistently trades (discount for multi-year term, timeline for reference, etc.); knows their walk-away point going in
4Shapes the negotiation proactively — anticipates likely asks and pre-positions trades before the buyer raises them
5Others on the team come to them before their own tough negotiations; could run a negotiation workshop for peers

Step 4: Validation Workshop (Pressure-Test With Top Performers)

A framework built by one person in a room is a guess. Validate it with a workshop.

Workshop script (60-90 min, 4-6 top performers + 1-2 managers):

  1. Open (5 min): "We're building a competency framework to guide coaching and hiring. You're here because you're strong at this job, and I want to know if this list actually describes what makes someone good at it."
  2. Individual review (15 min): Give each participant the taxonomy from Step 2. Ask them to silently mark: which competencies are missing, which are irrelevant, and which they'd merge.
  3. Group discussion (30 min): Go competency by competency. For each: "Does this describe something that actually separates strong performance from average here? Can you think of a real example?" Cut anything nobody can point to a real example for.
  4. Behavioral anchor spot-check (20 min): Pick 2-3 competencies and read the Level 3 vs Level 4 anchor aloud. Ask: "Would you be able to tell the difference between these two in a real call?" If they can't, the anchor needs to be sharper.
  5. Close (5-10 min): Thank them, tell them what happens next (rollout plan below), and that their names will be attached to specific competencies as the internal "peer example" reference where relevant.

Step 6: Rollout & Calibration Plan

  • Framework reviewed and signed off by workshop participants (not just leadership)
  • Behavioral anchors written for every competency in the final taxonomy
  • First round of scoring done by managers using the Call Coaching Observation Scorecard and/or Team Skills Matrix
  • Calibration session held: 2+ managers independently score the same call/rep, compare, and resolve anchor ambiguity
  • Framework communicated to the team with the "why" (career/role/results — not just "here's how you'll be graded")
  • Re-validation scheduled for 2 quarters out — frameworks drift as the market and motion change

How to use it

Work through the six steps in order — don't skip the validation workshop even under time pressure, since an unvalidated framework produces inconsistent scoring the moment two different managers use it.
