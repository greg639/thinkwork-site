Manager Time-to-Coach Ratio Calculator

A formula and benchmark table for calculating the real percentage of a sales manager's week spent coaching versus admin and forecasting, measured against industry norms, so "I don't have time to coach" becomes a number instead of a feeling.

What's inside

  • The weekly time-log method (how to capture real data, not guessed data)
  • The 4 time-use categories to log
  • The formula
  • Industry benchmark bands
  • Worked example
  • Common false-positives (activities that feel like coaching but aren't)
  • Action table by benchmark tier
  • A ready-to-copy weekly log template

Most managers believe they coach more than they do. This isn't a character flaw — it's because admin work is made of small, frequent tasks that don't feel like they add up, and coaching is a small number of blocks that feel disproportionately significant in memory. The only fix is to log a real week and do the math.

Step 1: Log One Full Week (Don't Estimate — Track)

For five working days, log time in 30-minute blocks into these four categories:

  1. Coaching — 1:1s with a development focus, ride-alongs/shadowing with debrief, live call coaching, GROW-model sessions, skill-focused deal reviews (a deal review only counts here if it names a skill gap, not just deal mechanics)
  2. Admin — CRM hygiene/data entry, internal reporting, expense approvals, scheduling, email triage, tool admin
  3. Forecasting & pipeline mechanics — forecast calls, pipeline review meetings that are purely deal-stage/number checks with no coaching component, QBR prep
  4. Everything else — recruiting/hiring, cross-functional meetings, strategy work, firefighting/escalations

Be honest about category 2 vs 1: a "1:1" that's actually a status update on deals with no named skill discussed is Admin/Forecasting, not Coaching. This is the single most common place managers over-count.

Step 2: The Formula

Time-to-Coach Ratio = (Hours logged as Coaching ÷ Total working hours that week) x 100

Step 3: Industry Benchmark Bands

Time-to-Coach RatioTierWhat it typically means
Under 10%Admin-buriedCoaching is happening only in emergencies; skill development is not being systematically built
10-15%Below benchmarkSome coaching exists but is reactive, not scheduled; usually the first thing cut when busy
15-25%HealthyCoaching is a protected, recurring part of the week — this is the range most high-performing sales orgs target
25-35%StrongCoaching-first management style; typical of managers with smaller teams or reduced admin load
Above 35%Verify it's realOften a sign that "coaching" hours actually include status meetings mislabeled as coaching — re-check Step 1 categorization before celebrating

Worked Example

Manager logs a 47-hour week:

CategoryHours
Coaching5.5
Admin18
Forecasting/pipeline mechanics14
Everything else9.5
Total47

Ratio = (5.5 ÷ 47) x 100 = 11.7%

This manager sits in the "Below Benchmark" tier — coaching is happening, but at less than half the healthy-range target.

Common False Positives (Don't Count These as Coaching)

  • A deal review where you only discussed stage, next steps, and close date — no skill named
  • A 1:1 that was entirely a status/administrative check-in
  • Sitting in on a call without a debrief afterward (observation without feedback isn't coaching — see the Call Shadowing & Ride-Along Checklist)
  • Slack messages giving quick deal advice ("say X to that prospect") — this is deal tactics, not skill coaching, and it's usually too short to build anything durable

How to use it

Log a real week (not a "typical" estimated week) once per quarter, calculate your ratio, place yourself in the benchmark table, and use the action-table row as your next concrete step rather than a vague intention to "coach more."
