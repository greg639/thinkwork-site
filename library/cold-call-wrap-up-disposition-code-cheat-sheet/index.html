Cold Call Wrap-Up & Disposition Code Cheat Sheet

Exact CRM disposition codes, follow-up time windows, next-touch channels, and note templates for every cold call outcome a rep actually encounters. Keeps pipeline hygiene intact in the 90 seconds after the call ends.

How to use it

Print this or pin it in a browser tab next to your CRM. After every call, match your outcome to the table, log the exact disposition code listed, paste the note template, and set the follow-up task before you dial the next number. Review your team's disposition code usage weekly for the first month to catch old habits.

What's inside

  • Full outcome-to-disposition-code reference table covering nine distinct call results
  • Specific follow-up time windows for each outcome (e.g. call back in 2 business days, not same day)
  • Next-touch channel guidance per outcome (call, email, LinkedIn, remove)
  • Copy-paste CRM note templates with bracketed fill-ins for every scenario
  • Named rules for gatekeeper blocks versus actual buyer refusals
  • DNC trigger recognition and immediate action protocol
  • Dead number and wrong number handling with record-update instructions
  • Two most common post-call logging mistakes with explanations of exactly what breaks when you make them
  • Quick-reference decision tree for the nine-second moment of "what do I do now"
  • When NOT to use standard disposition codes (bespoke CRM environments)

Why the 90 seconds after the call matter

Most reps mentally move on the moment the line goes dead. That 90-second window, from hanging up to dialling the next number, is where pipeline hygiene silently breaks down. Wrong dispositions corrupt sequence logic, duplicate follow-ups, and mis-attributed "not interested" flags that write off prospects who were never actually spoken to.

This cheat sheet gives you one decision per outcome, made in advance. No thinking required post-call.

The Two Most Common Post-Call Mistakes

Mistake 1: Logging "No Answer" when a voicemail was left

These are different disposition codes and they behave differently inside most sales engagement platforms. "No Answer" typically resets the sequence step and allows an immediate retry. "Voicemail Left" should pause the sequence for your defined window (3 business days in this cheat sheet) and trigger the follow-up email step.

If you log "No Answer" after leaving a voicemail, the platform may queue an immediate call-back, you may call again the same day, and you look like you have no idea what you said to someone 6 hours ago. Over a 200-prospect cadence, this compounds into a broken sequence that neither you nor your manager can trust.

Rule: If you heard a beep and spoke words into a phone, it is VOICEMAIL LEFT. Full stop.

Mistake 2: Marking a prospect "Not Interested" after a gatekeeper refusal

A gatekeeper saying "he's not available" or "we're not interested in any calls" is not a buyer disposition. It is a gatekeeper disposition. The actual decision-maker has not been spoken to.

Logging CONNECTED - NOT INTERESTED in this scenario permanently flags a live prospect as dead and removes them from cadences they should still be in. This is one of the most common reasons reps underperform on total meetings booked relative to total dials made: they are disqualifying prospects who were never actually contacted.

Rule: CONNECTED - NOT INTERESTED is only used when you have spoken directly to the identified decision-maker and that person, by name, has declined. Gatekeeper blocks always use GATEKEEPER - NOT REACHED.

Quick Decision Guide: The Nine-Second Check

Ask yourself two questions the moment the call ends:

1. Did I speak to the actual target person?

  • Yes, go to question 2.
  • No (voicemail, no answer, gatekeeper, wrong number, dead line): use the non-connected disposition codes above.

2. What did they agree to?

  • Meeting booked: CONNECTED - MEETING BOOKED.
  • Call back at a specific or vague future time: CONNECTED - CALL BACK REQUESTED.
  • Clear refusal: CONNECTED - NOT INTERESTED.
  • Asked to never call again: DNC - DO NOT CONTACT.

Disposition Code Customisation Note

These codes are written as plain-language labels. Most CRMs (Salesforce, HubSpot, Salesloft, Outreach) let you map custom disposition values. If your CRM uses numeric codes or abbreviated values, print this table, add a column for your internal code, and map them once. The logic and time windows stay the same regardless of what your system calls the field.

If your organisation uses a bespoke or heavily locked-down CRM where you cannot add disposition options, use the note template field to record the outcome in the exact wording above. The note becomes your audit trail.

Minimum Standards for Every Call Log

Regardless of outcome, every call record should contain:

  • Disposition code logged before moving to next dial
  • Timestamp accurate (do not back-fill at end of day)
  • Note template completed with no blank bracketed fields
  • Follow-up task created with a specific date, not "someday"
  • Sequence step advanced or paused correctly per outcome
  • DNC flag applied immediately if triggered (never deferred)

When NOT to Use This Cheat Sheet As Written

  • Your team runs a single-touch outbound model with no sequences. The time windows still apply, but some sequence logic notes are irrelevant.
  • You work in a regulated industry (financial services, healthcare) with specific FCA or sector guidance on contact frequency and DNC handling. This cheat sheet does not substitute for compliance requirements. Add your compliance rules as a column to the master table above.
  • Your CRM auto-logs outcomes from call recording. Verify the auto-classification against these definitions. Auto-log tools frequently miscategorise short calls as "no answer" when a voicemail was in fact left.
