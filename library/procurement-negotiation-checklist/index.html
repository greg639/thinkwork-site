Procurement Negotiation Checklist

The 22-point pre-call checklist for surviving a professional buyer's playbook — authority mapping, non-price levers, and escalation lines set before procurement ever gets you on the phone.

What's inside

  • 22 checkable items covering authority, terms, and process before the call
  • A section on identifying real decision-makers vs. gatekeepers
  • Pre-loaded non-price concessions so price isn't your only lever
  • Standard procurement tactics to recognize in real time
  • 5 quick-reference phrases for use mid-call
  • Post-call escalation and contract-turnaround checks

22 points to work through before you take a professional buyer's call

Procurement teams run a playbook. This checklist makes sure you're not improvising against one.

Before the call

  1. I know the buyer's procurement process stage (RFP, sole-source, renewal) and the tactics typical at this stage.
  2. I've identified who on the procurement team has real approval authority vs. who is gatekeeping.
  3. I know my walk-away price — BATNA Worksheet completed — before this call.
  4. I've separated "must-hold" terms from "flexible" terms in writing, not decided live.
  5. I know my minimum acceptable term length and payment schedule.
  6. I've confirmed the champion/business owner is aligned with what procurement is asking for — or whether procurement is negotiating without their sponsor's knowledge.
  7. I have at least 2 non-price concessions ready (payment terms, scope, timeline, service tier) before I need to touch price.
  8. I know our standard discount approval thresholds and who signs off above them.
  9. I've reviewed recent competitive wins/losses on pricing in this same buying process, if available.
  10. I know whether this is a competitive RFP or a "beat down the single vendor" negotiation — they require different responses.
  11. I've prepared my response to "send your best and final offer in writing" (never do this without a verbal conversation first).
  12. I've identified any auto-renewal, most-favored-nation, or unlimited-liability clauses procurement typically pushes, and my position on each.
  13. I know which proposed terms are genuinely negotiable vs. company policy (e.g., payment terms flexible, liability cap not).
  14. I've confirmed the total contract value they're evaluating against — are they bundling prior years or add-ons into the comparison?
  15. I have a clear, rehearsed answer for "why should we pay more than [competitor/alternative]?"

During the call

  1. I know my own approval authority limit — the discount/term I can approve myself vs. what needs escalation, and to whom, in real time.
  2. I've set an internal signal with anyone joining the call with me so we don't contradict each other live.
  3. I know the deal's real timeline pressure on THEIR side (budget cycle, contract expiry, compliance deadline) — not just mine.
  4. I've prepared to actually walk away from a specific term, not just bluff, and know what I'll say if I need to.

After the call

  1. I've confirmed what happens next — step, owner, date — so a verbal yes doesn't stall in procurement limbo.
  2. I have the final contract/redline document ready to send within hours of verbal agreement, not days.
  3. I've briefed my internal deal desk/finance on the likely final number range so there are no surprises at approval.

5 phrases to keep on hand mid-call

  • "Help me understand what's driving that number — is it a ceiling or a target?"
  • "I can look at that if we adjust [term/scope/timeline] — does that work on your side?"
  • "Let me check what I can approve directly versus what needs a quick internal check — give me a moment."
  • "I want to give you a real number, not a placeholder — let's finish comparing scope first."
  • "What does your approval process need from me to keep this moving this week?"

Common procurement tactics to recognize

  • The silent pause after a price is stated (designed to make you fill it with a concession).
  • "Send your best and final" before any real conversation has happened.
  • Bundling this year's ask with next year's renewal to inflate the apparent discount.
  • Introducing a new stakeholder late to reopen an already-settled term.
  • Deadline pressure that doesn't match any real internal budget cycle.

How to use it

Work through all 22 items in order before any call where procurement is present, checking each box as confirmed; keep the 5 quick-reference phrases visible on screen during the call itself.
