Straight answers

Questions we get asked every fortnight

Answers need your real details
How old do you have to be to play airsoft at 1066?
You can play from your 12th birthday. Players aged 12 to 17 need a parent or guardian to sign the consent form — it is part of the online booking, so there is nothing to print. Under-18s play on rental kit and are briefed separately before the main brief.
Do I need a UKARA registration to play?
No. UKARA is only needed to buy a realistic imitation firearm. You can rent and play with us as often as you like without it. If you do want to get registered, play three game days with us over a period of at least 56 days and we will register you — your account tracks the count automatically.
How much does a day cost?
£30 walk-on if you bring your own kit, £45 for the full rental package, £26 for juniors aged 12–17, £5 for spectators. Hot lunch and unlimited tea and coffee are included in every ticket. Season Pass holders pay £24 a day.
When is the next game day?
Game days run every other Sunday, all year round, plus scheduled night ops, junior cadet days and 6-hour milsim events. The full calendar with live remaining spaces is on the .
I have never done this before. Will I be out of my depth?
About a third of every game day is first-timers. Book the rental package, arrive at 08:00, and a marshal will put you in a beginners' squad with someone who knows what they are doing. You will be fine by the second game.
Can I book a birthday, stag do or work day out?
Yes — packages start at 8 players and include kit, lunch, a private squad and a marshal of your own. Groups of 40 or more can hire the whole site on a non-game-day Saturday. Instant prices are on the .
What happens if it rains?
We play. It is Sussex woodland — bring boots and a spare pair of socks. Game days are only called off for high winds or lightning, and if we cancel you keep the credit or take a full refund, your choice.
Where exactly are you?
Adams Fruit Farm, Ivyhouse Lane, Hastings, East Sussex — about ten minutes from the A21, with hard standing parking on site. Nearest station is Hastings; we can usually sort a lift from there if you ask in advance.

Two websites

One business, one address, two sites

1066airsoft.com and 1066paintball.com are the same company, the same farm, the same phone number and the same badge — the logo has both names on it. Right now they are two builds, two sets of hosting, two content jobs, two Eventbrite streams and two customer lists that never meet. Use the front-door switch in the toolbar to see the same system answering both domains.

What the split costs

  • Search authority is halved — two weak domains instead of one strong one
  • A paintball parent never learns airsoft exists, and the other way round
  • Two of everything to build, host, update and keep accurate
  • Assistants see two entities at one address and trust neither fully
  • No shared account, waiver, basket or membership across the two

Recommended: keep both doors, share the building

  • Both domains stay live — they carry real search value for their own name
  • One booking engine, one account, one waiver, one basket, one admin behind them
  • Each domain leads on its own sport, with a real route to the other
  • Organisation markup naming one business at one address, so search engines and assistants consolidate rather than guess
  • Write each sport's content once, on the domain that owns it — no duplicate pages

The alternative is to fold paintball into the airsoft domain and redirect the other. Simpler and stronger for search, but it throws away whatever 1066paintball.com has already earned. Worth checking its traffic before deciding — that is a question for the owner's analytics, not a guess.

The rest of the discovery work

  • A real URL per game day, so a specific Sunday can rank and be linked
  • FAQPage and Event markup validated in Search Console
  • Name, address and phone identical on site, Google Business Profile, Bing Places and every airsoft directory
  • Review markup wired to your Google reviews rather than typed-in quotes
  • robots.txt explicitly allowing GPTBot, ClaudeBot, PerplexityBot and Google-Extended
  • Text, not images, for prices and times — a JPEG price list is invisible to an assistant
  • Location pages for Hastings, Bexhill, Eastbourne, Rye and Tunbridge Wells
  • Sub-second first paint on 4G in a field — no page builder bloat
  • Fresh-content feed: each game day writes a short after-action report automatically
  • Accessible markup throughout, which is the same work as machine-readable markup

What comes in-house

No third parties in the money path

Ticketing platform commissionRemoved
Customer email listYours, not theirs
Paper waivers at the gateSigned at checkout
Phone-and-text group bookingsSelf-serve with deposit
Cash on the dayPaid before arrival
PaymentsDirect to your Stripe

Card processing is still ~1.5% + 20p to Stripe — that is unavoidable. What goes away is the 4–6% platform fee on top of it, and the fact that the platform owns the customer.

How it would be built

Three phases

Phase 1 — Both sites, booking, payments, waivers, memberships, pre-orders, vouchers, shopNow
Phase 2 — Marshal tablets: car park, reception check-in, chrono loggingAfter go-live
Phase 3 — Match results, player of the match, the screen in the safe zoneLater

Phase 1 is built as a proper data model with customer records and staff roles, so Phase 2 is a new screen over data that already exists rather than a rebuild.

This prototype is front-end only — the flows work, nothing is stored and no card is charged.
