Sales Team Resilience Audit Toolkit

A team-wide diagnostic bundle: an 18-item anonymous survey, a scoring rubric with variance checks, and a leader intervention playbook, for benchmarking your team's collective resilience instead of guessing rep-by-rep.

How to use it

Run the anonymous survey with your full team, score it dimension-by-dimension using the rubric (checking variance, not just averages), then work down the intervention playbook starting with your lowest-scoring dimension on the 30-60-90 cadence.

What's inside

  • 18-item anonymous team survey across 6 resilience dimensions
  • 5-point Likert scale with reverse-scored items clearly flagged
  • Administration protocol covering anonymity, minimum sample size, and timing
  • Per-dimension and team composite scoring rubric
  • 4-band interpretation: Strong / Stable / At Risk / Critical
  • Variance/dispersion check for spotting hidden pockets of risk under a healthy average
  • Dimension-by-dimension intervention playbook mapped to score bands, with owner and cadence
  • 30-60-90 day action cadence template
  • Ready-to-run team debrief meeting agenda (45 minutes, minute-by-minute)

Individual burnout tools tell you about one rep. This tells you about the team, whether resilience is a team-wide asset or whether you have hidden pockets of risk sitting underneath a healthy-looking average.

Part A, The Team Resilience Survey

Administer anonymously. Use a form tool that doesn't capture names or emails. Minimum 6 respondents before you report results back, smaller groups make individuals identifiable and kill honesty. Run it outside quarter-end crunch weeks; a survey taken in the last 3 days of Q4 measures panic, not baseline resilience.

Rate agreement with each statement: 1 = Strongly Disagree, 2 = Disagree, 3 = Neutral, 4 = Agree, 5 = Strongly Agree. Items marked (R) are reverse-scored, subtract the raw score from 6 before including it in the dimension total (a raw 5 becomes a 1).

Dimension 1, Psychological Safety

  1. I can admit I'm behind on my number in a team meeting without it being held against me.
  2. On this team, mistakes get treated as something to learn from, not something to hide.
  3. I hold back what's really going on with my pipeline when asked directly. (R)

Dimension 2, Recovery Norms

  1. It's genuinely normal on this team to log off at a reasonable hour, not just technically allowed.
  2. People on this team regularly work through evenings or weekends to keep up. (R)
  3. I feel pressure to respond to work messages outside of working hours. (R)

Dimension 3, Manager Support

  1. My manager checks in on how I'm doing, not just where my numbers are.
  2. When I raise a workload or capacity concern, something actually changes.
  3. I'd hesitate to tell my manager if I were struggling. (R)

Dimension 4, Workload Distribution

  1. Pipeline, territory, and account load feel fairly distributed across the team.
  2. A few people on this team are quietly carrying more than their share. (R)
  3. My current workload is sustainable at this pace for the next 6 months.

Dimension 5, Rejection Resilience Culture

  1. When someone loses a big deal or has a bad stretch, the team rallies rather than distances.
  2. We actively talk about how to handle rejection and setbacks as part of how we coach here, not just deal mechanics.
  3. A bad week here feels like it follows you. (R)

Dimension 6, Growth & Mastery Orientation

  1. I can see a clear path for what skill growth gets me to my next level here.
  2. Coaching conversations here are mostly about numbers, not about building capability. (R)
  3. I feel like I'm getting measurably better at this job, not just busier.

Part B: Scoring Rubric

Step 1: For each respondent, score all 18 items (reverse-scoring the (R) items first).

Step 2: Average the 3 items within each dimension → dimension score (1.0–5.0) per respondent.

Step 3: Average each dimension score across all respondents → team dimension average.

Step 4: Average the 6 team dimension averages → Team Composite Resilience Score.

Interpretation bands (apply to each dimension and to the composite)

ScoreBandRead
4.0–5.0StrongThis is a genuine asset, protect what's driving it
3.0–3.9StableFunctional but not resilient under real pressure
2.0–2.9At RiskActively undermining performance and retention
1.0–1.9CriticalImmediate leadership intervention required

Variance check, don't skip this

A healthy-looking average can hide a real problem. For each dimension, also look at the spread of individual scores, not just the mean.

  • If the standard deviation on any dimension is greater than 1.0, you likely have a subgroup (a tenure band, a specific pod, or people managed by one particular lead) experiencing something very different from the average.
  • Where you can do so without breaking anonymity (groups of 6+), re-cut the dimension average by tenure (0–6mo / 6–18mo / 18mo+) or by team/pod. A "Stable" 3.6 average sitting on top of a 4.5-to-1.8 spread is not a stable team, it's two teams.

Work dimension by dimension, worst score first. Don't try to fix all six at once.

Part D: 30-60-90 Cadence

  • Day 0–30: Administer survey, score, identify lowest 1–2 dimensions, launch matching intervention(s).
  • Day 30–60: Run interventions consistently. Do not re-survey yet, give it time to actually land.
  • Day 60–90: Re-survey (same instrument, same anonymity protocol). Compare dimension scores, not just the composite, a flat composite can still show real dimension-level movement.

Part E, Team Debrief Meeting Agenda (45 min)

  1. (5 min) Frame: "This is about the team system, not any individual's number."
  2. (10 min) Share composite score and dimension breakdown: the numbers only, no names, no attribution.
  3. (15 min) Open discussion: "What in here matches what you're seeing? What surprises you?"
  4. (10 min) Announce the 1–2 interventions you're starting, and why those two first.
  5. (5 min) Set the re-survey date out loud, so the team knows this is a loop, not a one-off.
