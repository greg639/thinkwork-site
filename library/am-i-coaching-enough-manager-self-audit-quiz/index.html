Am I Coaching Enough? Manager Self-Audit Quiz

A 15-question audit that scores how often and how deeply you're actually coaching your team — against what top-quartile sales managers do every single week, not what feels like enough.

What's inside

  • 15 scored questions across Frequency, Depth, Follow-Through, and Deal-vs-Skill Balance
  • A 0-3 point behavioral scale for every question (not vague agree/disagree)
  • A 45-point scoring key with four performance bands (Reactive to Top-quartile)
  • A 'what top-quartile managers do every week' benchmark checklist
  • Guidance for turning your lowest-scoring section into one concrete habit
  • A built-in prompt to re-take the quiz in 60 days and track movement

Fifteen questions, about 60 seconds each. Score yourself against the last 4 actual weeks — not your intentions, not a good week you remember, the real pattern. Nobody sees this but you.

Section A — Frequency (max 15)

  1. I hold a 1:1 with each rep at least every other week, and it isn't the first thing cancelled when my calendar fills up.
  2. I listen to or watch at least one full call/deal recording per rep per week.
  3. I do live call shadowing or joint calls with each rep at least monthly.
  4. I have a coaching conversation that is NOT about pipeline/forecast at least twice a month per rep.
  5. I block dedicated coaching time on my calendar before the week starts, rather than fitting it in reactively.

Section B — Depth & Skill Focus (max 15)

  1. In my last 3 coaching conversations, I could name the specific skill (not just "do better") we were working on.
  2. I give feedback tied to a named competency (e.g., "your discovery questioning," "your objection reframing") rather than general impressions.
  3. I connect the skill we're working on to what it unlocks for the rep's career or next role — not just this deal.
  4. I use a consistent rubric or level description (e.g., Developing → Proficient → Advanced) rather than a gut-feel "good/bad" call.
  5. I can describe, for each rep on my team, the ONE skill that would move their results the most right now.

Section C — Follow-Through (max 9)

  1. Commitments made in one 1:1 are referenced and checked in the next one.
  2. I keep a written log of what each rep committed to improving, and when.
  3. Reps on my team could tell you, unprompted, what skill they're currently working on.

Section D — Balance vs. Deal-Rescue Mode (max 6)

  1. Less than half of my "coaching" time in a typical week is spent troubleshooting a specific deal that's stuck.
  2. When a deal goes wrong, my first question to the rep is "what skill would have changed this," not "what should we do about this account."

Scoring (max 45)

ScoreBandWhat it means
0–15ReactiveCoaching is happening by accident, if at all. Deals get attention; skills don't.
16–27DevelopingSome cadence exists but it's inconsistent or deal-hijacked.
28–36SolidClose to top-quartile. Tighten follow-through and skill-naming.
37–45Top-quartileCoaching runs as a discipline, not a favor squeezed in when there's time.

What top-quartile managers do, every week

  • A standing 1:1 with every rep, minimum 30 minutes, that survives a busy week.
  • At least one recorded call reviewed per rep, with 2–3 specific, skill-named observations logged.
  • One explicit "here's the skill we're building and why it matters for your next role" conversation per rep, per month minimum.
  • A written record of what each rep committed to last time, checked before a new commitment is made.
  • Under 50% of coaching time spent rescuing a specific deal; the majority spent building a transferable skill.

Turning your score into action

  • Find your lowest-scoring section above.
  • Pick ONE behavior from that section to install this week — not all of them.
  • Pair it with the First 90 Days of Sales Manager Coaching Toolkit and 1:1 Meeting Notes & Action Tracker if you're newer in the seat.
  • Re-take this quiz in 60 days. The goal isn't a perfect score — it's a rising one.

How to use it

Score yourself honestly against the last 4 weeks (not your intentions), tally the four sections, and use your lowest-scoring section to pick the one habit you'll install this week.
