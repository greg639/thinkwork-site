Discounting Policy Toolkit

A ready-to-adopt bundle - approval matrix, escalation ladder, and rep-facing one-pager - for governing discounts before they quietly become the default way your team closes.

How to use it

Publish the approval matrix and one-pager to the whole team, require the Discount Request Form for every discount conversation, and route anything above a rep's tier through the escalation ladder rather than ad hoc Slack approvals.

What's inside

  • Discount approval matrix by tier and deal size
  • Escalation ladder with named approvers and SLA turnaround
  • Discount request form fields
  • Rep-facing one-pager: do's, don'ts, and approved trade language
  • Quarter-end guardrail rules
  • Floor pricing governance rule
  • Leader rollout checklist

4. Rep-facing one-pager

Do:

  • Get something back for every discount - term length, faster payment, a reference, a case study, an expansion commitment.
  • Lead with value justification before any number is discussed.
  • Use the approved trade language below rather than improvising under pressure.
  • Log every discount conversation in CRM, including ones you declined.

Don't:

  • Never offer a discount before the buyer has stated a specific objection to price.
  • Never say 'let me see what I can do' - it signals the price is soft before you've traded anything.
  • Never match a competitor's quote number without seeing it in writing.
  • Never approve your own discount above your tier, even 'just this once.'

Approved trade language:

  • 'I can work with you on price if we can also agree to a 2-year term.'
  • 'That number works if we move to quarterly payment in advance rather than monthly.'
  • 'I can bring that to my team, but I'll need something back - are you open to being a reference once you're live?'
  • 'I don't have room to move on price, but I can move on start date / onboarding scope / support tier.'

5. Quarter-end guardrails

  • No discount tier above 20% may be self-approved by a manager in the final 5 business days of the quarter - Director approval required regardless of deal size.
  • Any discount justified primarily by 'it's quarter-end' rather than a stated buyer trade must be flagged to Deal Desk before it is offered.
  • Discounts offered in the final week of quarter are reviewed the following week for pattern (see Closing Ratio & Win-Rate Calculator, discount-adjusted win rate).

6. Floor pricing rule

  • Every product/tier has a published floor price below which no approval level may go without CRO + Legal sign-off, regardless of deal size or competitive pressure.
  • Floor price is reviewed and republished at the start of each fiscal year - reps are not authorized to negotiate below the version in effect at deal-open date.

7. Leader rollout checklist

  • Publish the approval matrix and escalation ladder somewhere every rep can see it (not buried in a wiki)
  • Set the floor price per product/tier and communicate it to managers only, not reps
  • Require the Discount Request Form as a CRM field, not a side conversation
  • Review discount patterns monthly - flag any rep whose average discount is trending up quarter over quarter
  • Re-certify managers on the matrix at the start of each fiscal year
