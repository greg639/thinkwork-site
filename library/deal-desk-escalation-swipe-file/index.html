Deal Desk Escalation Swipe File

Ready-to-send scripts and an approval matrix for escalating discounts, legal redlines, and non-standard terms — so a deal doesn't stall in a three-day email chain waiting on an answer nobody committed to giving.

What's inside

  • Approval matrix table (who can approve what, at what threshold)
  • Rep-to-manager discount escalation email template
  • Verbal script for reps asking for discount approval (built around trading, not just asking)
  • Legal redline escalation template
  • Non-standard payment terms/SLA request template
  • Deal desk SLA-setting script
  • Executive (VP/CRO) escalation template
  • Word-for-word script for relaying a declined request to the customer

Adapt the bracketed fields. Everything else is ready to send or say as-is.

2. Rep → Manager: discount escalation (Slack/email)

Subject: Approval needed — [Account Name], [X]% discount Hey [Manager], need your sign-off to move this forward. Account: [Account Name] — [ARR/deal size] Requested discount: [X]% off list ([$ amount]) Why: [1–2 lines — competitive situation, budget ceiling, multi-year commitment, etc. — not just "they asked"] What I'm getting in return: [multi-year term / case study rights / faster close date / expansion commitment — a discount should never be one-directional] Close date at risk if not approved by: [date] Can you approve, or do you need 10 minutes to talk it through?

3. Rep asking manager verbally (word-for-word)

"I've got a customer asking for [X]% off list on [account]. Before I say yes or no, I want to walk you through what I'd get in exchange, because I don't want to just give it away. Here's the trade I'm proposing: [trade]. Does that work, or is there a better trade you'd make here?"

(This script trains reps to never bring a bare discount ask — always a trade. That's the habit worth installing, not just the approval.)

4. Rep → Deal Desk: legal redline escalation

Subject: Redline review needed — [Account Name] — [close date] Account: [Account Name] Clause(s) in question: [e.g., liability cap, indemnification, data processing terms] Customer's ask: [paste their redline or summarize] Our standard position: [what our paper says] Business context: [deal size, close date, whether this is a template-setting precedent for other prospects] My read on how hard they'll push back if we hold our line: [soft ask / hard requirement / unknown] Flagging this is time-sensitive — customer's legal wants a redline back by [date]. Can we get 15 minutes today or tomorrow?

5. Non-standard terms request (payment terms, custom SLA, etc.)

Subject: Non-standard terms request — [Account Name] What's being requested: [e.g., Net 90 payment terms / 99.99% uptime SLA / custom data residency] Why standard terms don't work for this account: [their internal process, regulatory requirement, etc. — get the real reason, not "they just asked"] Precedent risk: [is this a one-off, or will the next 10 deals ask for the same thing?] Recommendation: [approve as one-off / approve with sunset clause / decline and offer alternative]

6. Deal Desk → Rep: setting the SLA on turnaround (say this proactively, don't wait to be asked)

"Standard deal desk turnaround is [X business hours] for discount requests and [Y business days] for legal redlines. If you need faster, flag it as urgent with the close date and I'll do what I can — but build that lead time into your close plan now, not the day before quarter-end."

7. Escalating past your manager to VP/CRO (use sparingly — burn this bridge carefully)

Subject: Time-sensitive approval — [Account Name] — needs your call [Manager] and I have looked at this and it's above both our approval limits. The ask: [specific discount/term] The trade: [what we get] What happens if we say no: [realistic consequence — lost deal, delayed close, competitor wins — not inflated] What happens if we say yes: [precedent risk, margin impact] We need an answer by: [date/time] [Manager] can give you the full context in 5 minutes if useful.

8. Saying no (word-for-word, for the rep to relay to the customer)

"I took this to our deal desk, and here's where we landed: we can't do [X], but here's what we can do — [alternative]. That's really the best we can put together on our end. Does that work for you, or should we talk through what matters most so I can see if there's another way to get there?"

(Never relay a "no" without an alternative in the same sentence — it turns a rejection into a negotiation.)

How to use it

Fill in your own approval thresholds in the matrix, then have reps and managers copy the scripts directly into Slack or email when a deal needs sign-off.
