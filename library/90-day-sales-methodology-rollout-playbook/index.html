90-Day Sales Methodology Rollout Playbook

A week-by-week, four-phase plan for training, certifying, and enforcing a new sales methodology across 90 days without losing a quarter to change fatigue.

What's inside

  • 4-phase, week-by-week checklist covering Weeks 1 through 13
  • Concrete deliverables and owners for every week
  • Change-fatigue guardrails: single-initiative rule, 2-3 tracked leading indicators, scope caps
  • 'What we're NOT changing' communication tactic for Week 1
  • Full RACI table across Exec Sponsor, Enablement, Managers, RevOps, and Reps

Week-by-week. Four phases, each with a deliverable — not a vague "training happens sometime in Q2."

Phase 1 — Foundation (Weeks 1–2)

Week 1

  • Executive sponsor states the "why now" in one paragraph, communicated to the full team (not "we're adopting a new methodology," but the specific business problem it fixes: forecast accuracy, ramp time, win rate)
  • Methodology (or blend) selected — use the Sales Methodology Selection Decision Tree if undecided
  • Adoption metrics defined up front and tied to the CRM Compliance Scorecard fields, so "adoption" has a number attached from day one
  • Internal champions/certified trainers identified (senior reps or managers, not just the enablement team)
  • Explicit "what we are NOT changing" list published (comp plan structure, territories, tools) — reduces anxiety surface before training starts

Week 2

  • CRM fields built or modified, mapped to methodology elements (see Compliance Scorecard field map)
  • Reporting dashboard built so adoption is visible from week one, even at zero
  • Training curriculum and role-play Scenario Bank finalized
  • Pilot cohort selected — 10–20% of the team, a deliberate mix of top and mid performers, not just volunteers

Phase 2 — Pilot (Weeks 3–6)

Week 3 — Pilot cohort completes live training on the core framework; terminology quiz administered.

Week 4 — Pilot cohort completes role-play certification against the standard rubric; friction points and real objections from the field are logged.

Week 5 — Training materials revised based on pilot feedback; pilot cohort's managers trained on the Deal Review question bank.

Week 6 — Pilot cohort begins applying the methodology to live deals with manager co-review; 10 real, anonymized deal examples captured for use in full rollout training.

Phase 3 — Full Rollout (Weeks 7–9)

Week 7 — All-hands training kickoff using the refined curriculum plus the pilot's real deal examples; all managers trained on the Deal Review structure.

Week 8 — Full-team role-play certification, staggered by team over 2–3 days each; non-certified reps get a named remediation plan and date.

Week 9 — CRM go-live: legacy free-text fields deprecated or hidden; new structured fields become mandatory at stage-gate; certification becomes a prerequisite for running live, unsupervised discovery calls (ties directly to the Ramp Checklist).

Phase 4 — Enforcement & Reinforcement (Weeks 10–13 / Day 90)

Week 10 — First full-team Deal Reviews run on the new question bank; managers apply the red-flag list; Pipeline Hygiene Scorecard baseline captured.

Week 11 — First CRM Compliance Scorecard run org-wide; adoption % published by rep and team, no shaming — a target-setting conversation for anyone under 70%.

Week 12 — Recognition pass: spotlight reps hitting both compliance and pipeline hygiene targets; certified reps present a real deal to the full team (peer teach-back).

Week 13 (Day 90) — Executive readout: adoption %, pipeline hygiene trend, forecast accuracy trend if measurable yet, lessons learned. Lock in ongoing cadence (monthly compliance audit, quarterly re-certification). Ownership transitions from the rollout project team to frontline managers — the rollout is now just "how we run pipeline."

Change-fatigue guardrails

  • Never run more than one major adoption initiative at a time.
  • Cap new process asks to one new CRM field group and one new meeting ritual per rollout — piling on more guarantees rejection.
  • Tie adoption to recognition (and, ideally, a comp/spiff mechanism) so it isn't unrewarded extra work layered onto quota.
  • Track only 2–3 leading indicators for the full 90 days: certification %, CRM field compliance %, pipeline hygiene score. Resist adding more dashboards mid-rollout.
  • Publish the "what we're NOT changing" list in Week 1 and refer back to it — most rollout anxiety is about unstated scope, not the methodology itself.

How to use it

Follow the phase checklists in order starting Week 1, assign the RACI table to real names before kickoff, and hold the line on the change-fatigue guardrails (one initiative at a time, 2-3 tracked metrics only).
