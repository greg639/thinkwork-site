Manager's First 1:1 Guide for New Hires

A minute-by-minute agenda for a new hire's first one-on-one, built around trust instead of performance review, with exact scripts for what to say and a clear list of what to deliberately leave out.

What's inside

  • The single goal of session one (trust, not performance)
  • 4 things to deliberately skip
  • 5-part, 30-minute agenda with verbatim scripts
  • Sample question bank
  • Red flags to note (not act on) in session one
  • Cadence table for weeks 1 through 12

The first 1:1 sets the tone for the entire relationship. Get it wrong — too much process, not enough person — and you spend months undoing a bad first impression. This guide is exactly what to cover, what to deliberately skip, and the actual questions to ask.

The Goal of Session One

Not performance. Not process. Trust. A new hire who leaves their first 1:1 thinking "my manager is someone I can be honest with" will surface problems early, when they're cheap to fix. A new hire who leaves thinking "my manager just wants updates" will hide problems until they're expensive.

What to Skip in Session One

  • Do not review pipeline numbers or activity metrics — there's nothing real yet, and it signals the wrong priority
  • Do not dive into a full performance-expectations lecture — that's session two or three's job, once they've had a week to breathe
  • Do not fill the whole 30 minutes with logistics (badge, laptop, benefits) — that's HR/IT's job, not yours; confirm it's handled, don't re-do it
  • Do not make it entirely small talk either — new hires read an all-fluff first 1:1 as "my manager doesn't take this seriously"

Agenda (30 minutes)

1. Open human, not transactional (5 min)

"Before anything else — how's the week actually been? Not the polished answer, the real one."

Listen. Don't rush past whatever they say, even if it's mundane ("still figuring out where the bathroom is" is a fine, real answer).

2. Set the working relationship, explicitly (10 min)

Say these things out loud — don't assume they'll infer them:

  • "Here's how I like to work: [your actual style — e.g., 'I prefer direct feedback over cushioning it, I check Slack in the morning and evening not constantly, I'd rather you interrupt me with a blocker than sit on it']"
  • "Here's what a good week looks like for you at this stage — not numbers, just effort and behavior: [e.g., 'shadowing 3+ calls, asking your buddy questions, getting comfortable in the CRM']"
  • "Here's how often we'll meet and what these will be for: [cadence + purpose]"
  • Ask them directly: "What's your preferred way to get feedback — in the moment, or saved for our 1:1s? And is there anything from a past manager that worked really well for you, or really didn't?"

3. Ask about their "why" (5 min)

"What made you take this role, out of everything you could have done?"

This answer is your anchor for every future coaching conversation — refer back to it when things get hard in month three.

4. Name the safety net, specifically (5 min)

"You are going to say the wrong thing on a call, misclick in the CRM, or ask a question that feels obvious, in your first month. That's expected, not a sign you're behind. If something goes wrong, I'd rather hear about it from you in this room than find out some other way."

5. Close with a concrete next step (5 min)

  • Confirm their mentor/buddy pairing and when they first meet
  • Confirm what they should be doing between now and the next 1:1 (specific, small — e.g., "shadow 2 calls, meet your buddy once")
  • Set the date/time for 1:1 #2 right now, on the spot

Sample Questions Bank (pull 2–3, don't run all of them)

  • "What's made you nervous about starting, if anything?"
  • "Is there anything from your last role — good or bad — you want to bring with you or leave behind?"
  • "What does support look like for you when you're stuck — do you want me to jump in, or point you at someone, or just listen?"
  • "Anything logistically still unresolved that's bugging you?"

Red Flags to Notice (don't act on day one, just note them)

  • Deflecting every personal question back to "I'm fine, just excited to get started" repeatedly — may signal guardedness worth gently revisiting later
  • Asking zero questions about the role/team at all — may signal disengagement or overwhelm
  • Over-promising specifics ("I'll hit quota by month two") — worth a gentle reality-check now rather than a hard correction in month two

Cadence After Session One

Week1:1 Focus
1Trust + working relationship (this guide)
2Logistics/systems check-in, first shadow debrief
3–4Skill-building focus, first live call debrief
5–8Weekly, pipeline + skill mix
9–12Shift toward ramp milestone check-ins (see Ramp Milestone Tracker)

One-Line Summary to Remember

Session one builds the container; everything else you'll ever discuss in a 1:1 gets poured into it. Spend the 30 minutes on the container.

How to use it

Run the 30-minute agenda near-verbatim in week one of every new hire's start, and set the next 1:1 date before you end the meeting.
