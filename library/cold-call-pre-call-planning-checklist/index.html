Cold Call Pre-Call Planning Checklist

The 9-point research and mindset checklist top performers run through before every single dial — a 90-second ritual, not a one-time prep session.

What's inside

  • Contact verification (name, title, pronunciation)
  • Trigger/reason-for-calling-today identification
  • Time-capped company research (2 minutes)
  • Single call objective definition
  • Opening line rehearsed aloud
  • Anticipated objection with a ready response
  • Physical and vocal state check
  • Calendar open and visible
  • Result-independent mindset set before dialing
  • Guidance on which points to re-check for every dial in a block

Run this checklist before every dial — not just your first call of the day. Top performers treat this as a 90-second ritual, not a one-time prep session. Print it, laminate it, or keep it pinned next to your dialer.

The 9-Point Checklist

1. ☐ Contact verified — name, title, correct pronunciation Confirm you have the right person, current title, and that you can say their name correctly out loud once before dialing. Mispronouncing a name in the first five seconds costs credibility you can't get back.

2. ☐ One real trigger or reason identified for calling today Not "because they're on my list" — a specific, current reason: a hire, a funding round, a product launch, an expiring contract, a mutual connection. If you can't name one in five words, you're not ready to dial — a "reason for today" call outperforms a generic list-dial every time.

3. ☐ Company research capped at 2 minutes One pass: what they do, one recent news item, a headcount/size signal. Do not spend 15 minutes here — over-research is a form of stalling, not preparation.

4. ☐ Single objective for this call defined Decide the one next step you're calling to secure (a meeting, a qualifying answer, a referral to the right person) before you dial. A call without a defined objective drifts and rarely closes cleanly.

5. ☐ Opening line rehearsed out loud, not just in your head Say your Pattern Interrupt + Permission line out loud once before dialing. Lines that sound fine in your head often stumble the first time they hit air.

6. ☐ One likely objection anticipated, with a response ready Pick the single most probable objection for this contact type ("we already use a vendor," "send me an email," "not the right time") and have one calm response ready — not memorized word-for-word, but the shape of it.

7. ☐ Physical and vocal state checked Standing or sitting upright, shoulders back, genuine smile before the first ring. Tone carries before words do — a slouched, flat-voiced opener undoes a well-written script.

8. ☐ Calendar open and visible Have your calendar already open on screen before you dial. Fumbling to find availability mid-call after asking for a meeting kills momentum and signals disorganization.

9. ☐ Result-independent mindset set before the call connects Decide, before the phone rings, that a "no" is information, not a verdict on you. This isn't a mindset platitude — it's the mechanical fix for the vocal hesitation and rising pitch that "hoping for a yes" produces, which prospects hear as neediness.

How to use this in a live calling block

  • Run all 9 points for your very first dial of a session in full.
  • For dials 2 onward in the same block, points 1, 2, 4, and 6 need a fresh check every time — points 3, 5, 7, 8, 9 stay set for the whole block once locked in.
  • If you skip more than 2 of these 9 on a given dial, expect a weaker call — track this against your Daily Dial Sheet outcomes for a week and you'll see the correlation directly.

How to use it

Run all 9 points in full before your first dial of a session, then re-check items 1, 2, 4, and 6 fresh before every subsequent dial in the same block.
