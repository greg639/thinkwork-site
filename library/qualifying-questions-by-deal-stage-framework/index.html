Qualifying Questions by Deal Stage Framework

A framework mapping exactly which qualifying questions to ask at each pipeline stage, so qualification deepens call over call instead of repeating the same surface-level questions.

What's inside

  • The core rule: never repeat a question, always ask a deeper version
  • Stage 1 (Initial Discovery) questions and graduation criteria
  • Stage 2 (Qualified Opportunity / Evaluation) questions and graduation criteria
  • Stage 3 (Solution Validation / Business Case) questions and graduation criteria
  • Stage 4 (Negotiation / Procurement) questions and graduation criteria
  • A 'depth ladder' example showing one topic (budget) deepening across all 4 stages

Deepening Qualification Instead of Repeating It

The core rule: every stage revisits the same 4 qualification themes — budget, authority, need, timeline — but at a deeper level of specificity. Asking "what's your budget?" in stage 3 the same way you asked it in stage 1 signals you weren't listening the first time and stalls trust. Each stage below shows what "deeper" looks like.

Stage 1 — Initial Discovery (Suspect → Prospect)

Goal of qualification at this stage: confirm a real problem exists and there's a plausible path to a decision — not confirm a purchase.

Ask:

  • "What's driving you to look at this now?"
  • "Roughly, is this a five-figure problem, a six-figure problem, or bigger?"
  • "Who else, if anyone, cares about this besides you?"
  • "Is there a date this needs to be solved by, or is it open-ended?"
  • "Have you looked at other ways to solve this?"

Graduation criteria to Stage 2: a specific, named problem with a rough magnitude, and at least a first-name guess at who else is involved.

Stage 2 — Qualified Opportunity / Evaluation

Goal: move from "a problem exists" to "a funded, timed initiative with a defined evaluation process exists."

Ask (deeper versions of Stage 1):

  • "You mentioned this was roughly a six-figure problem — has that number been shared with whoever approves spend at that level?"
  • "You said [Name] cares about this too — have they been part of a call yet, or should we get them on the next one?"
  • "What does your evaluation process typically look like for something in this range — is there a formal RFP, or more informal?"
  • "Is the date you mentioned tied to something specific — a renewal, an audit, a fiscal year, a board commitment?"
  • "What would need to be true for this to lose to 'do nothing' internally?"

Graduation criteria to Stage 3: budget has a real funding source (not just a number), a named approver has engaged directly, and a defined evaluation process exists.

Stage 3 — Solution Validation / Business Case

Goal: confirm the specific solution maps to the specific business case, and surface any remaining stakeholders or objections before they become late-stage surprises.

Ask (deeper versions of Stage 2):

  • "Now that [Approver] has seen this — what's their read? Any concerns you're picking up on that we haven't addressed?"
  • "Walk me through what happens between now and a signature — who touches this next, in what order?"
  • "If I built the ROI case using the numbers we discussed, who needs to see it before it's credible internally?"
  • "Is there anyone who could still say no at this point that hasn't been part of the conversation?"
  • "What's the realistic risk to the timeline we discussed — what could push it?"

Graduation criteria to Stage 4: a validated, stakeholder-reviewed business case, a clear map of remaining approvers, and a live, credible date.

Stage 4 — Negotiation / Procurement

Goal: confirm nothing structural is left unresolved — this stage should surface process friction, not new pain or new stakeholders (if it does, the deal wasn't really Stage 4).

Ask (deeper versions of Stage 3):

  • "Legal/procurement — what's their typical turnaround, and is there anything about our paper that's flagged concern before?"
  • "Is the number we're discussing final, or is there a step where this gets revisited upward or downward?"
  • "Who has to sign, physically or via e-signature, and do they have any competing priority that could delay this specific week?"
  • "Is there anything, if we're honest, that could still cause this to not happen?"

Graduation criteria to Closed-Won: signature authority confirmed, no unresolved legal/procurement blockers, no unaddressed stakeholder concerns.

Depth Ladder Example — Budget, Across All 4 Stages

  1. Stage 1: "Roughly, is this a five-figure or six-figure problem?"
  2. Stage 2: "Has that number been shared with whoever approves spend at that level?"
  3. Stage 3: "Who needs to see the ROI case before the number is credible internally?"
  4. Stage 4: "Is the number final, or does it get revisited at signature?"

Same theme, four different levels of specificity — never the same question twice.

How to use it

Before each call, check which pipeline stage the deal is in, pull only that stage's question set, and use the graduation criteria to decide honestly whether the deal has actually earned its way to the next stage.
