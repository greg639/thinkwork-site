Sales Content Launch Toolkit

Four ready-to-use templates — battlecard, proposal, case study, and objection-handling script — plus a 30-day launch checklist, so a brand-new enablement function ships a credible content library in its first month instead of its first quarter.

What's inside

  • Competitor battlecard template with positioning statement, landmines, and objection responses
  • Proposal template with a solution-mapping table tied to discovery, not features
  • Case study template using the Snapshot / Challenge / Approach / Results structure
  • Word-for-word objection-handling script for the 5 most common stalls
  • 30-day launch sequencing checklist (what ships week 1 vs. week 4)
  • Content governance one-pager: ownership, review cadence, deprecation rule

A starter kit for the first person (or first hire) inside a brand-new enablement function. It bundles the four assets every sales team asks for in month one — a battlecard, a proposal, a case study, and an objection script — plus the sequencing and governance to keep them alive past launch week.

1. Battlecard Template

[Competitor Name] Battlecard Last updated: _____ · Owner: _____ · Confidence level (High/Medium/Low — how recent is the intel): _____

Snapshot

  • Who they are (2 sentences, no editorializing): _____
  • Where they genuinely win: _____
  • Where they genuinely lose: _____

Positioning Statement (fill in the blank)

"When prospects are choosing between us and [Competitor], we win because ___________, and we lose because ___________."

Landmines (plant these before the competitor comes up)

  1. "One thing worth asking any vendor you're evaluating — ___________." (a question only your product answers well)
  2. "Something that surprises people during the [Competitor] evaluation is ___________."
  3. "The teams who've regretted their choice usually say the same thing: ___________."

Proof Points

ClaimEvidenceSource

Objection Responses (competitor-specific)

  • "They said [Competitor] is cheaper." → "On sticker price, sometimes. Once you factor in [implementation time / hidden fees / the thing they charge extra for], most teams find total cost lands within [X]% of ours — and you get [differentiator] included. Want me to walk through the real math on your numbers?"
  • "They said [Competitor] has more integrations." → "They do have a longer list. What matters is whether the 3-4 integrations you actually run through daily are solid — want to check those specifically instead of the full catalog?"
  • "They said [Competitor] is the market leader." → "They've been around longer, that's true. Being the incumbent and being the best fit for where you are today aren't always the same thing — can I show you what changed for teams your size in the last 18 months?"

Traps to Avoid

  • Never bash the competitor by name in writing (email, proposal) — verbal only, and always paired with proof, never opinion.
  • Don't argue with a false claim directly — redirect to a differentiated proof point instead of a rebuttal.
  • If you don't have current intel, say "let me confirm and get back to you" — a stale claim is worse than no claim.

4. Objection-Handling Script

Structure for every objection: Acknowledge → Reframe → Evidence → Commit-question.

1. "It's too expensive / we don't have budget."

  • Acknowledge: "Totally fair — budget's one of the first things I'd want clarity on too."
  • Reframe: "When you say 'expensive' — is that relative to the value, or bigger than what's currently allocated?"
  • Evidence: "Most teams we work with find the cost of staying with the status quo — in lost deals, ramp time, or churn — runs 3-4x the investment in year one."
  • Commit: "If we could show payback inside two quarters, would budget still be the blocker, or is there something else underneath it?"

2. "We're happy with our current solution."

  • Acknowledge: "Good to hear — that tells me the fundamentals are already in place."
  • Reframe: "Out of curiosity, what would 'even better' look like, if you had a magic wand?"
  • Evidence: "A lot of the teams who talk to us start from exactly that spot — happy, not desperate — and the trigger ends up being a specific gap: [X]. Does that ever show up for you?"
  • Commit: "If nothing changes in the next 12 months, are you fully confident you hit [their stated goal]?"

3. "We need to think about it / loop in the team."

  • Acknowledge: "Makes sense — this shouldn't be a one-person call."
  • Reframe: "So I don't waste your time chasing — will the team be weighing the approach, the price, or whether this is a priority at all?"
  • Commit: "Can we put 20 minutes on the calendar with them while it's fresh, rather than let it go quiet?"

4. "Not a priority right now."

  • Acknowledge: "Appreciate you being straight with me."
  • Reframe: "Is it not a priority compared to other initiatives, or is the pain just not big enough yet?"
  • Evidence: [cite your cost-of-inaction proof point]
  • Commit: "What would need to be true for this to move up the list — and is that likely this half or further out?"

5. "Just send me some information."

  • Acknowledge: "Happy to — I want what I send to actually be useful, not a generic deck."
  • Reframe: "Two quick things so I tailor it: what's the #1 outcome you're chasing this year, and who else would read this?"
  • Commit: "Once you've had a look, does a 15-minute follow-up Thursday or Friday work?"

5. Launch Sequencing Checklist (First 30 Days)

Week 1

  • Interview 3-5 top reps on what they currently improvise without
  • Draft battlecard for competitor #1 (whichever shows up most in deals)
  • Identify best existing customer for case study #1; clear it with the customer/legal

Week 2

  • Finalize battlecard #1; get sign-off from a senior AE + sales leader
  • Draft proposal template; test it on one live deal
  • Conduct case study interview #1

Week 3

  • Ship objection-handling script; roleplay it live in a team meeting, not just email it
  • Draft case study #1 from the interview; get customer approval
  • Identify competitor #2 for the next battlecard

Week 4

  • Publish the content governance page and assign owners
  • Run a 30-minute "content library launch" session with the full team
  • Put the review cadence on the calendar now, not later
