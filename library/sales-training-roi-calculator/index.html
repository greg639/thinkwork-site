Sales Training ROI Calculator

A worked formula that models training cost against ramp-time reduction and attainment lift, with an honest attribution discount built in, so you can make (and defend) the enablement budget case to finance.

How to use it

Plug your own numbers into the fill-in table, choose an attribution-confidence tier honestly rather than optimistically, and present the discounted figure alongside the leading indicators if you're making the case before a full ramp cohort has completed.

What's inside

  • Full input list (reps trained, loaded cost, ramp times, attainment %, deal value, program cost)
  • Part A formula: Ramp Acceleration Value, with a worked numeric example
  • Part B formula: Attainment Lift Value, with a worked numeric example
  • The Attribution Discount step, why and how to discount the raw lift number
  • Total program cost breakdown (design, delivery, tooling, opportunity cost)
  • Net ROI and ROI % formulas
  • Payback period formula
  • Fill-in calculation table for your own numbers
  • Leading indicators to track before lagging ROI is provable

A three-part model: what the program is worth in accelerated ramp, what it's worth in attainment lift, discounted for honesty, minus what it cost.

Part A, Ramp Acceleration Value

Formula: Ramp Value = (R_before − R_after) × MQ × A_ramp × N

Worked example:

  • Ramp saved: 1.5 months
  • Monthly quota: $50,000
  • Typical ramp-period attainment: 60%
  • New hires per year: 20

1.5 × $50,000 × 0.60 × 20 = $900,000 in accelerated productivity value

Part B: Attainment Lift Value

Formula: Attainment Value = (A_after − A_before) × AQ × N_tenured

Worked example:

  • Attainment lift: 8 points (85% → 93%)
  • Annual quota: $600,000
  • Tenured reps covered: 80

0.08 × $600,000 × 80 = $3,840,000 in raw attributed value

The Attribution Discount (do not skip this step)

Training is never the only variable moving attainment: market conditions, comp plan changes, and management turnover all move the number too. Apply an Attribution Discount before you take this number anywhere near a CFO.

Confidence in isolating the training effectDiscount to apply
High, controlled cohort vs. baseline comparison30% attributed to training
Medium: before/after with no control group15-20% attributed to training
Low: anecdotal, no comparison data10% attributed to training, or don't present a hard number yet

Discounted Attainment Value = $3,840,000 × 0.20 = $768,000 (medium-confidence example)

A CFO respects a number that's been discounted more than one that hasn't: an undiscounted claim invites them to discount it themselves, at a worse rate than you would have chosen.

Total Program Cost

P_cost = Design/development cost + Delivery cost (facilitators, venue, travel) + Tooling/license cost + Opportunity cost of rep selling-time consumed

Opportunity cost formula: Hours out of field × hourly value of quota-carrying time

Net ROI and Payback

Net ROI = (Ramp Value + Discounted Attainment Value) − P_cost

ROI % = (Net ROI ÷ P_cost) × 100

Payback period (months) = P_cost ÷ (Monthly value generated by program)

Worked example total:

  • Ramp Value: $900,000
  • Discounted Attainment Value: $768,000
  • Total benefit: $1,668,000
  • Program cost: $400,000
  • Net ROI = $1,268,000
  • ROI % = 317%
  • Payback period ≈ 2.9 months

Leading Indicators to Track Before Lagging ROI Is Provable

Ramp and attainment lift take 2+ quarters to show up cleanly. Track these in the meantime so you're not flying blind:

  • Certification pass rate (first attempt)
  • Skill-observation score trend (see the Manager Skill-Observation Scorecard)
  • Time-to-first-qualified-deal for new hires
  • Manager-reported confidence in coaching to the new material
