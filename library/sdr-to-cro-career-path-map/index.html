SDR-to-CRO Career Path Map

Every rung from SDR to CRO mapped to the specific skills that actually gate each promotion — not just tenure or quota — plus what typically changes at each level.

What's inside

  • 10 career rungs from SDR/BDR to CRO
  • Gating skills required for each rung, mapped to the Core 12
  • Typical time-in-role for each step
  • What actually gets you promoted at each rung (evidence-based, not tenure-based)
  • Illustrative comp/outcome notes per rung

Every rung on this ladder is defined by which of the Core 12 Sales Competencies you've moved from Developing to Proficient (or Proficient to Advanced), not just by hitting number for a certain number of quarters. Use this to see what's actually being evaluated at each step — and what to go build before you ask for the promotion.

Note on compensation figures: ranges below are illustrative and vary enormously by industry, geography, and company stage — use them as rough orientation, not a benchmark to quote in a negotiation.

Rung 1 — SDR / BDR (Sales/Business Development Rep)

What the role does: generates and qualifies pipeline, first conversation with prospects. Gating skills: Discovery & Needs Diagnosis (Developing→Proficient), Active Listening & Rapport (Developing→Proficient), Product & Industry Fluency (Developing). Typical time in role: 9–18 months. What actually gets you promoted: consistent pipeline-quality metrics (not just volume) — meetings that convert to real opportunities; ability to run a qualified discovery conversation without a manager listening in. Illustrative outcome: entry-level sales comp band; the real payoff here is skill-building velocity, not the paycheck.

Rung 2 — Associate / Junior Account Executive

What the role does: owns smaller or simpler deals end-to-end. Gating skills: Discovery (Proficient), Value Articulation (Developing→Proficient), Closing & Next-Step Commitment (Developing→Proficient). Typical time in role: 12–24 months. What actually gets you promoted: closing full-cycle deals independently; forecast that's accurate more often than not; handling objections without escalating to a manager. Illustrative outcome: meaningful step up in OTE from SDR; first quota-carrying comp plan.

Rung 3 — Mid-Market / Full-Cycle Account Executive

What the role does: owns a full territory or segment, more complex multi-stakeholder deals. Gating skills: Multi-threading & Stakeholder Navigation (Developing→Proficient), Negotiation & Deal Structuring (Developing→Proficient), Forecasting Accuracy (Proficient). Typical time in role: 18–30 months. What actually gets you promoted: track record across multiple quarters, not one hot quarter; navigating a deal when the original champion goes quiet; forecast commits that hold up. Illustrative outcome: competitive market-rate OTE for the segment; first real negotiating leverage on comp/territory.

Rung 4 — Senior / Strategic / Enterprise Account Executive

What the role does: owns the largest, most complex deals — long cycles, big buying committees. Gating skills: Multi-threading (Advanced), Value/Business Case Articulation (Advanced), Negotiation & Deal Structuring (Advanced), Post-Sale Handoff & Expansion Awareness (Proficient). Typical time in role: 2–4 years (many stay here long-term by choice — a valid ceiling, not just a waypoint). What actually gets you promoted (into management, if that's the direction): peers and new hires start asking you for advice unprompted; you can articulate WHY a deal worked, not just that it did. Illustrative outcome: top-decile individual-contributor comp; often exceeds first-line manager pay, which is why many strong performers stay ICs by design.

Rung 5 — Team Lead / Player-Coach

What the role does: carries a reduced individual quota while mentoring 2–4 reps informally. Gating skills: Coachability & Self-Improvement Velocity flips into "coaching others" — naming a skill gap in someone else's call, not just your own. Typical time in role: 6–18 months (often a deliberate trial period before full management). What actually gets you promoted: reps you've informally mentored show measurable skill movement; you give specific, skill-named feedback rather than "nice work." Illustrative outcome: modest comp bump plus visibility; the real currency here is evidence you can manage before the title says so.

Rung 6 — Sales Manager (First-Line)

What the role does: owns a team's number through other people's skill development, not their own selling. Gating skills: all 12 Core competencies, now applied diagnostically to a team — recognizing each skill level in someone else's call. Typical time in role: 2–3 years. What actually gets you promoted: team-level forecast accuracy; documented coaching cadence; ramp time for new hires improving under your management. Illustrative outcome: management-track comp band; the first role where your own selling skill matters less than your ability to develop it in others.

Rung 7 — Senior Sales Manager / Regional Manager

What the role does: owns a larger or higher-stakes team/territory, often manages other team leads. Gating skills: Pipeline & Territory Management and Forecasting Accuracy at the aggregate/multi-team level; cross-functional stakeholder navigation (finance, marketing, product). Typical time in role: 2–4 years. What actually gets you promoted: consistent multi-quarter team performance across more than one manager reporting to you or a materially larger book; forecast trusted by leadership without heavy scrutiny.

Rung 8 — Director of Sales

What the role does: owns a function or region strategically — hiring, territory design, process, not just quota. Gating skills: shifts from coaching individuals to designing the systems (hiring bar, onboarding, comp structure) that produce the Core 12 at scale. Typical time in role: 2–5 years. What actually gets you promoted: demonstrated ability to build repeatable process that outlives any single hire.

Rung 9 — VP of Sales

What the role does: owns the full revenue-generating sales org and its strategy; sits with the executive team. Gating skills: cross-functional influence (product, marketing, customer success), board/executive-level forecasting and narrative, org design. Typical time in role: 3+ years, or a defined tenure at a given company stage. What actually gets you promoted (to CRO): demonstrated ownership of revenue outcomes beyond new-business sales alone — retention, expansion, and cross-functional alignment with marketing and customer success.

Rung 10 — Chief Revenue Officer (CRO)

What the role does: owns the entire revenue engine — new business, expansion, retention — as one integrated system, reporting to the CEO/board. Gating skills: everything above, applied at the level of company strategy: ensuring the Core 12 competencies are being built systematically across every revenue-facing team, not just within sales. What got them here: a track record of building people and systems that outperform any single deal or quarter they personally touched.

How to use this map

  1. Find your current rung.
  2. Look at the gating skills for the NEXT rung, not your current one.
  3. Cross-reference those skills against The Core 12 Sales Competency Framework for what "the next level up" looks like in plain English.
  4. Ask your manager directly: "which of these gating skills is the one holding me back from being ready for the next rung?" — use the How to Ask Your Manager for Better Feedback script if that conversation needs one.

How to use it

Find your current rung, read the gating skills required for the NEXT one (not your current one), and use those as your actual development targets instead of assuming tenure or quota alone will get you promoted.
