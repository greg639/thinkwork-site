Sales Buddy/Mentor Program Playbook

A complete framework for pairing every new hire with a peer mentor from day one: who qualifies to mentor, how to match pairs, exactly what to cover each week, and the specific signals that say it's time to end the pairing well instead of letting it fade.

How to use it

Copy the eligibility checklist and matching criteria into your onboarding process before the next hire starts, then hand the weekly agenda scripts directly to whoever you pair as mentor and mentee.

What's inside

  • Mentor eligibility checklist (5 criteria)
  • Matching criteria + anti-patterns to avoid
  • 12-week program structure (Immersion / Skill-building / Independence / Graduation)
  • Week-by-week agenda scripts with time allocations
  • Mentor role boundaries, what mentors must not do
  • The manager's role during the pairing
  • 3 signals it's time to end the pairing + a graduation script
  • Mentor recognition options
  • Program health scorecard with target metrics

Why This Program Exists

New hires get two kinds of help in their first 90 days: what their manager can give them, and what a peer can give them. These are not the same thing, and most onboarding programs only build the first one.

A manager can give a new hire: role clarity, performance expectations, coaching on grades/reviews, career path. A manager cannot easily give a new hire: the unfiltered truth about "what actually happens on calls," a safe person to ask a question they're embarrassed to ask their boss, or a model of "how someone who isn't perfect but is competent handles this job."

That's what a buddy/mentor does. This playbook is the whole program: who qualifies to mentor, how to pair people, what to talk about each week, and how to know when to end it cleanly instead of letting it fade into nothing.

Mentor Eligibility Checklist

A rep qualifies to be a Sales Buddy if they meet ALL five:

  1. Tenure: minimum 9 months in the role (long enough to have survived a slow quarter, short enough to still remember being new)
  2. Performance: at or above quota in 2 of the last 3 quarters (not necessarily a top performer: a solid, consistent performer teaches better habits than a boom-bust star)
  3. Willingness: opted in, not assigned. A dragooned mentor is worse than no mentor.
  4. Bandwidth: not currently ramping a territory change, not on a coaching plan, not already mentoring 2+ people
  5. No behavior flags: no active coaching plan for communication, integrity, or teamwork issues in the past 2 quarters

Matching Criteria (in priority order)

  1. Same segment/patch: an SMB new hire pairs with an SMB mentor, not enterprise. The muscle memory has to transfer directly.
  2. Personality complement, not clone: pair a quieter new hire with a mentor who draws people out in conversation, not with someone equally quiet. Two introverts produce a silent pairing.
  3. Physical/schedule proximity, same office or same working-hours block. Async-only mentoring dies within a month.
  4. Never the hiring manager's automatic favorite pick: ask the mentor candidate first, privately, before assuming.

Anti-patterns to avoid:

  • Don't pair two people who are direct competitors for the same open patch or promotion
  • Don't pair a new hire with the person who trained their predecessor who left badly
  • Don't assign more than one new hire to the same mentor in the same cohort: it splits attention
  • Don't let the mentor be the new hire's Slack-adjacent friend from outside work: the point is job modeling, not pre-existing rapport

Independence7–121x/week, 20 minTroubleshooting specific deals, confidence check-ins
GraduationWeek 121 final sessionFormal close-out (see below)

Weekly Agenda Templates

Weeks 1–2 agenda (30 min):

  • 5 min: How's the week going logistically (badge, laptop, calendar access all working?)
  • 10 min: "Who do I ask about ___": walk through org-chart questions
  • 10 min: Shadow debrief: one thing the new hire noticed, one thing that confused them
  • 5 min: Set next session's shadow call to watch for

Weeks 3–6 agenda (30 min):

  • 5 min: Check-in: how many live reps this week, how did they feel
  • 15 min: Debrief the hardest moment from a real call this week (an objection, a silence, a stakeholder curveball): mentor narrates how they'd have handled it, not "what you did wrong"
  • 5 min: One skill to deliberately practice before the next session
  • 5 min: Anything the manager should know that isn't a big deal to raise directly yet

Weeks 7–12 agenda (20 min):

  • 5 min: Pipeline gut-check: any deal stuck, any deal that feels off
  • 10 min: New hire brings ONE real, current problem; mentor helps them think it through (not solve it for them)
  • 5 min: Confidence check: 1–10 scale, what would move it up a point

Mentor Role Boundaries: What Mentors Do NOT Do

  • Do not grade or evaluate the new hire's calls: that's the manager's and/or the assessment process's job
  • Do not relay performance concerns to the manager without the new hire's knowledge first
  • Do not answer comp, HR, or disciplinary questions: redirect to manager or HR
  • Do not let the sessions become free labor (writing the new hire's emails, doing their prep for them): the goal is teaching the fish, not catching it

The Manager's Role During the Pairing

  • Confirm the pairing in writing (who, cadence, end date) in week 1
  • Do NOT sit in on buddy sessions: presence changes what gets said
  • Check in with the mentor privately at week 4 and week 8: "anything I should know, in general terms, not specifics"
  • Protect the mentor's calendar: don't schedule over buddy sessions

When to End the Pairing

End formally at Week 12, or earlier if any one of these three signals shows up:

  1. The new hire independently handles a full-cycle deal (discovery → close) without asking the mentor a single process question during it
  2. The new hire starts giving the mentor useful pushback or new ideas, not just receiving them
  3. The mentor reports the sessions feel redundant, "they don't need me for this anymore"

Do not let it end by simply fading out unacknowledged, that reads as abandonment to the new hire even when it's actually a graduation. Run a 15-minute formal close-out:

"This is our last scheduled session, you've earned it. From here you're a full peer, and I'm still around if something comes up, but not on a schedule. What's one thing from these 12 weeks you want to keep doing, and one thing you want to do differently now that you're on your own?"

Recognition Options for Mentors

Pick one, consistently, don't skip it:

  • Formal note in the mentor's file/performance review citing the mentoring as a leadership indicator
  • Small spot bonus or gift card at the 12-week close-out
  • Public shout-out at a team meeting when the new hire hits their first deal
  • First right of refusal to mentor again next cohort (mentors who do it well usually want to keep doing it)
