Multi-Channel Outbound Cadence Framework

A day-by-day 14-day cadence blending email, LinkedIn, and phone across 8 touches, so no single channel has to carry the whole sequence: with rules for sequencing, timing, and adapting it to your own sales cycle.

How to use it

Load the day/channel/action table directly into your sequencing tool as a template, then swap in the adaptation guidance for your specific deal size and sales cycle length before applying it to a new list.

A day-by-day 14-day cadence blending email, LinkedIn, and phone so no single channel carries the whole sequence. Built around 8 total touches across 3 channels.

The framework at a glance

DayChannelAction
1EmailTouch 1: personalized opener (research-based, no ask beyond a question)
1LinkedInSend connection request (no pitch in the note)
3CallTouch 2: first call attempt, leave a short voicemail if no answer
4EmailTouch 3: value-add follow-up (share a relevant insight, resource, or data point)
6LinkedInIf connected: short, no-ask message referencing touch 1's insight
7CallTouch 4: second call attempt, different time of day than touch 2
8EmailTouch 5: social proof touch (a relevant case study or peer result)
10LinkedInEngage with a recent post of theirs (comment, don't just like)
11CallTouch 6: third call attempt
12EmailTouch 7: direct, short ask ("worth 15 minutes this week or next?")
14EmailTouch 8, breakup email (see Sales Breakup Email Swipe File)

Rules that make the blend actually work

  • Never repeat the same channel twice in a row. The point of multi-channel is that a prospect who ignores email might notice LinkedIn, and a prospect who ignores both might pick up a call.
  • Vary call times across attempts. Try one morning (8-9:30am), one midday (11:30am-1pm), one end-of-day (4-5:30pm) attempt across the 3 call touches. Different windows catch different calendars.
  • Every email touch must add something new: a new insight, a new proof point, a new angle. An email that just re-sends the same pitch reads as a bump, not a touch, and trains the prospect to ignore the thread.
  • LinkedIn engagement touches (Day 6, Day 10) don't count if there's nothing to engage with. If the prospect hasn't posted anything recently, replace that touch with an additional call attempt or a short direct message instead.
  • Stop the cadence immediately on any reply, even a "not interested": a real response ends the automated sequence and moves the prospect to a manual, personalized track.

Sequencing logic (why this order)

  • Touches 1-3 (Days 1-4) establish visibility across channels quickly, before the prospect has formed an opinion either way.
  • Touches 4-6 (Days 6-8) shift from "here I am" to "here's something useful," building a reason to respond beyond politeness.
  • Touches 7-8 (Days 12-14) shift to direct asks and, finally, the exit: by day 14, continuing to circle without a direct ask reads as either desperate or automated.

Adapting the cadence

  • Enterprise / longer sales cycle: stretch to 21 days, add 2-3 more touches, slow the pace between touches 1-4.
  • Transactional / high-velocity: compress to 10 days, front-load touches 1-4 into the first week.
  • No phone number available: replace call touches with a second LinkedIn message or a short video touch (see Video Prospecting Script Swipe File) instead of dropping to 2-channel.
