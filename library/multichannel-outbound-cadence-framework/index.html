Multichannel Outbound Cadence Framework

A day-by-day, 15-touch sequencing model across call, email, and LinkedIn spanning a 3-week cadence, with exit criteria for when a live reply should override automation.

What's inside

  • Full 15-touch day-by-day table across 21 calendar days
  • Channel sequencing principle: never repeat the same channel back-to-back within 24 hours
  • LinkedIn connection-request rule: under 300 characters, no pitch
  • The urgency pivot point at Day 12–13
  • The break-up touch at Day 19–21
  • Exit criteria checklist: reply, connection accept, live answer, hard bounce, opt-out
  • Post-cadence nurture and trigger-event re-entry rules

A proven call, email, and LinkedIn sequencing model mapped day-by-day across a 3-week cadence. This is the structure to load into your sales engagement tool (or run manually) for every net-new prospect — 15 touches across 3 channels, timed so no single channel carries the whole cadence.

How to use this: Assign one owner (usually the rep) for the full cadence. Log every touch in the CRM the same day it happens. If the prospect replies or engages on any channel at any point, exit the cadence and move to a live conversation — don't let automation keep firing after a human responds.

Design Principles Behind the Sequencing

  1. Never repeat the same channel back-to-back within 24 hours. A call, then an email the next morning, feels like two different people paying attention — not one bot hammering a list.
  2. Front-load email and LinkedIn (low-friction), build to calls (high-friction) as the relationship warms. By week 2, the prospect has seen your name 3–4 times before the phone rings again — that context earns you a few extra seconds.
  3. LinkedIn never leads with a pitch. Connection request note stays under 300 characters, name-drops one specific reason for connecting, and does not ask for a meeting. The first message after connecting is value, not an ask.
  4. The urgency touch (Day 12–13) is the pivot point. Everything before it is pattern-building; everything after it is decision-forcing. If you don't have a genuine trigger event, use a real internal deadline (end of quarter outreach, capacity constraint) — never a fabricated one.
  5. The break-up (Day 19–21) is not optional. It's frequently the highest-reply-rate touch in the entire cadence because it removes pressure instead of adding it.
  6. Any reply, click-through, or LinkedIn accept pulls the prospect out of automation and into a live thread. Cadences are for silence, not for people who've shown a signal.

Exit Criteria (When to Stop the Cadence Early)

  • Prospect replied on any channel — even a "not now"
  • Prospect accepted LinkedIn connection and engaged with a message
  • Prospect answered a call live
  • Hard bounce on email or "wrong number" on phone — verify contact data before re-adding
  • Explicit opt-out ("take me off your list," unsubscribe) — remove immediately, no exceptions

After the Cadence Ends (No Response)

  • Move to long-term nurture list (quarterly check-in cadence, not daily/weekly)
  • Flag account for re-entry if a new trigger event fires (funding, new hire, job posting)
  • Log "no response after full cadence" as a data point — if this happens on >70% of a segment, the ICP or messaging needs revisiting, not just more touches

How to use it

Load the day-by-day table into your sales engagement tool, assign one owner per cadence, and pull any prospect out immediately the moment they reply, connect, or answer live on any channel.
