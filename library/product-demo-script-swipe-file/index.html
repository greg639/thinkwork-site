Product Demo Script Swipe File

A swipe file of 20+ adaptable demo openers, pivot lines, and closes organized by persona and industry, so you always start a demo speaking directly to who's in the room instead of opening with a generic product tour.

What's inside

  • The 3-part demo script template (open / pivot-to-value / close)
  • 8 persona-based swipe entries
  • 12 industry-based swipe entries
  • Adaptation rules for combining persona + industry
  • Generic-opener ban list

The 3-Part Demo Script Template

Every entry below follows this structure — adapt the bracketed content, keep the structure:

``` OPEN (10-15 sec): Name their world in one sentence + reference something specific from discovery.

PIVOT-TO-VALUE (1 sentence): Bridge from their stated pain directly into what you're about to show — never "let me show you our platform," always "here's how that plays out differently."

CLOSE (1 question): Check it landed, in their language, before moving on. ```

Persona Swipe Entries

1. VP of Sales

  • Open: "You mentioned pipeline visibility breaks down the second a deal touches more than one rep — that's exactly where forecasting goes wrong."
  • Pivot: "Here's how you'd see that deal the moment it goes multi-threaded, not three weeks later in a QBR."
  • Close: "Does that match what you'd actually need to trust the number on a Friday forecast call?"

2. CFO

  • Open: "You said the board wants payback under 12 months on anything new — that's the lens I'll use for everything I show you."
  • Pivot: "So instead of walking every feature, here's exactly where the cost comes out and how fast."
  • Close: "Does that math hold up against how your board actually evaluates this?"

3. Operations Manager

  • Open: "You described your team re-keying the same data three times a week — let's start exactly there."
  • Pivot: "Here's what that same workflow looks like once it's a single pass."
  • Close: "Would that actually remove the re-keying step, or is there a wrinkle I'm missing?"

4. IT / Security Lead

  • Open: "Before anything else, you flagged SSO and data residency as non-negotiable — let's clear that first."
  • Pivot: "Here's exactly how that's handled, and where you'd go to audit it yourself."
  • Close: "Does that satisfy what your review process needs, or is there a specific control you still need to see?"

5. Customer Success Leader

  • Open: "You mentioned churn signals show up too late to act on — that's the exact gap here."
  • Pivot: "Here's where that signal would surface, and how much earlier than what you're seeing today."
  • Close: "Would that have caught [specific account they mentioned] in time?"

6. Marketing Leader

  • Open: "You said attribution between marketing touches and closed revenue is basically a guess right now."
  • Pivot: "Here's how that connects end to end, without the manual spreadsheet stitching."
  • Close: "Is that the level of attribution your team actually needs to defend budget?"

7. Founder / SMB Owner

  • Open: "You're doing this yourself right now between everything else — so I'll keep this to exactly what saves you time."
  • Pivot: "Here's the part that gets off your plate first."
  • Close: "If that one thing worked the way I just showed you, would that be enough to be worth it on its own?"

8. Procurement Lead

  • Open: "You mentioned vendor consolidation is part of this evaluation, not just features."
  • Pivot: "Here's where this replaces rather than adds to what you're already paying for."
  • Close: "Does that consolidation story hold up against what you're trying to simplify?"

Industry Swipe Entries

9. SaaS

  • Open: "Your churn conversation starts at renewal, not 90 days before it — you said that's too late."
  • Pivot: "Here's the earlier signal and what it would have shown you on [specific account type]."
  • Close: "Would that have given your CS team enough runway?"

10. Financial Services

  • Open: "Compliance sign-off has killed two other tools you've tried to bring in — you were clear about that."
  • Pivot: "So here's the audit trail and control layer first, before anything else."
  • Close: "Does this clear the bar your compliance team actually sets?"

11. Healthcare

  • Open: "You said clinician time is the scarcest resource you have, and anything that adds clicks is dead on arrival."
  • Pivot: "Here's exactly where this removes clicks rather than adding them."
  • Close: "Would your clinicians actually feel that difference, or does it need to go further?"

12. Manufacturing

  • Open: "Downtime on the floor is the number you're actually measured on, not software adoption."
  • Pivot: "Here's how this shows up on the floor, not just in a dashboard nobody looks at."
  • Close: "Does that map to how downtime actually gets reported up to you?"

13. Retail / eCommerce

  • Open: "You said the busy season is where everything breaks, not the quiet months."
  • Pivot: "So let's specifically look at how this holds up at peak volume."
  • Close: "Would that have held during last [season/event]?"

14. Professional Services

  • Open: "Utilization and billable capture are the two numbers your partners actually watch."
  • Pivot: "Here's where this shows up in both, not just a general productivity claim."
  • Close: "Does that connect to how utilization actually gets reported at your firm?"

15. Real Estate

  • Open: "You mentioned lead response time is the single biggest lever you have right now."
  • Pivot: "Here's what that response time looks like end to end."
  • Close: "Would that have caught the lead you mentioned losing last month?"

16. Nonprofit

  • Open: "You said every dollar spent on tooling has to be defensible to your board against program spend."
  • Pivot: "Here's where the time saved translates directly into more program capacity, not just convenience."
  • Close: "Does that framing hold up in front of your board?"

17. Education

  • Open: "You mentioned adoption across faculty is harder than adoption across admin staff."
  • Pivot: "Here's specifically what changes for faculty day-to-day, not just the admin office."
  • Close: "Would that actually reduce the resistance you described?"

18. Logistics / Supply Chain

  • Open: "You said visibility breaks down the moment a shipment crosses a handoff point."
  • Pivot: "Here's what that handoff looks like tracked end to end instead of siloed."
  • Close: "Does that close the visibility gap you were describing?"

19. Insurance

  • Open: "Claims turnaround time is the metric that actually gets escalated to you, you said."
  • Pivot: "Here's where that turnaround compresses, specifically at the step you flagged as the bottleneck."
  • Close: "Would that actually move your turnaround number, or is the bottleneck elsewhere?"

20. Construction / Field Services

  • Open: "You said the office and the field are working off two different versions of the truth."
  • Pivot: "Here's how that becomes one version, updated from the field in real time."
  • Close: "Would that have prevented the mismatch you described on [specific job/project]?"

Adaptation Rules

  • Combine persona + industry: pull the persona's pivot/close structure, swap in industry-specific language from the industry entry.
  • Never use more than one swipe entry's "open" per demo — pick the single most senior/highest-influence stakeholder in the room to open against, then transition explicitly to the next stakeholder's pain.
  • If you don't have discovery-specific language to fill an [bracket], stop — go back to the Discovery-to-Demo Alignment Checklist first. A filled-in swipe file is not a substitute for actual discovery.

Generic-Opener Ban List (never open a demo this way)

  • "So let me give you a quick overview of the platform..."
  • "Let's start from the beginning..."
  • "This is our homepage/dashboard..."
  • "We do a lot of things, but let me show you the highlights..."
  • "Before I show you anything, let me tell you about our company..."

How to use it

Find the persona or industry entry closest to your buyer, adapt the bracketed language using your actual discovery notes, and use the 3-part template as-is — open, pivot, close — rather than freelancing a longer intro.
