Cold Call Objection-to-Pitch Pivot Scripts (Live Call Edition)

Word-for-word pivots for the 12 objections that kill cold calls before a pitch is ever delivered. Each script covers the critical 8-to-25-second window when most calls die.

How to use it

Print this sheet and keep it beside your phone during call blocks. Drill each pivot out loud until the words feel natural, not read. After a successful pivot, move straight to the qualifying question listed, then into your standard open.

What's inside

  • 12 word-for-word, two-line acknowledge-and-redirect scripts for live phone delivery
  • Named failure mode for each objection so you know exactly what kills the moment
  • One qualifying question per pivot to use if the redirect lands
  • A note on when to abandon the call rather than fight for it
  • Framing guidance on phone tone versus email tone for each script
  • A quick-reference summary table of all 12 objections, pivots, and qualifying questions

These scripts cover the window between second 8 and second 25 of a cold call. That is where most calls die, not because the pitch was weak, but because the rep never got to deliver one. Each entry gives you a two-line redirect, the failure mode that kills it, and one qualifying question to use if the pivot works.

Language here is short and spoken. Read it aloud before you use it.

How to read each entry

Objection: What they say, or words close enough to it. Pivot: Two lines, spoken naturally. The first line acknowledges; the second redirects. Failure mode: The specific thing reps do that collapses this moment. If it lands: One qualifying question to move the call forward.

The 12 Pivots

1. "I'm not interested."

Pivot: "Fair enough, you haven't heard what it's about yet. I'll be quick: [one-sentence context]. Worth thirty seconds?"

Failure mode: Asking why they're not interested. It sounds like an interrogation and they hang up.

If it lands: "Is [specific problem your product solves] something that's come up for you at all this year?"

2. "We already have something in place."

Pivot: "Good to know. Most people I speak with do. The question is usually whether it's covering everything, or just most of it. Is it doing the full job for you?"

Failure mode: Immediately attacking their incumbent. You sound like a vendor, not an adviser.

If it lands: "What's the one thing you'd change about how it works today if you could?"

3. "Send me an email."

Pivot: "I will, absolutely. Before I do, so the email is actually useful: is [problem area] something you're actively working on, or more of a back-burner thing right now?"

Failure mode: Saying "okay" and ending the call. The email goes nowhere and you've lost the thread.

If it lands: "What would make it worth a fifteen-minute call once you've seen it?"

4. "I'm in a meeting."

Pivot: "My fault for the timing. When's a better slot, later today or tomorrow morning?"

Failure mode: Launching into the pitch anyway. They're not listening and they'll resent it.

If it lands: Confirm the callback time before you hang up. "I'll ring you at [time] on [number], does that work?"

5. "Who are you again?"

Pivot: "[Your name], [Company]. We work with [type of company] on [specific problem]. I'll be honest, we've not spoken before. I called because [one-line reason specific to them]."

Failure mode: Over-explaining the company history. They asked who you are, not for a company overview.

If it lands: "Does [the specific problem you named] land for you at all, or wide of the mark?"

6. "How did you get my number?"

Pivot: "[Source, stated plainly, no waffle]. I won't take much of your time. I rang because [specific reason relevant to them]."

Failure mode: Being vague or defensive about the source. It makes the whole call feel shady.

If it lands: "Is [problem area] something that's on your radar at the moment?"

7. "We don't have the budget for this."

Pivot: "That's fine, I'm not calling to sell you anything today. I just want to find out if there's even a fit. Budget conversations come later, if at all."

Failure mode: Jumping to price justification. You haven't told them what it costs yet, and neither have they.

If it lands: "If the fit was there, is budget something that could be found, or is it genuinely off the table for the year?"

8. "We're not looking to change anything right now."

Pivot: "Understood. Most good decisions get made before there's urgency. Can I ask: is [specific area] running where you want it to be, or is it one of those things you're managing around?"

Failure mode: Accepting the objection and closing the call. "Not right now" is not "not ever."

If it lands: "What would have to change to make this worth revisiting in, say, Q3?"

9. "I'm not the right person."

Pivot: "Appreciate you saying so. Who would you point me to? And is there anything you'd want me to know before I call them?"

Failure mode: Asking for a transfer on the spot. Most people won't do it live. Get the name and call direct.

If it lands: "Would it be worth me mentioning your name when I reach out to [them], or would you rather I go in cold?"

10. "We tried something like this before and it didn't work."

Pivot: "That's useful context. What went wrong with it, if you don't mind me asking?"

Failure mode: Defending your product before you know what failed. You might be defending something they didn't even experience.

If it lands: "If that specific issue was off the table, would it be worth a quick look at whether this is actually different?"

11. "I'm too busy."

Pivot: "I'll be ninety seconds. If it's not relevant, I'll let you go and won't ring again."

Failure mode: Sympathising for too long. "I completely understand, everyone's so busy, I'll keep it brief, I just want to" burns the ninety seconds before you've said anything.

If it lands: "The one thing I wanted to check: is [problem] something you're dealing with, or not your world?"

12. "Just talk to my PA / assistant."

Pivot: "Of course. Before I do, just so I don't waste their time either: is [problem area] something that sits with you, or does it genuinely live with someone else in the team?"

Failure mode: Calling the PA and treating them as a gatekeeper to fight. They're a human being. Be straight with them.

If it lands: "If [your name] decides it's worth five minutes, would they have sight of the decision, or would someone else need to be involved?"

When to abandon the call

Not every call is worth fighting for. Walk away if:

  • They've said no twice to the same redirect in the same call.
  • They're audibly hostile, not just brisk.
  • The contact is genuinely wrong for the problem you solve.
  • You've been told the decision was made in the last 30 days and won't be revisited.

Chasing a dead call longer than ninety seconds costs you the next one.
