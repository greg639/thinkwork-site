Coaching Effectiveness Scorecard for Managers

A rubric leaders use to rate the quality of a manager's coaching: not the rep's output: scoring consistency, specificity, follow-through and skill-linkage so coaching quality becomes visible and improvable in its own right.

How to use it

Pull the listed evidence first, score all 6 criteria independently before totaling, then use the band and the single lowest-scoring criterion as the focus of your next coaching conversation with that manager.

What's inside

  • What this scorecard measures (and what it deliberately doesn't)
  • 6 scored criteria with 1-4 level descriptors each
  • Evidence sources to pull from per criterion
  • Scoring sheet and total-score bands
  • Calibration guidance for using this across multiple managers
  • Manager self-scoring version

This scorecard rates the manager's coaching behavior, not the team's sales results. A manager can have a strong-performing team and be a weak coach (inherited good reps, or coasting on a hot market), and a manager can be an excellent coach on a team still building toward its numbers. Score the behavior you can actually observe or find evidence of, not the scoreboard.

What to Pull as Evidence Before Scoring

  • 2-3 recent 1:1 notes or recordings
  • The manager's coaching cadence log (see the Manager Time-to-Coach Ratio Calculator)
  • One ride-along the manager did themselves, observed or debriefed with you
  • Any PIP or feedback documentation the manager has written in the last quarter

The 6 Criteria

1. Consistency: Does coaching happen on a predictable cadence, protected from being cancelled?

ScoreDescriptor
1Coaching is ad hoc; 1:1s are frequently cancelled or skipped under pressure
2A cadence exists on paper but is inconsistently held
3Cadence is mostly held; occasional justified exceptions, always rescheduled
4Cadence is fully protected; rarely cancelled, and reps can predict when their coaching time is

2. Specificity: Is feedback anchored to a named skill and a specific observed moment, or vague/generic?

ScoreDescriptor
1Feedback is generic ("be more confident," "push harder") with no reference to a specific moment
2Feedback names a general area but rarely a specific behavior or moment
3Feedback usually names a specific behavior and moment, occasionally slips into generic
4Feedback consistently names the skill, the specific moment, and what the alternative behavior would look like

3. Rep-Led Ratio: Does the rep do most of the talking and reach their own conclusions, or does the manager lecture?

ScoreDescriptor
1Manager talks through most of the session; rep is mostly receiving instructions
2Manager leads the diagnosis and hands the rep a plan
3Manager asks questions but often answers them before the rep does
4Rep does the majority of the talking; names their own goal, diagnosis, and plan (GROW-style)

4. Skill-Linkage: Is coaching tied to a named, transferable competency, or is it deal-specific tactics only?

ScoreDescriptor
1Coaching is entirely "say this in your next call/deal" with no named skill behind it
2Occasionally names a skill, but most coaching is deal-tactical
3Usually names the underlying skill, occasionally slips into pure deal tactics
4Every coaching conversation names the specific skill, ties it to why it matters for the rep's growth, and the deal is treated as the practice ground, not the point

5. Follow-Through, Are commitments from prior sessions actually checked and closed?

ScoreDescriptor
1No record of prior commitments; each session starts from zero
2Commitments are noted but rarely revisited
3Most sessions open by checking the prior commitment before moving to new material
4Every session opens with a check on the prior commitment, and there's a visible log of what's been tried, what worked, what didn't

6. Psychological Safety: Do reps raise problems, mistakes, and pushback without fear, and does the manager respond without defensiveness?

ScoreDescriptor
1Reps report holding back bad news or mistakes from this manager
2Reps share some things but visibly filter what they raise
3Reps generally raise issues; manager sometimes reacts defensively
4Reps raise mistakes and pushback freely; manager responds with curiosity, not defensiveness

Calibration Note for Leaders Scoring Multiple Managers

Score at least two managers back-to-back using the same evidence set before finalizing any single score, this catches leniency or harshness drift. If two managers score identically on paper but your gut says they're not equal, go back to the evidence: the gap is usually in Specificity or Skill-Linkage, the two criteria managers most often overclaim on their own.

Manager Self-Scoring Version

Have the manager score themselves on the same 6 criteria before you share your score. The gap between self-score and leader-score is itself useful coaching data, a manager who rates their own Specificity a 4 when the evidence says 2 has a self-awareness gap worth addressing directly.
