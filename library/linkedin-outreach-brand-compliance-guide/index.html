LinkedIn Outreach Brand & Compliance Guide

Do's and don'ts for outbound at scale on LinkedIn — protecting brand voice and account health while staying inside safe connection and messaging volume.

What's inside

  • Why this matters: account restriction and brand-damage risk, in plain terms
  • Safe-volume guidance for connection requests and messages (with a verify-current-caps caveat)
  • Connection request Do's and Don'ts
  • Messaging Do's and Don'ts
  • Profile and brand hygiene standards
  • Automation and third-party tool policy guidance
  • Comment/engagement tone standards
  • Escalation path for edge cases

Why this matters

Two separate risks live in the same activity: account risk (LinkedIn restricting or suspending a rep's account for volume or automation abuse) and brand risk (a rep's outreach, sent under the company's name, reads as spam and damages how the company is perceived at every target account it touches). This guide protects against both.

Safe-volume guidance

LinkedIn's connection and messaging limits are algorithmic and change over time, and they vary by account age, Premium/Sales Navigator tier, and standing. Treat the figures below as starting-point safe practice, not a guaranteed current cap — verify the live limit in your own account settings and LinkedIn's current help documentation before scaling volume on a new account.

  • New or unrestricted accounts: start conservatively (roughly 15–20 connection requests/day) and increase gradually over several weeks rather than starting at maximum volume
  • Established accounts in good standing: many reps sustain higher weekly volume, but any sudden spike from a stable baseline is a common trigger for a restriction, regardless of the absolute number
  • Personalized connection notes convert better and are treated more favorably than blank requests
  • Never mix an aggressive daily ramp on a brand-new account with automation — this combination is the fastest way to a restriction

Connection request Do's and Don'ts

Do:

  • Personalize the note with one specific, true detail about the recipient
  • Space requests out across the day/week rather than sending them all at once
  • Withdraw pending requests after 2–3 weeks with no response to keep your pending count healthy

Don't:

  • Send the same templated note to more than a handful of people in a row
  • Pitch in the connection note itself — it isn't accepted yet, so it isn't a conversation
  • Spike volume the same week you install a new automation tool

Messaging Do's and Don'ts

Do:

  • Reference something specific to the person before making any ask
  • Keep first messages short — under 500 characters reads as a person, not a campaign
  • Give an easy, low-friction way to say no

Don't:

  • Send an identical message to your entire new-connection list on the day they accept
  • Follow up more than twice without a response before moving to another channel
  • Use urgency language ("today only", "don't miss out") — it's a spam signal on this channel specifically

Profile & brand hygiene

  • Profile photo, headline, and banner reflect current role and company accurately
  • Headline states what you help people with, not just a job title
  • About section written in first person, no keyword-stuffed jargon
  • Recent activity (posts, reposts, comments) is consistent with company voice — a prospect will check this before replying

Automation & third-party tools

  • Any tool that auto-sends connection requests or messages on your behalf carries real account-suspension risk under LinkedIn's terms — confirm current policy before adopting any tool, and get written sign-off from your manager/RevOps before connecting one to a company account
  • Never automate replies to inbound messages — every reply should be read and sent by a human
  • If a tool is approved, cap its daily volume below what you'd consider safe for manual sending, since automated patterns are what detection systems flag first

Comment & engagement tone standards

  • Comments follow the same brand voice guidelines as outbound messages — see the LinkedIn Comment & Engagement Scripts resource for approved patterns
  • No comment should mention your product, company, or a call-to-action
  • Reps do not use engagement pods or reciprocal like/comment groups — this is a policy violation risk and reads as inauthentic to prospects who notice the same names on every post

Escalation

If a rep's account is restricted, or a prospect reports outreach as spam, escalate to the manager immediately — don't attempt to "ride it out" by continuing to send from the same account. Pause outreach on that account, document what was sent in the days prior, and review against this guide before resuming.

How to use it

Share this with every rep before they run outreach on a company or personal LinkedIn account, and use the Do's/Don'ts sections as the standard when auditing a rep's outreach for brand risk.
