Sales Org Design Blueprint

A structural template for mapping reporting lines, pods, and hunter/farmer/SDR-AE splits as you scale past 10, 30, and 75 reps — with ratios, role definitions, and a reorg rollout plan.

What's inside

  • 5 org design principles to apply before drawing any boxes
  • Stage 1 blueprint (1-10 reps): the Flat Pod structure
  • Stage 2 blueprint (11-30 reps): the Pod Model structure
  • Stage 3 blueprint (31-75 reps): the Segmented Machine structure
  • Manager:rep, SDR:AE, and CSM:AE ratio reference table
  • Role definition one-pagers with explicit handoff criteria (AE, SDR, CSM, SE, RevOps)
  • Redesign trigger checklist — signals it's time to restructure
  • Transition plan template for rolling out a reorg without spiking attrition

Design Principles (apply before drawing boxes)

  1. Structure follows motion, not the reverse — decide PLG / sales-led / hybrid before you design reporting lines.
  2. Segment before you specialize — don't split hunter/farmer until volume justifies it (rule of thumb: past ~30 reps).
  3. One manager, one span — 6-8 reps per frontline manager is the ceiling for real coaching; 12+ is a title, not a management relationship.
  4. Handoffs need owners on both sides, not a queue — every SDR→AE and AE→CSM handoff needs a named SLA.
  5. Design for the org you'll be in 12 months, not the one you're in today — but don't build Stage 3 structure at Stage 1 headcount.

Stage 1 (1-10 reps) — "Flat Pod"

`` Head of Sales / Founder-led ├─ AE 1 ├─ AE 2 ├─ AE 3 ├─ ... up to ~8 AEs (generalist, full-cycle: source, close, often onboard) └─ 1 Sales Ops/RevOps (part-time or fractional) `` No SDR split yet unless outbound is already the primary motion — in which case add 1-2 SDRs supporting the whole pod, not 1:1 with AEs. No manager layer yet; the founder/Head of Sales coaches directly. A 1:8-10 ratio is only acceptable here because there's no hiring/comp/forecasting overhead yet — drop to 6-8 the moment those responsibilities land.

Stage 2 (11-30 reps) — "Pod Model"

`` VP Sales ├─ Sales Manager A (6-8 AEs) ├─ Sales Manager B (6-8 AEs) ├─ SDR Manager (6-10 SDRs; typical 2:1 or 3:1 SDR:AE ratio at this stage) ├─ RevOps (1 FTE) └─ Sales Enablement (1 FTE — often the first dedicated hire) `` Introduce hunter/farmer split only if renewal/expansion volume is material (>20% of ARR at churn risk) — otherwise AEs stay full-cycle including year-one expansion, and a lightweight CS function (1-2 people) owns only onboarding + escalations.

Stage 3 (31-75 reps) — "Segmented Machine"

`` CRO / VP Sales ├─ Director, SMB Sales │ └─ Sales Manager x2 (8:1 ratio acceptable — high velocity, short cycle) ├─ Director, Mid-Market Sales │ └─ Sales Manager x2-3 (6:1 ratio) ├─ Director, Enterprise Sales │ └─ Sales Manager x1-2 + named Sales Engineer overlay (5:1 ratio) ├─ Director, SDR/BDR ├─ Director, Customer Success │ └─ CSM Manager x2 (renewal + expansion, distinct from onboarding specialists) ├─ RevOps (2-3 FTE: systems, forecasting/analytics, comp administration) └─ Sales Enablement (2+ FTE: content/certification + new-hire ramp program) `` Hunter/farmer becomes standard: AEs own net-new, CSMs own renewal + expansion, with a clear ARR-ownership handoff at contract signature — not "go-live." Ambiguity here is the #1 cause of dropped renewals at this stage.

Role Definitions (handoff-focused)

  • AE — Owns net-new bookings from qualified opportunity to signed contract. Handoff IN from SDR at "Sales Accepted Lead" (defined qualification bar met). Handoff OUT to CSM at "Contract Signed" with a completed deal-brief (why they bought, who the champion is, 90-day success definition).
  • SDR — Owns pipeline generation to Sales-Accepted-Lead. Success metric: SQLs that convert to opportunity and stay open 14+ days (filters false positives).
  • CSM — Owns retention + expansion post-signature. Splits into Onboarding CSM (0-90 days) vs. Renewal/Expansion CSM (90 days+) once book size justifies it (~Stage 3).
  • Sales Engineer / Solutions Consultant — Overlay role, pod-based (not 1:1 with AE) from Stage 2 on; comp tied to pod attainment, not individual deals.
  • RevOps — Owns systems, data integrity, forecasting cadence, comp-plan administration. Should exist (even fractionally) by Stage 2.

Redesign Trigger Checklist

  • Manager span has crept past 8-10 direct reports without a structure change
  • Win rates or ramp time are diverging noticeably between segments coached as one group
  • More than 15% of deals stall specifically at a handoff point (SDR→AE or AE→CSM), not mid-cycle
  • Forecast accuracy has degraded two quarters running with no obvious market cause
  • You're hiring a role (first SE, first dedicated CSM) reactively, not because a structural gap was already identified
  • Comp-plan complaints cluster around "my territory can't hit this" (see the Quota-Setting Calculator's fairness audit)

Transition Plan Template

  1. Name the "why" in one sentence tied to strategy, not headcount ("We're splitting SMB and Enterprise because the cycle and skill set are now different enough that one team can't be great at both").
  2. Announce structure + effective date to the full team before any 1:1s — avoids the rumor mill.
  3. Hold 1:1s with every rep whose manager or role scope changes, within 48 hours of the all-team announcement.
  4. Publish updated territory/book assignments and quota implications within 1 week — uncertainty here is the #1 driver of attrition during reorgs.
  5. Run a 30/60/90-day check-in on handoff friction and manager span before declaring the new structure "done."

How to use it

Match your current headcount to the closest stage, copy that structure as your starting draft, then run the redesign trigger checklist quarterly to know when it's time to move to the next stage.
