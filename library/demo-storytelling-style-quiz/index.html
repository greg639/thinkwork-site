Demo Storytelling Style Quiz

A 2-minute, 12-question self-assessment that identifies whether your natural narrative style is Challenger, Consultative, or Vision-Led — and how to demo in that style instead of fighting it.

What's inside

  • 12 scenario-based questions, each with 3 answer options mapped to a style
  • Tally-sheet scoring table
  • Full definitions of the 3 styles: Challenger, Consultative, Vision-Led
  • Strength and watch-out for each style
  • "How to demo in this style" guidance per style
  • Guidance for a tied/blended result
  • Recommended next resource based on your result

How to take this quiz

Answer instinctively — pick the option closest to what you'd actually do, not what you think sounds best. Takes about 2 minutes.

The 12 questions

1. A buyer says their current process "works fine." You: a) Push back — show them the cost they're not seeing in that "fine" process b) Ask more questions to understand what "fine" means to them before saying anything c) Paint a picture of what "great" would look like instead of "fine"

2. Your natural opening line in a demo is closer to: a) "Most teams in your position are making a mistake without realizing it — let me show you." b) "Tell me more about what you're hoping to get out of today." c) "Imagine this time next year — here's where you could be."

3. When a buyer disagrees with you mid-demo, you: a) Hold your position and explain why their current view is costing them b) Explore their disagreement fully before responding c) Reframe the disagreement around the bigger outcome they actually want

4. Colleagues would describe your demo style as: a) Provocative and confident b) Patient and tailored c) Energizing and big-picture

5. You're most comfortable when a demo: a) Has tension — you're teaching the buyer something they didn't know b) Follows their agenda closely, addressing their stated needs one by one c) Builds toward a future state, even if some details get worked out later

6. Your favorite part of a demo to deliver is: a) The moment you reframe how they think about their own problem b) The moment a specific pain point gets solved in front of them c) The moment they can "see" the future state

7. When you don't know the answer to a technical question, you: a) Give your best informed take and follow up with specifics b) Say you'll confirm and follow up precisely — don't want to mislead them c) Connect it back to the outcome so the technical gap doesn't derail the vision

8. Buyers tend to describe you as: a) "They really made me rethink how we do this." b) "They really listened and understood our situation." c) "They got me excited about where this could go."

9. Your ideal demo length and pacing: a) Fast, sharp, a little uncomfortable in a good way b) Matched exactly to their agenda and questions c) Expansive — you'd rather run long painting the future than rush it

10. When closing a demo, you tend to: a) Challenge them to act now before the cost of waiting grows b) Confirm mutual next steps based on what they need c) Leave them with a picture of what's possible if they move forward

11. You get the most energy from: a) Winning someone over who started skeptical b) Solving the exact problem someone brought to you c) Describing a future that doesn't exist yet

12. If your product were a person, you'd want a buyer to say: a) "They told me something I needed to hear." b) "They really got my situation." c) "They showed me something bigger than what I was looking for."

Scoring

Tally your answers:

StyleCount
(a) Challenger___ / 12
(b) Consultative___ / 12
(c) Vision-Led___ / 12

Your highest count is your primary style. If two styles are within 1 of each other, you're a blend — read both.

The 3 styles

Challenger

Definition: you lead by reframing the buyer's understanding of their own problem, sometimes creating productive tension before resolving it. Strength: memorable, differentiates you fast from competitors doing generic feature tours. Watch-out: can read as arrogant if you challenge before you've earned credibility through discovery — always challenge the problem, never the person. How to demo in this style: open with a reframe ("most teams think X, but here's what that's actually costing you"), use data to back the reframe immediately, and land on a specific action, not just the insight.

Consultative

Definition: you lead by deeply mirroring the buyer's stated situation and solving exactly what they asked for, in their language. Strength: builds trust fast, buyers feel heard, low risk of overreach. Watch-out: can stay too close to what they already asked for and miss the chance to expand the vision or differentiate on something they didn't know to ask about. How to demo in this style: lean hard on discovery-to-demo alignment (name their exact words back to them), use the Before-After-Bridge template's Before act as your anchor, and add one "here's something you didn't ask about but should know" moment per demo to avoid being purely reactive.

Vision-Led

Definition: you lead by painting a future state and letting the buyer's own ambition pull them toward it. Strength: energizing, works well with buyers motivated by growth/ambition rather than pain relief. Watch-out: can feel ungrounded if it isn't backed by specific numbers and proof points — vision without evidence reads as hype. How to demo in this style: lean on the After act of Before-After-Bridge, pair every vision statement with one hard number or customer proof point, and don't skip the mechanism (Bridge) — buyers need to believe the path, not just want the destination.

If you're a blend

Most experienced demoers blend two styles. Lead with your higher-scoring style for the first 5 minutes to set tone, then use your second style for the close — e.g., Challenger open + Consultative close reframes the problem, then proves you listened when it counts.

What to do with your result

  • Challenger → pair with the Demo Objection-Handling Swipe File; you'll surface pushback faster and need sharp responses ready.
  • Consultative → pair with the Before-After-Bridge Narrative Template; your strength is in nailing the Before act with precision.
  • Vision-Led → pair with the Demo ROI Calculator; ground your vision in a number the buyer built themselves.

How to use it

Answer all 12 questions instinctively, tally your a/b/c counts to find your primary (and any blended) style, then read the matching "how to demo in this style" guidance and pair it with the recommended companion resource.
