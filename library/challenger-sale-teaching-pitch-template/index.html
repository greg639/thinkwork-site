Challenger Sale Teaching Pitch Template

A fill-in structure for building your own commercial teaching pitch: Warm, Reframe, Rational Drowning, Emotional Impact, New Way, Your Solution.

What's inside

  • Warm-Up fill-in fields, template line, and worked example
  • Reframe fill-in fields for naming the customer's current wrong belief
  • Rational Drowning fill-in for 3-4 data/proof points
  • Emotional Impact fill-in connecting data to personal stakes
  • New Way fill-in for the conceptual shift introduced before the pitch
  • Your Solution fill-in kept brief and proof-backed
  • Full worked example strung together end-to-end
  • Delivery notes on pacing, proof-point count, and tone

Fill in each section for your own commercial teaching pitch. Guidance and example follow each.

1. The Warm-Up

Goal: establish credibility and relevance fast — you understand their world, not just your product.

Fill in:

  • Industry/role-specific observation: _______________________
  • Credibility marker (data point, number of similar customers, research finding): _______________________

Template line:

"We work with a lot of [role/industry] leaders, and one thing we keep seeing is [specific, credible observation] — is that something you're seeing too?"

Example:

"We work with a lot of VPs of Sales at 200-800 person B2B companies, and one thing we keep seeing across nearly 60% of them is that reps spend more time in the CRM logging what happened than actually prepping for what's next. Is that something you're seeing too?"

2. The Reframe

Goal: challenge their current mental model — name the wrong belief they hold, gently but directly.

Fill in:

  • Common belief in their world: _______________________
  • Why it's incomplete or wrong: _______________________

Template line:

"Most people in your seat believe [common belief]. What we've found is that's actually [the incomplete/wrong part] — and here's why that matters."

Example:

"Most sales leaders believe the fix for inconsistent pipeline is more activity — more calls, more emails. What we've found is that's treating a symptom: the real driver of inconsistent pipeline is inconsistent qualification early in the cycle, not volume."

3. Rational Drowning

Goal: overwhelm them briefly and deliberately with data that makes the old belief indefensible.

Fill in 3-4 data points:

  • Data point 1: _______________________
  • Data point 2: _______________________
  • Data point 3: _______________________
  • Data point 4 (optional): _______________________

Template line:

"Here's what the data actually shows: [stat 1]. On top of that, [stat 2]. And when you look at [stat 3], the pattern gets even clearer."

Example:

"Here's what the data shows: deals that pass a real MEDDIC qualification close at 3x the rate of ones that don't. On top of that, reps who ask fewer than 3 Implication questions per discovery call see 40% longer sales cycles. And when you look at win-rate by whether an Economic Buyer was engaged before proposal stage, it more than doubles."

Purpose: make the old way feel intellectually indefensible — factual, not yet emotional.

4. Emotional Impact

Goal: make it personal — connect the data to what it means for THEM specifically.

Fill in:

  • Who specifically feels this pain: _______________________
  • What it costs them personally (career, stress, credibility): _______________________

Template line:

"For you specifically, that means [personal/team-level consequence] — and that's the part that doesn't show up in a slide, but shows up every [timeframe] in [specific personal cost]."

Example:

"For you specifically, that means every quarter you're forecasting deals you can't actually trust — and that's the part that doesn't show up in a board deck, but shows up every QBR when you have to explain a miss you didn't see coming."

5. The New Way

Goal: introduce a new approach conceptually — before mentioning your product.

Fill in:

  • The new mental model/approach: _______________________
  • Why it resolves the tension you've created: _______________________

Template line:

"The organizations getting ahead of this aren't doing [old approach] harder — they're [new approach], which means [core benefit]."

Example:

"The sales orgs getting ahead of this aren't pushing more activity — they're qualifying earlier and harder, using a consistent framework every rep applies the same way, which means deals only enter forecast once they can survive scrutiny."

6. Your Solution

Goal: NOW introduce your product as the vehicle for the new way — kept brief, since the teaching did the selling.

Fill in:

  • Your solution, described as the enabler of the New Way (not a feature list): _______________________
  • One proof point specific to their situation: _______________________

Template line:

"That's exactly what [Your Solution] is built to do — [core mechanism], which is why [proof point relevant to them]."

Example:

"That's exactly what [Product] is built to do — it enforces MEDDIC qualification and surfaces Implication/Need-Payoff coaching in real time during discovery, which is why teams using it see forecast accuracy improve within the first full quarter."

Full worked example (all 6 sections back to back)

"We work with a lot of VPs of Sales at 200-800 person B2B companies, and one thing we keep seeing across nearly 60% of them is that reps spend more time logging what happened in the CRM than prepping for what's next. Most sales leaders believe the fix for inconsistent pipeline is more activity. What we've found is that's treating a symptom — the real driver is inconsistent qualification early in the cycle. Here's what the data shows: deals that pass real MEDDIC qualification close at 3x the rate. Reps asking fewer than 3 Implication questions per call see 40% longer cycles. And win rate more than doubles when an Economic Buyer is engaged before proposal stage. For you specifically, that means every quarter you're forecasting deals you can't fully trust — and that shows up every QBR when you explain a miss you didn't see coming. The orgs getting ahead of this aren't pushing more activity — they're qualifying earlier and harder, with every rep applying the same framework, so only deals that can survive scrutiny make the forecast. That's exactly what [Product] does — it enforces that qualification and coaches the hardest questions in real time, which is why teams using it see forecast accuracy improve within a single quarter."

Delivery notes

  • Keep Warm-Up under 20 seconds — it's a bridge, not the pitch
  • The Reframe must name a belief the customer actually holds — generic reframes fall flat
  • Rational Drowning works best with 3-4 data points, not one; one stat is an anecdote, three is a pattern
  • Emotional Impact should land as an observation, never a guilt trip
  • Your Solution section should be the shortest of the six — if the teaching worked, the product feels obvious, not sold

How to use it

Fill in all six sections once to build your standard teaching pitch, rehearse it end-to-end using the worked example as a pacing guide, then adapt the Warm-Up and proof points per prospect while keeping the Reframe and Insight identical every time you deliver it.
