POC/Pilot Win Plan Template

A structured template that turns a technical proof-of-concept into a mutually agreed success plan with named owners, measurable criteria, and a go/no-go decision framework — so it converts instead of stalling.

What's inside

  • Overview and parties block
  • Business objective statement field
  • Success criteria table: metric, baseline, target, measurement method, data source, owner
  • Scope and explicit out-of-scope fields
  • Timeline/milestone table for a 4-6 week pilot
  • Roles and responsibilities table (vendor vs. customer)
  • Weekly checkpoint agenda template
  • Exit criteria and go/no-go decision framework
  • Sign-off block

Most pilots stall not because the product failed, but because nobody defined what "success" meant before starting. This template forces that definition up front, in writing, signed by both sides.

Weekly checkpoint agenda (recurring, 30 min)

  1. Status against each success criterion (2 min per metric)
  2. Blockers raised this week and resolution owner
  3. Any scope creep or out-of-scope requests — flag and park
  4. Confirm next week's milestone is on track
  5. Action items with owners and dates

Exit criteria and decision framework

Go (convert to full deployment) if:

  • At least 4 of 5 (or all, if only 3-4 defined) success criteria are met or exceeded
  • No unresolved critical blocker remains open
  • Executive sponsor on the customer side confirms business case still holds

No-go (do not proceed) if:

  • Fewer than half of success criteria were met, with no credible plan to close the gap
  • A critical blocker (security, integration, data) remains unresolved at pilot end

Extend (add a defined additional period, not indefinite) if:

  • Criteria are trending toward target but need more time/data to confirm
  • A specific, named blocker has a clear resolution date within 2 weeks

An extension must have its own new end date and its own go/no-go criteria — an open-ended "let's keep going" extension is a no-go in disguise.

How to use it

Complete this template jointly with the customer before the pilot starts, get both executive sponsors to sign the success criteria and exit framework, and run the weekly checkpoint agenda every week so the go/no-go decision at the end is a formality, not a surprise.
