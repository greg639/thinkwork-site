Sales Qualification Framework Directory

A single, searchable reference of the qualification methodologies used across B2B sales — what each acronym stands for, where it came from, and which deal shape it fits — so you stop forcing the wrong framework onto the wrong deal.

What's inside

  • BANT — the original 1950s-60s IBM checklist
  • MEDDIC — enterprise SaaS standard from PTC
  • MEDDPICC — MEDDIC extended for procurement + competition
  • ANUM — authority-first reordering of BANT
  • FAINT — funds/interest model for modern value selling
  • CHAMP — challenge-first alternative to BANT
  • SPICED — Winning by Design's SaaS framework
  • N.E.A.T. — Harris Consulting Group's impact-led model
  • GPCTBA/C&I — HubSpot's inbound qualification model
  • SCOTSMAN — UK enterprise/competitive-bid framework
  • PACT — outbound/ICP-driven qualification model
  • Decision guide: which framework fits which deal shape

FrameworkStands ForOrigin / SourceBest FitWatch-Outs
BANTBudget, Authority, Need, TimelineDeveloped internally at IBM (1950s-60s) as a simple rep checklistTransactional deals, top-of-funnel SDR qualification, short cyclesBudget-first framing assumes budget exists before value is shown; too linear for multi-stakeholder deals
MEDDICMetrics, Economic Buyer, Decision Criteria, Decision Process, Identify Pain, ChampionCreated by Jack Napoli and Dick Dunkel at PTC (Parametric Technology Corporation) in the early 1990s; widely taught via MEDDIC AcademyComplex, multi-stakeholder enterprise deals with long cyclesHeavy for SMB motions; only pays off with disciplined CRM hygiene
MEDDPICCMEDDIC + Paper Process, CompetitionExtension of MEDDIC popularized by enterprise sales methodology consultancies (e.g. Force Management-adjacent practices)Enterprise deals with heavy procurement/legal friction and known competitive pressureEasy to confuse with "MEDDICC" (Competition added, no separate Paper Process) — confirm which variant your org actually uses
ANUMAuthority, Need, Urgency, MoneyInformal reordering of BANT that emerged in sales training circles to fix BANT's budget-first weaknessDeals where confirming the right decision-maker matters more upfront than budgetStill checklist-shaped; doesn't address multi-threading
FAINTFunds, Authority, Interest, Need, TimingAttributed to the modern sales enablement community (popularized via InsightSquared-adjacent training) as a response to buyers creating budget after they see valueValue-led selling motions where budget is fluid rather than pre-allocated"Funds" is softer than "Budget" — requires more rep judgment to qualify accurately
CHAMPChallenges, Authority, Money, PrioritizationPopularized by InsightSquared as a challenge-first alternative to BANTConsultative/solution selling where uncovering the problem earns the right to discuss budgetPrioritization stage is easy to skip under time pressure — don't let it become CHAM
SPICEDSituation, Pain, Impact, Critical Event, DecisionDeveloped by Winning by Design as part of their revenue architecture methodologySaaS/subscription motions — new business, expansion, and renewal"Critical Event" requires real discovery discipline to surface; easy to fake with a soft "end of quarter" answer
N.E.A.T.Need, Economic Impact, Access to Authority, TimelineDeveloped by The Harris Consulting Group with Sales Hacker as a modern, need-first alternative to BANTValue/impact-led selling where quantifying economic impact matters more than an early budget checkRequires reps comfortable with financial/ROI conversations early
GPCTBA/C&IGoals, Plans, Challenges, Timeline, Budget, Authority, Negative Consequences, Positive ImplicationsBuilt by HubSpot for their inbound sales methodologyInbound-led motions and PLG-adjacent SaaS with self-educated leadsLongest acronym in the directory — teams often only use the first four letters in practice
SCOTSMANSolution, Competition, Originality, Timescale, Size, Money, Authority, NeedUsed heavily in UK enterprise/complex sales training (IT, telecom)Competitive enterprise bids needing explicit differentiation and competitive trackingDense — best used as a deal-review framework rather than a live-call script
PACTPain, Authority, Consequence, Target ProfileAssociated with modern outbound-era sales methodology writers building for engagement-heavy prospecting motionsOutbound SDR qualification where ICP fit matters as much as pain"Target Profile" needs a well-defined ICP to be useful — weak without one

Which Framework Fits Which Deal — Decision Guide

If your deal is...Start with
Short cycle, single decision-maker, transactionalBANT or ANUM
Long cycle, multiple stakeholders, enterpriseMEDDIC or MEDDPICC
Competitive bid with formal procurementSCOTSMAN or MEDDPICC
SaaS/subscription, land-and-expand motionSPICED
Inbound-led, buyer already self-educatedGPCTBA/C&I
Value-led, budget created rather than pre-existingN.E.A.T. or FAINT
Outbound-heavy, ICP-driven prospectingPACT
Consultative, problem-first selling cultureCHAMP

Notes on Sourcing

Where a framework's origin is well-documented (BANT/IBM, MEDDIC/PTC, GPCTBA/HubSpot, SPICED/Winning by Design, N.E.A.T./Harris Consulting Group), that's stated directly. Where attribution is contested or informally evolved across the industry (ANUM, FAINT, CHAMP, SCOTSMAN, PACT), that's flagged — search the named organization or consultancy directly for their current published materials rather than relying on a single secondary source.

How to use it

Ctrl+F the acronym or deal type you're dealing with, check the Best Fit and Watch-Outs columns before adopting a framework wholesale, and use the decision guide to pick between close contenders like MEDDIC vs. MEDDPICC.
