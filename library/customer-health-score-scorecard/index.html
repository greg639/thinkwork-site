Customer Health Score Scorecard

A weighted, four-category rubric — usage, engagement, sentiment, and support load — that turns "this account feels fine" into a consistent, defensible number every AM on the team calculates the same way.

A health score is only useful if every account manager calculates it the same way. This scorecard weights four categories, gives fixed 1–5 rubric language for each, and rolls up to a single 0–100 score with a required action per band.

The Four Categories and Their Weights

CategoryWeight
Product Usage & Adoption35%
Engagement & Relationship25%
Sentiment & Satisfaction20%
Support Load & Friction20%

Scoring Rubric — Score Each Category 1 to 5

1. Product Usage & Adoption (weight 35%)

ScoreDefinition
5Using core + advanced features, adoption growing month over month, multiple use cases live
4Core features fully adopted, usage stable or slightly growing, one clear use case live
3Core features adopted but flat usage for 60+ days, single use case, no expansion of scope
2Partial adoption — key modules unused, usage declining over the last 60 days
1Minimal or no active usage in the last 30 days; license largely dormant

2. Engagement & Relationship (weight 25%)

ScoreDefinition
5Multi-threaded across 3+ stakeholders including an executive; proactive outreach from customer
4Champion actively engaged, responsive within 2 business days, attends scheduled check-ins
3Single point of contact, responds but needs chasing, attends some but not all meetings
2Slow or inconsistent responses (5+ business days), meetings frequently rescheduled or skipped
1Unresponsive for 2+ weeks, champion role changed with no successor identified, or single-threaded with no relationship above end-user level

3. Sentiment & Satisfaction (weight 20%)

ScoreDefinition
5Unprompted positive feedback, active reference/case-study participant, high NPS/CSAT (9–10)
4Positive when asked directly, would recommend, NPS/CSAT 7–8
3Neutral — no complaints but no enthusiasm, NPS/CSAT 5–6, or no recent survey data
2Voiced frustration in the last 90 days, NPS/CSAT 3–4, or a competitor actively mentioned
1Formal complaint on file, explicit dissatisfaction expressed to a stakeholder, NPS/CSAT 0–2

4. Support Load & Friction (weight 20%)

ScoreDefinition
5Zero or low-volume tickets, all resolved same-week, no escalations in 90 days
4Normal ticket volume for account size, resolved within SLA, no unresolved escalations
3Ticket volume up but manageable, one resolved escalation in the last 90 days
2Ticket volume up 25%+ vs. baseline, or one unresolved escalation open
1Repeated escalations, an executive-level complaint, or a ticket explicitly citing intent to leave

The Weighted Formula

Health Score = (Usage score × 0.35 + Engagement score × 0.25 + Sentiment score × 0.20 + Support score × 0.20) × 20

(The ×20 converts a 1–5 weighted average into a 0–100 scale.)

Score Bands and Required Action

BandScoreColorRequired Action
Healthy80–100GreenStandard cadence — monitor monthly, prioritize for case studies/references
Stable60–79YellowProactive check-in within 2 weeks; confirm a specific risk isn't developing
At Risk40–59OrangeSave play required — manager-reviewed action plan within 5 business days
Critical0–39RedExecutive escalation same week; joint recovery plan with CS leadership and, if renewal is within 90 days, sales leadership

Calibration Rules

  • Score monthly, on the same day each cycle, using the same lookback window (last 30 days for usage/support, last 90 days for sentiment).
  • Never average two categories together informally — score each independently against the rubric text, not against your general impression of the account.
  • If two AMs would score the same account more than one point apart on any category, that category's rubric language needs a team discussion — bring it to the next team meeting rather than resolving it one-off.
  • A single catastrophic signal (e.g., a formal complaint, a churn threat in writing) should cap Sentiment at 1 regardless of other positive signals that month.

How to use it

Score each of the four categories 1–5 against the rubric descriptions once a month per account, multiply by the category weight, sum for a score out of 100, then use the color band to decide whether the account needs monitoring, outreach, or an escalation.
