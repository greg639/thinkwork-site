Discovery Call Structure Cheat Sheet

A minute-by-minute map of a 30-minute discovery call, from open to next steps, so you never freeze on what comes next or run out of time before you've actually qualified the deal.

What's inside

  • 0:00–2:00 Open & Frame block with exact agenda-setting language
  • 2:00–5:00 Context & Stakes block for surfacing the trigger event
  • 5:00–15:00 Situation & Problem Discovery block (the longest segment, by design)
  • 15:00–22:00 Qualification block covering budget, authority, timeline, process
  • 22:00–26:00 Vision & Mutual-Fit-Check block
  • 26:00–29:00 Next Steps / Mutual Action Plan block
  • 29:00–30:00 Close & Confirm block
  • Talk-time ratio target (30% rep / 70% buyer) with a self-check method
  • Green-flag and red-flag signal list for each block
  • 'Running long' triage rule for which blocks to cut first

The 30-Minute Flow

Print this. Keep it next to your keyboard for your first 20 discovery calls, then let it become instinct. The order matters more than the wording — problem before qualification, qualification before vision, vision before next steps. Selling out of order is the single most common reason discovery calls fail to convert to a next step.

Talk-time target for the whole call: you talk ~30%, they talk ~70%. If you notice you've talked for more than 45 seconds straight, stop and ask a question.

0:00–2:00 — Open & Frame

Objective: Earn the right to ask hard questions later by setting a clear, low-pressure agenda.

Say this:

"Thanks for making time, [Name]. Here's what I'd love to do in the next 30 minutes: spend the first chunk understanding what's driving this for you right now, then ask some pointed questions about how you'd evaluate and decide on something like this — and if it looks like a fit on both sides, we'll figure out a sensible next step together. If at any point it's clearly not a fit, I'll tell you. Sound fair?"

Green flag: They add to the agenda ("Actually, can we also cover X?") — that's engagement. Red flag: They say "just show me what you've got" — reframe: "I will, but 10 minutes on your situation means I show you the right thing, not a generic demo. Deal?"

2:00–5:00 — Context & Stakes

Objective: Find the trigger event. Nothing changes without one.

Ask:

  • "What made now the right time to look at this, versus six months ago or six months from now?"
  • "What happens if nothing changes here in the next two quarters?"
  • "Who else is feeling this besides you?"

Green flag: A specific date, event, or number ("Our renewal is in September," "We lost two reps to this last quarter"). Red flag: Vague answers ("Just always looking to improve") — the deal has no urgency yet. Probe once more, then flag it internally as a longer-cycle, lower-priority opportunity.

5:00–15:00 — Situation & Problem Discovery (longest block — protect this time)

Objective: Understand current state, the specific problem, and its business impact in their words, not yours.

Ask, in this order:

  1. "Walk me through how this works today, end to end."
  2. "Where does it break down, or where does it get painful?"
  3. "How are you handling that today — workaround, spreadsheet, nothing?"
  4. "What does that cost you — in time, money, morale, or risk?"
  5. "If we fixed only one part of this, which part would matter most?"

Rule: Ask "what does that mean for your team / the business?" after every pain point. A stated problem without a quantified impact is not yet a qualified problem.

Green flag: They start explaining internal politics or naming names unprompted — high engagement. Red flag: One-word answers, checking phone, "it's fine, just exploring" — this call may need to end early with a lighter next step (send info) rather than push toward qualification.

15:00–22:00 — Qualification

Objective: Confirm budget, authority, timeline, and decision process without sounding like an interrogation. Frame it as helping them, not gatekeeping.

Bridge line:

"This sounds like a real problem worth solving. So I don't waste your time later, can I ask a few practical questions about how a decision like this would actually get made at [Company]?"

Ask:

  • "Has budget been allocated for this, or would it need to be created / reallocated?"
  • "Besides you, who else needs to sign off before this moves forward?"
  • "What's driving the timeline — is there a date this needs to be solved by?"
  • "What does your evaluation process usually look like for something in this range?"
  • "Have you looked at other ways to solve this — internally built, another vendor, or doing nothing?"

Green flag: Specific names, a real number, a real date. Red flag: "I'd have to check" on every question — you're likely talking to an influencer, not a buyer. Note it, and plan to multithread before the next call.

22:00–26:00 — Vision & Mutual Fit Check

Objective: Paint the "after" state in their language and confirm — out loud — that both sides see a fit.

Say this:

"Based on what you've told me — [restate the trigger, the pain, and the impact in their words] — here's roughly how companies like yours use this to fix that. [1–2 sentence relevant example, not a feature list.] Does that track with what you're picturing?"

Then ask directly:

"Genuinely — on a scale of 1 to 10, how much of a priority is solving this right now?"

Green flag: 7+ with a reason attached. Red flag: 4–6, or a number with no conviction behind it — do not push to next steps yet; ask what would move the number up.

26:00–29:00 — Next Steps / Mutual Action Plan

Objective: Leave with a specific, dated, two-sided commitment — not "I'll follow up."

Say this:

"Here's what I'd suggest: I'll [send X / prepare Y] by [date]. On your end, could you [loop in Z / gather the data on W] by [date]? Then let's get 30 minutes on the calendar for [specific date/time] to go through it together — does that work?"

Rule: Never end a discovery call with only your next action. A next step with no ask of them is a signal the deal isn't real yet.

29:00–30:00 — Close & Confirm

Say this:

"Great — I've got you down for [their action] by [date], and I'll have [your action] over by [date]. I'll send a quick recap by email in the next hour so we're both aligned. Anything I should have asked you today that I didn't?"

That last question routinely surfaces the real blocker.

If You're Running Long

Cut blocks in this order — never cut Qualification or Next Steps:

  1. Trim Context & Stakes to one question.
  2. Trim Vision to a single sentence.
  3. Never cut Situation & Problem Discovery or Qualification — without them you have nothing to qualify or forecast on.

How to use it

Print or pin this beside your video call window and follow the time blocks in order on your next 30-minute discovery call, using the bracketed scripts as a starting point you adapt to your own voice.
