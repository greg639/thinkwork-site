The 9 Interview Questions That Actually Predict Sales Performance (Ranked Against the 6 Everyone Still Asks)

Most interview questions test likeability. These test the competencies that actually show up in month four.

I once watched a VP of Sales spend forty minutes interviewing a candidate, nodding along enthusiastically, then turn to me afterwards and say "I really liked her energy." That was the entire debrief. Not a skill mentioned. Not a competency named. Just a vibe, dressed up as a hiring decision. Four months later she missed ramp by four points and the same VP was asking why nobody caught it in the interview. Nobody caught it because nobody was looking for it, the questions asked couldn't have surfaced it if they'd tried.

That's the actual failure mode in sales hiring. Not bad candidates. Bad questions. Most interview questions are built to produce a comfortable feeling in the interviewer, not evidence against a named skill. You can tell which is which with one test: could you take the answer, hold it up against a competency on your framework, and score it one through five: or could two interviewers watch the same answer and land on completely different verdicts, because there's no skill attached to argue about?

Here's the ranked list. Nine questions that produce scoreable evidence, sitting against six that everyone still asks because they're comfortable, not because they work.

The ranked 15

RankQuestionWhat it actually testsWhy it sits here
1"Walk me through your last lost deal: not the account, the actual call where you felt it slipping."Discovery depth, self-diagnosisForces a specific moment, not a narrative. You can score whether they identify a real skill gap or blame the prospect.
2Cold-call roleplay, interviewer plays a prospect who cuts them off 20 seconds inObjection handling, poise under real pressureLive behaviour beats reported behaviour every time. You watch the skill happen instead of hearing about it.
3"Tell me about a discovery call where you found something the prospect hadn't told anyone else on their team."Discovery / qualification rigourDistinguishes reps who ask questions from reps who ask the right questions. Vague answers are diagnostic in themselves.
4"What's a technique you tried for a full quarter and then dropped? Why didn't it work for you?"Self-diagnosis, coachabilityNobody can fake this without actual reps under their belt. Weak candidates can't name a single thing they stopped doing.
5"Describe a deal where your champion went dark. What did you do, in what order?"Stakeholder management, deal controlSequence matters more than the ending. Good reps have a system; average reps have a story.
6"What does your qualification framework miss? Where does it break down?"Structured discovery, self-awareness of methodAnyone can recite MEDDIC. Few can tell you honestly where their own version of it fails.
7"A prospect says 'just send me some info.' What do you actually say back, word for word?"Objection handling as a technique, not a mood"Word for word" kills the theatre. You're scoring a specific move, not a philosophy.
8"What's the worst feedback a manager ever gave you, and what changed afterwards?"Coachability, evidenced not claimedCandidates who say "I don't really get much negative feedback" are answering a different, more useful question than the one you asked.
9"What number do you track every week that isn't in the CRM?"Rigour, ownership of the pipelineWeak reps have no answer. Strong reps have an idiosyncratic, slightly obsessive one.
10"Sell me this pen."Improv, confidence under a spotlightTests performance skill, not sales skill. There's no competency on any framework called "handling novelty objects."
11"Why do you want to work here?"Rehearsed enthusiasmEvery candidate has the same three answers prepped: mission, growth, team. None of them are falsifiable.
12"What's your biggest weakness?"Whether they've read the same interview-prep advice you haveThe honest answer gets punished, so nobody gives it. You're scoring performance of humility, not humility.
13"How motivated are you by money?"Nothing scoreableEvery candidate says "very, in a healthy way." There's no wrong answer, which means there's no signal.
14"Walk me through your résumé."Narrative constructionYou'll learn what order things happened in. You won't learn whether they were good at any of them.
15"Where do you see yourself in five years?"Career-planning theatreAnswered from a script that has nothing to do with whether they can run a discovery call on Tuesday.

The dividing line

Look at the gap between rank 9 and rank 10. Everything above it names a behaviour you can watch or a decision you can interrogate. Everything below it invites a monologue. That's not a coincidence, it's the actual test. Before you ask a question in an interview, ask yourself which competency on your framework the answer would count as evidence for. If you can't name one, you're not interviewing. You're auditioning.

This matters more than it sounds like it should, because the cost of getting it wrong isn't abstract. A mis-hire on an AE role runs you most of a year in fully-loaded comp, ramp time, and the pipeline they either wasted or poisoned with bad activity, before you even count what it does to the rest of the team watching someone coast. The interview is the cheapest point in the entire employment relationship at which to catch a skill gap. It's also the point most managers spend the least rigour on, because a conversation feels like due diligence even when it isn't.

The other thing rank one through nine have in common: every one of them can be turned into a live exercise rather than a recollection. Reported behaviour ("I handled it by...") is always weaker evidence than observed behaviour, watching them actually handle it. If you've only got budget for one change this month, make it question two: put the candidate into an actual cold-call roleplay and watch what they do when it goes wrong, because it will go wrong, and that's the point. The Cold Call & Discovery Roleplay Interview Script gives you a structured version of this you can run in fifteen minutes without having to improvise the prospect persona yourself.

Building it into a scorecard, not a vibe

The fix isn't asking harder questions. It's asking questions that map to a fixed list of named competencies before the candidate ever walks in, so two interviewers scoring the same answer land in the same place. If you're doing this from scratch, start from a framework rather than inventing competency names on the fly: The Core 12 Sales Competency Framework gives you the vocabulary so "discovery" means the same specific thing to everyone on your panel, and "objection handling" isn't just a feeling you had about how confident someone sounded.

None of this makes hiring easy. It makes it arguable, which is the whole point. A hiring decision you can defend with named evidence is one you can actually learn from when it goes wrong. A hiring decision built on "I liked her energy" just repeats itself, quietly, every single quarter.
