Win/Loss Analysis Interview Guide

The full question set, scoring method, and reporting template for running win/loss interviews that get past 'price was too high' to the real reason a deal was won or lost.

How to use it

Assign interviews to someone other than the deal's AE, run them within 10 days of the decision, and tag every answer against the GTM gap taxonomy before rolling results into your quarterly report.

What's inside

  • Interview setup rules (who should never run it, timing, recording)
  • Universal opening script
  • Full closed-lost question set (12 questions)
  • Closed-won and no-decision question sets
  • GTM gap tagging taxonomy (7 categories)
  • Scoring method (primary + contributing reasons, quote capture)
  • Quarterly reporting template with owned actions

Why this exists

"Price was too high" is an answer buyers give when they don't want to explain the real reason, or when the interviewer didn't dig. This guide gets you past that.

Setup rules

  • Interview 100% of losses over [$X] deal size, plus a random 20% sample of smaller losses, not just the ones your team feels safe asking about.
  • The AE does NOT run this interview. Use a peer AE from a different team, sales ops, product marketing, or a founder/exec. Buyers are candid with someone who wasn't trying to sell them.
  • Call within 10 business days of the decision, memory decays fast.
  • Record with permission; if declined, take verbatim notes.
  • Offer something in return for their time: a summary of market trends you're seeing, or simply "we use this to get better, no sales follow-up."
  • Run closed-won interviews too: losses tell you what's broken, wins tell you what's actually working (and it's often not what your team thinks).

Opening script (all interview types)

"Thanks for taking the time: this isn't a sales call, and I'm not going to try to win you back. I'm on the team that looks at how we compete, and the most useful thing you can do for me is be blunt. Nothing you say here changes your relationship with [Company]. Can I ask you to walk me through the decision from when you first started looking?"

Closed-Lost question set

  1. Walk me through the timeline, when did the problem become urgent enough to go looking for a solution?
  2. Who else did you evaluate, and how did you first find them?
  3. At what point did you form an early favorite, and why?
  4. What were the top 3 criteria your decision actually came down to? (Not the RFP list, the real ones.)
  5. Where did we lose ground against [competitor/status quo]: was it in the demo, the proposal, the trial, or somewhere else?
  6. Was there a moment you almost picked us and then didn't? What happened right before that?
  7. If price hadn't been a factor at all, would you have picked us? (This single question separates real price-losses from everything else hiding behind "price.")
  8. Was there anyone on your team who wanted us but got overruled? What was the overruling argument?
  9. What would we have needed to show you, prove, or change to win this?
  10. On a scale of 1–10, how well did our sales team understand your business? What's the one thing they missed?
  11. Anything about the process: response time, technical depth, executive involvement, that tipped it one way or the other?
  12. Would you consider us in the future, and under what circumstance?

Closed-Won question set

  1. What was the moment you knew you were going to pick us?
  2. What almost made you not pick us?
  3. Who else did you seriously consider, and why did they fall short?
  4. If you had to describe the value of this to a peer at another company in one sentence, what would you say?
  5. What part of the buying experience nearly caused you to walk away or slow down?
  6. Was there a specific person, moment, or artifact (deck, demo, reference call) that tipped it?
  7. What do you wish we'd shown you earlier in the process?

No-Decision / Went Dark question set

  1. What changed internally that put this on hold or off the table entirely?
  2. Was there a competing internal priority that beat this out? What was it?
  3. If budget/timing opens back up, would we be the default pick, or would you re-run the evaluation?
  4. Was there a point where our process felt too slow, too pushy, or mismatched to your timeline?

Scoring & tagging method

For every interview, tag the primary reason (pick one: resist tagging three "co-primary" reasons, it defeats the purpose) and up to two contributing reasons, using this taxonomy:

CategoryWhat it actually means
ICP/FitWe were never going to win this: wrong company size, wrong tech stack, wrong use case
Product gapA named, specific feature or capability we don't have
Pricing/packagingPrice relative to perceived value, or packaging didn't match how they wanted to buy
Competitive executionCompetitor simply ran a better process, demo, or relationship
Sales executionOur rep missed discovery, multithreading, or executive engagement: a skill gap, not a market gap
Timing/priorityReal business change unrelated to us (budget freeze, reorg, M&A)
Trust/riskSecurity, references, company stability, or implementation risk concerns

Score each interview 1 (weak signal) to 3 (strong, repeated signal) on the primary category, and log a direct quote for every category tagged: quotes are what make the report land in a QBR, not just a percentage.

Quarterly reporting template

  • N interviews completed (won / lost / no-decision split)
  • Top 3 loss reasons this quarter, with % of tagged interviews and 1 verbatim quote each
  • Trend vs. last quarter: what moved, what didn't
  • What's a sales execution problem (coachable, fixable in weeks) vs. a GTM problem (product/pricing/ICP: fixable in quarters, needs exec sponsorship)
  • 3 recommended actions, each with an owner and a date, a win/loss report with no owned actions is a report nobody reads twice.
