Pricing Objection Battlecard

A single print-ready page purpose-built for 'you're too expensive' and every variant it shows up in, starting with the one diagnostic question that tells you which response to use.

What's inside

  • A diagnostic question to identify the real cause behind "too expensive"
  • Four response frameworks: competing quote, budget/cash-flow, unclear ROI, internal-selling problem
  • Word-for-word rebuttals for 7 pricing-objection variants
  • A Do's and Don'ts reference table
  • A "never say" list of phrases that soften your position before you've traded anything
  • A fill-in discount-governance/approval-threshold section

Print-Ready — One Page for "You're Too Expensive" and Every Variant

Step 1: Diagnose before you respond

Price objections are rarely about price. Ask ONE diagnostic question first — the response framework changes depending on the answer:

Diagnostic questionIf the answer reveals...Go to
"When you say expensive — compared to what?"A real competing quoteSection A
"Is it the total number, or how it's structured?"A budget/cash-flow issueSection B
"Is the price the issue, or is the value still unclear?"An unproven ROISection C
"Is this your call, or does someone else need to sign off on the number?"An internal-selling problemSection D

Section A — Competing on a real lower quote

"If we're being compared apples-to-apples, send me the quote — I want to make sure it includes the same scope, support level, and implementation. Most 'cheaper' quotes I've seen are missing something that shows up as a cost later." → Never discount blind. Always request the comparison quote first.

Section B — Budget / cash-flow issue

"Let's separate the total number from how it's paid. Would restructuring the payment schedule — quarterly instead of annual, or a phased rollout — solve the cash-flow problem without changing the total investment?" → Trade structure, not price, wherever possible.

Section C — Unclear ROI ("I don't see the value at this price")

"Let's build the ROI with your numbers, not a generic case study. What's the cost today of [the problem], even roughly?" → then walk the math live: (current cost of problem) − (cost of solution) = net gain, annualized.

Section D — Internal-selling problem (buyer believes it, their boss/CFO won't)

"What number does [decision-maker] need to see for this to be an easy yes? Let's build that one slide together right now." → Give them the exact one-pager they need to sell it internally — don't make them improvise your pitch.

Word-for-Word Rebuttals by Variant

"It's too expensive." → "Expensive compared to what — the alternative, or the cost of the problem you have today?"

"[Competitor] is cheaper." → "Send me their quote — if it's the same scope, I'll match it. If it's missing something, that's worth knowing before you sign."

"We don't have budget." → "Is this a timing problem or a priority problem? Those need different solutions."

"Can you just discount it?" → "I can move on structure — term length, payment schedule, scope — what would actually solve this for you?"

"The per-seat cost adds up." → "Let's model total cost of ownership against what you're paying today across tools, workarounds, and time — the per-seat number in isolation is misleading."

"This is a big jump from what we paid last year." (renewal price increase) → "Fair to flag. Here's exactly what's changed — [new capability/scope/cost basis] — and here's what stayed flat."

"We need to think about the ROI more." → "Let's put real numbers on it together right now instead of guessing separately."

Do's and Don'ts

DoDon't
Ask the diagnostic question before respondingJump straight to a discount
Trade term/structure/scope instead of priceDiscount without getting something back (term, scope, timeline, reference)
Request the competing quote before matching itAssume a "cheaper" quote is comparable
Build ROI with the buyer's own numbersLead with a generic ROI slide
Give the internal champion a leave-behind they can forwardMake the champion improvise your pitch to their boss

Never Say

  • "Let me see what I can do" (signals the price is soft before you've traded anything)
  • "I'll ask my manager" as a stalling tactic without a real reason
  • Any number you haven't cleared with your discount-approval policy

Discount Governance (fill in for your org)

  • Discounts up to ____% : rep-approved
  • ____% to ____% : manager approval required
  • Above ____% : VP/Finance approval required
  • Standard trade-offs available without approval: ______________________

How to use it

Print single-sided and keep on your desk or pull up live; always run the Step 1 diagnostic before reaching for a rebuttal, since the right response depends on which of the four causes is actually driving the objection.
