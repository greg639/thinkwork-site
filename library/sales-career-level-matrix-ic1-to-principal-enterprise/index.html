Sales Career Level Matrix (IC1 → Principal/Enterprise)

A leveling framework defining exactly what competency mastery — not tenure or quota alone — qualifies a rep for each career tier, with a behavioral matrix and a promotion-readiness checklist for every transition.

What's inside

  • 4-level definitions from IC1 Associate AE through IC4 Principal/Enterprise AE
  • A 12-competency x 4-level behavioral-anchor matrix
  • An explicit "what does NOT qualify you" list (tenure, quota-alone, likability)
  • A promotion readiness checklist for each transition: IC1→IC2, IC2→IC3, IC3→IC4
  • A calibration-session run sheet for managers scoring the same rep
  • Role-scope definitions per level (deal complexity, stakeholder count)

Sales Career Level Matrix — IC1 → Principal/Enterprise

Purpose

Use this to set expectations, run promotion conversations, and score readiness on competency mastery — not tenure or quota attainment alone. Quota tells you if someone is performing at their current level; it does not tell you whether they're ready for the next one. A rep can hit 130% of quota at IC2 by working harder within IC2-level skills, and still lack the IC3 competencies that make the next tier's larger deals winnable.

The 4 levels

  • IC1 — Associate Account Executive: owns smaller/simpler deals, single-stakeholder or low-complexity accounts, works from a defined playbook.
  • IC2 — Account Executive: owns full-cycle deals of standard complexity, multiple stakeholders, some negotiation.
  • IC3 — Senior Account Executive: owns complex, multi-stakeholder deals, mentors IC1s, adapts approach rather than following a fixed playbook.
  • IC4 — Principal / Enterprise Account Executive: owns strategic/named accounts, C-level relationships, shapes deal strategy and internal process, is a de facto coach.

What does NOT qualify someone for the next level (say this out loud in calibration meetings)

  • Hitting quota at the current level for multiple consecutive quarters — necessary, never sufficient.
  • Tenure alone ("they've been here 3 years").
  • Being liked/well regarded without observable competency evidence.
  • One standout deal that doesn't reflect a repeatable skill.

Promotion readiness checklist (use per transition)

IC1 → IC2

  • Clears IC2 bar on Discovery, Qualification, and Objection Handling independently (no manager assist observed in last 90 days)
  • Forecast accuracy within an agreed % for 2 consecutive quarters
  • Has run at least [N] full-cycle deals solo

IC2 → IC3

  • Clears IC3 bar on Negotiation and Stakeholder Mapping
  • Has won at least [N] deals with 3+ stakeholders
  • Peer or manager can cite a specific instance of "uncovering pain the buyer hadn't named yet"

IC3 → IC4

  • Clears IC4 bar on Executive Presence and Cross-functional Collaboration
  • Has led at least [N] strategic/named-account deals
  • Is already informally mentoring — this makes it formal
  • Has demonstrated the ability to shape internal process (deal desk, pricing exception, etc.), not just follow it

How to run a calibration session with this matrix

  1. Each manager scores their rep 1-5 per competency independently, using recent observed calls as evidence, not gut feel.
  2. Compare scores across managers for reps near a level boundary — recalibrate on evidence, not seniority of the manager speaking loudest.
  3. Where a rep is below bar on a competency required for promotion, that becomes their next IDP quarter's skill focus.

How to use it

Managers use the matrix to score a rep 1-5 per competency against the target level's bar during calibration, then hand the below-bar competencies directly into that rep's next Individual Development Plan quarter.
