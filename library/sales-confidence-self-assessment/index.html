Sales Confidence Self-Assessment

A scored self-assessment across six core selling skills that separates a real competence gap from a confidence gap, so you know whether to train the skill or just get more reps of it.

What's inside

  • 6 core skill areas, each scored on 2 separate axes: Evidence and Feeling
  • Exact evidence-question wording per skill area (cold outreach, discovery, objections, pricing, closing, exec conversations)
  • 1-5 scoring anchors for both the Evidence and Feeling axes
  • A 4-quadrant interpretation table (confidence gap / blind spot / real skill gap / strength)
  • A specific recommended action for each quadrant combination
  • Guidance for reading your overall pattern across all 6 areas

Are you actually under-skilled, or just under-confident?

Most reps treat "I feel shaky doing X" as proof they need more training. Sometimes that's true. Just as often, the skill is already there and what's missing is reps — actual volume of doing the thing while feeling the fear, until the fear stops being useful data. This assessment scores evidence (what you can actually point to) separately from feeling (how confident you feel), across six core skill areas, so you know which lever to pull.

How to score each skill area

For each of the 6 areas below, answer both questions on a 1–5 scale.

Evidence question — score based on what you can actually point to in your last 10–15 calls (not how you feel about it): 1 = Rarely/never happens · 3 = Happens about half the time · 5 = Happens consistently, I could show you the recording

Feeling question — score based on how you feel before or during the moment, honestly: 1 = Dread/avoid it if I can · 3 = Neutral, do it because I have to · 5 = Genuinely comfortable, don't think twice

1. Cold outreach / prospecting

  • Evidence: "In my last 15 cold touches, how often did I get to a real qualifying conversation (not just a voicemail or brush-off)?"
  • Feeling: "How much do I dread sitting down to prospect?"

2. Discovery questioning

  • Evidence: "In my last 10 discovery calls, how often did I uncover a specific, quantified pain point before I started pitching?"
  • Feeling: "How confident do I feel asking a fourth or fifth follow-up question instead of moving on?"

3. Objection handling

  • Evidence: "When a real objection comes up, how often do I address the actual concern rather than reciting a rebuttal?"
  • Feeling: "How much does my stomach drop when I hear an objection coming?"

4. Negotiating / holding price

  • Evidence: "In my last 5 pricing conversations, how often did I hold the number without being the first to offer a discount?"
  • Feeling: "How confident do I feel saying the price out loud and then staying silent?"

5. Asking for the close

  • Evidence: "In my last 10 qualified conversations, how often did I explicitly ask for next steps or a decision, out loud?"
  • Feeling: "How much do I avoid or soften the ask because it feels pushy?"

6. Executive-level conversations

  • Evidence: "In my last 5 conversations with a VP+ title, how often did I keep my structure and questions the same as with any other buyer?"
  • Feeling: "How much does the title on the invite change how nervous I feel walking in?"

Scoring your gap per skill area

For each of the six areas, plot Evidence vs. Feeling:

EvidenceFeelingWhat this meansWhat to do
High (4–5)Low (1–2)Confidence gap — the skill is there, the feeling hasn't caught upDon't train the skill again — get more reps of it while paying attention to the evidence, not the feeling. Ask a manager or peer to confirm the evidence score is accurate; often it's higher than you think.
Low (1–2)High (4–5)Blind spot / overconfidence — dangerous, because nothing is pushing you to improveGet recorded and reviewed by someone else — your self-read is off. This is the quadrant most likely to be invisible to you alone.
Low (1–2)Low (1–2)Real skill gap — feeling and evidence agree, and both say "not there yet"This is genuine training need — role-play reps, shadowing, structured coaching on this specific skill, not just "do it more."
High (4–5)High (4–5)StrengthNothing to fix — consider mentoring someone else in this area, teaching solidifies it further.

Reading your overall pattern

  • If most areas are Evidence-High / Feeling-Low: you're a stronger seller than your internal narrative gives you credit for. The fix is exposure and reps, not more courses.
  • If most areas are Evidence-Low / Feeling-Low: this is an honest, fixable skill-building phase — pick the single lowest-scoring area and drill only that one for two weeks before moving to the next.
  • If any area is Evidence-Low / Feeling-High: flag this to your manager directly and ask for a recorded call review — this is the one combination you cannot self-diagnose accurately.

How to use it

Score all six areas honestly using your actual last 10-15 calls as evidence (not vibes), plot each on the table, and work on only the lowest-scoring one or two areas at a time rather than everything at once.
