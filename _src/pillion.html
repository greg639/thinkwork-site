Why two wheels

We didn't invent the gap. Highway engineering did.

A standard British urban traffic lane is 3.65 metres wide. A family car is about 1.80 metres across. Put two cars side by side in neighbouring lanes and roughly 1.85 metres of empty road opens up along the white line between them.

A 125 is under 0.80 metres across the bars. It fits, with better than half a metre of air either side. Filtering through slow and stationary traffic is lawful in the UK and taught as a core riding skill — it is the entire reason a two-wheeled commute doesn't queue.

Filtering is lawful where it's done safely and at a sensible speed difference. It's also where the risk sits, which is exactly why we're starting with one corridor and experienced riders instead of a fleet.

How a pilot works

Three steps, and you can stop after any of them.

You don't buy anything to find out whether this works for your people. You give us two postcodes, we spend a fortnight riding the route at your hours, and you get the times in writing. Then you decide.

For employers

When the commute already costs you, this is the cheapest thing you can try.

It suits companies of roughly 200 to 800 people, where a slow commute shows up in late starts, tired afternoons and the people you lose to a shorter one.

  • Time back, measured on your routes. Not an industry average — your postcodes, your hours, published whether or not it flatters us.
  • A commute people don't arrive angry from. Standing on a platform at Bank in August is a wellbeing cost you already pay, in sick days and in attrition.
  • No fleet, no capital, nothing on your balance sheet. Riders are licensed, insured and assessed by us. You buy seats, not vehicles.
  • You can walk away after the study. The two-week measurement costs you nothing, and you keep the data either way.
For riders

You own the machine. We bring the work.

The brand, the kit and a passenger who's expecting you — at both ends of the day, on a route you already know by heart.

  • Your bike stays yours. We don't lease you a machine and we don't take a cut of one. We help you onto the right cover and the right licence class.
  • Branding, uniform and a spare lid. Jacket, top box, passenger helmet, visor wipes — so your passenger steps off looking like they came from an office, not a rally.
  • Booked commutes, not a feed of pings. Regular passengers, regular hours, a known route. Peak-hour work without hunting for it.
  • Experienced riders only, and we mean it. Full licence, clean record, and an assessed ride with one of us before you ever carry a passenger. This is the part we'll be unreasonable about.
Straight answers

What we're not claiming yet.

Every pre-launch transport company on the internet promises you a number. We'd rather tell you which of ours are real, which are being measured, and which we've only made a plan to find out.

We have never carried a paying passenger.

Not one. Everything on this page is a plan with a date on it, not a track record. If you need a supplier with references, come back after the pilot.

Our fuel figure isn't verified.

Small bikes get quoted anywhere from 100 to 180 mpg, a range wide enough to be useless. We're metering our own machines on our own routes and will publish the method next to the number.

We're not calling ourselves green yet.

Petrol is petrol. The honest comparison is per passenger-kilometre against a single-occupancy car, a full bus and an EV — and we haven't finished that sum. When we have, it goes here whichever way it lands.

You won't be asked to download anything.

The first corridor runs on a phone number, a named rider and a fixed pick-up time outside your building. If an app would genuinely make your mornings easier, we'll build one then.
