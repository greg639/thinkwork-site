Executive Demo Readiness Checklist

The pre-flight checklist for demoing to a VP or C-suite audience with 12 minutes and zero patience for a feature tour — covering research, message discipline, the opening/closing two minutes, and technical fail-safes.

What's inside

  • 48-hours-before checklist (research, headline number, agenda sent)
  • Morning-of checklist (tech check, one-pager, briefing your internal team)
  • First 2 minutes opening checklist (no small talk drift, lead with the outcome)
  • During-the-demo 12-minute discipline checklist (no feature tour, time-boxing rules)
  • Last 2 minutes closing checklist (specific next step, no vague follow-up)
  • Technical fail-safe checklist (backup slides, recording, connectivity)

Use this before any demo where a VP, C-suite, or other senior executive is in the room and your allotted time is 15 minutes or less. Executives don't want a tour — they want to know it works and what it's worth in the time it takes to read this checklist twice.

48 hours before

  • Confirmed the executive's specific role and what they're accountable for (revenue, cost, risk, headcount — know which)
  • Identified the ONE headline number you'll lead with (cost of status quo or ROI — pick one, not both)
  • Confirmed with your internal champion what this executive cares about specifically, not generically
  • Sent a 3-line agenda in advance — no surprises about what will and won't be covered
  • Cut every planned feature that doesn't map directly to this executive's accountability
  • Prepared a 1-page leave-behind (not a 20-slide deck) with the headline number and 3 proof points
  • Rehearsed the full demo against a strict timer, not an estimate

Morning of

  • Tested screen-share, audio, and camera on the actual device you'll present from
  • Closed every notification source (email, Slack, calendar pop-ups) on the demo device
  • Loaded backup static slides in case of a live product outage or connectivity failure
  • Briefed any internal teammates joining: their one job is to stay silent unless asked a direct question
  • Confirmed the executive is still attending and hasn't been replaced by a delegate (if so, adjust the headline number's framing)
  • Printed or had ready the 1-page leave-behind

First 2 minutes — the opening

  • Skipped small talk beyond 30 seconds — executives read extended rapport-building as a signal you have nothing sharper to lead with
  • Opened with the headline number or outcome, not an agenda recap ("Today I'll show you how to recover $X" beats "Today we'll cover three things")
  • Confirmed time available out loud ("I know you've got 12 minutes — I'll make sure we land on time")
  • Named the specific business outcome this executive is accountable for, explicitly

During the demo — 12-minute discipline

  • Showed no more than 2-3 screens total — if you're clicking through more than that, you've reverted to a feature tour
  • Every screen shown is tied out loud to the headline number or outcome before you click
  • Skipped any "and here's another cool thing" tangent, no matter how good the feature is
  • Checked the time at the halfway mark and cut content if running long — never cut the close
  • Answered technical or detail questions in one sentence, then offered to go deeper after the meeting rather than derailing the executive's time
  • Kept language in business outcomes (revenue, cost, risk, time) not product terminology

Last 2 minutes — the close

  • Restated the headline number once more, using their own words if they reacted to it earlier
  • Proposed one specific, concrete next step with a name and a date attached — not "I'll follow up"
  • Asked directly: "Is there anything that would stop this from moving forward?" and handled what came back on the spot
  • Ended on time or early — never late
  • Sent the 1-page leave-behind and the agreed next step in writing within 2 hours

Technical fail-safes

  • Backup slides ready and tested in case live software fails
  • Recording of a clean, prior run of this exact demo available to share if live product breaks entirely
  • Second internet connection (mobile hotspot) ready as a fallback
  • Confirmed the executive's calendar invite has a dial-in as backup to video
  • Demo environment pre-loaded with realistic (not placeholder) data before the call starts

How to use it

Work through each section in order in the 48 hours before the meeting, run the morning-of and technical fail-safe items right before you go live, and use the during/closing checklists as your on-the-spot discipline during the 12-minute window itself.
