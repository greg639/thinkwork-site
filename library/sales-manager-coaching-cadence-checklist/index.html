Sales Manager Coaching Cadence Checklist

The exact weekly, monthly, and quarterly rhythm top sales managers run to build rep skill on purpose, instead of only coaching when a forecast is already in trouble.

How to use it

Print it and run it as your standing operating cadence: check items off in real 1:1s and pipeline reviews, and use the monthly heat map to pick your team's next skill focus.

What's inside

  • Weekly 1:1 agenda built around one named skill, not deal status
  • Weekly call/recording review checklist (minimum 2 calls per rep)
  • Weekly pipeline coaching pass to diagnose the skill behind stuck deals
  • Monthly skill scorecard update per rep
  • Monthly live ride-along/shadow session
  • Monthly team skill-gap heat map
  • Quarterly peer-manager calibration session
  • Quarterly Individual Development Plan (IDP) refresh
  • Quarterly team skill investment plan
  • Annual full skill audit + manager self-audit

Use this as your running operating rhythm. Print it, pin it, or drop it into your weekly planner. Each cadence block has a purpose statement, so you never confuse "checking the numbers" with "coaching the skill."

Weekly (every week, no exceptions)

1:1 with each rep (30 min)

Purpose: build skill, not just review deals.

  • Rep opens: "What's one thing you tried this week that worked, and one that didn't?"
  • Review 1 specific skill you're both tracking on their development plan (not the whole pipeline)
  • Ask: "What's the hardest conversation on your calendar next week, and what skill does it call for?"
  • Give one piece of specific, behavioral feedback from a call or email you observed this week (not "good job": name the technique)
  • Agree on one micro-commitment for the coming week (a specific technique to try, not "close more deals")
  • Close by asking: "What do you need from me to get unstuck?"

Call/recording review (minimum 2 calls per rep per week)

Purpose: catch skill patterns before they become quota misses.

  • Pick calls at different pipeline stages (not just late-stage: you'll miss discovery and qualification gaps)
  • Score against your competency rubric (not a gut-feel "that was fine")
  • Note 1 strength to reinforce and 1 gap to develop: never more than one of each per call
  • Timestamp the exact moment the skill showed up (or should have) so feedback is concrete
  • Log the observation against the rep's skill scorecard, not just in your head

Pipeline coaching pass (before your forecast call)

Purpose: diagnose why a deal is stuck: the skill behind the stall, not just the stage.

  • For every deal stuck >14 days in a stage, ask: "What skill is missing here: discovery, multithreading, negotiation, or something else?"
  • Separate "deal has a real blocker" from "rep hasn't done the qualifying work"
  • Coach the skill gap in the 1:1, not in a public pipeline review
  • Flag any deal where the rep can't answer "what does the customer's world look like if this fails": that's a discovery gap, not a deal problem

Monthly

  • Update each rep's skill scorecard (competency-by-competency, with evidence/examples, not just a number)
  • Run one live ride-along or shadow session per rep (or shadow a live call if remote): observe, don't rescue
  • Build a team skill-gap heat map: which 2–3 competencies are weakest across the team right now?
  • Pick one team-wide skill focus for the month based on the heat map (e.g., "multithreading into economic buyer") and reinforce it in every 1:1
  • Recognize one rep publicly for a specific skill demonstration (not just a number hit) in a team forum
  • Review your own coaching notes from the month, are you giving the same feedback repeatedly? That's a training gap, not a rep gap

Quarterly

  • Run a calibration session with peer managers: compare how you're each scoring the same competency, using 2–3 shared call recordings
  • Refresh each rep's Individual Development Plan (IDP): current level per competency, target level, and the specific practice plan to close the gap
  • Review team skill investment: what training, peer-shadowing, or content does the team need next quarter?
  • Revisit your weakest-competency heat map from 90 days ago: did coaching actually move it, or just the number?
  • Identify your bench: which reps are ready for stretch accounts, mentoring juniors, or a leadership track, based on skill evidence (not tenure)

Annual

  • Full skill audit per rep against your competency framework: feed this into comp/promotion conversations, not just quota attainment
  • Manager self-audit: pull 5 of your own coaching notes from across the year: are they specific and skill-based, or generic ("great job," "keep pushing")?
  • Rebuild next year's team skill investment plan from this year's evidence, not from what's fashionable

Rule of thumb: if a 1:1 or pipeline review ends without a rep naming a specific skill they're working on, it wasn't coaching, it was a status update.
