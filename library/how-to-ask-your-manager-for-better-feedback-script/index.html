How to Ask Your Manager for Better Feedback (Script)

Exact, word-for-word phrasing for turning a vague 'you're doing fine' into feedback specific enough to actually act on — for six common situations.

What's inside

  • Word-for-word scripts for six real situations (post-call, vague 1:1 feedback, skill-specific ask, post-loss, manager-too-busy, feedback still vague)
  • A follow-up line for when the first ask still gets a vague answer
  • A one-line fallback phrase to keep on hand in any feedback conversation

"You're doing fine" is not feedback — it's a placeholder for a manager who hasn't had time to be specific. These are exact phrases that make it easy (and fast) for a busy manager to give you something you can actually use.

Scenario 1 — Right after a call/deal your manager just watched or listened to

Say:

"Before we move on — can you give me one specific thing from that call, tied to a skill, that I should do differently next time? Doesn't need to be big."

Why it works: the ask is small (one thing), specific (tied to a skill, not a vague vibe), and low-effort (right after, while it's fresh) — hard to deflect with "you're fine."

Scenario 2 — In a 1:1, after you get "you're doing fine" / "keep it up"

Say:

"I appreciate that — but I want to actually get better, not just stay steady. If you had to name ONE skill I should be sharper at in the next 30 days, what would it be, even if it's a small thing?"

If they still stay vague:

"Can you think of a specific call or moment in the last few weeks where that skill showed up? I learn better from a real example than a general note."

Scenario 3 — Asking for feedback on a specific skill you're already working on

Say:

"I've been working on [name the skill — e.g., 'asking better discovery questions before I pitch']. Have you noticed a change in the last few calls — better, worse, same? What's one thing I'm still doing that gets in the way of it?"

Why it works: you've removed the burden of your manager identifying the skill from scratch — they just have to react to what you've already named.

Scenario 4 — After a lost deal

Say:

"Before we file this one away — if you rewound to the point where this deal started slipping, what's the one thing you'd have coached me to do differently there? I'd rather know now than repeat it on the next one."

Scenario 5 — Manager is too busy / feedback keeps not happening

Say (in a 1:1 or over message):

"I know time's tight — could we do a 10-minute version instead of skipping it? I'll bring a specific call and one question, so it's quick for you: [name the call and the question]."

Follow-up if it still doesn't happen after 2 attempts:

"I want to make sure I'm improving on the right thing even if we can't find time together — is there someone else on the team, or a recording, you'd point me to instead this week?"

Scenario 6 — When the feedback given is still too vague to act on

Say:

"That's helpful directionally — can you give me a 'this, not that' version? Like, 'do X instead of Y' — something I could literally do differently on my very next call."

The one-line version to keep in your back pocket

"Can you make that specific enough that I could act on it in my next call?"

How to use it

Pick the scenario that matches your situation, use the phrasing close to word-for-word, and always push once from vague ('you're doing fine') toward specific (a named skill and a real example) before letting the conversation move on.
