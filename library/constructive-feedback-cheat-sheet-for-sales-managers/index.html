Constructive Feedback Cheat Sheet for Sales Managers

A swap-in phrase reference that replaces vague or harsh feedback lines with specific, behavior-anchored alternatives reps can actually act on, organized by the exact situations managers get stuck on most.

How to use it

Keep this open during 1:1 or ride-along prep; when you catch yourself about to say a vague or harsh line, find it in the swap table (or build a new one with the Situation-Behavior-Impact-Ask formula) before you say anything out loud.

What's inside

  • The underlying feedback formula (Situation-Behavior-Impact-Ask)
  • 12 vague/harsh-to-specific phrase swaps covering talk time, close rate, urgency, listening, email quality, qualification, energy, rapport, objections, forecasting, negotiation, and receptiveness to feedback
  • Fill-in-the-blank template using the formula
  • 3 rules for what makes feedback actionable
  • Common feedback traps to avoid

Vague feedback ("be more confident") and harsh feedback ("that call was bad") both fail for the same reason, neither one tells the rep what to actually do differently next time. Every swap below replaces both failure modes with something behavior-anchored and actionable.

The Underlying Formula: Situation → Behavior → Impact → Ask

Use this structure any time you're building feedback that isn't already in the swap list below:

  1. Situation, "In yesterday's call with [account]..."
  2. Behavior, "...when the buyer raised a price objection, you responded with three reasons the price was fair, back to back..."
  3. Impact, "...and I noticed the buyer went quiet and the conversation moved on without the objection actually being resolved."
  4. Ask: "What do you think was really behind that objection, and how might you check that first next time?"

Phrase Swaps: By Scenario

Vague or harsh lineSpecific, behavior-anchored swap
"You talk too much on calls.""In the last three calls I've listened to, you spoke for more than 70% of the call time. Discovery calls work best closer to 40/60 in the buyer's favor, where in this last call could you have asked a question instead of adding another point?"
"Your close rate is bad.""Your close rate on deals that reach proposal stage is 18%, against a team average of 34%. Looking at your last five losses, three stalled right after proposal with no next step confirmed on the call, that's the specific gap worth working on."
"You need to be more aggressive.""On the last two deals, the buyer went quiet for over a week after your last touch, and I didn't see a specific next step proposed on the call. What would proposing a concrete next step, out loud, before the call ends look like for you?"
"You're not listening to the customer.""On the call with [account], the buyer mentioned budget constraints twice, and the next thing you said both times was a feature description rather than a follow-up question. What might you ask instead, next time that comes up?"
"Your emails are sloppy.""The last three emails to [account] had typos in the subject line and no clear single ask, each buyer had to figure out what you wanted them to do next. What would a one-sentence, single-ask close look like on your next email?"
"You didn't qualify that lead properly.""This deal moved to proposal without a confirmed budget range or decision process on file, that's why it's been stuck for three weeks. What questions would get you that information before you propose next time?"
"You need more energy on calls.""On your last two calls, your recorded speaking pace and tone were noticeably flatter than in your best calls this quarter, the buyer's engagement (their talk time) dropped by half after the first five minutes both times. What's different about your best calls that you could bring back?"
"You're not building rapport.""In your last three first calls, you moved to your discovery questions within the first 90 seconds without asking anything about the buyer's context or situation first. What's one thing you could ask before question one, next time?"
"You handled that objection badly.""When the buyer said 'this is more than we budgeted for,' you responded immediately with a discount offer before asking what 'budgeted' meant to them: was it truly no budget, wrong budget, or wrong approver? What would asking that first sound like?"
"Your forecast is never accurate.""Your committed deals closed at 58% over the last two quarters, against an 85%+ target for 'commit' category. Looking at the misses, four of six had no confirmed next step from the buyer at the time you committed them, what would you need to see before calling a deal 'commit' going forward?"
"You're too passive in negotiations.""In the last renegotiation, the buyer asked for a 20% discount and you agreed within the same call without countering or asking what else was on the table. What's one thing you could trade for instead of just discounting, next time?"
"You don't take feedback well.""The last two times I raised a coaching point, the conversation moved to explaining why the situation was different rather than to what you'd try next time. What would help you feel safer trying something differently, even once, before deciding it won't work?"

Three Rules for Actionable Feedback

  1. Name the behavior, not the trait. "You were unprepared" is a trait judgment. "You didn't have the account's last quarter results ready when the buyer asked" is a behavior, the rep can fix a behavior; they can't directly fix a trait.
  2. Use a number wherever one exists. Talk-time ratio, close rate, days-to-next-step, forecast accuracy, a number removes "that's not how I remember it" from the conversation.
  3. End every piece of feedback with a question, not a directive. "What would you try differently?" builds the rep's own diagnostic skill. "Do X next time" builds compliance, not skill.

Fill-In-the-Blank Template

"In [situation], when [specific behavior], [specific impact happened]. What do you think you could try differently next time?"

Common Feedback Traps to Avoid

  • The compliment sandwich, burying real feedback between two pieces of praise trains reps to wait for the "but." Say the real thing directly, with respect, not padding.
  • Feedback with no evidence: if you can't point to a specific call, deal, or moment, you're not ready to give the feedback yet; go find the evidence first.
  • Multiple points in one sitting, pick the single highest-leverage piece of feedback. Three at once means the rep remembers none of them.
