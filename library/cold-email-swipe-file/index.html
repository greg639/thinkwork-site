Cold Email Swipe File

Thirty outbound email templates organized by scenario — cold, referral, breakup, and re-engagement — each with subject-line variants ready to copy and personalize today.

What's inside

  • 12 Cold outreach templates across 6 angles (trigger-event, problem-first, curiosity, social-proof, pattern-interrupt, peer-comparison)
  • 4 Referral-based templates
  • 6 Breakup/last-attempt templates
  • 8 Re-engagement templates for gone-quiet and dormant leads
  • 2 subject-line variants for every template (60 total subject lines)
  • Personalization bracket guide — what to swap in before sending
  • Send-sequence guidance — which templates pair together across a cadence
  • Rules on what NOT to do (banned openers, length limits)

30 templates, organized by scenario, each with subject-line variants. Replace every [bracket] before sending — nothing in brackets should survive to send.

Personalization Bracket Guide

  • [First Name], [Company] — obvious, never skip
  • [Trigger] — a real, specific, recent event: funding round, job change, product launch, hiring post, LinkedIn post they wrote
  • [Peer Company] — a named, comparable company (with permission if using them as a reference)
  • [Specific Metric/Result] — a real number, never “significant results”
  • [Mutual Connection] — a real named person, only if genuinely mutual

Rule: an email with more than one unfilled bracket does not go out.

SECTION A — Cold Outreach (12 templates, 6 angles × 2 each)

A1. Trigger-Event (i)

Subject A: noticed [Trigger] / Subject B: congrats on [Trigger]

"Hi [First Name] — saw [Trigger] and figured [logical consequence of trigger, e.g. 'your team's probably scaling fast right now']. That's usually when [Problem] starts showing up. Worth a quick chat, or not a priority right now?"

A2. Trigger-Event (ii)

Subject A: question re: [Trigger] / Subject B: [Company] + [Trigger]

"[First Name] — congrats on [Trigger]. Companies at that stage usually run into [Problem] around month 3–6. If that's not you yet, no worries — if it is, happy to share what's worked for similar teams."

A3. Problem-First (i)

Subject A: [Problem], solved differently / Subject B: how [Peer Company] handles [Problem]

"Hi [First Name] — most [Role]s I talk to are dealing with [Problem], usually because [root cause]. We built a way to fix that without [common unwanted tradeoff]. Is this on your radar at all right now?"

A4. Problem-First (ii)

Subject A: the [Problem] tax / Subject B: quick one on [Problem]

"[First Name] — quick question: how much time does your team lose to [Problem] in a typical month? Most people underestimate it until they measure it. Happy to share how we measure it if useful."

A5. Curiosity (i)

Subject A: unusual question / Subject B: is this still true?

"[First Name] — unusual question: is [specific process/assumption] still handled manually at [Company]? Most teams your size have automated it by now, so genuinely curious either way."

A6. Curiosity (ii)

Subject A: 12% / Subject B: the 12% number

"[First Name] — companies like [Company] typically lose about [Specific Metric] to [Problem] before fixing it. Not sure if that's true for you — worth 10 minutes to find out?"

A7. Social-Proof (i)

Subject A: what [Peer Company] did about [Problem] / Subject B: [Peer Company] case

"Hi [First Name] — [Peer Company] had the same [Problem] you're likely dealing with at [Company]'s stage. They fixed it and saw [Specific Metric/Result]. Want the two-minute version of how?"

A8. Social-Proof (ii)

Subject A: why [Peer Company] switched / Subject B: re: [Category] at [Company]

"[First Name] — a handful of [Industry] companies your size (including [Peer Company]) have moved off [old approach] recently. The reason, consistently, is [Problem]. Curious if you're seeing the same thing."

A9. Pattern-Interrupt (i)

Subject A: not a pitch / Subject B: genuinely just a question

"[First Name] — not trying to sell you anything in this email, just want to ask: is [Problem] actually a priority for [Company] this quarter, or lower on the list? Either answer is useful for me to know."

A10. Pattern-Interrupt (ii)

Subject A: wrong person? / Subject B: am I off base

"[First Name] — might be totally off base here, but is [Problem] something you own, or should I be talking to someone else on your team?"

A11. Peer-Comparison (i)

Subject A: how you compare / Subject B: benchmarking [Category]

"[First Name] — we've been tracking how companies like [Company] handle [Problem] compared to peers. Happy to send over where you'd likely land, no strings — useful either way?"

A12. Peer-Comparison (ii)

Subject A: you vs. the field / Subject B: quick benchmark

"Hi [First Name] — most [Role]s at companies your size are around [Specific Metric] on [Problem]. Curious where [Company] sits — want me to send the quick comparison?"

SECTION B — Referral-Based (4 templates)

B1. Warm Intro Ask

Subject A: [Mutual Connection] suggested I reach out / Subject B: intro via [Mutual Connection]

"Hi [First Name] — [Mutual Connection] mentioned you might be dealing with [Problem] and thought it'd be worth connecting. No pressure either way — happy to share what we worked on together if it's relevant."

B2. Customer Referral

Subject A: [Customer] recommended I reach out / Subject B: from [Customer]'s team

"[First Name] — I work with [Customer], and your name came up as someone who might be dealing with the same [Problem] they had before we fixed it. Want the quick version of what changed for them?"

B3. Community/Group Referral

Subject A: fellow member of [Group] / Subject B: saw you in [Group]

"Hi [First Name] — noticed we're both in [Group]. A few members have mentioned [Problem] recently — is that something you're dealing with too, or not really?"

B4. Second-Degree Ask

Subject A: [Mutual Connection]'s colleague / Subject B: reaching out via [Mutual Connection]

"[First Name] — I know [Mutual Connection] from [context], and they mentioned you lead [function] at [Company]. Given what I do around [Problem], thought it was worth a quick note — useful, or not the right time?"

SECTION C — Breakup / Last Attempt (6 templates)

C1. Straightforward Close-Out

Subject A: closing the loop / Subject B: should I stop reaching out?

"[First Name] — haven't heard back, so I'll assume the timing's off or this isn't a priority. I'll close this out on my end — if that changes, just reply and I'll pick it back up."

C2. Permission-Based

Subject A: ok to check back later? / Subject B: one last thing

"Hi [First Name] — I don't want to keep filling your inbox. Is it fair to say now's not the time, and ok if I check back in [timeframe] instead?"

C3. Value-Add Final

Subject A: last note, useful either way / Subject B: one resource before I go

"[First Name] — last email from me on this. Whether or not we ever work together, thought this [resource/insight related to Problem] might be useful. Good luck with [relevant priority]."

C4. Direct Ask

Subject A: yes, no, or not yet? / Subject B: quick one before I stop

"[First Name] — totally fine either way, but which of these is true: (a) not relevant, (b) relevant but bad timing, (c) worth a quick call? A one-word reply is all I need."

C5. Humor/Light Touch

Subject A: taking the hint / Subject B: I'll stop now

"[First Name] — I've emailed enough times that I'm now the annoying part of your inbox, so I'll stop here. If [Problem] ever becomes urgent, my door's open — no hard feelings either way."

C6. Reassign/Redirect

Subject A: wrong contact? / Subject B: before I close this out

"[First Name] — before I stop reaching out, is there someone else at [Company] who owns [Problem] that I should be speaking to instead?"

SECTION D — Re-Engagement / Gone Quiet (8 templates)

D1. Time-Based Check-In

Subject A: it's been a while / Subject B: checking back in

"[First Name] — it's been [timeframe] since we last spoke about [Problem]. Has anything changed on your end, or still not the right time?"

D2. New Trigger Event

Subject A: saw [New Trigger] / Subject B: timing might be different now

"Hi [First Name] — saw [New Trigger] and thought the timing on [Problem] might have changed since we last spoke. Worth revisiting, or still not a priority?"

D3. New Proof Point

Subject A: update since we last spoke / Subject B: new result worth sharing

"[First Name] — since we last talked, we helped [Peer Company] achieve [New Result]. Given where you left things, thought it was worth flagging. Interested in a quick update call?"

D4. Product/Feature Update

Subject A: we built the thing you asked about / Subject B: this didn't exist last time we spoke

"Hi [First Name] — last time we spoke, [specific gap/objection] was the blocker. That's changed — we now [new capability]. Worth another look?"

D5. Direct Status Check

Subject A: still relevant? / Subject B: where did this land?

"[First Name] — following up from [timeframe] ago on [Problem]. Did you end up solving this a different way, or is it still open?"

D6. Internal Champion Check

Subject A: did priorities shift? / Subject B: checking if this is still yours

"Hi [First Name] — wanted to check whether [Problem] is still something you're focused on, or if priorities have shifted since we last spoke."

D7. Seasonal/Budget-Cycle

Subject A: planning season / Subject B: budget timing

"[First Name] — given it's [budget cycle/quarter], thought I'd check whether [Problem] has moved back onto the list for planning purposes."

D8. Soft Re-Open

Subject A: no agenda, just checking in / Subject B: hope things are going well

"Hi [First Name] — no agenda here, just wanted to check in since it's been a while. If [Problem] is still around, happy to pick the conversation back up whenever suits."

Send-Sequence Guidance

A standard cold cadence: A-template (day 1) → A or B variant (day 4) → D-style value-add (day 9) → C-template breakup (day 16). Never send two Cold Outreach templates back-to-back without a value-add or breakup in between — repetition without new information reads as spam.

Rules — What Not To Do

  • No subject line in ALL CAPS or with more than one exclamation mark
  • No “just following up” as a standalone subject line — always pair with a reason
  • No email over 90 words — cold emails are read on a phone in 8 seconds
  • Never send a Breakup template before at least 2 prior touches
  • Never use a Peer Company reference without permission to name them

How to use it

Pick the template matching your scenario and angle, fill every bracket with real specifics (no unfilled brackets ship), and sequence templates across a cadence per the Send-Sequence Guidance rather than sending the same angle twice in a row.
