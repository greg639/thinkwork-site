Sales Candidate Scorecard Calculator

A weighted scorecard that turns interviewer gut-feel into an objective hire/no-hire number: set your must-have competencies, enter each panelist's scores, and let the formula do the deciding.

How to use it

Copy the tables into Excel or Google Sheets, set your weights and Must-Haves before the interview loop starts, have every interviewer score independently using the Step 2 rubric, then apply the Step 4 formula to get the objective number for the debrief.

What's inside

  • Default 9-competency framework with recommended weight ranges
  • 1-5 anchored scoring rubric with behavioral descriptors for every point on the scale
  • Must-have veto rule that overrides the composite score
  • Weighted-average formula, spreadsheet-ready (Excel/Google Sheets)
  • Multi-interviewer roll-up table structure
  • Hire-decision thresholds: Strong Hire / Hire / Lean No / No Hire
  • Fully worked numerical example
  • Calibration notes to prevent halo effect, recency bias, and deference in the debrief

What this does

Turns a panel of interviewers' subjective impressions into one weighted number, so the hire/no-hire call is made on evidence instead of whoever spoke loudest in the debrief.

Adjust weights to your stage: earlier-stage teams should weight Discovery and Resilience higher; scaled teams with a defined playbook should weight Pipeline Management and Business Acumen higher.

Step 2: The 1-5 scoring rubric

Use this anchor scale for every competency, every interviewer:

ScoreLabelAnchor
1Below barClear evidence candidate cannot do this today; no relevant example produced when asked directly
2Approaching barSome evidence, but shallow, generic, or requires heavy prompting to surface
3Meets barSolid, specific example with a clear outcome; would perform this competency adequately in the role from day one
4Exceeds barSpecific example plus evidence of range: has adapted this skill across different situations
5ExceptionalRare; candidate teaches you something in the interview about this competency

Step 3: The Must-Have veto rule

If ANY competency flagged as Must-Have receives an average score of 2 or below from the panel, the final recommendation is automatically capped at No Hire, regardless of the composite score below. Document this explicitly in the debrief so no one argues the composite number around it.

Step 4: The formula

For each interviewer:

`` Interviewer Weighted Score = SUM(competency score × competency weight) / 100 ``

Spreadsheet formula (weights in column B, one interviewer's scores in column C, 9 competency rows starting row 2):

`` =SUMPRODUCT(C2:C10, $B2:$B10) / 100 ``

Then average across all interviewers on the panel to get the Final Composite Score:

`` Final Composite = AVERAGE(Interviewer 1 Weighted, Interviewer 2 Weighted, Interviewer 3 Weighted...) ``

Calibration notes for the debrief

  • Have every interviewer submit their table BEFORE the debrief conversation starts, nobody sees anyone else's scores first.
  • Debrief order: most junior interviewer speaks first, most senior speaks last. Prevents deference bias collapsing the room to whatever the most senior person thinks.
  • Anchor every score to a specific moment in the interview, not a general impression. If an interviewer can't cite the moment, the score gets re-examined.
  • Watch for halo effect: a candidate who is excellent at Communication tends to get inflated scores everywhere else. Score competencies independently, one at a time, not candidate-by-candidate.
  • Recency bias: the last interview of the day is remembered best. If you're comparing multiple candidates, score immediately after each interview, not at the end of the day.
