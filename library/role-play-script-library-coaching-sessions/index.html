Role-Play Script Library for Coaching Sessions

20 realistic buyer objection and stall scenarios, organized by category, that reps can rehearse solo or with a manager before the moment shows up live on a real call.

How to use it

Assign 2-3 scenarios per week matched to a rep's current skill gap, run them either solo-recorded or live with a manager playing the buyer, and use the attached debrief questions immediately after, before moving to the next scenario.

What's inside

  • Price & Budget objection scenarios (4 scripts)

  • Trust, Risk & Status Quo scenarios (4 scripts)

  • Debrief question set attached to every scenario
  • Solo-rehearsal variant instructions (record-and-review)

20 buyer scenarios, grouped by category. Each includes what the buyer says, the context a rep needs to play it straight, what the rep is being drilled on, and 2-3 debrief questions to run immediately after.

Two ways to use these:

  • Solo: Rep records themselves responding out loud to the buyer line (phone voice memo is fine), plays it back, and answers the debrief questions in writing before their next 1:1
  • Manager-led: Manager (or a peer) plays the buyer using the exact line and context below, pushes back once if the rep's first response is generic, then runs the debrief live

A. Price & Budget Objections

A1: The Flat No Buyer says: "Honestly, it's just more than we budgeted for. We're going to have to pass." Context: Third call, buyer has seen the proposal, budget wasn't discussed until now. Drilling: Isolating whether this is truly budget or a proxy for unconvinced value. Debrief: Did you isolate before you discounted or justified? What did you learn about their actual budget process?

A2, The Comparison Trap Buyer says: "Your competitor quoted us 30% less for what looks like the same thing." Context: Buyer has a competing quote in hand and is testing your reaction. Drilling: Reframing on differentiated value without disparaging the competitor. Debrief: Did you ask what "the same thing" means to them specifically, or did you assume you knew?

A3: The Internal Champion's Problem Buyer says: "I love it, but my CFO is going to ask why we're not just using the free tool we already have." Context: Champion is on your side but needs ammunition, not a rebuttal aimed at them. Drilling: Arming a champion instead of re-pitching the person in front of you. Debrief: Did you coach the champion for their internal conversation, or did you keep selling to them directly?

A4, The Phased Ask Buyer says: "Could we start with a smaller piece of this to prove it out before committing to the full number?" Context: Genuine interest, genuine budget caution, not a stall. Drilling: Distinguishing a legitimate phased-rollout request from a stall, and responding to each differently. Debrief: What told you this was real caution and not an exit ramp? How did your response differ from A1?

B. Timing & Stall Tactics

B1: The Quarter-End Push Buyer says: "This all looks right, let's just pick it back up next quarter once things settle down." Context: No new information has emerged since the last call; buyer sounds mildly checked out. Drilling: Isolating whether "next quarter" is a real constraint or a polite decline. Debrief: What specific question did you ask to test whether this was real or a stall?

B2: The Reorg Excuse Buyer says: "We're in the middle of a reorg right now, this isn't the right time." Context: Reorg is real and verifiable, but the buyer hasn't said whether their role/budget is affected. Drilling: Respecting a real constraint while still finding out what actually changes for them. Debrief: Did you find out if THEIR budget or authority is affected, or did you accept the reorg as a full stop?

B3: The Silent Champion Buyer says (after 2 weeks of no response, finally replying): "Sorry, been slammed, still very interested, just need to find the time to regroup internally." Context: Re-engaging a gone-cold deal without sounding needy or accusatory. Drilling: Re-opening a stalled deal with a low-pressure, specific ask. Debrief: Did your re-engagement message create a reason to reply, or just ask "any update?"

B4, The Perpetual Evaluator Buyer says: "We want to keep evaluating a couple more options before we lock anything in." Context: Fourth month of "evaluating," no clear criteria has ever been shared. Drilling: Getting a buyer to commit to actual decision criteria and a date, not just more time. Debrief: Did you ask what "done evaluating" actually looks like to them?

C. Competitor & Alternative Objections

C1, The Named Competitor Buyer says: "We're also looking at [specific well-known competitor] and honestly leaning that way." Context: Buyer names a real, credible alternative directly. Drilling: Differentiating on substance without badmouthing. Debrief: Did you ask what's drawing them to the competitor before responding? Did you disparage, or differentiate?

C2, The Build-vs-Buy Buyer says: "Our engineering team thinks we could just build this internally." Context: A build-it-yourself objection from a technically confident buyer. Drilling: Reframing on total cost and opportunity cost without sounding threatened by their team's capability. Debrief: Did you validate their team's capability before reframing on cost/time/focus?

C3: The Status Quo Vendor Buyer says: "We already have a vendor for this, switching feels like a lot of disruption for uncertain gain." Context: Incumbent vendor is mediocre but "good enough," switching cost is the real objection. Drilling: Making switching cost feel proportionate to the gap in outcomes. Debrief: Did you quantify the gap, or just assert that your solution is better?

C4, The Feature Checklist Buyer says: "Their spec sheet has three features you don't have. Can you match those?" Context: Buyer is running a checkbox comparison, possibly at a colleague's request. Drilling: Redirecting from feature parity to outcome relevance without dodging the question. Debrief: Did you answer honestly first, then redirect to what actually matters for their use case?

D. Trust, Risk & Status Quo Objections

D1, The Burned Buyer Buyer says: "We tried something like this before and it was a disaster. I'm gun-shy." Context: Genuine past bad experience, buyer is projecting it onto you. Drilling: Acknowledging without becoming defensive about a product they haven't tried yet. Debrief: Did you find out specifically what went wrong last time before responding?

D2, The Risk-Averse Exec Buyer says: "What happens to us if this doesn't work out? What's our downside?" Context: Buyer is evaluating worst-case, not best-case. Drilling: Answering the downside question directly and specifically instead of pivoting back to upside. Debrief: Did you actually answer the downside question, or redirect to benefits?

D3: The "It's Fine As Is" Objection Buyer says: "Honestly, what we have isn't great, but it's fine. I'm not sure the juice is worth the squeeze." Context: Classic status-quo bias, pain is real but change-aversion is stronger. Drilling: Quantifying the cost of "fine" without manufacturing urgency that isn't real. Debrief: Did you help them quantify the actual cost of the status quo, in their own numbers?

D4, The Reference Check Stall Buyer says: "We'd want to talk to a couple of your customers before we go further." Context: Reasonable diligence request, could also be a soft stall if not followed through. Drilling: Turning a reasonable request into a forward-moving next step with a date attached. Debrief: Did you get a specific date for the reference calls, or leave it open-ended?

E. Access, Authority & Multi-Stakeholder Stalls

E1: The Absent Decision-Maker Buyer says: "This all sounds good, but I'll need to run it by my boss, who hasn't been part of any of these calls." Context: You're 3 calls deep and just learned the real decision-maker isn't in the room. Drilling: Getting access to the real decision-maker without going around your contact. Debrief: How did you ask for access without making your champion feel bypassed?

E2: The Committee Buyer Buyer says: "There are actually five of us who need to sign off on this, and we haven't all met yet." Context: Multi-stakeholder buying committee revealed late in the process. Drilling: Mapping the committee and each stakeholder's likely concern instead of treating them as one buyer. Debrief: Did you ask who else is involved and what each person cares about, specifically?

E3: The Procurement Gatekeeper Buyer says: "Great, I'll pass this to our procurement team, they'll take it from here." Context: Champion is trying to hand off, risking loss of relationship and context. Drilling: Staying involved through procurement without overstepping the champion. Debrief: Did you secure a way to stay in the loop, or did you let the deal go dark into procurement?

E4: The Silent Stakeholder in the Room Buyer says (mid-call, one attendee has said nothing for 20 minutes): [no verbal objection: the scenario is the silence itself] Context: A stakeholder on the call hasn't engaged at all; could be disinterest, disagreement, or just quiet. Drilling: Actively drawing out a silent stakeholder without putting them on the spot. Debrief: What did you do or say to invite the silent person in? What did their answer reveal?

Solo-Rehearsal Protocol

  1. Read the buyer line and context out loud
  2. Record yourself responding as if it's live, no pausing to compose the perfect answer
  3. Play it back once, cold
  4. Answer the debrief questions in writing
  5. Re-record once, incorporating what you noticed

Manager-Led Protocol

  1. Manager plays the buyer, delivers the line cold, in character
  2. Rep responds live
  3. Manager pushes back once if the first response is generic ("I hear that a lot, but I'm still not sure...")
  4. Stop and debrief immediately using the attached questions, don't let 3 scenarios run before debriefing any of them
