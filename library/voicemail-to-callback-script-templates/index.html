Voicemail-to-Callback Script Templates

12 voicemail scripts, each engineered around a specific psychological trigger and benchmarked against typical callback-rate patterns, so you can match the right script to the right contact.

The 4 rules every script below follows

  1. Under 20 seconds. Longer voicemails get skipped mid-message. Time yourself.
  2. Name and number twice — once at the start, once at the end, spoken slowly enough to write down without replaying.
  3. One reason, not a pitch. A voicemail's only job is to earn a callback — not to sell.
  4. State-dependent tone. Smile before you press record. Flat, monotone voicemails are the single biggest driver of no-callbacks regardless of script quality.

Each script below includes a relative callback-rate note — which pattern tends to outperform a generic "just checking in" voicemail (the weakest-performing category in cold-outreach data) and why.

1. The Curiosity Gap

"Hi [Name], it's [Your name] with [Company]. I came across something about [specific, real detail — a role they're hiring for, a product launch, a market shift] and had a quick thought I wanted to run by you — not a sales thing, just wanted your take. I'm at [number], or I'll try you again in a couple of days."

Why it earns callbacks: open loops are uncomfortable to leave unresolved — the vaguer the "thought," the more curiosity does the work. Outperforms generic voicemails by a wide margin because it withholds information instead of pitching it.

2. The Specific Insight

"Hi [Name], [Your name] from [Company]. I noticed [specific, verifiable detail about their business]. We've seen [related pattern] with a couple of teams like yours, and I had one idea specific to that I wanted to share — two minutes, no pitch. Call me back at [number] whenever's easy."

Why it earns callbacks: specificity is the credibility signal a voicemail has to establish alone, with no gatekeeper or tone to help. Generic voicemails ("wanted to introduce myself") get deleted because they could have been left for anyone.

3. The Peer Comparison

"Hi [Name], it's [Your name] with [Company]. We're working with a couple of other [role/title] at companies like [comparable company or industry] on [specific problem], and I had a thought specific to how you're set up. Worth two minutes — I'm at [number]."

Why it earns callbacks: social proof plus specificity to their situation. Works best when the comparable company is a genuine, named peer — vague "companies like yours" performs worse.

4. The Referral Name-Drop

"Hi [Name], it's [Your name] — [Referrer name] suggested I reach out. I'll keep this quick: [one-sentence reason]. Call me back at [number], or I'll try you again in a few days."

Why it earns callbacks: highest-performing category when a real referral exists — borrowed trust converts far better than any wording. Never fabricate the referral; it's checkable and the damage from being caught outweighs any callback.

5. The Pattern-Interrupt / Humor

"Hi [Name], [Your name] from [Company]. I'll be honest — this is a cold call voicemail, and I know you get a hundred of these. I'll spare you the script: [one real, specific reason for calling]. If it's not relevant, tell me to lose your number — otherwise I'm at [number]."

Why it earns callbacks: naming the awkwardness disarms the automatic "sales voicemail" filter. Works especially well with senior, time-poor titles who respond to directness over polish.

6. The Deadline-Bound

"Hi [Name], [Your name] with [Company]. Quick one — we're [closing enrollment / wrapping a pilot / finalizing pricing] on [specific real date], and I wanted to see if it's worth a look before then. Two minutes, I'm at [number]."

Why it earns callbacks: real deadlines create a reason to act now. Only use a genuine constraint — fabricated urgency is detectable and erodes trust on the callback itself.

7. The Short-and-Direct (under 10 seconds)

"[Name], [Your name], [Company], [number]. Call me back."

Why it earns callbacks: for senior executives who scan voicemail transcripts rather than listen, brevity out-converts elaboration. Best paired with a text or email sent within the hour that carries the actual detail.

8. The Second-Attempt Callback

Use after one prior unanswered voicemail — reference it, don't repeat it verbatim.

"Hi [Name], [Your name] again from [Company] — left you a message last week about [one-phrase reminder]. Still think it's worth two minutes; I'm at [number], or happy to just send something over instead if that's easier."

Why it earns callbacks: the "or send something over" close gives an easy secondary yes when a callback feels like too much friction, and often converts into an email thread that leads to the call anyway.

9. The Executive-to-Executive

For C-level or VP-level targets — peer framing outperforms vendor framing.

"[Name], it's [Your name], [Your title] at [Company]. I'll keep this direct — [one-sentence business reason framed as a peer observation, not a pitch]. If it's worth a conversation, I'm at [number]; if not, no hard feelings."

Why it earns callbacks: "no hard feelings" removes the pressure that makes senior executives screen calls in the first place — paradoxically increasing response rate.

10. The Trigger-Event

Tied to a real, timely event — funding round, new hire, expansion, press mention, earnings call.

"Hi [Name], [Your name] with [Company] — saw [specific trigger event, named]. Congrats. That usually means [specific implication], and I had a thought on that I wanted to share — two minutes, I'm at [number]."

Why it earns callbacks: trigger-event voicemails consistently outperform generic ones because they prove the call is about the prospect's world, not your product's.

11. The Break-Up (final attempt)

After 3–4 unanswered attempts — this often gets the callback the others didn't.

"Hi [Name], [Your name] from [Company] — this'll be my last call on this. If the timing's just not right, totally understand, no more voicemails from me. If it is worth two minutes, I'm at [number] — otherwise, take care."

Why it earns callbacks: removing the pressure to respond is itself the trigger — prospects who felt no urgency to call back an earlier pursuit voicemail often call back the one that releases them from the obligation.

12. The Text-Voicemail Pair

Leave this voicemail, then send a text within 5 minutes:

Voicemail: "Hi [Name], [Your name] with [Company], just left — sending a quick text with my number so you don't have to write it down." Text: "Hi [Name] — [Your name] from [Company], following up on my voicemail. [One-sentence reason]. Free for 2 min this week? — [number]"

Why it earns callbacks: removes the friction of replaying a voicemail to catch a phone number — the single biggest reason callbacks never happen. Text response rates typically exceed voicemail-only callback rates.

How to use it

Pick the script that matches the contact's seniority and how many prior touches you've made, fill in the brackets, and track which scripts you used against your actual callback rate over a few weeks to see which perform best for your list.
