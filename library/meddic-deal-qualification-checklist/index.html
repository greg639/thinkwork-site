MEDDIC Deal Qualification Checklist

A one-page pre-call checklist that forces you to answer Metrics, Economic Buyer, Decision Criteria, Decision Process, Identify Pain, and Champion before you log the opportunity.

What's inside

  • Metrics section — 5 diagnostic checks for quantified business impact
  • Economic Buyer section — access and engagement checks
  • Decision Criteria section — technical and business scoring checks
  • Decision Process section — timeline, procurement, and stakeholder checks
  • Identify Pain section — root-cause validation checks
  • Champion section — 5-point strength test
  • Red/Yellow/Green scoring rubric per MEDDIC element
  • Overall deal-health scoring table (Commit / Best Case / Pipeline / Not Qualified)

Run this before you log any opportunity as Commit or Best Case. If you can't check at least 80% of a section, that section is your next call's agenda — not a gap to paper over in the CRM.

M — Metrics

What quantified business impact will this deal deliver, and can the customer say the number back to you?

  • I know the specific metric(s) this solution will move, named, not vague
  • I have a customer-provided number, not just my own estimate
  • The economic buyer — not just the champion — has acknowledged this metric matters
  • I can calculate or estimate ROI/payback period from this metric
  • The customer has stated a "before" baseline I can measure the "after" against

Score: Green = all 5. Yellow = 3-4. Red = below 3 — you're selling on assumption, not evidence.

E — Economic Buyer

The person with final authority to release budget — not the person who will use the product.

  • I have identified the specific person (name + title) who can approve this spend without further sign-off
  • I have spoken to them directly — not just heard about them from my champion
  • I understand what THEY personally care about
  • I know their approval process — alone, or a committee/board step above them
  • I have a scheduled or completed meeting with them in the current sales cycle

Score: Green = all 5. Yellow = 3-4 (EB identified but not engaged — highest-leverage gap). Red = below 3 — you do not have a real deal yet.

D — Decision Criteria

The formal and informal standards they'll judge every vendor against.

  • I know the technical/functional criteria (features, integrations, security, performance)
  • I know the business criteria (price, contract terms, vendor stability, implementation timeline)
  • I know how these criteria are weighted
  • I have had input into the criteria, not just reacted to a pre-written RFP
  • I know how our solution stacks up against each criterion, honestly, including weaknesses

Score: Green = all 5. Yellow = 3-4. Red = below 3 — you're being evaluated against a scorecard you've never seen.

D — Decision Process

The steps, people, and paper that turn a verbal "yes" into a signed contract.

  • I know every step between now and signature (technical review, security review, legal, procurement, board approval)
  • I know who is involved at each step and whether any are new stakeholders
  • I have a mutual close plan with dates the customer has agreed to — not just my own forecast date
  • I know their procurement/legal specifics (redline turnaround, security docs, PO process)
  • I know what could stall this deal internally (budget cycle, competing initiatives, fiscal year-end)

Score: Green = all 5. Yellow = 3-4. Red = below 3 — your close date is a guess.

I — Identify Pain

The real, root-cause business problem — not the surface-level feature request.

  • I can state the pain in the customer's own words, not my product's marketing language
  • I understand why this pain exists NOW
  • I have quantified the cost of doing nothing
  • The pain is acknowledged by the economic buyer, not just the end-user/champion
  • I have surfaced at least one layer beneath the stated pain

Score: Green = all 5. Yellow = 3-4. Red = below 3 — you're solving a symptom, and so is your competitor.

C — Champion

Someone with influence who actively sells on your behalf when you're not in the room.

  • They have power/credibility inside the org
  • They have personally benefited (or will benefit) from this deal closing
  • They have taken a real action for you without being asked twice
  • They have coached you on how to win internally
  • They will be around and invested post-sale

Score: Green = all 5. Yellow = 3-4. Red = below 3 — you have a "friendly," not a champion. See the companion Champion Identification & Development Checklist.

Rule of thumb: Economic Buyer and Champion kill deals silently. A deal Green on Metrics, Decision Criteria, and Pain can still die if EB access and a real Champion aren't there — check those two first on every deal review.

How to use it

Run through all six letters before every discovery call as a pre-call brief, and again before any deal is logged as Commit or Best Case — treat anything below Yellow as your next call's agenda, not a forecasting footnote.
