Difficult Conversation Scripts: Missed Quota & Behavior Issues

Verbatim openers, de-escalation lines, and closing scripts for the three hardest manager conversations — a quota miss, an attitude problem, and no improvement after coaching — so you're not improvising in the moment.

These are the three conversations managers put off longest, and the ones where winging it costs the most — a defensive rep, an HR-risk comment, or a conversation that produces sympathy instead of change. Read the script beforehand, adapt the specifics, and don't skip the documentation line at the end of each.

1. Missed Quota

Verbatim opener: "[Name], I want to talk directly about numbers — you closed at [X]% of target this [period], and that's the second [period] in a row below plan. I want to understand what's driving it before we talk about what happens next. Walk me through your own read on it."

De-escalation lines for common pushback:

  • If rep blames territory/leads: "That's worth digging into properly — let's actually look at your pipeline coverage numbers against the team average, not just the outcome, so we know if that's the real driver."
  • If rep blames the product/market: "Other reps in the same market hit plan this period, so I want to understand what's different in how you're working it, not dismiss the market factor — tell me what you're seeing that they might not be."
  • If rep gets emotional or upset: "I hear that this is hard to hear. I want to be clear this conversation is about getting you back on track, not about writing you off — take a second if you need it."
  • If rep goes silent/shuts down: "I'd rather you push back on me than go quiet — what part of this doesn't match how you see it?"

Close and next-steps script: "Here's what I want us to walk away with today: one specific thing we're changing starting this week, a date we check back in, and what 'back on track' looks like in numbers. [Specific change]. Let's check in on [date]. I'll document what we agreed so we're both working from the same page — does that match what you heard?"

Documentation line: Conversation held [date] re: quota performance [X]% of target for [period]; agreed action: [specific change]; check-in date: [date].

2. Attitude / Behavior Issue

Verbatim opener: "[Name], I want to raise something specific I've noticed, not a general impression. In [specific instance — a meeting, a message, an interaction], [specific observable behavior] happened. I want to understand what was going on for you at that moment, because it's not what I'd expect from you."

De-escalation lines for common pushback:

  • If rep denies it happened: "I want to make sure we're talking about the same moment — here's specifically what I saw/heard [describe]. Does that match your memory of it, or did I misread something?"
  • If rep says others do it too: "I'll look into that separately if there's a broader pattern worth addressing, but right now I'm talking to you about your behavior specifically, because that's the one I can act on today."
  • If rep gets defensive about character ("you're saying I'm a bad person"): "No — I'm not making a judgment about who you are, I'm flagging one specific behavior in one specific moment. That's a much smaller, much more fixable thing."
  • If rep minimizes ("it wasn't a big deal"): "It might not have felt that way from where you were standing — here's the impact it had on [specific person/team/outcome], which is why I'm raising it now rather than letting it go."

Close and next-steps script: "What I need going forward is [specific behavior change], starting now. I'm not going to let this sit unaddressed if it happens again, so I want us both clear on what changes. Is there anything getting in the way of that, that I should know about?"

Documentation line: Conversation held [date] re: [specific behavior] observed [date/context]; expectation reset: [specific behavior change]; rep response: [brief note].

3. Non-Improvement After Coaching (Last-Chance Conversation)

Verbatim opener: "[Name], I want to be very direct, because I owe you that. We've talked about [specific skill/behavior] on [dates/number of occasions], and I haven't seen it change in a way that's showing up in your results. I need this conversation to land differently than the others have."

Close and next-steps script: "Here's where that leaves us: I need to see [specific, measurable change] by [specific date] — not effort, the actual change showing up in [specific metric/behavior]. I'm going to check in on [interim date] so this isn't a surprise either way at the deadline. I want you to succeed here, and I also need to be honest that if this doesn't change, there are real consequences, up to and including [state the actual consequence you're prepared to enforce — e.g., a formal performance plan, role change]. What questions do you have about any of that?"

Documentation line: Conversation held [date] — final coaching conversation re: [specific skill/behavior], following prior coaching on [dates]; specific improvement required by [date]: [detail]; interim check-in: [date]; consequence stated: [detail].

Using These Scripts

  • Fill in every bracket before the conversation — a script with visible placeholders read out loud sounds worse than no script at all
  • Practice the opener once out loud, alone, before the conversation — the goal is to say it naturally, not read it
  • Always leave with a specific next date on the calendar before the conversation ends — an open-ended "let's see how it goes" undoes the entire conversation
  • Complete the documentation line the same day, while the specifics are still accurate

How to use it

Read the relevant script closely beforehand, adapt the specifics (numbers, dates, behaviors) to the real situation, and keep the de-escalation lines within reach during the conversation for the moment the rep pushes back.
