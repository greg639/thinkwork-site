Pipeline Hygiene Scorecard

A weekly scoring rubric that grades every open deal on data completeness, next-step clarity, and close-date realism, so managers can spot a bad pipeline three to five weeks before it shows up in bookings.

What's inside

  • 3-category weighted scoring formula (Completeness 40% / Next-Step Clarity 30% / Close-Date Realism 30%)
  • 8-point data completeness checklist per deal, scored 1/0
  • 0-3 next-step clarity scale with a mutual-commitment test
  • 0-3 close-date realism scale with 'zombie deal' and push-count triggers
  • Automatic-fail red-flag list that overrides the weighted score
  • Weekly 10-minute manager workflow, step by step
  • Per-rep rollup table for week-over-week trend tracking
  • Healthy / At Risk / Critical scoring bands

Grade every open deal in under 2 minutes, roll it up to a per-rep score in under 10 minutes a week. Three categories, weighted, with a hard-fail list that overrides everything else.

How the score is built

CategoryWeightWhat it measures
Data Completeness40%Are the fields a real human filled in, or is this a shell record?
Next-Step Clarity30%Is there a specific, dated, mutually-committed next action?
Close-Date Realism30%Does the close date reflect the buying process, or hope?

Weighted Deal Score = (Completeness % × 0.4) + (Next-Step % × 0.3) + (Close-Date % × 0.3)

Bands: 85–100 Healthy · 65–84 At Risk · Below 65 Critical

Category A — Data Completeness (1 point per field present and real, 0 if blank/placeholder)

For each open deal, check:

  1. Deal amount is entered and matches the actual quoted/proposed number (not a round-number placeholder like $10,000 sitting untouched since creation)
  2. Close date is a specific calendar date (not "end of quarter" typed into a date field, not the untouched auto-default)
  3. Stage matches buying-process reality, not aspiration (a deal sitting in "Proposal Sent" with no proposal on file fails this)
  4. Economic buyer is named — full name and title, not "the decision maker"
  5. Next Step field is populated with something more specific than "follow up"
  6. Competitor field is filled — either a named competitor or an explicit "No competitor identified — validated with buyer," never blank
  7. Source/campaign attribution is filled
  8. An activity (call, email, meeting) is logged within the last 7 business days, unless the deal is in an explicit "buyer is dark, working reconnect" status

Completeness % = (fields present ÷ 8) × 100

Category B — Next-Step Clarity (score the single best-documented next step, 0–3)

  • 0 — No next step, or a vague one ("check in," "follow up," "circle back")
  • 1 — Next step is named but has no date, no owner, or both
  • 2 — Next step has a date and an owner, but it's rep-driven only — nothing the buyer has agreed to do
  • 3 — Next step is a dated, named, mutual commitment: there's a calendar invite or written confirmation, and the buyer owes an action too (send org chart, loop in security, confirm attendees)

Next-Step % = (score ÷ 3) × 100

Category C — Close-Date Realism (score 0–3)

  • 0 — Close date has already passed and hasn't been updated ("zombie deal")
  • 1 — Close date has been pushed 3+ times with no material change in stage or buyer engagement
  • 2 — Close date has been pushed 1–2 times, OR the date doesn't match a realistic cycle length for this deal's stage/size
  • 3 — Close date is stage-appropriate, has moved at most once, and is corroborated by a dated next step landing on or before it

Close-Date % = (score ÷ 3) × 100

Automatic-fail red flags (override the weighted score — deal is Critical regardless of points)

  • Close date is in the past and untouched
  • Deal amount is $0 or blank
  • No logged activity in 21+ calendar days on a deal still marked open
  • Stage says "Verbal Commit" or later with no economic buyer named anywhere on the record

Weekly manager workflow (targets under 10 minutes)

  1. Pull the pipeline report filtered to: open deals, closing this quarter or next, above your minimum deal-size threshold.
  2. Score the 5–8 largest or most at-risk deals using the table above (30–60 seconds per deal once the checklist is memorized).
  3. Log flags in the rollup sheet below.
  4. Pick the single worst-scoring deal that's still forecasted as Commit/Best Case and raise it in your next 1:1 — not as "why is this messy" but as "walk me through what you actually know here."

How to use it

Score your top 5-8 deals against the three categories each week (under 10 minutes total), log the weighted score in the rollup sheet, and take the worst-scoring forecasted deal into your next 1:1.
