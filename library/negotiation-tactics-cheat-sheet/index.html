Negotiation Tactics Cheat Sheet

Names the 12 tactics buyers use to extract concessions in negotiation, with a recognition cue and a word-for-word counter for each.

What's inside

  • 12 named buyer negotiation tactics (Good Cop/Bad Cop, Vanishing Budget, Competitor Bluff, and more)
  • A "what it looks like" recognition cue for each tactic
  • A "why it works" psychology note for each tactic
  • A one-line, word-for-word counter for each tactic
  • Covers both opening tactics and late-stage post-agreement tactics (the Nibble, Higher-Authority Pull)
  • Print-and-reference format for live negotiation calls

12 Tactics Buyers Use Against Reps — and Your One-Line Counter

How to use: Recognizing the tactic in real time is 80% of the battle — most reps lose ground simply because they don't clock what's happening. Memorize the "what it looks like" column so you can name it silently to yourself mid-call, then deploy the counter.

1. Good Cop / Bad Cop What it looks like: A friendly champion suddenly brings in a tougher stakeholder (CFO, procurement lead) late in the deal who pushes hard on price or terms. Why it works: Splits your relationship — you don't want to upset the friendly champion by pushing back on the "bad cop." Counter: "I want to solve this for both of you — what specifically does [bad cop] need to see to say yes?"

2. The Vanishing Budget What it looks like: "We only have $X, take it or leave it" — presented as a hard, non-negotiable ceiling. Why it works: Creates artificial scarcity and time pressure before you've established real value. Counter: "Let's separate the budget number from the outcome you need — what does that number need to deliver?"

3. The Competitor Bluff What it looks like: "Competitor X is offering the same thing for half the price." Why it works: Tests whether you'll cave on price without verification. Counter: "If that's a true apples-to-apples comparison, I'd match it — can you share the quote so I can see exactly what's included?"

4. Reverse Deadline Pressure What it looks like: "We need to decide by Friday" — used to rush you into a worse deal, not to reflect real urgency. Why it works: Manufactured urgency pressures reps into concessions to "save" the timeline. Counter: "Happy to move fast — what happens on your end on Friday if we don't move?"

5. Strategic Silence What it looks like: Buyer goes quiet after you state a price, letting the silence pressure you into self-discounting. Why it works: Most reps can't tolerate silence and fill it with a concession nobody asked for. Counter: Stay silent back. If you must speak: "Take your time — what questions does that number raise?"

6. The Nibble What it looks like: After verbal agreement, "just one more thing" — an extra seat, a faster SLA, a free month — added for free. Why it works: Deal fatigue makes reps want to close rather than reopen negotiation over a "small" ask. Counter: "I can make that work if we adjust [term] to offset it — otherwise let's keep the deal as agreed."

7. Higher-Authority Pull What it looks like: "Let me run this by my boss" late-stage, used to extract one more concession before final signature. Why it works: Creates a second negotiation round you didn't plan for. Counter: "Let's get your boss on a call now so we solve it once, not twice."

8. Free Bundling Squeeze What it looks like: "Since we're already signing, can you throw in [extra module/service] for free?" Why it works: Frames the ask as trivial relative to the deal already "won." Counter: "I can include that — what can move on the commercial side to make that work?"

9. RFP Fishing Expedition What it looks like: Detailed, demanding info requests with no real signal of buying intent — often used to benchmark or pressure an incumbent vendor. Why it works: Costs you real time and pricing transparency for a deal that may never close. Counter: "Happy to go deep — what's the decision timeline and who else is in this process?"

10. The Quarter-End Squeeze What it looks like: Buyer stalls until they know it's your quarter-end, then asks for a steep discount to "help you hit your number." Why it works: Correctly bets that reps under quota pressure will self-discount. Counter: "I can be flexible on timing, not on price — what would make the current terms work today?"

11. Multi-Vendor Triangulation What it looks like: Buyer openly shares (or implies) competing quotes to play vendors off each other on price. Why it works: Creates a race-to-the-bottom dynamic that erodes your margin and your positioning. Counter: "I'd rather compete on outcome than price — what does the winning vendor need to prove?"

12. The Walk-Away Bluff What it looks like: "We're prepared to walk away from this deal" — stated to force last-minute concessions. Why it works: Fear of losing the deal makes reps cave rather than test the bluff. Counter: "I understand — if this isn't the right fit at these terms, I'd rather know now than after we've both invested more time."

How to use it

Print and keep visible on negotiation calls; the moment you recognize a tactic from the 'what it looks like' column, use the matching counter verbatim instead of improvising under pressure.
