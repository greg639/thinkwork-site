Enterprise Discovery Call Playbook

A multi-call, multi-stakeholder discovery playbook built for complex enterprise sales cycles with six or more people in the buying committee — because one great call with one contact doesn't qualify an enterprise deal.

Why Single-Threaded Discovery Fails at Enterprise Scale

In deals with six or more buying-committee members, one great discovery call with one champion produces a false-positive qualified deal. Enterprise discovery isn't one call — it's a deliberate sequence engineered to touch every functional stakeholder before a proposal gets built.

The Buying-Committee Map — 6 Roles to Identify Before Building a Proposal

  1. Economic Buyer — controls/releases budget, final sign-off
  2. Champion — internal advocate who sells on your behalf when you're not in the room
  3. Technical Evaluator — security, IT, integration, data; can veto on technical grounds
  4. End User Representative — the people who'll actually use it daily; adoption risk lives here
  5. Procurement/Legal Gatekeeper — controls paper process, contract terms, vendor risk review
  6. Influencer / Coach or Skeptic — no formal authority but shapes the room's opinion

Pre-Engagement Research Checklist (before Call 1)

  • Org chart pulled for the buying function
  • Recent public signals reviewed: earnings calls, job postings, leadership changes, press releases
  • Existing relationships/warm paths mapped (mutual connections, existing customers, prior touchpoints)
  • Known incumbent vendor(s) identified if this is a replaceable category
  • Hypothesis document drafted: which of the 6 roles you expect to encounter, and who they might be

The 5-Call Enterprise Discovery Sequence

Call 1 — Business Discovery (Champion / initial contact)

Goal: Confirm the business pain, quantify impact, get the champion to help map the room.

  • Context-setting (2 min)
  • Situation & pain questions (15 min)
  • Impact quantification (10 min)
  • "Who else needs to be part of this" mapping (10 min)
  • Next steps: agree who Call 2 is with

Call 2 — Economic Buyer Alignment

Goal: Validate the pain resonates at the budget-holder level; confirm what "success" means in their terms.

  • Business context from their vantage point (10 min)
  • Confirm/challenge the pain and impact numbers from Call 1 (10 min)
  • Decision process and timeline from their seat (10 min)
  • What makes this an easy yes vs. a hard no (5 min)

Call 3 — Technical / Security Discovery

Goal: Surface technical requirements early enough to design around them, not discover them at contract stage.

  • Current stack and integration points (15 min)
  • Security/compliance requirements and review process (10 min)
  • Data migration/implementation constraints (10 min)
  • What's killed similar projects technically before (5 min)

Call 4 — End-User Discovery

Goal: Understand day-to-day workflow reality and adoption risk.

  • Current workflow walkthrough (15 min)
  • Pain points in their own words (10 min)
  • What would make them resist a new tool (10 min)
  • Early reaction to a rough solution outline (10 min)

Call 5 — Mutual Close Plan Session (Champion + Economic Buyer, ideally both present)

Goal: Build a jointly-owned timeline from today to signed, with named owners on both sides.

  • Recap validated pain + quantified impact across stakeholders (10 min)
  • Walk the mutual action plan template line by line (20 min)
  • Confirm procurement/legal process and paper timeline (10 min)
  • Lock the next call date before ending this one (5 min)

Internal Deal-Review Cadence for Leaders

  • Weekly: any enterprise deal without documented touches to at least 3 of the 6 roles gets flagged.
  • Bi-weekly: forecast call requires the Stakeholder Alignment Tracker to be current, not verbal-only updates.
  • Monthly: risk register reviewed for any deal in Proposal stage 45+ days.

How to use it

Use the 5-call sequence as your standard enterprise engagement plan, and keep the Stakeholder Alignment Tracker and Mutual Action Plan updated live during the deal rather than reconstructing them for forecast calls.
