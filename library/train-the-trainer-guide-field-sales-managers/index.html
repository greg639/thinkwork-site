Train-the-Trainer Guide for Field Sales Managers

A practical guide that equips frontline managers to run a 20-minute weekly skill-drill huddle from centrally-built curriculum, instead of letting it die the day it leaves the enablement team.

What's inside

  • Manager self-check before running any huddle (including a certification-level gate)
  • The full 20-minute huddle format: Open, Demo, Drill, Debrief, Close
  • Word-for-word scripted lines for the Open, Debrief and Close
  • Common facilitation mistakes to avoid (deal-review drift, charisma grading, one rep dominating)
  • Feedback loop template for reporting content gaps back to central enablement
  • Certification requirement for trainers themselves (must be Advanced+ on what they coach)

Equips frontline managers to deliver and reinforce centrally-built curriculum inside their own team huddles — instead of centrally-built content dying the day it leaves the enablement team's hands.

Why This Matters

A competency taught once, centrally, decays within weeks without local reinforcement. The manager who sees the rep every day — not the enablement team who sees them once a quarter — is the one who actually determines whether a skill sticks.

Before You Run a Huddle: Manager Self-Check

  • Am I certified at Advanced or above on the competency I'm about to coach? If not, pair-facilitate with someone who is — don't coach past your own certified level.
  • Do I have one specific graded example (recording or roleplay) ready, not just a slide?
  • Have I picked one scenario from the bank, not an open-ended "let's talk about deals"?

The Huddle Drill Format (20 minutes, repeatable weekly)

Open (1 min) — name the skill, not the mood.

Script: "Today we're drilling [named competency] — specifically [the one behavior]. This isn't a general pep talk, it's one skill, one behavior."

Demo (3 min) — show the target level. Play a recording or read a transcript excerpt of the behavior at Proficient/Advanced level. Ask: "What did you notice them do that's different from a Developing-level response?"

Drill (10 min) — pair roleplay. Assign one scenario from the bank matched to the team's current certification stage. Pair reps; each gets 3-4 minutes live, the other observes with the rubric in hand.

Debrief (5 min) — rate against the rubric, out loud.

Script: "On a scale of Novice to Expert, using the actual criteria — not 'good job' — where did that land, and what's the one specific thing that would move it up one level?"

Close (1 min) — assign the micro-practice.

Script: "This week, do this once, on a real call, and bring back what happened next huddle."

Common Facilitation Mistakes to Avoid

  • Letting it drift into open deal review. Deal review has its own forum (pipeline meeting); the huddle exists to move one named competency, not to troubleshoot every open opportunity.
  • Grading on charisma or confidence, not the named behavior. The most confident-sounding rep in the roleplay is not automatically the highest-scoring one — check against the actual rubric criteria.
  • Letting the strongest rep dominate the drill. Rotate who goes first; give quieter/newer reps the first live rep slot in the debrief so they're not just watching the same person every week.
  • Treating the huddle as optional when the pipeline is busy. The weeks reps are busiest are exactly the weeks skills decay fastest without reinforcement — this is the first thing that should NOT get cancelled.

Feedback Loop Back to Central Enablement

After each huddle cycle (monthly), report back:

  1. Which scenario(s) from the bank didn't land — reps found them unrealistic or too easy/hard
  2. Any competency where your team's ratings seem to be drifting from the central rubric's calibration (over- or under-grading)
  3. Any content gap you had to improvise around because nothing in the central library covered it

This feedback is the direct input to the next Content Audit cycle — the field is where curriculum gaps actually surface first.

Certification for Trainers

A manager should not run huddles on a competency they are not certified Advanced or above on themselves. If every manager on a team is below Advanced on a given competency, that is a signal to bring in a central enablement facilitator or an Expert-level peer from another team, not to run the huddle anyway.

How to use it

Managers run the 20-minute format every week using scenarios from the Role-Play Scenario Bank, and file the monthly feedback loop back to enablement to feed the next Content Audit cycle.
