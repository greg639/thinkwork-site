Certification Exam Question Bank

Sixteen scenario-based questions across four core skill categories and four competency levels: with answer keys, rationale, and a scoring rubric, ready to assemble into a real onboarding certification exam rather than a knowledge quiz that tests recall instead of skill.

How to use it

Load the 16 questions into your exam platform (or print as a paper test), apply the tiered scoring rule exactly as written, and require a live roleplay demonstration alongside the written exam for anyone certifying above Developing level.

What's inside

16 scenario-based questions across 4 skill categories × 4 levels (Foundational, Developing, Proficient, Advanced). Each is multiple choice with a correct answer and rationale, assemble into your own exam platform or print as a paper test.

Discovery

Q1 (Foundational): A prospect says "we're just looking into options right now, nothing urgent." Best next move? A) Thank them and follow up in 3 months B) Ask what triggered them to start looking now, even without urgency C) Immediately pitch differentiators to create urgency D) Ask for a demo slot next week Correct: B, uncovering the trigger event reveals whether latent urgency exists before you disqualify or push too hard.

Q2 (Developing): A buyer describes a workaround they built, in painstaking detail. What should you do? A) Move on quickly, it's not your product's issue B) Ask what the workaround costs them, in time or money, to keep running C) Explain how your product replaces it D) Note it and return to your question list Correct: B, quantifying the workaround's cost is what turns a discovery detail into a business case.

Q3 (Proficient), You ask "what's the impact if this isn't solved this year?" and get a vague answer ("it'd just be annoying"). Strongest follow-up? A) Accept it and move to qualification B) Ask who else in the business feels that "annoyance" and how it shows up for them C) Suggest a specific consequence to see if they agree D) Reframe and ask the same question again Correct: B, routing the impact question to a different stakeholder's experience often surfaces the real cost the speaker was underselling.

Q4 (Advanced), The buyer's stated problem doesn't match what you're observing in their own data/systems. Correct move? A) Point out the discrepancy directly B) Ask a question that lets them discover the gap themselves C) Ignore it and proceed with their framing D) Escalate to your manager before continuing Correct: B, buyers accept conclusions they reach themselves far more readily than ones delivered to them.

Qualification

Q5 (Foundational), Which is NOT a core element to confirm before calling a deal "qualified"? A) Is there a named economic buyer? B) Does the prospect have a LinkedIn profile? C) Is there an active budget or path to one? D) Is there a decision timeline? Correct: B

Q6 (Developing): Your champion says "I can get this approved, don't worry about the CFO." What should you do? A) Trust the champion and skip economic-buyer confirmation B) Politely ask what the actual sign-off process looks like, even if the champion is confident C) Ask your manager to call the CFO directly D) Add a discount to speed the champion's internal case Correct: B, champion confidence isn't the same as a confirmed process.

Q7 (Proficient): Two weeks before a forecasted close date, you still can't name who signs the contract. Right move? A) Keep the same forecast category and hope it resolves B) Downgrade the forecast category and re-run economic-buyer identification with your champion C) Ask the champion to sign on the company's behalf D) Escalate only if the deal slips Correct: B

Q8 (Advanced), A large deal has every MEDDIC box checked except paper process (procurement/legal). Champion insists it'll be fast. Advanced move? A) Trust the champion's read and forecast commit B) Independently map the procurement/legal steps and timeline with someone in that function C) Add buffer time without further investigation D) Ask finance to expedite regardless Correct: B, paper process is disproportionately where late-stage slips come from.

Value Articulation

Q9 (Foundational), Which best demonstrates value tied to a business outcome vs. a feature? A) "Our platform has real-time dashboards and 40+ integrations" B) "Teams like yours typically cut reporting time by 6 hours a week: about $40K/year in reclaimed manager time" C) "We were named a leader in the latest analyst report" D) "Our UI is really easy to use" Correct: B

Q10 (Developing): Buyer says "this all sounds nice, but what's it actually worth to us?" Best response? A) Repeat the feature list differently B) Ask what metric they'd use internally to judge success, then build the case around it C) Quote your average customer ROI untailored D) Offer a discount Correct: B

Q11 (Proficient), You're presenting to both an end-user champion and a CFO in the same room. Proficient value-framing move? A) One unified pitch for consistency B) Frame the same underlying value in operational terms for the champion and financial/risk terms for the CFO, in the same conversation C) Address only the CFO D) Ask the champion to translate afterward Correct: B

Q12 (Advanced): Your ROI case is strong, but the buyer's real hesitation is unstated risk-aversion about switching systems. Advanced move? A) Reinforce the ROI numbers further B) Explicitly name the switching-risk concern, then address it directly (references, phased rollout, guarantees) alongside the ROI case C) Offer a bigger discount to offset risk D) Wait for them to raise it Correct: B

Objection Handling

Q13 (Foundational), Buyer: "Your price is higher than [competitor]." Best first response? A) Immediately match the competitor's price B) Ask what they're comparing: same scope, same outcomes, before responding to price C) Explain all the ways you're better D) Say price isn't up for discussion Correct: B

Q14 (Developing), Buyer: "We tried something like this before and it didn't work." Best move? A) Explain why your product is different B) Ask what specifically didn't work last time before explaining anything C) Reassure them it'll be different D) Move to a different topic Correct: B

Q15 (Proficient): The same objection resurfaces three times in different forms across a deal ("too expensive," "need to check budget," "seeing what else is out there"). Proficient read? A) Three separate objections needing three separate rebuttals B) Likely one unresolved underlying belief resurfacing: address the root belief C) The deal is dead D) Offer a discount to end the pattern Correct: B

Q16 (Advanced): Late in a deal, a new stakeholder raises an objection the champion already agreed was resolved. Advanced response? A) Redirect the new stakeholder to the champion B) Re-engage directly with the new stakeholder's version of the objection as if hearing it fresh C) Point out it was already addressed D) Escalate to your manager Correct: B

Scoring & Certification Rules

  • 16 questions total, 4 per skill category, 4 levels each
  • Foundational-tier questions (Q1, Q5, Q9, Q13): must score 100% to pass at all: these are floor competencies
  • Developing + Proficient tiers combined: need 75% correct (6 of 8)
  • Advanced tier: not required for base certification, tracked separately: 2+ correct = eligible for "Advanced Practitioner" badge
  • Overall Foundational Certification pass threshold: 13/16 correct (81%) AND 100% on the 4 Foundational questions specifically
  • Re-certification required every 12 months, or immediately after any framework/messaging change
  • Format note: pair this written bank with a live scenario roleplay for anyone certifying at Proficient or Advanced: a certification that only tests recall, never demonstration, doesn't verify a real skill
