Skill Mastery Self-Check Quiz

A monthly, three-skill diagnostic across Discovery, Negotiation, and Objection Handling, scored against specific behaviors you either did or didn't do on real calls, not vague self-perception.

What's inside

  • 8 behavior-specific statements for Discovery, scored 0-4
  • 8 behavior-specific statements for Negotiation, scored 0-4
  • 8 behavior-specific statements for Objection Handling, scored 0-4
  • Scoring bands: Developing / Practicing / Proficient / Advanced per skill
  • A rule requiring evidence from your last 10 real calls, not intention
  • A peer/manager co-rating check to catch blind spots
  • A why-it-matters line per skill (career, role, results)
  • A monthly tracking log to plot the same 3 scores over time
  • Guidance on picking one drill-behavior per month from your lowest-scoring item

A short, repeatable diagnostic across three core selling competencies: Discovery, Negotiation, and Objection Handling. Retake it monthly. The point isn't the score — it's watching the same score move because you deliberately drilled one behavior.

How to score yourself honestly

  • Base every rating on your last 10 real calls or deals — not on how you'd like to behave.
  • Scale for every statement: 0 = Never · 1 = Rarely · 2 = Sometimes · 3 = Often · 4 = Consistently
  • If you're unsure, score lower. Optimistic self-scoring defeats the purpose.
  • Once a quarter, have a manager or peer who has actually heard you on a call score you on the same statements using a recording. A gap of 2+ points on any single item is a blind spot — trust their score over yours.

Module 1 — Discovery

Why master this: Discovery is the skill that determines whether every downstream stage — proposal, negotiation, close — is built on real information or on guesswork. Career: discovery mastery is what moves you from single-threaded transactional deals into complex, multi-stakeholder sales — the deals that get you promoted. Role: it's what lets you run a call without a script, because you're actually curious and know what to listen for. Results: weak discovery is the single most common root cause of stalled deals and late-stage "surprise" objections.

#StatementScore (0-4)
1I identify the prospect's current state — metrics, process, tools in use — before proposing anything, on every call.
2I ask at least one question that surfaces the cost of inaction (what happens if nothing changes).
3I uncover the decision process and other stakeholders before I'm asked "who else is involved?"
4I ask about a previously attempted or failed solution and why it didn't stick.
5I connect at least one discovered pain to a business metric the prospect already tracks.
6I let silence sit after an open question instead of filling it with my own commentary.
7I summarize what I heard back in the prospect's own language before moving to my pitch.
8I leave every discovery call with a mutually agreed next step tied to a business reason, not just a calendar slot.

Module 2 — Negotiation

Why master this: Negotiation is the skill that protects everything discovery and the pitch built. Career: reps who negotiate well are trusted with the deals that carry margin risk and board visibility. Role: it's the difference between a deal that closes on your terms and one that closes on theirs. Results: negotiation skill shows up directly in discount leakage, average deal value, and how often "one more approval" quietly kills a quarter.

#StatementScore (0-4)
1I know my walk-away point and my ideal outcome before the conversation starts.
2When asked for a discount, I trade it for something of value (scope, timeline, term, referral) rather than giving it away.
3I separate the person from the position — I stay warm while holding firm on terms.
4I let the other side name the first number on price wherever possible.
5I identify the economic buyer's real constraint (budget cycle, board approval, competing priority) before negotiating around it.
6I use silence after stating my position instead of negotiating against myself.
7I confirm agreed terms in writing within 24 hours to prevent renegotiation drift.
8I know at least two real alternatives (my BATNA) if this deal doesn't close, and it shows in my composure.

Module 3 — Objection Handling

Why master this: Objection handling is a listening skill wearing a rebuttal costume. Career: it's what separates reps who get rattled from reps who get calm — the latter get the harder accounts. Role: it stops you from arguing with a prospect who was never objecting to what you think they were objecting to. Results: unresolved objections don't disappear — they resurface as silence, ghosting, and "going with someone else."

#StatementScore (0-4)
1I let the prospect finish the objection completely before I respond.
2I distinguish a real objection from a stall or a smokescreen before I answer it.
3I acknowledge the objection's validity before I pivot to addressing it.
4I ask a clarifying question to find the objection behind the objection at least once per call.
5I have a specific proof point (case study, data point, reference) ready for the 3 objections I hear most.
6I check that my response actually landed ("does that address the concern?") instead of assuming it did.
7I don't take objections personally, and it doesn't leak into my tone or pace.
8I track which objections recur across my pipeline so I can prep for them proactively.

Rule: pick exactly one behavior to drill each month — the single lowest-scoring statement, not the lowest module. Mastery is built one behavior at a time, not three skills at once.

How to use it

Score yourself honestly against your last 10 real calls, retake on the same date each month, and let the single lowest-scoring statement (not the overall number) tell you what to drill next.
