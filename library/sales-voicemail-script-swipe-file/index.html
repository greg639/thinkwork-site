Sales Voicemail Script Swipe File

Short, curiosity-driven voicemail scripts for six common scenarios, built around a repeatable formula that survives past second 3 instead of getting deleted.

  • After-email voicemail script (references the email sent)
  • Referral voicemail script

Short, curiosity-driven voicemail scripts built to survive past second 3 — the point where most cold voicemails get deleted. Every script here is under 20 seconds read aloud and ends on a reason to call back, not a recap of your entire pitch.

How to use this: Match the scenario, fill in the brackets, and practice saying it out loud until it takes 15-20 seconds — not 45. Record yourself once and listen back; if it sounds like you're reading, re-record until it doesn't.

The Voicemail Formula (Every Script Below Follows This)

  1. Name + company (2 seconds — say it fast, don't over-enunciate like a robot)
  2. One specific reason for calling (the pain, the trigger event, or the reference point — never generic)
  3. One reason to call back that isn't "learn more about our product"
  4. Your number, said twice, slowly (the #1 reason callbacks don't happen is a number said once, fast)

Scenario 1: First-Touch Voicemail (No Prior Contact)

"Hi [Name], it's [Your Name] from [Company]. I'll keep this quick — I work with [role/title] dealing with [specific pain point], and wanted to see if that's something on your plate right now. My number's [number] — that's [number] again. Thanks, [Name]."

Scenario 2: After-Email Voicemail (Reference the Email)

"Hi [Name], [Your Name] again from [Company] — I sent an email a couple days ago about [specific topic], figured I'd follow up by phone in case it's easier to just talk it through. Give me a call at [number] — [number] again — or feel free to just reply to the email, whichever's easier."

Scenario 3: Referral Voicemail

"Hi [Name], this is [Your Name] from [Company] — [Referrer Name] suggested I reach out given what you're working on with [specific context]. Would love five minutes whenever works. My number's [number], one more time that's [number]. Thanks."

Scenario 4: Trigger-Event Voicemail

"Hi [Name], [Your Name] from [Company]. Saw [trigger event — the raise, the new role, the expansion] — congrats. That's usually the point where [specific pain] either gets ahead of the business or falls behind it, so wanted to check which one it's looking like for you. Number's [number], again [number]."

Scenario 5: Break-Up Voicemail (Final Touch)

"Hi [Name], [Your Name] from [Company] one last time. I've reached out a few times about [specific topic] and don't want to keep cluttering your voicemail, so this'll be my last call. If it's ever relevant, my number's [number] — [number] again. Either way, all the best."

Scenario 6: Post-No-Show Voicemail (They Missed a Booked Call)

"Hi [Name], [Your Name] from [Company] — looks like we missed each other for our [time] call, no worries at all, these things happen. Wanted to see if it's worth grabbing a new time, or if something's come up that's changed priorities. Number's [number], again [number]."

Rules for Voicemails That Get Callbacks

  • Timing: 15–20 seconds, never over 30. Anything longer gets skipped or deleted before the number is even heard.
  • Say the phone number twice, slowly, at normal volume — not faster than the rest of the message. This is the single most common reason a genuinely interested prospect never calls back.
  • Name a specific pain or trigger, never "touching base" or "following up." Both phrases are auto-delete triggers for most professionals.
  • Don't recap the whole pitch. The voicemail's only job is to earn a callback or make the next email/call feel expected, not to close anything.
  • Match voicemail to the cadence touch it supports — a first-touch voicemail should feel different from a break-up voicemail; if every voicemail sounds the same, prospects tune out by the second one.
  • Leave voicemails inconsistently, not on every single call attempt — if a prospect gets 6 identical voicemails across 6 calls, it reads as automation. Skip a voicemail on 1 in 3 attempts.

How to use it

Match the scenario, fill in the brackets, practice out loud until it runs 15-20 seconds, and always say your phone number twice, slowly, at the end.
