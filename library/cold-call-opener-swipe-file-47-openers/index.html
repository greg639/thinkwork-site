Cold Call Opener Swipe File (47 Openers)

47 tested cold-call opening lines, organized into 10 categories, that a new rep can use word-for-word before they've built their own voice on the phone.

What's inside

  • 5 Permission-Based openers
  • 5 Pattern-Interrupt openers
  • 5 Referral / Name-Drop openers
  • 5 Trigger-Event openers
  • 5 Problem-First openers
  • 5 Curiosity-Gap openers
  • 5 Direct / No-Fluff openers
  • 4 Humor / Disarm openers
  • 4 Research-Based / Personalized openers
  • 4 Executive / Senior-Level openers

Replace every [Name], [Your Name], [Company], and bracketed detail with your own information. Say these out loud a few times before your first live call — read verbatim off a screen and it sounds exactly like what it is.

A. Permission-Based

Works because it de-risks the interaction for the prospect and signals respect for their time.

  1. "Hi [Name], this is [Your Name] calling from [Company] — I know I'm calling out of the blue. Do you have 27 seconds for me to tell you why, and you can tell me to get lost after?"
  2. "[Name], it's [Your Name] with [Company]. You don't know me, this is a cold call — can I have 30 seconds, and if it's not relevant you can hang up guilt-free?"
  3. "Hi [Name], [Your Name] here from [Company]. Bad time, or do you have a minute?"
  4. "[Name], this is [Your Name]. I'll be upfront — this is a sales call. Worth 20 seconds to see if it's relevant, or should I let you go?"
  5. "Hi [Name], quick one — did I catch you between things, or is now actually okay?"

B. Pattern Interrupt

Works because it names the awkwardness the prospect is already feeling instead of pretending it isn't a cold call.

  1. "[Name], I have to be honest — I have no idea if this is relevant to you. Got 30 seconds to help me find out?"
  2. "Hi [Name], this is a cold call, so feel free to hang up — but if you give me 20 seconds I'll tell you why I called specifically you."
  3. "[Name], I'm going to guess you get 10 of these calls a week. Want to know the one thing that makes this one different?"
  4. "Hi [Name], can I ask you a slightly unusual question before I pitch you anything?"
  5. "[Name], I promise this isn't the call you think it is. Give me 15 seconds?"

C. Referral / Name-Drop

Works because a shared connection borrows credibility instantly — use only when the referral is real.

  1. "Hi [Name], [Referrer Name] suggested I give you a call — said you'd been dealing with [specific problem]. Is that still on your plate?"
  2. "[Name], I just got off the phone with [Referrer / peer at similar company] — they mentioned you're the person who owns [area]. Fair to say?"
  3. "Hi [Name], I work with a few other [role/title]s at companies like [Similar Company] — your name came up as someone worth talking to about [topic]."
  4. "[Name], [Mutual Connection] pointed me your way. Said you'd tell me straight if this wasn't useful — fair trade?"
  5. "Hi [Name], I saw you and [Person] are connected — they mentioned [specific context]. Got a minute?"

D. Trigger-Event

Works because it's timely — tie the call to something that actually just happened at their company.

  1. "Hi [Name], congrats on the [funding round / new role / expansion] — that usually means [specific operational challenge]. Is that on your radar right now?"
  2. "[Name], I noticed [Company] just posted openings for [X roles] — normally that means [the team is scaling into a specific problem]. Accurate?"
  3. "Hi [Name], saw the news about [acquisition / product launch / leadership change] — companies going through that usually hit [specific friction point] around month 3. Ringing any bells?"
  4. "[Name], I noticed you moved into the [new title] role a few months back — usually the first thing that lands on a new [title]'s desk is [specific problem]. Is that you right now?"
  5. "Hi [Name], saw [Company] just expanded into [new market/region] — that transition usually breaks [specific process]. Has it for you?"

E. Problem-First

Works because it skips the pleasantries and gets straight to whether there's a reason to keep talking.

  1. "Hi [Name], I'll cut to it — most [role]s I talk to are dealing with [specific, named problem]. Is that showing up for you, or are you in good shape there?"
  2. "[Name], quick question: is [specific symptom, e.g. 'new reps taking 6+ months to hit quota'] still a problem for your team, or have you already solved it?"
  3. "Hi [Name], I work with [role]s who are frustrated by [specific pain]. Does that land, or am I off base for your team?"
  4. "[Name], most people in your seat tell me [specific metric] is harder to move than it should be. True for you?"
  5. "Hi [Name], I'll ask directly — if [specific problem] went away tomorrow, would that actually matter, or is it not really a priority?"

F. Curiosity Gap

Works because it earns 30 more seconds without giving away the whole pitch.

  1. "Hi [Name], I'm calling because we just helped a company almost exactly like yours fix something I think you're dealing with too — got 30 seconds for me to explain?"
  2. "[Name], I have a slightly odd reason for calling you specifically today — mind if I share it?"
  3. "Hi [Name], I'll tell you exactly why I called and you can decide in 10 seconds if it's worth 2 more minutes."
  4. "[Name], there's a number I want to run by you that I think might surprise you about [specific metric]. Interested?"
  5. "Hi [Name], I promise not to waste your time — can I ask one question that'll tell us both if this call should continue?"

G. Direct / No-Fluff

Works with senior or time-poor buyers who reward efficiency over rapport-building.

  1. "[Name], [Your Name], [Company]. I help [role]s at [industry] companies fix [problem]. Is that a fit conversation or not?"
  2. "Hi [Name], I'll be brief — we do [X], for companies like [Y]. Worth 2 minutes, or no?"
  3. "[Name], I called to ask one thing: who owns [specific initiative] on your team — is that you?"
  4. "Hi [Name], I'm not going to dance around it — I'm calling to see if [specific outcome] is something you're actively trying to solve this quarter."
  5. "[Name], straight question: are you the right person to talk to about [specific area], or should I be talking to someone else?"

H. Humor / Disarm

Use sparingly and only if it matches your natural tone — forced humor reads worse than no humor.

  1. "Hi [Name], I know cold calls rank somewhere between dentist appointments and jury duty — I'll try to make this the better of the two. Got a minute?"
  2. "[Name], full disclosure, I'm probably the 4th person to call you today. Want to guess what I'm selling, or should I just tell you?"
  3. "Hi [Name], I'll trade you: 20 seconds of your time for one useful thing you didn't know 2 minutes ago. Deal?"
  4. "[Name], I promise I'm not calling about your car's extended warranty. Got 30 seconds for something actually relevant?"

I. Research-Based / Personalized

Requires 2 minutes of prep before dialing — highest hit rate when you have a real detail to reference.

  1. "Hi [Name], I read your [post/interview/podcast appearance] about [specific topic] — the point about [specific detail] is exactly why I called."
  2. "[Name], I noticed on your [LinkedIn / company blog] that you're focused on [specific initiative] this year — that's directly what we help with."
  3. "Hi [Name], I pulled up [Company]'s [job posting / press release / earnings call] and noticed [specific detail] — that usually means [inference]. Am I reading that right?"
  4. "[Name], I saw you posted about [specific pain point] recently — figured it was worth a real conversation instead of a LinkedIn comment."

J. Executive / Senior-Level

Assumes the buyer's time is the scarcest resource in the building — lead with brevity, not warmth.

  1. "[Name], I know your calendar is brutal, so I'll be efficient: one sentence on why I called, then you tell me if it's worth continuing."
  2. "Hi [Name], I don't usually get time with someone at your level without an introduction, so I'll use it well — 30 seconds?"
  3. "[Name], I'm calling you directly instead of your team because this is a [specific level of] decision — is that a fair read, or does someone else own it?"
  4. "Hi [Name], at your level I assume time is the scarcest resource in the building, so here's the 10-second version of why I called."

How to use it

Pick 3-5 openers that match your product and buyer, practice them out loud until they sound natural rather than read, and rotate categories based on how much personalization data you have on the prospect before you dial.
