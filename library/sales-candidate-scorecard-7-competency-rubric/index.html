Sales Candidate Scorecard: The 7-Competency Hiring Rubric

Score candidates against the seven competencies your top performers already have, with behavioral anchors at each level and clear hire/no-hire thresholds — not gut feel.

What's inside

  • The 7 core competencies with definitions
  • Behavioral anchors at Below Bar / Meets Bar / Exceeds Bar for each competency
  • 1-5 scoring scale with weighting by competency
  • Weighted scoring worksheet
  • Hire / hire-with-development / no-hire decision thresholds
  • The single-competency-1 guardrail rule
  • Interviewer instructions and evidence-gathering prompts
  • Calibration notes for panel interviews

Score the competency, not the charisma

Use this for every sales candidate, every round, same rubric. Each interviewer scores independently before comparing notes — comparing before scoring produces anchoring bias, not calibration.

The 7 Competencies

1. Discovery & Diagnostic Questioning

Can they uncover the real business problem, not just surface pain?

  • 1 (Below Bar): Asks generic, scripted questions; accepts the first answer at face value; can't distinguish symptom from root cause.
  • 3 (Meets Bar): Asks layered follow-up questions; gets to a specific business impact (cost, time, risk) behind the stated pain.
  • 5 (Exceeds Bar): Uncovers problems the prospect hadn't fully articulated themselves; connects the problem to quantifiable business impact unprompted.

2. Active Listening & Objection Navigation

Do they actually hear what's said, and respond to the real objection instead of a rehearsed rebuttal?

  • 1: Interrupts, talks past objections, or responds with a canned rebuttal that doesn't match what was actually said.
  • 3: Acknowledges the objection accurately, asks a clarifying question before responding, addresses the real concern.
  • 5: Reframes the objection as a signal, uncovers the concern beneath the stated objection, and moves the conversation forward without being defensive.

3. Value Articulation & Business Case Building

Can they connect their solution to a quantifiable business outcome, not just features?

  • 1: Leads with features/product specs; can't translate to business impact when asked "so what."
  • 3: Connects capability to a specific outcome relevant to the stated pain.
  • 5: Builds a mini business case unprompted — quantifies the cost of the status quo and the value of change in the prospect's own terms.

4. Process Discipline & Pipeline Management

Do they run a repeatable process, or wing it deal by deal?

  • 1: Can't describe their own sales process; pipeline stories are vague on next steps, timelines, or stakeholders.
  • 3: Describes a clear process with defined stages and next-step discipline; can walk through a real deal with specifics.
  • 5: Process is rigorous and self-correcting — describes how they catch and fix a stalling deal before it goes cold.

5. Coachability & Resilience

How do they respond to real-time feedback, and how do they talk about past setbacks?

  • 1: Defensive or dismissive when challenged; setback stories blame external factors exclusively.
  • 3: Accepts feedback, adjusts in the moment, takes visible ownership in setback stories.
  • 5: Actively seeks the critique, applies it immediately and visibly in the same conversation, and treats past failures as specific, named lessons.

6. Communication & Executive Presence

Can they hold a room, structure a message, and adjust register for seniority?

  • 1: Rambling, unstructured, or noticeably different (weaker) energy/clarity when the persona is senior.
  • 3: Clear, structured, confident; adjusts appropriately for the audience.
  • 5: Commands the room without dominating it; simplifies complex ideas instantly; peer-level presence with a senior persona.

7. Drive & Self-Generation

Do they generate their own opportunity, or wait for pipeline to be handed to them?

  • 1: All examples of past success involve inbound or handed-to-them opportunity; no evidence of self-sourced pipeline.
  • 3: Clear examples of self-generated pipeline (prospecting, referral generation, expansion identification) with specific numbers.
  • 5: Track record of consistently exceeding self-generation targets, with a repeatable method they can articulate.

Decision Thresholds

  • ≥ 4.0 weighted score: Strong hire.
  • 3.3–3.9: Hire with a named 90-day development plan targeting the weakest-scored competency.
  • 2.5–3.2: No-hire unless the candidate scored 5 on two or more competencies AND the role has structural support for the gap (e.g., a strong deal desk offsetting weak process discipline).
  • < 2.5: No-hire regardless of any single standout score.

Guardrail Rule

Any single competency scored a 1 is an automatic no-hire, regardless of total weighted score. A high average built on a critical gap (e.g., strong communicator, zero coachability) is a bigger long-term risk than a lower but even profile.

Interviewer Instructions

  • Score immediately after the interview, before discussing with other panel members.
  • For every score of 4 or 5, write the specific evidence (a quote or example) that justified it — "good vibe" is not evidence.
  • For every score of 1 or 2, write the specific moment that revealed the gap.
  • In panel debrief, compare evidence before comparing scores — this is what prevents the loudest interviewer's opinion from setting the outcome.

How to use it

Print one worksheet per interviewer per candidate, score independently right after each interview round, then reconcile as a panel using the evidence notes — not first impressions — before applying the decision thresholds.
