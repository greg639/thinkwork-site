Call Coaching Observation Scorecard

A structured rubric for scoring recorded or live calls against six named skills with specific behavioral anchors: so feedback is about competency, not vibes.

How to use it

Score a recorded or live call in real time against the six skills, using the evidence field to force yourself to cite a specific moment, then use the roll-up to drive the next 1:1 (pair with the Skill-Based 1:1 Template).

What's inside

  • Six named, scoreable skills (Discovery, Listening, Value Articulation, Objection Navigation, Stakeholder Mapping, Deal Control)
  • A 1-4 behavioral-anchor scale for every skill
  • An evidence field forcing a specific quote or moment behind every score
  • A composite roll-up table
  • The 'one strength, one growth edge, one commitment' close-out format
  • Inter-rater calibration guidance

Purpose

Score calls against named skills with specific behavioral anchors, so feedback sounds like "your discovery depth was a 2 because you accepted the first stated pain without probing for business impact" instead of "that call felt a bit thin."

Rule Zero

If you can't point to a specific sentence, question, or moment in the call as evidence, you cannot assign a score. Vibes are not evidence.

Scoring Scale

ScoreLabel
1Not Yet: skill largely absent or worked against the rep
2Developing: attempted, inconsistent, or shallow
3Proficient: solid, reliable execution
4Advanced: sharp, adaptive, would be a good peer example

Skill 1: Discovery Depth & Quality of Questions

ScoreBehavioral Anchor
1Accepts the first stated pain at face value; no follow-up probing; moves to pitch within 1-2 questions
2Asks 2-3 follow-up questions but stays surface-level (what, not why/impact)
3Consistently follows stated pain with impact/cost questions ("what does that cost you when it happens?")
4Uncovers pain the prospect hadn't already named; connects multiple threads into a root cause

ScoreBehavioral Anchor
1Talks over or past prospect answers; visibly moves to next question on script regardless of answer
2Listens but rarely reflects back what was heard; occasional talk-over
3Reflects/paraphrases prospect statements before responding; talk-to-listen ratio reasonable
4Reflects AND builds on it: uses the prospect's own language later in the call to reinforce value

Skill 3: Value Articulation

ScoreBehavioral Anchor
1Generic feature pitch, disconnected from anything the prospect said
2References prospect's stated pain but explanation is generic/templated
3Explicitly connects a capability to the specific pain and impact the prospect named
4Quantifies the value in the prospect's own terms (their metrics, their language)

Skill 4: Objection Navigation

ScoreBehavioral Anchor
1Gets defensive, argues, or capitulates immediately without exploring the objection
2Responds to the surface objection but doesn't check if it's the real concern
3Acknowledges, asks a clarifying question to find the real concern, then responds to that
4Surfaces the objection before the prospect has to raise it, or reframes it as a shared problem to solve

Skill 5: Stakeholder Mapping

ScoreBehavioral Anchor
1No mention of, or curiosity about, anyone else involved in the decision
2Asks "who else is involved" once, accepts a vague answer, doesn't follow up
3Identifies specific other stakeholders, their role, and their likely concern
4Has a plan, stated in-call, for how to reach/involve each stakeholder

Evidence: _______________________________________________ Score: ___ /4

Skill 6: Deal Control & Next-Step Clarity

ScoreBehavioral Anchor
1Call ends with no agreed next step, or "I'll follow up" with no date/owner
2Next step stated but vague ("let's touch base soon") or owned only by the rep
3Specific next step, date, and mutual owner agreed before the call ends
4Next step ties to a mutually-agreed success criterion / decision-process milestone

Calibration Notes for Reviewers

  • Score the skill as demonstrated in this call, not your overall opinion of the rep.
  • A rep can be a 4 on Value Articulation and a 1 on Stakeholder Mapping in the same call: that's useful information, don't average it away in your head.
  • If two reviewers score the same call more than 1 point apart on any skill, talk it through, that's a sign the anchor language needs to get more specific for your team's context.
