Objection-Handling Skill Practice Swipe File

40 real objection patterns grouped by the underlying skill they train — Confirming, Isolating, Reframing, Contrasting, and Evidencing — so you drill the muscle behind the words instead of memorizing a script.

What's inside

  • The 5-skill framework behind every objection response: Confirming, Isolating, Reframing, Contrasting, Evidencing
  • 40 real objection patterns across 8 categories: Price & Budget, Timing, Competitor/Status Quo, Authority/Stakeholder, Trust/Risk, Fit/Need, Negotiation/Terms, Stall/Disengagement
  • A word-for-word response pattern with brackets to customize, for every single objection
  • A named "skill in play" tag on every line — never a generic tactic
  • A practice cue on every entry telling you exactly what to drill, not what to say
  • A weekly drill routine: skill-of-the-day, cold peer roleplay, recorded self-score

The 5 skills underneath every objection response

  1. Confirming — reflecting the objection back accurately before you respond, so the buyer feels heard and you're not fixing the wrong problem.
  2. Isolating — separating the stated objection from the real, underlying one ("is that the only thing holding you back, or is there something else?").
  3. Reframing — shifting the frame the buyer is using (cost → investment, feature → outcome, risk → reversibility).
  4. Contrasting — putting two paths side by side so the cost of inaction becomes visible.
  5. Evidencing — replacing your claim with someone else's proof (peer example, data point, named outcome).

How to practice this file (read before you drill)

  • Don't memorize the italicized lines. They're a pattern, not a script — say it once, then redo the same objection in your own words two more times.
  • Practice one skill at a time. Pick a skill for the day and drill only the objections tagged with it.
  • Record yourself on 5 objections per session. Play it back and score: did you actually use the named skill, or did you default to persuading?
  • Goal state: you can hear any of these 40 objections and correctly name which skill it's calling for before you open your mouth.

Category 1 — Price & Budget

  1. "It's too expensive." — Skill: Reframing (cost → value ratio)

Pattern: "Too expensive relative to what you're comparing it to, or relative to the problem it solves? Walk me through the comparison you're making." Practice cue: drill naming what the buyer is anchoring price against before you defend the number.

  1. "We don't have budget for this right now." — Skill: Isolating (budget vs. priority)

Pattern: "Is this a real ceiling on the budget, or is it that this hasn't been prioritized against what else is competing for it?" Practice cue: ask this diagnostically, not like you're calling their bluff.

  1. "Your competitor is cheaper." — Skill: Contrasting (price vs. total cost)

Pattern: "They might be cheaper on the invoice — what have you found the total cost looks like once you include implementation and support?" Practice cue: drill contrasting sticker price vs. total cost without bad-mouthing the competitor.

  1. "We need to cut costs across the board this quarter." — Skill: Reframing (cost → cost-avoidance)

Pattern: "Makes sense. Where does this sit for you — a cost to cut, or the thing currently preventing a bigger cost somewhere else?" Practice cue: hold the question, let them answer before you pitch ROI.

  1. "I can get something similar for free internally." — Skill: Isolating (true cost of "free")

Pattern: "What would it take internally to get that built and maintained — roughly, in time and people?" Practice cue: ask, don't tell — the buyer should surface the hidden cost themselves.

Category 2 — Timing

  1. "Now's not a good time." — Skill: Isolating (real reason vs. politeness)

Pattern: "Fair enough — is it the timing itself, or something else about this giving you pause?" Practice cue: say it, then count to 3 in your head before filling the silence.

  1. "Let's revisit this next quarter." — Skill: Confirming + Contrasting (cost of delay)

Pattern: "Got it. So if we revisit in Q[X], what changes between now and then that makes it a better moment?" Practice cue: test whether "next quarter" is a real plan or a soft no, without sounding pushy.

  1. "We just started something else." — Skill: Isolating (fit vs. bandwidth)

Pattern: "Is it that you don't have bandwidth right now, or that what you started covers this already?" Practice cue: distinguish bandwidth objections from redundancy objections — they need different next steps.

  1. "I need to finish this project first." — Skill: Confirming

Pattern: "Makes sense — what does 'finished' look like, and roughly when do you expect to hit that?" Practice cue: get a specific date, not a vague season.

  1. "Call me back in 6 months." — Skill: Isolating

Pattern: "Happy to. Help me understand what needs to be true in 6 months that isn't true today?" Practice cue: turn a stall into a diagnostic question rather than accepting it at face value.

Category 3 — Competitor / Status Quo

  1. "We're happy with our current provider." — Skill: Isolating (satisfaction vs. habit)

Pattern: "Good to hear. What would 'even better' look like, if anything — or is it genuinely solved end to end?" Practice cue: ask with real curiosity, not skepticism.

  1. "We already do this in-house." — Skill: Reframing (build vs. buy)

Pattern: "How's that working — is it something one person maintains, or built into how the whole team operates?" Practice cue: surface fragility without directly criticizing their build.

  1. "We evaluated you before and went another way." — Skill: Confirming

Pattern: "What was the deciding factor last time, and has that changed on either side since?" Practice cue: ask without getting defensive about the past loss.

  1. "Your competitor already has us locked into a contract." — Skill: Contrasting (switching cost vs. opportunity cost)

Pattern: "Understood — what's the term length, and is there value in evaluating before renewal rather than at it?" Practice cue: float a parallel-evaluation path instead of pushing an immediate switch.

  1. "We don't see enough difference between you and them." — Skill: Contrasting

Pattern: "Where specifically did you land on that — capability, price, or how it was positioned to you?" Practice cue: isolate which axis the "no difference" verdict is actually being made on.

Category 4 — Authority / Stakeholder

  1. "I need to check with my manager." — Skill: Isolating (real approver)

Pattern: "Makes sense — what will they want to know that I haven't covered, so we get it right the first time?" Practice cue: ask to help prep them, not to bypass them.

  1. "It's not my decision." — Skill: Confirming

Pattern: "Got it — who else is part of this, and what's their main lens on a decision like this?" Practice cue: map the buying group out loud without it feeling like an interrogation.

  1. "Procurement will need to review this." — Skill: Isolating (process vs. objection)

Pattern: "Understood — is that standard for anything over [X], or is something specific about this deal triggering it?" Practice cue: separate routine process from a stall dressed up as process.

  1. "I'm not the technical buyer, I can't speak to that." — Skill: Confirming

Pattern: "Fair — what would you want your technical lead to hear directly versus relayed through you?" Practice cue: offer a bridge (call, one-pager) rather than pressing beyond their lane.

  1. "The team that would use this isn't bought in yet." — Skill: Isolating

Pattern: "What have you heard from them so far — resistance to the idea, or resistance to change in general?" Practice cue: distinguish product resistance from change-fatigue.

Category 5 — Trust / Risk / Proof

  1. "How do I know this actually works?" — Skill: Evidencing

Pattern: "Fair question — who's a team similar to yours I can point you to, so you're not taking my word for it?" Practice cue: keep 2-3 specific peer examples ready by heart, not generic "customers love us."

  1. "What if it doesn't work for our specific use case?" — Skill: Isolating (specific risk)

Pattern: "What's the specific scenario you're worried won't be covered? Let's test that one directly." Practice cue: get the buyer to name the actual edge case instead of responding to a vague fear.

  1. "We got burned by a similar tool before." — Skill: Confirming

Pattern: "That's a real scar — what went wrong last time, so I know what we need to prove differently?" Practice cue: sit with it without getting defensive on behalf of "tools like this."

  1. "I don't trust the ROI numbers you're showing me." — Skill: Reframing (claim → proof)

Pattern: "Reasonable — what would a number you did trust look like: something we build together off your own data?" Practice cue: offer to co-build the business case instead of re-pitching your slide.

  1. "This seems too good to be true." — Skill: Isolating

Pattern: "What part specifically feels like the catch — the price, the timeline, or the outcome?" Practice cue: name the skepticism as valid before addressing it.

Category 6 — Fit / Need

  1. "We don't need this." — Skill: Isolating (need vs. awareness)

Pattern: "Help me understand how that gets handled today — sounds like you've got a way around it already." Practice cue: re-open discovery instead of arguing the problem exists.

  1. "This is more than we need — we're a small team." — Skill: Reframing (scale to fit)

Pattern: "What would the right-sized version of this look like for a team your size?" Practice cue: test whether it's a fit objection or a price objection wearing a costume.

  1. "We only need one feature, not the whole platform." — Skill: Confirming

Pattern: "Got it — if we started with just that, what would need to be true for the rest to become relevant later?" Practice cue: accept a narrower entry point without abandoning the larger opportunity.

  1. "Our team won't adopt something new." — Skill: Isolating

Pattern: "What's made past rollouts stick or not stick here — the tool, or how it got introduced?" Practice cue: separate a tooling objection from a change-management objection.

  1. "This doesn't integrate with what we already use." — Skill: Confirming

Pattern: "Which systems specifically — let's check that before we go further so we're not solving a problem you don't have." Practice cue: get specific system names instead of accepting a blanket claim.

Category 7 — Negotiation / Contract Terms

  1. "Can you throw in [X] for free?" — Skill: Isolating (real ask behind the ask)

Pattern: "What would having that unlock for you, that the current scope doesn't?" Practice cue: find the underlying need before trading anything away.

  1. "We need a shorter contract term." — Skill: Reframing (term → risk)

Pattern: "What's driving that — wanting to prove value first, or a budgeting cycle constraint?" Practice cue: distinguish a trust-based ask from a finance-based constraint — the trade differs.

  1. "Your payment terms don't work for us." — Skill: Confirming

Pattern: "What terms does your finance team typically work within, so I know what I'm actually negotiating against?" Practice cue: get their real constraint before proposing a counter.

  1. "We want a discount to sign this month." — Skill: Isolating (real urgency)

Pattern: "What's driving the end-of-month timeline on your side, so I understand what we're both working with?" Practice cue: check whether their urgency is real before you use your own.

  1. "Your competitor offered better terms." — Skill: Contrasting

Pattern: "What specifically was better — the number, the flexibility, or something in the fine print?" Practice cue: ask for the specific term instead of matching a vague "better deal" blind.

Category 8 — Stall / Disengagement

  1. "Just send me some information." — Skill: Isolating (real engagement level)

Pattern: "Happy to — what's the one thing that information needs to answer for you to take the next step?" Practice cue: turn a stall into a concrete next-step commitment.

  1. "I'll loop you in when we're ready." — Skill: Confirming

Pattern: "Appreciate that — what does 'ready' look like on your end, roughly?" Practice cue: get a trigger event, not a vague future.

  1. Prospect goes quiet after a strong first call. — Skill: Isolating (silence diagnosis)

Pattern: "Wanted to check whether this dropped in priority, hit an internal snag, or just got buried — which is it?" Practice cue: give 3 easy-to-pick reasons rather than an open "any update?"

  1. "We're not ready to move forward yet." — Skill: Isolating

Pattern: "What's still missing for you between where we are and 'ready'?" Practice cue: treat this as a genuine gap-finding question, not a close in disguise.

  1. "Can we push this to next year?" — Skill: Contrasting (cost of delay)

Pattern: "We can. What's the cost of the problem staying unsolved for those extra months, on your side?" Practice cue: let the buyer calculate the cost of delay out loud instead of stating it for them.

Weekly drill routine

  • Monday: Pick one skill. Read all objections tagged with it out loud, twice each, in your own words.
  • Wednesday: Roleplay 10 objections with a peer, cold — they pick, you don't know which.
  • Friday: Record yourself on 5 objections. Self-score using the Active Listening Skill Checklist and Negotiation Skill-Level Rubric for calibration.

How to use it

Read one skill category out loud with a peer, cold — they read the objection, you respond in your own words using the named skill — then record and self-score against the practice cue, never memorizing the italicized line itself.
