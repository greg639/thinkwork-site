Gatekeeper Navigation Cheat Sheet

Word-for-word openers and stall-line responses for turning switchboards, EAs, and office managers into allies instead of stonewalls — so calls reach the decision-maker instead of voicemail.

What's inside

  • Mindset-shift framing: gatekeeper as ally, not obstacle
  • Word-for-word openers for switchboard, EA, and department gatekeepers
  • Verbatim responses to 6 common stall lines ("what's this regarding," "send an email," "they're not available," etc.)
  • Ally-building tactics: name use, direct questions, thank-yous
  • Voicemail-avoidance timing and callback cadence rules
  • Push-through vs. multi-thread decision table
  • Do-not-say phrase list

The mindset shift that changes everything

A gatekeeper is not the boss's shield — they're the person who decides whether you're worth an introduction. Every line in this cheat sheet aims at one goal: give the gatekeeper a reason to become your internal advocate, not just to get past them.

Three rules govern every interaction below:

  1. Never lie about who you are or why you're calling. It gets discovered and burns the account.
  2. Treat the gatekeeper like the professional they are — use their name, ask for their help, thank them by name.
  3. Sound like you already belong there. Confidence and specificity beat scripts recited word-for-word.

Opening lines by gatekeeper type

The switchboard / receptionist

"Hi, this is [Your Name] with [Company]. Could you connect me with [First Name] [Last Name] in [department], please?"

If they ask for a reason before connecting:

"Of course — I'm following up on [specific, real reason: a report they downloaded / a mutual connection / a trigger event]. [First Name] will know what it's regarding."

The executive assistant (EA)

EAs guard calendars, not gates — they respect people who respect their time.

"Hi [EA's Name], this is [Your Name] from [Company]. I know [Executive]'s calendar is tightly managed, so I'll be quick — I'm hoping to get 15 minutes on their calendar to talk about [specific business outcome]. What's the best way to get that in front of them?"

The office manager / department gatekeeper

"Hi, this is [Your Name] with [Company]. I work with [Title]s like [Executive] on [specific outcome] — could you point me to whoever owns that on your team?"

Word-for-word responses to the six most common stall lines

"What is this regarding?"

"Totally fair question. I help [title]s at companies like [peer company] solve [specific problem] — I'd like 10 minutes with [Name] to see if it's relevant to what you're working on."

"Can you send an email / information?"

"Happy to — and I'll also try [Name] directly so it doesn't get lost. Is [name]@[domain] still the best address, or is there someone else who owns [topic]?"

(This confirms the contact, gets a name-check, and keeps the door open — it doesn't surrender the call.)

"[Name] doesn't take cold calls."

"That makes sense — most people in that seat don't. Is there someone on the team who does look at [specific topic], or is [Name] still the right person even if calls aren't the way in?"

"They're not available right now."

"No problem at all — when's usually a better time to catch them, morning or afternoon? I don't want to keep calling at the wrong moment."

"Are you a client / vendor already?"

"Not yet — I'm reaching out because [specific, credible reason]. That's exactly the kind of thing I'd want [Name]'s take on."

"I'll pass along a message."

"Thank you — could I leave it with you directly rather than voicemail? [30-second, specific message]. And can I ask your name so I know who to thank when I follow up?"

Turning the gatekeeper into an ally

  • Use their name at least twice in the call — people help people who see them as people.
  • Ask a real question: "What's the best way to actually reach [Name] — email, calendar hold, or is there someone else who screens for them?"
  • Say thank you by name at the end, every time, whether or not you get through.
  • Call back and reference them: "Hi [gatekeeper name], it's [Your Name] again — we spoke last Tuesday about reaching [Name]."

Voicemail-avoidance techniques

  • Call outside the gatekeeper's typical screening hours (early — 8:00–8:30am, or 4:30–5:30pm) when EAs are less likely to be actively screening.
  • Try the direct extension if the auto-attendant offers a directory — many EAs don't screen direct-dial numbers as tightly as the switchboard.
  • If routed to voicemail, hang up and try again in 2–3 hours rather than leaving a message every time — repetition through the switchboard, not the inbox, builds recognition.
  • When you do leave a voicemail, keep it to 3 specific sentences and always pair it with a one-line email the same day — the two together outperform either alone.

When to push through vs. go around (multi-threading)

SituationMove
Gatekeeper is warming up, giving you informationKeep working the same path — you're one call from a yes
Same gatekeeper has blocked you 3+ times with no new informationMulti-thread: find a peer or the target's manager via LinkedIn
Gatekeeper explicitly says "this isn't a fit"Ask one clarifying question, then respect it and redirect to a different contact
You have a specific trigger event or referralSkip the switchboard — go straight to a personalized email or LinkedIn message referencing it

Do-not-say list

  • "Is this a bad time?" (invites an easy no)
  • "I'll just take two minutes" (undersells your value)
  • "Just following up" (gives them nothing to act on)
  • Anything implying you already have a relationship with the executive when you don't — it's checked and it burns trust immediately.

Print this, tape it next to your dial pad, and read the response lines aloud once before your first call block each day — cadence matters as much as wording.

How to use it

Keep it open next to your dial pad during call blocks; read the matching stall-line response verbatim until it becomes second nature, then adapt the wording to your own voice.
