Gatekeeper Bypass Script Pack

Word-for-word language for getting past reception, executive assistants, and screeners — built on confidence and partnership instead of evasion, so it never sounds like a trick.

What's inside

  • The 3 principles that make gatekeeper scripts work (confidence, brevity, partnership)
  • The Colleague Assumption opener for reception desks
  • The Direct Ask for internal transfers and dial-by-name directories
  • The EA Partnership script for executive assistants
  • Word-for-word responses to the 6 most common gatekeeper blocks
  • The auto-attendant / phone-tree navigation method
  • The Re-Approach script for after you've been blocked once
  • A do's-and-don'ts field guide

Why most gatekeeper scripts fail

Most reps get blocked because they sound like they're asking permission to exist. Gatekeepers are trained to filter out anyone who sounds uncertain, apologetic, or vague about why they're calling. The scripts below work because they do three things weak scripts skip:

  1. They assume legitimacy — tone signals you have a reason to be on this call, not that you're hoping to sneak past.
  2. They are short — every extra sentence is a chance for the gatekeeper to find a reason to screen you out.
  3. They treat the gatekeeper as a colleague, not an obstacle — the moment you talk at them instead of through them, they protect their exec harder.

None of these scripts involve lying about who you are or why you're calling. Bypass isn't deception — it's removing the friction that makes screeners suspicious in the first place.

Before you dial

  • Get the gatekeeper's name if you can (LinkedIn, company directory, a prior call). Using a name changes the entire interaction.
  • Know the exact name and correct pronunciation of who you're trying to reach.
  • Decide your "reason for calling" in five words or fewer. If you can't compress it, you're not ready to dial.

Script 1 — The Colleague Assumption (reception desk, first attempt)

Use a tone like you already know the extension — because in your world, you do.

"Hi [Gatekeeper name / 'there' if unknown] — [Your first name] for [Contact first name], please."

That's it. Pause. Let silence do the work. Most reps ruin this by adding "...is he available?" or "...if that's okay" — both signal you expect to be blocked.

If asked "Can I ask what this is regarding?"

"Sure — it's about [one short phrase: 'the renewal on their account' / 'a project we're kicking off with their team' / 'something [Contact] asked me to follow up on']. Is [Contact] around?"

Script 2 — The Direct Ask (internal transfer / dial-by-name directory)

"[Contact]'s office, please." or "Can you connect me to [Contact], please?"

Said with the same energy you'd use asking for a conference room. No throat-clearing, no "hi, how are you," no self-introduction unless asked.

Script 3 — The EA Partnership Script (executive assistants)

EAs are not receptionists — they are gatekeepers with real authority and long memories. Never try to "get past" an EA. Recruit them.

"Hi [EA name], I'm [Your name] with [Company]. I know [Contact]'s calendar goes through you, so I wanted to ask you directly rather than cold-call in: what's the best way to get 15 minutes on [Contact]'s calendar for something on [topic]? I'm happy to send background over first if that's useful to you."

This does three things: uses their name, acknowledges their real authority, and offers to make their job easier. EAs who feel respected become internal advocates — they'll tell you when the exec is receptive, when travel makes a call pointless, and sometimes hand you the direct line.

Follow-up once trust is built (second or third contact):

"[EA name], it's [Your name] again — any read on whether this week or next is better for [Contact]?"

Script 4 — Responses to the 6 Most Common Blocks

Gatekeeper saysYour response
"Can I ask what this is regarding?""Absolutely — it's about [5-word reason]. Is [Contact] free?"
"She's not available right now.""No problem — when's she usually easiest to reach, morning or afternoon?"
"Are you trying to sell something?""I'm calling to see if there's a fit — if there's not, I'll say so myself in under a minute. Can you put me through?"
"Can you send an email instead?""I can, and I will either way — but a two-minute conversation will save both of us time figuring out if this is even relevant. Is [Contact] around right now?"
"He's in a meeting.""Got it — what's the best time to try back, or is there a better number to reach him directly?"
"We're not interested." (screener answering unprompted)"Understood — can I ask, is that a decision you're able to make on [Contact]'s behalf, or would it be worth a quick two minutes with [Contact] directly so I'm not wasting your time on the wrong read?"

Notice none of these escalate. Each asks a forward question instead of defending your call.

Script 5 — Navigating the Auto-Attendant / Phone Tree

  • Dial "0" or "operator" first — most trees route to a live person faster than guessing extensions.
  • If you have the contact's name, try "dial by name directory" before giving up and hitting voicemail.
  • If routed to a department line, use Script 1 verbatim once a human answers.

Script 6 — The Re-Approach (after being blocked once)

Being blocked once is data, not a verdict. Wait 3–5 business days, then:

"Hi [Gatekeeper name], it's [Your name] again from [Company] — I know we spoke last week. I won't take much of your time: is there a better day this week to catch [Contact], or would you rather I try [Contact]'s direct line if you have it?"

Naming the prior contact signals persistence without pressure, and the two-option close gives the gatekeeper an easy way to help you instead of block you again.

Do's and Don'ts

Do

  • Use the gatekeeper's name every time you have it.
  • Match their pace — brisk and efficient in, brisk and efficient back.
  • Say thank you by name when they help, even a little. This is what turns a one-time block into a standing relationship.

Don't

  • Don't lie about your name, company, or reason for calling — it destroys trust the moment it's discovered, and gatekeepers compare notes.
  • Don't use fake urgency ("it's an emergency," "he's expecting my call" when he isn't) — it works once and burns the relationship permanently.
  • Don't over-explain. The longer your reason for calling, the more it sounds like a pitch, and pitches get screened.

How to use it

Keep this open during calling blocks and reach for the script matching whoever answers (reception vs. EA vs. screener); use the Re-Approach script for anyone who blocked you in a prior attempt.
