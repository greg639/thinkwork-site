Objection Handling Scorecard for Call Reviews

The rubric managers use to score how a rep actually handled objections on a recorded call — 8 criteria scored 1-5, not a gut-feel 'good job' or 'needs work.'

What's inside

  • An 8-criterion rubric scored 1-5 (recognition, acknowledgment, isolation, diagnosis, reframe, evidence, forward motion, tone)
  • Behavioral definitions of what a '5' looks like for each criterion
  • A 40-point total scoring band with coaching guidance per band
  • Calibration notes so two managers score within 3 points of each other
  • A recommended weekly call-review cadence
  • Guidance for distinguishing handling quality from deal outcome

A rubric for managers to score how a rep actually handled objections on a recorded call — not a gut-feel "good job" or "needs work," but a repeatable 1-5 scale across the behaviors that separate objection-handling that advances a deal from objection-handling that just survives it.

Scoring Scale (apply to each criterion)

  • 1 = Not attempted / handled poorly, damaged the conversation
  • 2 = Attempted but weak — objection not really addressed
  • 3 = Adequate — objection addressed, deal survived
  • 4 = Strong — objection addressed and deal advanced
  • 5 = Excellent — objection turned into a stronger position than before it surfaced

The Rubric

#CriterionWhat a "5" Looks LikeScore (1-5)
1Recognition speedRep identifies the real objection immediately, doesn't need it repeated
2AcknowledgmentRep validates the objection without arguing, conceding, or getting defensive
3IsolationRep confirms this is the real/only objection before responding to it
4Root-cause diagnosisRep asks a question that surfaces the underlying driver, not just the surface phrasing
5Reframe qualityResponse reframes the objection productively rather than just rebutting it
6Evidence usedRep backs the response with a specific, relevant proof point (not generic)
7Forward motionCall ends with a concrete next step tied to resolving the objection
8Tone & confidenceDelivered calmly, no rushing, no visible defensiveness or over-talking

Scoring Bands

  • 34-40 — Exemplary: Use this call as a training clip for the team.
  • 26-33 — Solid: Coach on the 1-2 lowest-scoring criteria only.
  • 18-25 — Developing: Needs structured practice on isolation and root-cause diagnosis specifically.
  • Below 18 — Needs Intervention: Pair with a stronger rep for live shadowing before the next call.

Calibration Notes for Managers

  • Score the call, not the outcome — a rep can score a 5 on handling and still lose the deal for reasons outside their control (budget freeze, timing). Don't conflate the two.
  • Two managers scoring the same call should land within 3 points of each other. If not, recalibrate against the criterion definitions before your next review cycle.
  • Use timestamps: note the minute mark of the objection and the response so the rep can re-listen to the exact moment in coaching.

Call Review Cadence

Score at least one recorded call per rep per week. Rotate which objection category you're specifically listening for (price one week, status-quo the next) so coaching stays targeted rather than generic.

How to use it

Score at least one recorded call per rep per week against the 8 criteria, use the banding to decide coaching priority, and share the timestamped moment with the rep for review.
