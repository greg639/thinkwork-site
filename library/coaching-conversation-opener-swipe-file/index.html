Coaching Conversation Opener Swipe File

Word-for-word openers for the everyday hard coaching conversation — a skipped step, a slipping number, feedback that didn't land last time — built to get past the first 30 seconds without triggering defensiveness.

The first 30 seconds of a hard coaching conversation decide whether the rep hears you or starts building their defense. These openers are built on one rule: name the pattern, state your intent, then hand them the floor — in that order, every time.

The Anatomy of a Non-Defensive Opener

Every opener below follows the same 3-part structure:

  1. Name the specific, observable pattern — not a character judgment ("I noticed X happened on 3 of your last 5 calls," not "you seem unmotivated")
  2. State your intent plainly — why you're raising it, framed as helping them, not managing them ("I want to make sure this isn't costing you deals you'd otherwise win")
  3. Hand them the floor with a real question — not a rhetorical one ("What's your read on it?" not "Does that make sense?")

Skipping step 3 — going straight from naming the pattern into your own explanation — is the single most common way managers accidentally trigger defensiveness.

Opener: Missed or Skipped Process Step

"Hey [name], I want to flag something specific before we get into anything else. On your last two demos, the discovery questions we agreed on didn't come up — the calls went straight to the product walkthrough. I'm not assuming why, I want to understand what happened. Walk me through what those two calls looked like from your side."

Opener: Declining Metric Trend

"I looked at your numbers over the last 6 weeks, and [specific metric] has dropped from [X] to [Y]. I wanted to raise it now, early, rather than wait until it's a bigger conversation. Before I say anything else — what's your own read on what's changed?"

Opener: Feedback That Didn't Land Last Time

"Last time we talked about [specific skill/behavior], and I don't think I landed it the way I meant to, because I'm not seeing it show up differently yet. That's on how I coached it, not just on you. Can we try this again — what did you take away from that conversation, in your own words?"

Opener: Low Activity or Effort Concerns

"I want to be direct: your activity numbers this month are noticeably below where they've been. I'm raising it now because I'd rather understand what's going on than guess. Is something getting in the way — workload, motivation, something outside work — or is this something else?"

Opener: A Rep Who Got Defensive Last Time

"Before we get into today's topic, I want to go back to our last conversation for a second — I think it came across more critical than I meant it to, and I want to reset that. My goal in these conversations is always to help you close the specific gap, not to make a point. Can we start there — did it land differently than I intended?"

Opener: Re-Opening Contact After a Rep Goes Quiet

"I noticed you've been harder to reach the last couple of weeks, and I wanted to check in directly rather than let it sit. No specific agenda from my side beyond making sure you're okay and unblocked — what's going on?"

Opener: Correcting After a Genuine Win (Sandwich Variant — Use Sparingly)

"That close on [deal] was genuinely well handled, especially [specific thing they did well] — I want that on the record before anything else. There's one thing from that same call I want to flag, because I think it'll matter more as deals get bigger: [specific pattern]. What was going through your head at that point?"

Use this variant only when the win is real and specific — a generic compliment bolted onto criticism is quickly recognized as a formula and stops working.

Phrases to Avoid, and Why

AvoidWhy it backfires
"We need to talk."Maximizes anxiety before the rep has any information to respond to
"You always..." / "You never..."Character judgment, not a specific pattern — invites denial rather than reflection
"Does that make sense?" as the only questionInvites a reflexive yes, not a real answer — replace with "what's your read on it?"
"I'm not trying to blame you, but..."The word "but" cancels the reassurance that came before it
Opening with your own explanation of what happenedRep hasn't had the chance to give their account first, so it feels like a verdict, not a conversation

How to use it

Match the situation to the closest opener below, say it close to verbatim in the first 30 seconds of the conversation, then stop talking and let the rep respond before you move to the substance of the feedback.
