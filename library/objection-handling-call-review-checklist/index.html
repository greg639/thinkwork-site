Objection Handling Call Review Checklist

A section-by-section scorecard for auditing a rep's recorded objection-handling calls — covering pre-objection setup, the acknowledgment moment, diagnostic depth, and loop closure, with a scoring rubric and grade bands. Built for use during call reviews, coaching prep, or QA sampling.

What's inside

  • Section A: Before the Objection Surfaces — 4 pre-objection listening cues
  • Section B: The First 3 Seconds After Impact — interruption/rebuttal/pause audit
  • Section C: Acknowledgment Check — 4 items including the 'premature but' flag
  • Section D: Diagnostic Questioning Depth — 5 items distinguishing stall vs. real objection
  • Section E: Response-to-Root-Cause Match — 5 items on proof, proportion, and held frame
  • Section F: Loop-Closed Confirmation — 3 items on explicit check-back and next steps
  • Section G: 4 deal-killing red flags that override the score
  • Scoring rubric and 4 grade bands (Novice through Advanced) with coaching guidance

For managers auditing a recorded call, 1:1, or live shadow session

How to score: For each item mark Y (clearly did it), P (partial / inconsistent), N (did not do it), or N/A. Note the call timestamp next to anything you'll bring up in coaching. Score = 2 pts per Y, 1 pt per P, 0 pts per N.

A. Before the Objection Surfaces (4 items — 8 pts)

  • A1. Discovery depth — Rep asked at least one question that could have surfaced this objection before pitching (budget range, current tools, prior bad experience, timeline drivers).
  • A2. Objection-inviting language — Rep used an open invitation at some point ("What's going to make this a hard yes for you?" / "Where do you see this falling apart?") rather than only pitch-and-pause.
  • A3. Pacing — Rep wasn't rushing toward a close in a way that visibly triggered a defensive reaction (listen for the prospect interrupting or going quiet right before the objection).
  • A4. Tone set — Rep's energy up to this point was consultative, not "salesy" (rep used the prospect's language back, not scripted jargon).

B. The First 3 Seconds After the Objection Lands (3 items — 6 pts)

  • B1. No interruption — Rep let the prospect finish the objection completely before responding.
  • B2. No instant rebuttal — Rep did not immediately counter-argue, justify, or defend ("no, but—", "actually—", "well, the reason is—").
  • B3. Audible pause — There's a beat (1–2 seconds) of silence or a verbal "let me make sure I've got that" before the rep speaks — evidence they processed instead of reflexed.

C. Acknowledgment (before any selling) (4 items — 8 pts)

  • C1. Named the objection back — Rep restated what they heard in their own words, not a script line ("So budget's been reallocated for the rest of the year — that's real.").
  • C2. Validated without conceding the deal — Acknowledgment sounds like respect, not surrender. Listen for the difference between "That makes sense" (fine) and "You're right, this probably isn't worth pursuing" (concession — flag it).
  • C3. No premature "but" — Rep didn't pair the acknowledgment with an immediate pivot ("I hear you, BUT—") that cancels it out.
  • C4. Tone match — If the objection was emotionally charged (frustration, past bad experience), rep's acknowledgment tone slowed down and warmed up rather than staying in pitch-mode.

D. Diagnostic Questioning — Is This the Real Objection? (5 items — 10 pts)

  • D1. At least one open diagnostic question asked before responding ("Help me understand what's driving that" / "Is it the cost, the timing, or something else?").
  • D2. Rep distinguished stall vs. genuine constraint — did they check for a decision-maker issue, a competing priority, or a past bad experience underneath the surface objection?
  • D3. Rep didn't argue with an unverified assumption — i.e., didn't respond to "no budget" with an ROI pitch before confirming budget really is the blocker.
  • D4. Follow-up question after the first answer — one diagnostic question is rarely enough; rep dug at least one layer deeper.
  • D5. Rep captured specifics (a number, a name, a date, a prior vendor) rather than accepting a vague answer.

E. Response Matched to Root Cause (5 items — 10 pts)

  • **E1. Response addressed the diagnosed objection, not the surface one.**
  • E2. Response included proof — a customer example, data point, or specific mechanism, not just reassurance.
  • E3. Response was proportionate — rep didn't over-explain or launch a 3-minute monologue in response to a one-line objection.
  • E4. Rep offered a concrete next step tied to the objection (e.g., a phased pilot for a budget objection, a reference call for a trust objection) rather than a generic "does that make sense?"
  • E5. Rep held the frame — didn't discount, over-promise, or make a commitment the company can't keep just to make the objection go away.

F. Confirming the Loop Is Closed (3 items — 6 pts)

  • F1. Rep explicitly checked resolution ("Does that address the budget concern, or is there more to it?") rather than assuming and moving on.
  • F2. Prospect's own words confirmed resolution — listen for the prospect re-engaging in their own language, not just a flat "okay."
  • F3. Rep re-anchored to a next step with a date/owner attached, not a vague "I'll follow up."

G. Red Flags — Any ONE of these overrides the score above

  • Rep argued with the prospect or got defensive in tone.
  • Rep discounted price or over-promised scope within 60 seconds of the objection, with no diagnosis first.
  • Rep went silent/froze and let the prospect fill the silence with more objections.
  • Rep agreed the deal was dead without attempting a single diagnostic question.

Scoring & Grade Bands

Total possible: 48 pts (excluding Section G)

ScoreGradeWhat it means
43–48AdvancedTextbook objection handling — use this call as a training clip
33–42ProficientSolid fundamentals, 1–2 specific gaps to coach
20–32DevelopingPattern of skipping diagnosis or acknowledgment — needs a coaching plan, not a one-off note
Below 20NoviceFoundational gap — pair with a role-play session before the next live call

Any Section G red flag checked = cap the grade at Developing regardless of total score.

What to bring to the 1:1

Pick the lowest-scoring section, not the lowest-scoring item — coach the pattern, not the moment. Bring 1–2 timestamped clips as evidence.

How to use it

Score live or recorded calls section-by-section while listening, timestamp anything you'll reference in coaching, then use the grade bands to decide whether the rep needs a quick note or a structured coaching plan.
