Negotiation Roleplay Scorecard (Manager Edition)

A behavioral scorecard for grading negotiation roleplays in 1:1s, team practice, and new-hire certification, with eight scored competencies and four-level behavioral anchors, so 'good negotiating' becomes a repeatable, defensible grade instead of a gut call.

How to use it

Print the scoring sheet before each roleplay, assign a scenario brief, and have the grader score independently and in writing before the debrief conversation begins.

What's inside

  • 8 scored negotiation competencies with 1-4 behavioral anchors
  • Pre-roleplay setup instructions (roles, timing, scenario assignment)
  • Full scoring sheet with weighting guidance
  • Composite score bands: Developing / Competent / Proficient / Expert
  • Calibration rules for grading multiple reps consistently
  • 4 ready-to-run scenario briefs (discount pressure, procurement RFP, competitive displacement, multi-year renewal)
  • Word-for-word post-roleplay debrief script
  • Automatic red-flag disqualifiers list

Use this to grade any negotiation roleplay with the rigor of a certified competency assessment. Every score needs a specific behavioral moment as evidence - if you can't point to what the rep said or did, don't score it.

Running the roleplay

  1. Assign roles 24 hours ahead: one rep negotiates, one manager or peer plays the buyer from a written brief, one observer scores silently.
  2. Run 12-15 minutes uninterrupted. No mid-roleplay coaching.
  3. Score immediately after, before the debrief conversation - write numbers down first.
  4. In a cohort, all graders score independently, then reconcile any gap of 2+ points before finalizing.

Weighting note: competencies 3 (Concession Architecture) and 7 (BATNA Discipline) are deal-outcome determinative - if either scores a 1, cap the composite band at 'Developing' regardless of total.

Composite bands

  • 8-15: Developing - do not send solo into a live negotiation above this band without shadowing
  • 16-23: Competent - can run standard negotiations, needs support on multi-stakeholder or high-discount pressure
  • 24-28: Proficient - can run negotiations solo, ready to mentor
  • 29-32: Expert - certifiable to coach other reps on negotiation

Calibration rules for managers

  • Score behavior, not likability. A confident rep who caves on price still scores low on competency 2.
  • Require a specific quote or action as evidence for every score above 2. 'They seemed prepared' is not evidence.
  • Do not average away a disqualifier (see below) - it caps the whole grade.
  • Re-calibrate quarterly: have two managers grade the same recorded roleplay and compare before grading live sessions independently.

Automatic red-flag disqualifiers (cap composite at 'Developing')

  • Discloses actual walk-away price or discount floor to the buyer-player
  • Leads with a price cut before any value question is asked
  • Agrees to buyer's contract paper or terms without flagging it for review
  • Asks zero discovery questions in the first 3 minutes
  • Commits to a term outside their known approval authority without noting it needs sign-off

4 ready-to-run scenario briefs

Scenario A - Discount Pressure at Quarter-End: Buyer opens with 'We can sign today if you match the 30% our incumbent offered.' Buyer-player pushes hard on urgency, references a real competing quote, and threatens to push the deal to next quarter if the rep doesn't move fast. Good performance holds the anchor, trades term-length or payment-timing for any discount, and never matches blind.

Scenario B - Procurement RFP Re-Open: Buyer-player is a procurement lead who says legal/security flagged the deal for a formal RFP process after verbal agreement was reached with the business sponsor. Good performance re-engages the original champion, asks what specifically triggered the re-open, and negotiates scope/timeline rather than re-negotiating price from zero.

Scenario C - Competitive Displacement: Buyer-player says the incumbent vendor 'is fine, just cheaper' and asks the rep to justify the premium or lose the deal. Good performance quantifies the cost of the status quo rather than attacking the competitor, and asks what 'fine' is actually costing them today.

Scenario D - Multi-Year Renewal Resistance: Buyer-player refuses a 3-year commitment, wants annual with an opt-out. Good performance trades term length for something the buyer values (price hold, capacity, priority support) rather than dropping the ask outright.

Post-roleplay debrief script (ask in this order)

  1. 'Walk me through the moment you felt the most pressure - what did you do?'
  2. 'Where did you give something away, and what did you get back for it?'
  3. 'If you ran this again, what's the one thing you'd change in the first two minutes?'

Score the scorecard before this conversation. Use the debrief to build the rep's own next-rep plan, not to relitigate the grade.
