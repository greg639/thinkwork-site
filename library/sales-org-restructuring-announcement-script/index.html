Sales Org Restructuring Announcement Script

Word-for-word scripts for announcing territory changes, team splits, and comp plan changes — built to get ahead of the resignation wave instead of triggering one.

What's inside

  • Manager pre-brief script (24-48 hrs before all-hands)
  • All-hands scripts for territory realignment, team splits, and comp plan changes
  • 1:1 scripts for a rep losing an account and a rep getting a new manager
  • FAQ document template
  • Written follow-up communication template
  • Retention-risk triage checklist to run before you announce

Territory changes, team splits, and comp plan changes are where good sales orgs lose their best people — not because the change was wrong, but because it was announced badly. Use these scripts as written; the specificity is the point.

Before you announce anything: brief your managers first (24–48 hours prior)

"I'm about to walk you through a change to [territories/team structure/comp plan] that goes live [date]. I want you to hear it from me first, with time to ask me anything, before your team hears it in the all-hands. Your job after this meeting is not to sell it — it's to be able to answer your team's questions honestly, including 'I don't love every part of this either, but here's why it's happening.' Let's go through it." [Walk managers through the what, the why, and a pre-built FAQ — see below. Give them 24-48 hours to sit with it before the org-wide announcement, and flag which of their reports are most likely to be upset so they can plan a same-day 1:1.]

All-hands announcement: Territory realignment

"I want to talk through a change to how territories are assigned, effective [date]. Here's what's changing: [specific — e.g., "we're moving from geographic territories to named-account segments based on company size"]. Here's why: [the actual business reason — market has shifted, some territories were significantly over- or under-indexed, we're entering a new segment — be specific, not "to better serve our customers," which nobody believes]. Here's what's not changing: [comp plan structure, your manager, your title — name what stays the same so people aren't scared of everything at once]. Here's what happens to deals already in your pipeline: [explicit rule — e.g., "any opportunity with a signed mutual close plan and an activity in the last 30 days stays with the current owner through close"]. Here's the timeline: [when the new territories take effect, when account lists go out, when the transition period ends]. I know this means some of you are picking up new accounts and some of you are handing accounts off. I'm doing 1:1s with everyone directly affected over the next [X days] — if you're worried about what this means for you specifically, you'll hear from me or your manager before this becomes 'old news' in a Slack channel. Questions now, or in the doc I'm about to share?"

All-hands announcement: Team split / new manager layer

"As the team has grown to [N] people, I'm restructuring so no one manager is stretched across more people than they can actually coach well. Here's what that means. The team is splitting into [N] pods, each with a manager. [Name managers and, if newly promoted, one sentence on why: "X has been informally mentoring half this team for two quarters already — this makes it official."] Here's how you'll know which pod you're on: [when the list goes out, how]. What doesn't change: your comp plan, your quota, your title. What does change: who runs your 1:1s and coaches your pipeline day to day. [If you, the current leader, are moving up a layer:] I'm still very much around — I'm just not going to be the one running your weekly pipeline review anymore, and that's by design so you get more coaching time, not less. I'd rather you ask the uncomfortable question here than wonder about it for two weeks. What's on your mind?"

All-hands announcement: Comp plan change

"I'm walking you through changes to the comp plan for [next period], effective [date]. What's changing and why: [be exact — e.g., "we're moving accelerators to kick in at 100% instead of 80%, because last year's plan paid out disproportionately to reps who front-loaded renewals rather than new logos, which isn't the behavior we want to reward"]. Tie every change to a specific behavior you're trying to encourage — a comp change with no stated "why" reads as a pay cut no matter what the math says. What this means for your take-home if you perform at the same level as last year: [model 2-3 concrete scenarios — e.g., "a rep who hit 100% last year would have earned about the same under the new plan; a rep who hit 130% would have earned more"]. Show the math. Vague reassurance ("most people will be fine") reads as a cover-up even when it's true. Effective date and any grandfathering: [explicit — e.g., "deals already closed under the old plan are paid under the old plan; the new plan applies to bookings from (date) forward"]. I'll have office hours this week for anyone who wants to run their specific number through the new plan with me before it goes live. This isn't a 'trust me' ask — bring your calculator."

1:1 script: rep losing an account/territory

"I want to talk through your new territory before you see the account list land in your inbox. You're losing [account/region] — here's specifically why: [reason, tied to the stated criteria, not "because we decided to"]. Here's what you're gaining: [new accounts/territory — lead with what's coming, not just what's leaving]. I know [lost account] was one you'd built real relationship equity in. That doesn't just evaporate — [if applicable: you'll get a transition credit / a warm handoff role / recognition in the account history]. What's your reaction to this — and what do you need from me to make the transition to the new patch productive fast, not slow?"

1:1 script: rep getting a new manager

"You're moving under [new manager] starting [date]. I want to be straight with you about why, and give you space to react. [Reason — team growth, specialization, capacity — be honest, not diplomatic-sounding.] Here's what doesn't change: [your comp, your quota, your standing on the team]. I'm not disappearing — [describe your ongoing relationship, if any]. But day-to-day coaching and your pipeline reviews move to [new manager], and I think that's actually going to mean you get more attention, not less, because [reason]. What questions or concerns do you have — including ones about [new manager] specifically that you'd rather ask me directly than wonder about?"

FAQ document template (share alongside the announcement, don't wait for questions to trickle in)

  • Q: Does this affect my comp plan? [Direct answer.]
  • Q: What happens to deals I'm already working? [The exact rule, no exceptions language.]
  • Q: Is my job at risk? [Answer directly — silence here is read as "yes."]
  • Q: Why now? [The real trigger — a bad quarter, a growth milestone, a strategic pivot.]
  • Q: Who decided this and can it be appealed? [Be honest about how final this is.]
  • Q: When do I get my specifics (new territory/team/number)? [Exact date.]

Follow-up written communication (send within 2 hours of the announcement, don't let it sit overnight as the only source of truth)

Subject: [Change] — what it means for you, in writing Following up on today's conversation in writing so you have something to refer back to (not because anything's changed from what I said live). [3-5 bullet recap of exactly what was said — the what, why, timeline, and what doesn't change.] Full FAQ is linked here: [link]. If your question isn't in it, reply directly to me — I'll answer personally, not route you to HR unless it's genuinely an HR matter.

Retention risk triage (do this before you announce, not after someone resigns)

  • List every rep losing something material (accounts, title, autonomy) in this change
  • Rank by flight risk (top performer + losing something = highest priority same-day 1:1)
  • Have a retention conversation ready for your top 2-3 flight risks before the all-hands, not reactively after they've already updated LinkedIn

How to use it

Brief managers with the pre-announcement script 24-48 hours before the all-hands, adapt the relevant announcement script to your specifics, then run the retention-risk triage before you present, not after someone resigns.
