Cold Calling Confidence & Call Reluctance Self-Assessment

A 15-question diagnostic that identifies which of the 12 documented forms of call reluctance is capping your dial volume, plus a Call Reluctance Index and a targeted fix for each type.

What's inside

  • 12 core statements, one per documented form of call reluctance
  • 3 overall-impact statements for the Call Reluctance Index (CRI)
  • 1–5 scoring scale and instructions
  • CRI bands (Low / Moderate / High impact)
  • Full descriptions of all 12 forms (Doomsayer, Over-Preparer, Hyper-Pro, Stage Fright, Role Rejection, Yielder, Social Self-Consciousness, Separationist, Emotionally Unemancipated, Oppositional Reflex, Telephobia, Referral Aversion)
  • A "where to start" fix mapped to each of the 12 forms
  • A 30-day retake protocol

Call reluctance isn't laziness or lack of drive — it's a set of specific, learned avoidance patterns that quietly cap dial volume even in reps who genuinely want to perform. This assessment surfaces which of the 12 documented forms is doing the most damage to your numbers, so you can fix the actual cause instead of relying on generic "just be more confident" advice.

How to take it

Rate each of the 12 statements below from 1 to 5: 1 = Never true of me · 2 = Rarely true · 3 = Sometimes true · 4 = Often true · 5 = Almost always true

Answer based on how you actually behave, not how you'd like to behave. Take it in under 5 minutes, in one sitting, without over-thinking any single item.

Part 1 — The 12 forms (Q1–Q12)

#StatementScore (1–5)
1I mentally rehearse worst-case reactions (anger, rudeness, rejection) before I dial, more than I picture it going well.
2I keep "getting ready" — more research, more list-building, more script tweaking — instead of actually dialing.
3I feel like cold calling is beneath my skill level, so I only do it when I've run out of "better" ways to prospect.
4The idea of my voice going out to a stranger who could react badly makes my heart rate rise before I dial.
5I feel a flash of embarrassment about being "in sales" when I introduce myself, and it shows in my tone.
6I soften or shorten my ask because I don't want to feel like I'm imposing on the prospect's time.
7I get noticeably more anxious calling someone senior, wealthy, or high-status than calling a peer-level contact.
8I avoid calling people I actually know (past colleagues, friends, warm contacts) far more than calling strangers.
9Part of my hesitation to push harder in sales comes from how my family or people close to me view "sales" as a career.
10I dislike using a script or a proven structure and tend to go off-book even when the proven version works better.
11The phone itself — not the conversation, the physical act of dialing — is what I put off.
12I'll ask for the sale or the next step but I actively avoid asking for a referral, even from happy customers.

Part 2 — Overall impact (Q13–Q15)

#StatementScore (1–5)
13In an average week, I dial noticeably fewer calls than my target because I find reasons to do "other" work instead.
14I've gone an entire day where I should have been calling and made excuses to avoid starting.
15If I'm honest, call reluctance has cost me measurable pipeline or income this quarter.

Scoring

Step 1 — Identify your top forms. Your two highest-scoring items in Q1–Q12 (ties allowed) are your primary call reluctance patterns. Anything scoring 4 or 5 is active and worth addressing regardless of rank.

Step 2 — Calculate your Call Reluctance Index (CRI). Add Q13+Q14+Q15 (range 3–15):

  • 3–6: Low impact — reluctance patterns exist but aren't materially capping your numbers yet.
  • 7–11: Moderate impact — reluctance is measurably reducing your dial volume or pipeline; worth a deliberate 2–4 week correction.
  • 12–15: High impact — reluctance is a primary constraint on your performance; treat this as the top priority skill to fix, above objection handling or closing technique.

The 12 forms — what each one means and how to address it

#FormWhat it looks likeWhere to start
1DoomsayerCatastrophizing the reaction before you dialRun Pre-Call Planning Checklist item 9 (result-independent mindset) before every block
2Over-PreparerEndless research/list-building as a stall tacticCap research at 2 minutes (Checklist item 3) and dial anyway
3Hyper-ProFeeling "above" cold calling, doing it only as a last resortReframe cold calling as the core rep skill — track it as a metric you're proud of, not a chore
4Stage FrightPhysiological anxiety at the thought of a live, unscripted reactionUse the 30-Second Cold Call Framework to remove improvisation from the first 30 seconds
5Role RejectionEmbarrassment about being "in sales," leaking into tonePractice your opener out loud daily until your name + role feels neutral to say
6YielderSoftening the ask to avoid feeling imposingRehearse the Question stage of the 30-Second framework until the ask feels routine, not intrusive
7Social Self-ConsciousnessExtra anxiety calling senior/high-status contactsUse Executive-to-Executive framing — peer tone, not supplicant tone
8SeparationistAvoiding calls to people you actually knowBuild a short warm-list block separate from cold dials, with a lower-pressure script
9Emotionally UnemancipatedHesitation rooted in family/social approval of a sales careerSeparate identity from activity — you're building a skill, not seeking approval on every call
10Oppositional ReflexResisting scripts/structure even when it worksRun the 30-Second Framework verbatim for 20 calls before judging whether it "works for you"
11TelephobiaAvoidance of the phone itself, distinct from the conversationUse Power Hour blocks to build tolerance through repetition — volume desensitizes the trigger
12Referral AversionAvoiding the ask for referrals specificallyScript the referral ask word-for-word in advance so it isn't a live decision

What to do with your result

  1. Note your top 1–2 forms and your CRI band.
  2. Pick the single "where to start" action for your top form and run it for 5 consecutive calling blocks — not indefinitely, just long enough to break the avoidance pattern.
  3. Retake this assessment in 30 days. A genuine drop in your top form's score is the leading indicator that dial volume will follow.

How to use it

Score all 15 items in one sitting, identify your top 1–2 reluctance forms and your Call Reluctance Index band, then apply the matching fix for 5 consecutive calling blocks before retaking it.
