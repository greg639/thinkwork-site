Rep Motivation Drivers Framework

Maps every rep on your team against six intrinsic and extrinsic motivation drivers so incentives, recognition, and coaching actually land, instead of defaulting to a leaderboard and a cash spiff for everyone.

How to use it

Score each rep on the Mapping Grid after 2-4 weeks of observation, cross-check with the self-report survey, then pick one Manager Action per rep from their top-2 drivers to run this month.

What's inside

  • 6 driver profiles: Mastery, Autonomy, Purpose, Recognition, Competition, Reward
  • Observable signals for spotting each driver in real behavior, not self-report alone
  • What lights each driver up and what quietly kills it
  • A Manager Action Bank, 3 concrete moves per driver
  • A Driver Mapping Grid to score every rep across all 6
  • A 5-question self-report survey to cross-check your observations
  • A Common Mismatch Patterns list showing where managers default wrong

Why this exists

Most incentive design defaults to two levers: a leaderboard and a cash spiff. Those work for reps wired for competition and reward: and actively demotivate reps wired for mastery, autonomy, or purpose, who read a public leaderboard as pressure rather than fuel. This framework gives you six named drivers, how to spot each one in real behavior, and a specific manager move for each, so incentive design stops being one-size-fits-all.

The 6 Drivers

1. Mastery (intrinsic)

Definition: Motivated by getting visibly better at the craft: technique, precision, becoming excellent. Spot it by: Asks for feedback unprompted. Rewatches their own calls. Asks "why did that work" more than "did that work." Gets more energized by a skill breakthrough than by a single win. What lights it up: Naming the specific skill they leveled up. Access to harder reps or accounts once a skill is proven. A visible skill-level or certification system. What kills it: Praise that only mentions the number ("great month") with no mention of what got better. Being told to "just keep doing what you're doing" instead of what's next. Manager Action Bank:

  • Debrief wins by asking "what specifically did you do differently" before moving on.
  • Give them the hardest account or the trickiest objection pattern to work, framed as a stretch, not a punishment.
  • Build a skill-level ladder they can see themselves climbing, separate from the comp plan.

2. Autonomy (intrinsic)

Definition: Motivated by control over how the work gets done: approach, schedule, sequencing. Spot it by: Pushes back on scripts. Asks "why" before "how." Performs worse under close oversight, better when given a goal and left alone. What lights it up: Being handed a goal and genuinely left to choose the method. Being asked for their approach before being told the standard one. What kills it: Step-by-step call scripts with no room to adapt. Being shadowed or micromanaged right after a win, as if success bought less freedom instead of more. Manager Action Bank:

  • Set the outcome and the guardrails, then explicitly say "how you get there is yours."
  • Ask them to pressure-test the team's standard playbook and suggest one change.
  • Give them one account or territory they run with minimal check-ins as a trust signal.

3. Purpose (intrinsic)

Definition: Motivated by meaning, whose life or business the work actually touches. Spot it by: Talks about the client's outcome unprompted. Gets more animated recounting a client result than a commission number. Asks about the "why" behind a product change. What lights it up: Direct, specific stories of what the deal did for the client's business or life. Visibility into downstream impact: renewal, expansion, a client testimonial. What kills it: Framing every conversation in quota-only language. No line of sight from their activity to a real outcome for a real person. Manager Action Bank:

  • Read them the client's own words when a deal renews or expands, not just the number.
  • Loop them into a customer success story their deal enabled.
  • In 1:1s, ask "what result are you proudest of this month" before "what's your number."

4. Recognition (extrinsic)

Definition: Motivated by being seen: praised by name, in front of others. Spot it by: Lights up visibly in team shoutouts. References past public praise. Deflates when a good result goes unmentioned. What lights it up: Specific, named, public praise: Slack shoutout, standup mention, a call clip shared with the team. What kills it: Generic team-wide praise that never uses their name. Wins that go unacknowledged because "the number speaks for itself." Manager Action Bank:

  • Name them specifically in the next team channel post: not "great team effort," but "Maria closed X by doing Y."
  • Nominate them for the next internal spotlight, newsletter mention, or leadership shoutout.
  • Ask their permission to share a specific moment from their call as a team example.

5. Competition (extrinsic)

Definition: Motivated by rank, comparison, and winning against a visible field. Spot it by: Checks the leaderboard unprompted. Talks in relative terms ("I'm 2 behind"). Performs better in contests than in steady-state months. What lights it up: Live leaderboards, head-to-head contests, a visible rank they can move. What kills it: A flat comp structure with no visible rank. Contests that only reward the top 1 with nothing for most-improved. Manager Action Bank:

  • Put them in short, frequent contests (weekly, not just quarterly), competitive drivers fatigue on long time horizons.
  • Add a most-improved or biggest-mover category so rank has more than one axis to win.
  • Give them a rival, a peer at a similar level, and let the two push each other.

6. Reward (extrinsic)

Definition: Motivated by tangible payoff: money, prizes, concrete things earned. Spot it by: Asks about comp mechanics in detail. Talks about what they're saving or buying toward. Motivated more by SPIFF structure than by praise. What lights it up: Clear, fast, tangible payoff tied directly to the specific behavior you want more of. What kills it: Vague or delayed payout. Incentive structures that change without notice. Being told "the recognition should be enough." Manager Action Bank:

  • Run short-cycle SPIFFs (this week, not this quarter) for reward-driven reps, the payoff needs to feel close.
  • Be concrete about what a specific action is worth before they do it, not after.
  • Never substitute praise for a promised payout: it reads as a downgrade, not a bonus.

5-Question Self-Report Cross-Check

Have each rep answer these, then compare against your observed scores, big gaps are worth a direct conversation.

  1. "Think of the best week you've had here. What made it feel good: the result, the recognition, the challenge, or something else?"
  2. "Would you rather have your manager tell the whole team about a win, or tell you privately in detail what you did well?"
  3. "Do you do your best work with a clear goal and total freedom on method, or with a defined step-by-step process?"
  4. "When you picture your best possible year here, what's the first image that comes to mind: a number, a rank, a skill, or a client story?"
  5. "What's demotivated you most in a past role: being micromanaged, being ignored, losing a contest, or work that felt meaningless?"

Common Mismatch Patterns

  • Mastery-driven rep put on a pure leaderboard incentive, disengages because rank says nothing about whether they're actually improving.
  • Autonomy-driven rep given a rigid word-for-word script, either quietly stops using it or under-performs while resenting the oversight.
  • Purpose-driven rep managed only on quota language: starts to describe the job as "just sales," an early flight-risk signal.
  • Reward-driven rep given only public praise instead of the promised payout, reads generosity as a bait-and-switch.
  • Competition-driven rep left out of short-cycle contests because they "already win the long game", burns out on the long time horizon with no visible near-term wins.
