1:1 Coaching Metrics Scorecard

The specific numbers and skill signals a manager should walk into every 1:1 with, scored against a 1–4 rubric instead of eyeballed, plus a 30-minute session structure that closes the loop from one 1:1 to the next.

What's inside

  • 30-minute session structure (numbers review, deal review, skill focus, commitments)
  • Section A: activity & pipeline health leading indicators
  • Section B: 5 skill-execution criteria (discovery, objection handling, multi-threading, value articulation, negotiation/closing)
  • 1–4 rubric with a written descriptor for every score level
  • Section C: deal-specific review fields (next step, champion, mutual action plan, risk flag)
  • Section D: development loop that checks last 1:1's assigned focus and action against this period's evidence

The specific numbers and skill signals a manager should walk into every 1:1 with — scored against a rubric instead of eyeballed, so coaching quality doesn't depend on how sharp the manager happens to feel that week.

Session Structure (30 minutes)

SegmentTimeContent
Numbers review5 minWalk the metrics below, rep talks first
Deal review10 minTop 3 deals + any at-risk deal
Skill focus10 minOne named skill, tied to a real call/moment
Commitments5 minOne action the rep owns, one action the manager owns, both written down

Section A — Activity & Pipeline Health (leading indicators)

MetricThis periodTrend vs. last periodScore (1–4)
Activity volume vs. plan (calls/emails/meetings)
Pipeline coverage ratio (open pipeline ÷ quota)
Stage velocity (days in stage vs. team benchmark)
Forecast accuracy (last period's committed vs. actual)

Section B — Skill Execution (scored from a real call or deal, not self-report)

SkillEvidence (which call/deal)Score (1–4)
Discovery quality — uncovers real budget/authority/need/timeline, not surface pain
Objection handling — addresses the real objection instead of talking past it
Multi-threading — actively engaging more than one stakeholder on active deals
Value articulation — ties the solution to the buyer's own stated business outcome, not a feature list
Negotiation & closing — drives toward a clear, dated next step, not an open-ended "let me know"

Rubric — Score Definitions (apply to every row in Section B)

ScoreLabelDescription
1Not evidentSkill did not show up in the reviewed call/deal; no attempt observed
2EmergingAttempted but inconsistent — works in easy moments, breaks down under pressure or with senior stakeholders
3ProficientReliably executes this skill to the bar expected for their level/tenure
4AdvancedExecutes at a level worth recording as a peer example for the rest of the team

How to Use the Rubric Score Over Time

Track the Section B scores across consecutive 1:1s per rep. A skill stuck at 2 for three consecutive periods despite being the assigned focus area is a signal to change the method of development (shadowing, roleplay, formal training) rather than repeating the same coaching conversation a fourth time.

How to use it

Fill out Sections A-D before each 1:1 using real call/deal evidence for Section B rather than self-report, and carry Section D's commitments forward as the first line of the next 1:1.
