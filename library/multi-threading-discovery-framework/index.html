Multi-Threading Discovery Framework

A structured framework for mapping and qualifying every stakeholder in a deal, not just the friendly contact who took your call, so you stop finding out about the real decision-maker after you've already lost to them.

How to use it

Fill in the stakeholder map as you identify each of the 6 roles, score each person on the Influence × Support grid, and use the get-introduced script the first time you need to move from your original contact to someone new.

What's inside

  • The 6-role stakeholder map with what each role cares about
  • Discovery questions that surface each role
  • The Influence × Support scoring matrix
  • The stakeholder map template
  • Multi-threading trigger points
  • Red flags indicating single-threading risk
  • Get-introduced script for reaching new stakeholders

The Problem This Solves

Single-threaded deals: where your only relationship is with one friendly, engaged contact, are the single biggest predictor of a stalled or lost complex deal. The contact who took your call is rarely the only person with a vote.

Step 1: Map the 6 Roles

RoleWhat They Care About
Economic BuyerROI, budget risk, strategic fit
ChampionLooking good internally for backing this, solving the pain they personally feel
Technical EvaluatorIntegration risk, security, maintainability
End UserDaily friction, learning curve, whether it makes their job harder or easier
Procurement/LegalContract risk, vendor terms, compliance
Influencer/SkepticBeing right, protecting the org from a bad decision (or advancing their own agenda)

Step 2: Discovery Questions That Surface Each Role

  • "Besides you, who else would need to sign off before this could move forward?" (Economic Buyer + Procurement)
  • "Who on the technical side would need to weigh in on something like this?" (Technical Evaluator)
  • "Who's actually going to be using this day to day?" (End User)
  • "Has anyone tried to bring in something like this before, what happened?" (Influencer/Skeptic history)
  • "When something like this has been approved before, who's typically in the room?" (whole committee, indirectly)
  • "Who would be the hardest person in the building to convince?" (Skeptic, directly)

Step 3, Score Every Stakeholder: Influence × Support

Plot each identified stakeholder:

  • Influence: High / Medium / Low, how much their opinion moves the final decision
  • Support: Champion / Neutral / Skeptic / Blocker

Priority order for your time:

  1. High Influence + Skeptic/Blocker → address directly and early, don't avoid
  2. High Influence + Neutral → invest the most discovery time here
  3. High Influence + Champion → arm them with what they need to sell internally
  4. Low Influence + any → maintain lightweight touch, don't over-invest

Step 5, Multi-Threading Trigger Points (force a new thread when...)

  • Deal has been "in discovery" more than 30 days with only one named contact
  • Your single contact can't answer a budget or process question ("I'll have to check")
  • Deal size crosses a threshold that structurally requires committee approval
  • Your contact goes quiet for more than 5 business days at Proposal stage or later

Red Flags Indicating Single-Threading Risk

  • You've never had a live call with anyone above your contact's level
  • Your contact answers "I think so" more than once to a budget/authority/timeline question
  • No one else has been copied on email or attended a call in 3+ weeks
  • Your contact discourages you from reaching out to others ("Let me handle that internally")

Get-Introduced Script

Through your champion:

"[Champion name], to make sure this lands well with [target stakeholder], would it make sense to loop them in directly: a quick three-way call, or I can send something short they can review on their own time? Whichever's easier for you."

Direct outreach once introduced (or independently):

"Hi [Name], [Champion] suggested I loop you in given your role in [function]. I don't want to waste your time repeating everything from scratch: happy to send a 2-minute summary of where things stand, or grab 15 minutes if that's easier. What's more useful for you?"
