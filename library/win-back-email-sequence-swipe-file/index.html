Win-Back Email Sequence Swipe File

A 4-email sequence for re-engaging churned or lapsed accounts that reads as genuinely useful, not desperate - built to reopen the door without begging for it.

What's inside

  • Email 1: the no-ask check-in (pure value, zero pitch)
  • Email 2: the 'what's changed' update with concrete product/outcome proof
  • Email 3: the specific, time-bound offer
  • Email 4: the break-up email that closes the loop without burning the bridge
  • Subject lines for all 4 emails
  • Send-cadence and spacing guidance
  • Reply-handling notes for each email
  • Signals that mean 'stop the sequence and call instead'

Send cadence: Email 1 on day 0, Email 2 on day 7, Email 3 on day 16, Email 4 on day 30. Stop the sequence immediately and call if anyone replies at any point.

Email 1 - The No-Ask Check-In (Day 0)

Subject: No pitch here, just a thought on [their goal/industry]

"Hi [Name],

It's been a while since [Company] and [Product] worked together, and I wanted to reach out without an agenda. I saw [something specific - a company update, a role change, an industry shift relevant to them] and thought of your team.

No ask here - just wanted to say hi and see how things are going on your end.

[Your name]"

Reply handling: if they respond with anything beyond a one-line pleasantry, that's your opening - ask a genuine question about their current setup before mentioning your product at all.

Email 2 - What's Changed (Day 7)

Subject: What's different since you left

"Hi [Name],

Following up briefly - since we last worked together, we've shipped [1-2 concrete, relevant changes: a feature that solves a problem they specifically had, a pricing/packaging change, a proof point from a similar company]. Given what your team was running into before, I thought this was worth flagging directly.

No need to do anything with this - just wanted you to have it.

[Your name]"

Reply handling: if they ask a clarifying question about the change, answer it fully and specifically, then stop - do not pivot straight into a pitch.

Email 3 - The Specific Offer (Day 16)

Subject: An idea, if the timing's right

"Hi [Name],

I'll be direct: if it ever makes sense to pick this back up, I can put together [a specific, concrete offer - e.g., a 30-day pilot on the new feature set, your previous rate honored for one term, a fast re-onboarding with your old configuration restored]. No pressure either way - just wanted the option on the table in case the timing's better now than it was before.

Worth 15 minutes to talk through, or should I check back in a few months?

[Your name]"

Reply handling: this is the email most likely to get a response. If they say "maybe later," ask directly: "what would need to be true for the timing to be right?" and log the answer.

Email 4 - The Break-Up Email (Day 30)

Subject: Closing the loop

"Hi [Name],

I don't want to keep filling your inbox, so this is my last note for now. If the timing's ever right to revisit [Product], my door's open - just reply any time, no need to wait for an email from me.

In the meantime, wishing your team well with [their current priority, if known].

[Your name]"

Reply handling: break-up emails convert more often than expected because they remove pressure. If there's no reply, move the account to a quarterly nurture cadence rather than continuing weekly outreach.

Stop-The-Sequence Signals

  • Any reply at all, even a brush-off - switch to 1:1 conversation, not more emails
  • News that they've signed with a direct competitor - shift to a 6-month check-back instead of continuing
  • A bounce or "no longer at this company" auto-reply - re-research the new champion before sending anything else

How to use it

Load the four emails into your sequencing tool on the day-0/7/16/30 cadence, personalize the bracketed sections per account before sending, and pull anyone who replies out of the automated sequence into a live conversation immediately.
