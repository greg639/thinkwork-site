Structured questions for hiring your next sales leader, with what a strong answer sounds like versus a weak one — so every interviewer on the panel is calibrated to the same bar, not their own gut feel.

Panel structure recommendation

  • CEO/CRO: Strategic GTM judgment + culture fit
  • Peer function leader (Marketing/CS/Product): Cross-functional influence
  • A current frontline manager or senior rep: Coaching/talent evaluation, and "would I want to work for this person"
  • Finance/RevOps: Forecasting rigor + comp plan literacy
  • Reference calls: Save for after final round — see verification questions at the end, and always ask for at least one reference not on the candidate's provided list

1. Strategic / GTM Judgment

Q: Walk me through a GTM motion you inherited that was wrong for the stage or market the company was in. What did you see, and what did you change?

  • Strong answer sounds like: Names a specific structural mismatch (e.g., "we were running enterprise-style multi-touch sales cycles on $8K ACV deals"), explains the diagnostic signal that revealed it (long sales cycles, poor rep productivity, high CAC), and describes a specific structural change with a before/after metric.
  • Weak answer sounds like: A generic story about "improving processes" with no named mechanism, no metric, or credits the change to hiring "better people" rather than a structural fix.

Q: What's a GTM decision you made that didn't work, and what did you do when you realized it?

  • Strong: Owns the miss specifically, describes the leading indicator that should have caught it sooner, and names a process change they made afterward to catch it earlier next time.
  • Weak: Deflects to market conditions, a bad hire, or "leadership above me changed direction" with no ownership of their own read.

2. Talent — Hiring, Coaching, and Managing Out

Q: Tell me about a rep you inherited who was underperforming. Walk me through your diagnosis and what you did.

  • Strong: Describes a specific diagnostic process (call reviews, pipeline audit, skill assessment against a competency framework) before jumping to a conclusion; distinguishes a skill gap from a fit/effort problem; gives a concrete outcome, including if the outcome was managing the person out — done fairly and on a documented timeline.
  • Weak: "I just worked with them more" with no specifics, or immediately jumps to "I let them go" with no diagnostic step in between (signals a coach-last, cut-first instinct — or the opposite: kept an underperformer on far too long with no plan).

Q: Describe your best hire and your worst hire as a sales manager or leader. What did you learn from each?

  • Strong: Specific, named traits/signals for both — and the worst hire answer includes what changed in their interview process afterward as a result.
  • Weak: The "worst hire" story blames the candidate entirely with no reflection on what the leader missed in the process.

Q: How do you personally calibrate whether a manager on your team is actually coaching, versus just managing a spreadsheet?

  • Strong: Names concrete artifacts they check — sits in on 1:1s or call reviews periodically, looks for specific/behavioral feedback in coaching notes, checks whether skill gaps identified 90 days ago actually closed.
  • Weak: "I trust my managers" or "I look at their team's numbers" as the sole proxy — number performance alone doesn't tell you if coaching is happening or just luck/market tailwind.

3. Forecasting Rigor & Operating Cadence

Q: Walk me through your forecasting methodology — how do you get from rep-level commit to a number you'd defend to the board.

  • Strong: Describes a specific method (weighted pipeline by stage, historical conversion-rate calibration, commit/best-case/pipeline categories), how they stress-test rep-reported commits against their own historical accuracy, and how far out they forecast with confidence vs. caveat.
  • Weak: "I roll up what my reps tell me" with no independent calibration or sanity-check layer.

Q: Tell me about a quarter you missed the number badly. When did you know, and how did you communicate it upward?

  • Strong: Names the specific week/signal they knew it was slipping, and describes communicating early and precisely (not just at quarter close) with a clear reforecast and a plan.
  • Weak: Signals they only knew at the very end, or that they "stayed optimistic" too long — a pattern that will repeat with your board.

4. Cross-Functional Influence

Q: Tell me about a time Marketing, Product, or CS disagreed with a call you were making. How did you resolve it?

  • Strong: Describes genuinely engaging with the other function's data/perspective, not just "winning the argument"; names a specific compromise or a case where they changed their position.
  • Weak: Every story ends with "and I convinced them" — signals someone who runs over peer functions rather than partnering with them.

5. Comp Plan & Incentive Design Literacy

Q: Design a comp plan on the spot for a team selling a $40K ACV product with a 4-month sales cycle and a strategic push toward multi-year contracts this year. Walk me through your structure and why.

  • Strong: Talks through base/OTE split appropriate to deal complexity, ties an accelerator or SPIF specifically to the multi-year push (not just overall revenue), and flags the tradeoff/risk of whatever structure they propose (e.g., "a multi-year SPIF risks reps pushing multi-year terms on deals that don't need them — I'd cap it or require a discount ceiling alongside it").
  • Weak: Recites a generic plan structure with no connection to the specific strategic push mentioned in the question, or ignores the tradeoffs and risks.

6. Track Record Verification (probe past the resume bullet)

Q: Your resume says you grew the team from $X to $Y ARR. What was already in motion before you started, and what specifically changed after you arrived?

  • Strong: Distinguishes tailwind/market timing/product changes from their own contribution explicitly; can point to specific mechanisms they introduced.
  • Weak: Takes full, undifferentiated credit for the growth number with no separation of causes.

Q: If I called your last 3 direct reports right now, what would they say your biggest blind spot is?

  • Strong: Names something specific and plausible, ideally something they've actively worked on since; doesn't deflect into a humble-brag ("I care too much").
  • Weak: Can't name anything, or gives an answer that's actually a strength in disguise.

7. Culture / Values Fit

Q: What's a company value or leadership behavior you'd refuse to compromise on, even under quota pressure?

  • Strong: Specific and concrete (e.g., "I won't let a rep misrepresent a product capability to close a deal, even in Q4"), with a story attached where they actually held the line.
  • Weak: Generic values-speak with no example of it costing them anything.

Reference-check questions (ask these, not "would you recommend them")

  • "What was their team's attrition rate under them, and do you know why the people who left, left?"
  • "Tell me about a manager on their team who wasn't performing — what did they do?"
  • "What's one thing you wish they'd done differently?"
  • "Would you personally follow them to their next company? Why or why not?"

How to use it

Assign question categories across your panel per the suggested structure, score each candidate on the scorecard template, and save the reference-check questions for after the final round.
