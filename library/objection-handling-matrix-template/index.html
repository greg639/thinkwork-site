Objection Handling Matrix Template

A fill-in spreadsheet template that maps every objection your team hears to its category, root cause, and one agreed-upon response — so reps stop freelancing answers in the moment.

What's inside

  • A ready-to-fill matrix with 8 columns: objection, category, root cause, response, proof point, escalation trigger, owner, review date
  • 3 fully worked example rows to model from (price, status-quo, procrastination)
  • A 5-step workshop process for populating it as a team from real call recordings
  • Field-by-field definitions so every rep fills it in consistently
  • Guidance on quarterly review cadence and row ownership
  • Ties directly to the 7 Objection Types taxonomy for consistent categorization

A living document your team fills in together so every rep hears the same "approved" response to the same objection — instead of freelancing in the moment. Run this as a 90-minute team workshop: pull real objections from call recordings, sort them, and assign an approved response and owner to each.

How to Run the Workshop

  1. Pull 15-20 verbatim objections from recent call recordings or CRM notes (not paraphrased — actual words used).
  2. Sort each into one of the 7 objection categories (see The 7 Objection Types Every Rep Must Recognize framework: Price, Timing, Authority, Need, Trust, Competition, Procrastination).
  3. For each, debate and agree on ONE approved response as a team — not five competing versions.
  4. Assign an owner responsible for updating that row as the market/product changes.
  5. Review and refresh the matrix quarterly, or immediately after a new competitor move or pricing change.

Field Definitions

  • Objection (verbatim): the exact words a prospect used — not your interpretation of what they meant.
  • Category: one of the 7 objection types (Price, Timing, Authority, Need, Trust, Competition, Procrastination).
  • Root Cause: the underlying driver behind the surface phrasing — this is what the response should actually target.
  • Approved Response: the single word-track the whole team uses, agreed on together — not five competing versions.
  • Proof Point to Attach: the specific asset (case study, calculator, battlecard, reference) that backs up the response.
  • Escalation Trigger: the condition under which a rep should loop in a manager or sales engineer rather than handle it solo.
  • Owner: whoever is accountable for keeping that row accurate as the market changes.
  • Last Reviewed: date of last update — stale rows get flagged at the next quarterly refresh.

How to use it

Run the included 90-minute workshop process with your team to populate the matrix from real call recordings, assign an owner to each row, and refresh it quarterly.
