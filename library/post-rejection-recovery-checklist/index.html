Post-Rejection Recovery Checklist

A 10-minute, minute-by-minute ritual to run within an hour of losing a deal, so the loss stays contained to itself instead of leaking into your tone, pacing, and confidence on the next call.

How to use it

Print or pin this somewhere you'll actually see it right after a "no": run it minute-by-minute with a timer the moment you hang up or get the rejection email, before your next call or task.

What's inside

  • The 90-second physiological reset (box-breathing sequence) before you touch the CRM
  • The "say it out loud" venting step that discharges the emotional charge
  • The 3-line fact-vs-story debrief format
  • The one-line CRM loss-reason entry template
  • The "contamination check" question to ask before your next call
  • The exact re-entry line to say out loud before you dial again
  • The 24-hour rule for not making big decisions right after a loss
  • The 3 triggers that mean you should skip this and go straight to your manager

A 10-minute reset to run within one hour of losing a deal

Why this exists: The loss itself costs you one deal. An un-processed loss costs you the next three calls, because it sits in your tone, your pacing, and your willingness to ask for the close. This checklist is built to be run alone, at your desk, with a timer. Do not skip steps to save time, the sequence matters more than the speed.

Rule 0: Set a 10-minute timer before you start. If you don't have 10 minutes right now, put a placeholder in your calendar for the next gap and do NOT take your next call until you've run this.

Minutes 0–2: Discharge (before you touch anything)

  • Stand up. Leave your desk for 60 seconds: hallway, stairwell, outside if you can.
  • Do 8 slow breaths: inhale 4 counts, hold 4, exhale 6. (Exhale longer than inhale: that's what down-regulates the stress response, not the breathing itself.)
  • Say the loss out loud, once, in the most unfiltered version you'd never put in Slack. ("They strung me along for six weeks and picked the incumbent.") Get the emotional charge out of your body before you try to think straight about it.

Minutes 2–5: Debrief (fact vs. story)

Answer these three questions in writing: one line each, no paragraphs:

  • What actually happened? (Facts only: "Champion went dark after the pricing call, VP signed with incumbent 3 weeks later.")
  • What story am I telling myself about it? ("I'm losing my edge / they never wanted us / I should have discounted.")
  • Is the story true, or is it just loud? Cross-check the story against the facts line. Most post-loss stories fail this test immediately once they're sitting next to the facts.

Minutes 5–7: Close the loop

  • Write the one-line CRM loss reason using this template (not a paragraph: one line, factual, useful to future-you):

LOST: [stage]: [primary reason]: [one thing I'd do differently] Example: LOST: proposal: budget moved to incumbent renewal: should have qualified renewal date in discovery

  • Flag anything that needs a human follow-up (a thank-you note to the champion, a note to your manager about a pattern), but don't send it yet. Draft only. Send after the 24-hour flag below.

Minutes 7–9: The contamination check

  • Ask yourself directly: "If my next prospect could feel my mood right now, what would they feel?" If the honest answer is anything other than "ready and neutral," you are not done, go back to Minutes 0–2.
  • Say your re-entry line out loud, once: "That deal is closed. This one hasn't started yet." It sounds trivial. Run this checklist for a month and notice it isn't.

Minute 9–10: Re-entry check

  • Physical reset: shoulders down, one more slow exhale, stand before you sit back down.
  • Look at your next call's account notes for 60 seconds before you dial, this pulls your attention forward onto a real person instead of backward onto the loss.

The 24-hour rule

  • Do not make any big decision today because of this loss: no "I'm going to stop prospecting this segment," no "I need to change my whole pitch." Big conclusions drawn inside 24 hours of a loss are almost always the story talking, not the facts. Revisit tomorrow with a clear head; if the conclusion still holds, act on it then.

When to skip this and go straight to your manager

Run this checklist solo for a normal loss. Skip straight to a 1:1 instead if:

  • This is the 3rd loss this week with the same loss reason (pattern, not a one-off)
  • The story from Minutes 2–5 won't quiet down even after two runs of this checklist
  • The loss involved something outside your control that's eating at you (pricing, product gap, internal handoff)

A one-off loss needs 10 minutes. A pattern needs a conversation. Know which one you're in before you decide to just push through.
