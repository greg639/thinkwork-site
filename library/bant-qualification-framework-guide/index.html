BANT Qualification Framework Guide

The classic Budget-Authority-Need-Timeline model broken into real questions and dialogue for each pillar, plus a scoring grid so you know exactly when to pursue, nurture, or walk away.

  • Authority pillar — 5 questions that map the buying committee and expose single-threaded deals
  • Need pillar — 5 questions that separate a funded, painful problem from a nice-to-have
  • Timeline pillar — 5 questions that test for a real compelling event vs. fake urgency
  • BANT scoring grid: 0/1/2 per pillar with pursue / nurture / disqualify thresholds
  • Rebuttal lines for prospects who dodge a BANT question

When to use BANT

BANT (Budget, Authority, Need, Timeline) is a fast go/no-go filter, built for transactional and mid-market deals with short cycles. It is not built for multi-stakeholder enterprise deals — use MEDDPICC there. Never open a call by asking about budget directly; work it in through the dialogue below.

Pillar 1 — Budget

Goal: confirm money is (or can be) allocated, not just that they like the product.

  • What's the current cost of not solving this — in hours, dollars, or lost deals?
  • Have you set aside spend for this category this year, or would this be a new line item?
  • Roughly, what would you expect to invest to fix this properly?
  • Who signs off once a number is on the table?
  • If the ROI is clear, is budget the blocker, or is it something else?

Pillar 2 — Authority

Goal: find every person who can say yes and every person who can say no.

  • Walk me through who else touches a decision like this.
  • Who has final sign-off, and who needs to be comfortable before it gets there?
  • Has a similar purchase happened here before — who ran that process?
  • If we get everyone else excited, who could still say no?
  • Would it help to get [economic buyer] in the next call?

Pillar 3 — Need

Goal: confirm a funded, painful problem — not a wish list item.

  • What happens if this stays unsolved for another two quarters?
  • What have you already tried, and why didn't it stick?
  • Who else in the business feels this pain besides you?
  • On a 1–10, how painful is this today — and what makes it a 10, not a 6?
  • What does success look like in concrete numbers?

Pillar 4 — Timeline

Goal: expose a real compelling event, not a soft 'sometime this year.'

  • What's driving the timing — a renewal, a board date, a budget cycle, a hire?
  • What happens on your end if this slips a quarter?
  • Is there a date something else is contingent on this decision?
  • What would need to be true for you to have a vendor picked by [date]?
  • Who set that date, and how firm is it really?

Scoring grid

Pillar0 pts1 pt2 pts
Budgetnone identifiedplausible, unconfirmedconfirmed & allocated
Authorityunknownidentified, not engagedengaged directly
Needvaguereal but not costedcosted & urgent
Timelinenonesoft datecompelling event with date

7–8 pts: pursue actively. 4–6 pts: nurture, re-qualify in 30 days. 0–3 pts: disqualify or park.

Common mistakes

  1. Asking budget first — ask Need first, Budget follows naturally.
  2. Treating one 'yes' as full Authority — always ask 'who else.'
  3. Accepting a soft date as Timeline — always ask what forces that date.

How to use it

Run the four pillars in order during discovery, score the deal against the grid at the end of the call, and route it pursue/nurture/disqualify before it enters your pipeline stage.
