Sales Proposal Template

A buyer-tested proposal structure reps customize deal-by-deal, built to be skimmed by an executive in 90 seconds and defended in detail by a champion afterward.

How to use it

Copy the whole document, fill Sections 1-2 straight from your discovery-call notes before adding pricing, then customize the investment tiers and timeline per deal rather than rebuilding the structure each time.

What's inside

  • Cover page fields
  • Executive summary formula
  • Understanding Your Situation section with a discovery-quote slot
  • Proposed solution table with explicit out-of-scope line
  • Expected outcomes/ROI table with a methodology line for defensibility
  • Implementation timeline table
  • Good/better/best pricing tier table
  • Why-us proof section (kept deliberately short)
  • Terms & assumptions section
  • Next-steps checklist with signature call to action

A buyer-tested structure your reps customize deal-by-deal, built to be skimmed by an executive in 90 seconds and defended in detail by a champion in a follow-up meeting. Replace every bracketed field; delete guidance notes (in italics) before sending.

Cover Page

[Your Company Logo]

Proposal for [Prospect Company Name]

[One-line description of the engagement, e.g., "A Plan to Reduce Onboarding Time by 30% in Two Quarters"]

Prepared for: [Champion Name, Title] and [Economic Buyer Name, Title] Prepared by: [Your Name, Title] · [Your Email] · [Your Phone] Date: [Date] · Valid until: [Date + 30 days]

1. Executive Summary

3-5 sentences. Written so a busy exec who reads nothing else still understands the deal.

[Prospect Company] is facing [core business challenge, in their language]. This proposal outlines how [Your Company] addresses that through [one-sentence solution summary], resulting in [headline expected outcome, a number if you have one]. Total investment: [price range or tier summary]. We recommend starting [target start date] to [reason for the timing: a business trigger, not a sales deadline].

2. Understanding Your Situation

Prove you listened. Pull direct language from discovery calls.

  • Current state: [What they do today, and where it breaks down]
  • Cost of the status quo: [Lost revenue, wasted hours, churn risk, compliance exposure: quantify if possible]
  • What we heard from your team:
"[Direct quote from a discovery call, attributed if they're comfortable being quoted internally]"
  • Why now: [The specific trigger: a deadline, a new hire, a board commitment, a competitive threat]

Out of scope (be explicit, prevents scope-creep disputes later): [What this proposal does NOT include]

7. Why [Your Company]

Proof, not adjectives. 2-3 items max: this section should be the shortest, not the longest.

  • Similar customer: [Name/logo if permitted], [1-sentence result achieved]. Full case study: [link]
  • Relevant credential/differentiator: [whatever is specifically relevant to this prospect's stated concern]
  • Reference available: [offer a live reference call with a named similar customer]

8. Terms & Assumptions

  • This proposal assumes [key assumption, e.g., "access to [system] will be provided by [date]"]
  • Pricing valid through [date]; subject to change after
  • [Any other assumption that, if wrong, changes scope or price]
  • Standard terms: [link to or attach MSA/DPA if applicable]

9. Next Steps

  1. [Prospect] confirms [decision/tier] by [date]
  2. [Your Company] sends order form / contract for signature
  3. Kickoff call scheduled for [target date]

Questions? [Your Name] · [Email] · [Phone], happy to walk through any section live.

How to use this template

Fill Sections 1-2 from your discovery notes before touching pricing, a proposal that leads with price before proving you understood the problem reads as generic. Keep Section 7 short; over-selling credentials there signals you don't have enough proof in Sections 2 and 4.
