Pre-Boarding Email Sequence Templates

Five ready-to-send emails covering the gap between a signed offer and day one, so new hires arrive already oriented, connected to their team, and clear on what to expect, instead of walking in cold.

How to use it

Copy each email into your ATS or email client, fill in the bracketed fields once per hire, and schedule them to fire automatically at the listed intervals relative to the signed start date.

What's inside

  • Email 1, Welcome & Excitement (send same day offer is signed)
  • Email 2, Paperwork & Logistics (send 10–14 days before start)
  • Email 3, Meet Your Team (send 5–7 days before start)
  • Email 4, What to Expect Week One (send 2–3 days before start)
  • Email 5, See You Tomorrow (send the evening before start)
  • Manager send-checklist mapping each email to exact timing and sender
  • Fill-in fields for buddy assignment, team intros, and day-one logistics

Why this sequence exists

The gap between "offer signed" and "day one" is where new hires quietly cool off, get poached by counter-offers, or just show up nervous and disoriented. Five short, well-timed emails close that gap. Send exactly these five, at exactly these intervals, copy-paste and fill the brackets.

Email 1: Welcome & Excitement

Send: Same day the offer is signed From: Hiring manager Subject: Welcome to the team, [First Name]!

Hi [First Name],

I just saw the offer come back signed and wanted to be the first to say it properly: welcome to [Company Name]. I'm genuinely glad you're joining us.

A few things before we dive into logistics (you'll get those from [HR/People Ops contact] separately):

  • Your start date is [Start Date], and you'll be working out of [Location / Remote setup].
  • You'll be joining the [Team Name] team, reporting to me.
  • Over the next [X weeks], you'll hear from us a handful of times: nothing to action, just context so day one feels familiar instead of overwhelming.

If anything comes up between now and then: a question, a nerve, a "wait, what do I wear", just reply to this email directly. I mean that.

Looking forward to it, [Manager Name] [Title] · [Company Name]

Email 2: Paperwork & Logistics

Send: 10–14 days before start date From: HR / People Ops Subject: A few things to sort before [Start Date]

Hi [First Name],

Excited for you to join us on [Start Date]! A short, practical email:

Before you start, please:

  • Complete [background check / right-to-work / tax forms] via [link]
  • Return signed [contract / NDA / benefits enrollment] by [date]
  • Set up direct deposit: [link/instructions]
  • Let us know any equipment preferences (laptop OS, monitor, etc.)

Logistics for day one:

  • Where: [Office address / remote login instructions]
  • When: Arrive by [time]. First session starts at [time].
  • Who to ask for: [Reception contact / IT contact]
  • Parking / transit note: [detail]
  • Dress: [guidance]

Your equipment will ship / be waiting for you by [date]. If anything above is unclear or you hit a snag, reply here or call [phone number], a real person will pick up.

Talk soon, [HR Contact Name]

Email 3: Meet Your Team

Send: 5–7 days before start date From: Hiring manager Subject: Meet the people you'll be working with

Hi [First Name],

One week to go. Wanted to put some names to faces before you arrive, since walking into a room of strangers on day one is the worst part of any new job.

Your manager: Me, [Manager Name]. [One line on background/style.]

Your onboarding buddy: [Buddy Name], [Role]. They've been at [Company] for [X time] and will be your go-to for the "dumb questions" (there are no dumb questions) during your first month. They'll reach out separately to introduce themselves before you start.

Your immediate team:

  • [Name]: [Role]: [one line: what they do / fun fact]
  • [Name]: [Role]: [one line]
  • [Name]: [Role]: [one line]

Who you'll meet in week one beyond the team: [Skip-level manager], [key cross-functional partner], [enablement/L&D contact].

We'll do proper introductions in person, but figured a name and a face beforehand takes the edge off.

[Manager Name]

Email 4: What to Expect Week One

Send: 2–3 days before start date From: Hiring manager or enablement lead Subject: Your first week, mapped out

Hi [First Name],

Here's the actual shape of your first week, so there's no mystery Monday morning.

Day 1: Orientation, equipment setup, team welcome [lunch/coffee], intro to [CRM/core tools]. Day 2–3: Product/domain fundamentals with [trainer/enablement contact]. Shadow [X] live calls with [rep name(s)]. Day 4–5: [Role-specific ramp activity, e.g., first practice calls, first accounts assigned, certification quiz].

What we do NOT expect from you in week one: results, quota progress, or having it figured out. Week one is about orientation and absorption, that's the whole job.

One thing to bring: questions. Write down anything that confuses you; we'll cover it in your first 1:1 on [date].

See you Monday, [Manager Name]

Email 5: See You Tomorrow

Send: Evening before start date From: Hiring manager Subject: See you tomorrow

Hi [First Name],

Quick one, tomorrow's the day.

  • Arrive: [time] at [location] / Log in: [time] via [link]
  • Ask for: [contact name] at the door / on the call
  • Bring: [ID for badge / laptop charger / nothing, we've got you]

[Buddy Name] will meet you first thing. I'll see you at [specific time/event, e.g., "10am team standup"].

Genuinely glad you're starting tomorrow. Sleep well.

[Manager Name]
