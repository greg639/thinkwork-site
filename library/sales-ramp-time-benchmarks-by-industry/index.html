Sales Ramp-Time Benchmarks by Industry

Typical full-ramp ranges across seven sales segments — from transactional SaaS to field sales — so you know whether your new rep's timeline is normal or a sign the onboarding program itself is broken.

What's inside

  • Benchmark table across 7 segments (SaaS SMB, SaaS Mid-Market, SaaS Enterprise, Financial Services, Field Sales/Industrial, Professional Services, Tech Hardware/Channel)
  • Primary ramp bottleneck named per segment
  • 'Where to focus first' recommendation per segment
  • 4-step how-to-use-this protocol
  • 3 red flags that a slow timeline is structural, not individual
  • What the fast end of your range typically requires (3 criteria)

Typical full-ramp ranges commonly reported across sales enablement benchmarking studies (Bridge Group-style SDR/AE research, CSO Insights/Sales Mastery-style ramp studies, and vendor-reported onboarding data). Use these as a sanity check on your own timeline, not a hard target — your product complexity and deal cycle matter more than your industry label.

How to use this

  1. Find your segment (or the closest match) and note the range
  2. Compare against your own Day-30/60/90 Skill Gap Diagnostic and Call Review Rubric trend data — the benchmark tells you if your timeline is normal; your own data tells you why if it isn't
  3. If you're materially slower than the range for your segment, the gap is more likely structural (no formal onboarding, unclear ICP, tech stack friction) than an individual rep problem — audit the program before individual reps
  4. If you're materially faster, verify it's real ramp (hitting full quota sustainably) and not an early-quarter spike that collapses later

Red flags that your timeline isn't "slow," it's broken

  • No two reps ramp in a similar timeframe — high variance signals no repeatable process, not individual talent variance
  • Ramp time has been quietly increasing cohort over cohort — usually means product/process complexity has grown but onboarding hasn't been updated
  • Reps hit "ramped" status on paper (tenure) without hitting it on activity (quota attainment) — a calendar-based ramp definition, not a competency-based one

What "on the fast end" of your range usually requires

  • A written, repeatable curriculum (not tribal knowledge from whichever manager they got)
  • A defined certification gate before full independent quota (see Sales Onboarding Curriculum Toolkit)
  • Weekly call review from day 1, not just at 90-day review time

How to use it

Find your closest segment, compare your own ramp data against the range, and if you're running slower than benchmark, audit the onboarding program itself before assuming it's an individual rep problem.
