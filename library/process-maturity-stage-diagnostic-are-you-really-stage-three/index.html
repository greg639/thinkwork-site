Process Maturity Stage Diagnostic: Are You Really at Stage Three?

Most sales orgs self-rate at Stage Three (Managed) when observable manager behaviour places them firmly at Stage Two (Documented). This scorecard tests that gap using a single diagnostic question, a manager response rubric, and a set of behavioural checks that artefacts alone cannot fake.

How to use it

Run this diagnostic with your sales leadership team before any methodology rollout or QBR planning cycle. Score three dimensions independently, then map to the output table for your confirmed stage and the single next action. Revisit every quarter: stage drift is real, and most orgs slide back under hiring or pipeline pressure.

What's inside

  • The core diagnostic test: one question that separates Stage Two from Stage Three instantly
  • Manager response rubric scored 1-3, with word-for-word example answers at each level
  • Worked example: a Stage Two vs. Stage Three manager response to the same deal scenario
  • Yes/no artefact checklist to anchor Stage One and Stage Two objectively
  • Observable behaviour checklist to confirm Stage Three (cannot be gamed by documentation)

  • Scored output table mapping total score to confirmed maturity stage
  • Single next action for each stage, named and specific
  • Named failure modes for orgs that misclassify themselves

Why this exists

Most sales organisations describe themselves as "Stage Three: Managed." Ask their managers to describe a rep's skill gap on a live deal and you get a pipeline update. That gap between self-assessment and observable behaviour is the single most expensive misdiagnosis in sales operations.

This scorecard operationalises one test: can your managers name a competency-level skill gap for a named rep, not a deal risk? Everything else follows from that.

The Four Stages (reference definitions)

StageLabelPlain description
1Ad HocNo defined process. Each rep sells their way. Manager intuition drives coaching.
2DocumentedA process exists and is named (MEDDIC, SPIN, Challenger, bespoke). CRM fields reflect it. Artefacts are present. Manager reviews deals against the process.
3ManagedManagers coach to competency gaps, not just deal gaps. They can name what a rep cannot do yet, not just what the deal is missing.
4OptimisingCompetency data feeds hiring, onboarding and territory design. The process improves itself through structured feedback loops.

The most common misclassification: organisations at Stage Two with a completed methodology playbook and populated CRM fields call themselves Stage Three. The artefacts are real. The coaching behaviour has not changed.

The Core Diagnostic Test

Ask any frontline sales manager this question, cold, about a named rep on a live deal:

"Tell me about [Rep name]'s last deal review. What skill gap showed up, and what are you doing about it?"

Score the response using the rubric below.

Manager Response Rubric (1-3 scale)

Score 1: Deal description, no diagnosis

The manager describes the deal. They may reference a methodology field ("we haven't confirmed economic buyer") but cannot separate the deal gap from a rep skill gap. The problem lives in the deal, not in the rep.

Example answer at Score 1:

"Sarah's working a mid-market account in financial services. The economic buyer hasn't been confirmed and the champion is weak. We're going back in next week to try and get an exec meeting."

What is missing: no mention of why Sarah has not confirmed the economic buyer across multiple deals, no coaching action tied to a repeatable behaviour.

Score 2: Partial diagnosis, artefact-anchored

The manager names a process step the rep is weak on but frames it as a deal fix, not a skill development action. Coaching is reactive and deal-specific.

Example answer at Score 2:

"Sarah struggles to get above the champion. On this deal and the last one she's stayed at mid-level. I've been reminding her to ask for intros upward at each review."

What is missing: no named competency, no structured development plan, no pattern tracked across time. "Reminding" is not coaching.

Score 3: Competency-level diagnosis with a development action

The manager names a transferable skill gap, explains why they believe it is a gap (evidence across deals), and describes a specific coaching action that is not deal rescue.

Example answer at Score 3:

"Sarah has a strong technical pitch but she defaults to pitching when she feels pressure. That means she never gets to provoke a business-level conversation. I've seen it on three deals this quarter. We're doing a 20-minute role-play each week where I cut her off mid-pitch and make her pivot to a financial impact question. She knows about it, she's bought in."

What is present: named competency (business-level conversation, pitching avoidance), evidence pattern (three deals), structured non-deal coaching action, rep awareness.

Worked Example: Same Deal, Two Managers

Scenario: Rep has a Q3 target deal stalled after a demo. Economic buyer has not been engaged. Second demo requested by champion.

Stage Two manager response:

"We need to get the CFO involved before we do another demo. I've told Jamie to hold on scheduling until we get that meeting locked. Deal might slip to Q4."

This is deal triage. The manager is managing the pipeline, not developing Jamie.

Stage Three manager response:

"Jamie consistently confuses champion enthusiasm with organisational buy-in. He assumes the champion will pull the deal up. I've coached this three times now. This week we're working on how to qualify multi-stakeholder commitment before investing in a second demo. The deal itself, I've told him to pause it and explain why to the champion directly."

The manager has separated the deal action (pause, explain) from the development action (qualify multi-stakeholder commitment). Both are explicit and distinct.

Section 1: Artefact Checklist (Stage One and Two anchors)

These are yes/no. They confirm Stage One or Two. Ticking all of them does NOT confirm Stage Three.

  • 3-6 Yes: Stage Two (Documented). Stop here if Section 2 scores below threshold.

Named Failure Modes

"We have a scorecard so we must be Stage Three." Scorecards are artefacts. Stage Three is defined by what managers say in an unscripted moment, not what they fill in.

"Our reps know the methodology." Knowledge is Stage Two. Application under pressure is Stage Three. The test is whether managers can diagnose why a rep does not apply it, not whether the rep passed the certification.

"Our win rate improved after we rolled out the methodology." Win rate improvement often lags adoption by two quarters and may reflect market conditions, headcount or territory changes. It is not diagnostic of maturity stage.

"We do monthly pipeline reviews against MEDDIC." Pipeline reviews are deal triage. Coaching to competency requires a separate conversation with a separate agenda.

When not to use this diagnostic

Do not run this immediately after a methodology launch. Give the manager population at least one full quarter of deal reviews before scoring observable behaviour. Scoring too early produces artificially low results and demoralises managers who are genuinely trying to change.
