Customer Success Tech Stack Directory

A categorized directory of the health-scoring, QBR, and renewal-automation tools worth evaluating in 2026 — organized by job-to-be-done with what to evaluate vendors on.

What's inside

  • Six categories covering the full CS tech landscape: health scoring, QBR automation, renewal/contract automation, digital adoption, VoC/surveys, and community/education
  • What each category actually solves and what to evaluate vendors on
  • A representative vendor shortlist per category to start your evaluation
  • A 5-item buying checklist to run before any vendor demo

How to use this directory

This is organized by job-to-be-done, not by vendor size or popularity. For each category: what it solves, what to look for when evaluating, and representative vendors worth putting on a shortlist. Vendor landscapes shift fast — treat the names below as a starting shortlist for your own evaluation, not an endorsement, and verify current feature sets/pricing directly before buying.

1. Health Scoring & Core CS Platforms

Solves: Centralizing usage, sentiment, and engagement data into a single account health signal; playbook automation; renewal forecasting. Evaluate for: Native integrations with your product analytics and billing system, how configurable the health-score model is, and whether it supports both AM-led and digital-led (low-touch) motions. Shortlist: Gainsight, Totango, ChurnZero, Vitally, Catalyst, ClientSuccess, Planhat.

2. QBR & Reporting Automation

Solves: Turning raw usage/health data into a presentable QBR deck or dashboard without hours of manual slide-building. Evaluate for: Whether it auto-populates from your CS platform/data warehouse, customization for different account tiers, and whether it exports a client-ready deck vs. an internal-only dashboard. Shortlist: Most core CS platforms above now include QBR/reporting modules natively (Gainsight, ClientSuccess); for BI-style rollups, dashboards built on your data warehouse (e.g., with Databox or a similar BI layer) are common alternatives for leadership reporting.

3. Renewal & Contract Automation

Solves: Tracking renewal dates, automating renewal notices, and managing contract terms/redlines without manual spreadsheet chasing. Evaluate for: Integration with your CRM and billing system, auto-renewal notice workflows, and e-signature/redlining built in vs. bolted on. Shortlist: Ironclad, DocuSign CLM, Concord, Dock, and subscription-billing platforms like Chargebee/Recurly/Maxio for usage-based or subscription renewal automation.

4. Digital Adoption & Onboarding

Solves: In-product guidance, milestone tracking, and structured onboarding project management so time-to-value doesn't depend entirely on a human AM's calendar. Evaluate for: Whether it's in-product guidance (walkthroughs/tooltips) vs. onboarding project management (task tracking with the customer), and how easily non-engineers can build/update flows. Shortlist: Pendo, WalkMe, Whatfix (in-product guidance); Rocketlane, GuideCX, Arrows (customer-facing onboarding project management).

5. Voice of Customer & Surveys

Solves: Structured, repeatable sentiment capture (NPS, CSAT, CES) instead of relying on anecdote from whichever AM happens to ask. Evaluate for: In-product vs. email survey delivery, response-rate benchmarks for your industry, and closed-loop follow-up workflows (does a detractor response actually route to a human?). Shortlist: Delighted, Wootric/InMoment, Qualtrics, SurveyMonkey (for lighter-weight needs).

6. Customer Education & Community

Solves: Scaling product knowledge and peer support beyond 1:1 AM time — critical for long-tail/SMB accounts where high-touch isn't economical. Evaluate for: Whether it's a standalone LMS-style academy vs. an embedded community platform, and whether completion/engagement data feeds back into your health score. Shortlist: Skilljar, Thought Industries (education/academy); Higher Logic, Circle, inSided (community).

Buying checklist before you commit to any category

  • Does it integrate natively with your product usage data, or will you need custom engineering to pipe data in?
  • Does the vendor's own customer success/support experience reflect what you'd want your own customers to feel? (Test this during the sales process.)
  • What's the actual implementation timeline, not the one in the pitch deck — ask for 2–3 reference customers who went live in the last 6 months.
  • Is pricing per-seat, per-account, or usage-based, and does that align with how your CS team is structured today and in 2 years?
  • Can you export your data cleanly if you switch platforms later? (Ask this explicitly — it's the question vendors answer least honestly.)

How to use it

Start with the category matching your single biggest gap (usually health scoring if you don't have it yet), shortlist 2–3 vendors from the list, and run them through the buying checklist before any demo — it keeps the evaluation grounded in your integration reality, not the vendor's pitch.
