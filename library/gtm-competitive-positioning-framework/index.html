GTM Competitive Positioning Framework

A five-step framework for converting raw competitive intel into a wedge, proof point, and objection reframe reps can actually use mid-deal: replacing the static battlecard nobody reads with a living, in-workflow system.

How to use it

Fill in one Wedge worksheet per named competitor, ship the discovery questions and reframes into the tool reps already use during a call, and run the monthly feedback-loop review to keep it current.

What's inside

  • Step 1: threat-type categorization (Incumbent, Disruptor, DIY, No-Decision) and matching positioning approach
  • Step 2: fill-in Wedge worksheet per competitor (why we lose, why we win, wedge question, proof point, land mine, objection reframe)
  • Step 3: the three in-deal artifacts to ship instead of a battlecard PDF
  • Step 4: distribution rule for where positioning must live in the rep's actual workflow
  • Step 5: the feedback-loop mechanism that keeps positioning from going stale
  • Owner and review-cadence table for every artifact

Why battlecards fail

Most competitive battlecards are a feature-comparison table nobody opens mid-deal. This framework replaces the static battlecard with a live, in-deal positioning system: it turns competitive intel into questions and reframes a rep can actually use in the room, and it builds in the feedback loop so the positioning stays current.

Step 1, Categorize the competitive threat (not just the competitor)

Every competitor you face falls into one of four threat types. The type determines the positioning approach, a feature comparison only works against one of these four.

Threat typeWhat it looks likePositioning approach
IncumbentProspect already has a tool/vendor doing this jobDon't attack the incumbent directly: attack the status quo cost of staying
DisruptorA newer/cheaper competitor is in the dealCompete on outcome and risk, not price
DIY / Build-it-yourselfProspect thinks they can solve this internallyCompete on time-to-value and opportunity cost, not raw capability
No-DecisionThe real competitor is inactionCompete on cost of delay, not against a named vendor

Step 2: Build the Wedge per competitor (fill in for each named competitor)

Competitor name: _______________ Threat type (from Step 1): _______________ Where they show up in the deal (stage, persona): _______________

Why we lose to them: _______________ Why we win against them: _______________

The Wedge Question: a discovery question that surfaces the gap without naming the competitor:

_______________

The Proof Point: one concrete, specific piece of evidence (customer story, data point, capability) that supports the wedge:

_______________

The Land Mine: a question the prospect should ask the competitor that exposes their actual weakness (give this to the prospect, don't just tell the rep):

"When you're evaluating [competitor], ask them: _______________"

The Objection Reframe, when the prospect says "[Competitor] does X" or "we're already looking at [Competitor]":

Rep says: "_______________"

Step 3, Translate into the three in-deal artifacts

Don't ship a PDF. Ship these three things, living where reps already work:

  1. Discovery question set (5-7 questions per competitor, embedded in the CRM opportunity record or call-prep tool), surfaces whether this competitor is actually in play before the rep guesses
  2. Proof point library (2-3 per competitor, tagged by persona and vertical, searchable, not buried in a slide deck)
  3. Objection reframe scripts (word-for-word, for the top 3 objections per competitor)

Step 4: Distribution rule

Positioning lives in the tool reps use during the call (CRM opp record, call-prep sidebar, or coaching platform), never only in a shared drive folder. If a rep has to leave their workflow to find it, it doesn't get used. Set a rule: no new competitive intel ships without a "where does this live in the rep's actual workflow" answer.

Step 5, The Feedback Loop (the part most GTM teams skip)

Competitive positioning goes stale in one direction only: reps encounter something new in the field and it never gets back to whoever owns the wedge.

  • A standing Slack/Teams channel where reps post real competitive moments (what was said, what worked, what didn't): not a form, a channel people actually use
  • Monthly 30-minute review: product marketing + top 3 reps by competitive win rate review what's changed
  • Every wedge, proof point, and reframe has a "last verified" date, anything older than one quarter gets re-validated or retired
  • Win/loss interviews explicitly ask: "What did [competitor] say about us that we should know?"
