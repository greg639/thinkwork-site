Cold Call Qualifying Questions Swipe File

55 ready-to-use qualifying questions organized by buying stage and objection type, word-for-word and copy-paste ready for your next call.

What's inside

  • Opening/situation questions for the first 60 seconds of a cold call
  • Pain and problem discovery questions
  • Impact and cost-of-inaction questions
  • Authority and decision-process questions
  • Budget questions
  • Timeline and urgency questions
  • Objection-specific questions for 6 common brush-offs
  • Post-demo and late-stage/closing qualifying questions

55 questions, organized by where you are in the call. Copy-paste ready — adjust bracketed terms to your product category.

A. Opening / Situation Questions (First 60 Seconds)

  1. "Before I go further — is now a genuinely bad time, or just a normal 'didn't expect a call' moment?"
  2. "Can I ask — how are you currently handling [process/problem area] today?"
  3. "Who else touches [process] besides you, day to day?"
  4. "What does your current setup for [category] actually look like — one tool, a few, or mostly manual?"
  5. "How long has it been set up this way?"
  6. "On a scale of 1–10, how would you rate how well that's working right now?"
  7. "What prompted you to even take this call today?"
  8. "Is [problem area] something you're actively trying to fix right now, or more of a 'someday' item?"

B. Pain & Problem Discovery

  1. "What's the most frustrating part of how this works today?"
  2. "When was the last time [problem] actually caused a real headache — what happened?"
  3. "Is this a new problem, or has it been nagging at you a while?"
  4. "If you had to guess, how much time does your team lose to this every week?"
  5. "What have you already tried to fix it?"
  6. "Why didn't that stick, or work?"
  7. "Who feels this pain the most on your team?"
  8. "What would 'solved' actually look like for you?"

C. Impact & Cost-of-Inaction

  1. "What does it cost you — in time, money, or people — to keep doing it this way?"
  2. "If nothing changes in the next 6 months, what happens?"
  3. "Has this ever cost you a deal, a customer, or a hire? Can you give me an example?"
  4. "How does this show up in your numbers — is there a metric it's dragging down?"
  5. "Whose problem does this become if it's still unsolved at the end of the year?"
  6. "What's the actual dollar impact, if I could put a number on it?"

D. Authority & Decision Process

  1. "Walk me through how a decision like this typically gets made on your team."
  2. "Besides you, who else would need to sign off?"
  3. "Has your team bought something like this before — what did that process look like?"
  4. "If this checked every box for you, what would the next step look like internally?"
  5. "Is there a procurement or legal review step we should plan for?"
  6. "Who would be the biggest skeptic in the room, and what would they need to see?"

E. Budget

  1. "Is there budget already allocated for this, or would it need to be built into next quarter's or next year's plan?"
  2. "Roughly what range have you seen solutions like this land in?"
  3. "If the ROI was clear, is budget the kind of thing that can move, or is it fixed for the year?"
  4. "What's this costing you today, even if it's not a line item — time, workarounds, missed opportunities?"

F. Timeline & Urgency

  1. "Is there a specific date or event driving why you're looking at this now?"
  2. "What happens if this is still unsolved 90 days from now?"
  3. "Where does this rank against the other 2–3 things on your plate this quarter?"
  4. "If we moved forward, when would you actually want this live?"

G. Objection-Specific Questions

"Not interested"

  1. "Totally fair — can I ask, is it 'not interested in this problem' or 'not interested in solving it right now'?"
  2. "What would need to be true for this to be worth 5 minutes of your time?"

"We already have a solution / vendor"

  1. "Good to know — what do you like most about what you're using today?"
  2. "Is there anything about it that still bugs you, even a little?"
  3. "What would have to change for you to even consider a switch?"

"No budget right now"

  1. "Understood — if budget opened up, is this a priority you'd want funded first, or further down the list?"
  2. "When does budget planning happen for next cycle, and is this worth flagging for it?"

"Just send me an email"

  1. "Happy to — so I send you something useful and not another generic pitch, what's the one thing that would make it relevant to you?"
  2. "Can I ask what you'll be looking for when you open it?"

"I'm too busy right now"

  1. "No problem — is there a better day this week, or should I check back in a few weeks?"
  2. "Before I let you go — is [problem] even on your radar right now, or truly not a priority?"

"We're happy with our current setup"

  1. "Great to hear — what's made it work so well for you?"
  2. "Is there anything, even minor, you'd change if you could?"

H. Buying-Stage Specific Questions

Post-Demo / Mid-Funnel

  1. "Now that you've seen it, what stood out as most useful to your team?"
  2. "Is there anything you saw today that raised a concern?"
  3. "Who else on your team needs to see this before you can move forward?"

Late-Stage / Closing

  1. "What's still standing between here and a decision?"
  2. "If we resolved [outstanding concern], is there anything else that would hold this up?"
  3. "What does success look like 90 days after this is live, and how will you know it worked?"

How to use it

Keep this open during live calls and pull the matching question set for wherever the call is heading — opening, discovery, or a specific objection — rather than working from memory.
