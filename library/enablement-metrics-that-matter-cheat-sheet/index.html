Enablement Metrics That Matter Cheat Sheet

The 10 enablement metrics that actually correlate skill development to revenue outcomes, and the 6 vanity metrics to stop putting in your QBR deck.

The 6 vanity metrics to stop reporting

#Vanity metricWhy it's misleading
1Training completion rateMeasures attendance, not capability change. A 100% completion rate with flat win rates means nothing transferred.
2Content/course library size"We have 400 modules" says nothing about whether the right 15 competencies are covered well.
3LMS login frequencyCorrelates with mandate enforcement, not skill growth.
4NPS on training sessionsReps rate sessions highly when they're entertaining, not when they're the ones that actually moved behavior.
5Number of coaching sessions loggedA manager can log 20 five-minute check-ins with zero observed behavior change; volume isn't quality.
6Certification pass rate at first attemptHigh pass rates usually mean the bar is too low, not that reps are excelling — compare against #4 above instead.

The one precondition

Every metric above depends on having accurate, current, per-rep skill-level data — without it you're back to vanity metrics by default, because completion and login counts are the only thing left to measure. If your competency coverage rate (#5) is below ~70%, fix that first; every other number on this sheet will be noise until it is.

How to use it

Replace each vanity metric currently in your enablement dashboard with its swap-table equivalent, and don't report metrics 1–10 org-wide until your Competency Coverage Rate (#5) is above roughly 70%.
