Prospecting Habit-Stack Template

A fill-in habit-stacking template that anchors your hardest prospecting habit to a routine you already do automatically, so it survives the weeks motivation doesn't show up.

What's inside

  • The exact habit-stacking formula: "After I ___, I will ___, [where], for ___"
  • A menu of 6 common, genuinely-automatic anchor habits to choose from
  • A menu of 5 prospecting micro-habits sized to actually stick
  • A fill-in table for building up to 3 stacked habits at once
  • The visible-tracking rule and the "never miss twice" recovery rule
  • Troubleshooting fixes for the 3 most common ways a habit stack fails

Anchor your hardest prospecting habit to something you already do without thinking

Motivation is unreliable by design — it's a mood, not a system. Habit-stacking works because it borrows the automaticity of a habit you've already built (making coffee, sitting down at your desk, closing out of your last meeting) and attaches the new, harder behavior to it as a trigger. You don't decide to prospect each day; the anchor decides for you.

The formula

After I [existing habit — specific and automatic], I will [prospecting micro-habit — specific and small], [where], for [how long / how many reps].

Fill in your own:

After I _______________________________ I will _______________________________ [location] _______________________________ for _______________________________

Example: "After I close my laptop from the morning stand-up, I will send 10 personalized LinkedIn connection requests, at my desk, before I open my inbox."

Menu of common anchors (pick one you genuinely never skip)

  • After my first coffee finishes brewing
  • After I close my laptop from the morning stand-up / team meeting
  • After I sit down at my desk with my second coffee
  • After I finish my lunch
  • After I end my last call before a scheduled prospecting block
  • After I close my calendar app each morning

Menu of prospecting micro-habits (start smaller than feels necessary)

  • 10 personalized outreach messages (not templated blasts)
  • 5 cold calls, dialed back-to-back before checking anything else
  • 1 hand-written-feel voice note to a warm lead who's gone quiet
  • 15 minutes of researching 3 target accounts before outreach
  • 5 follow-ups to people who opened/replied but went quiet

Start smaller than feels ambitious. A habit that takes 5 minutes and that you actually do every day beats a 45-minute block you do three days a week and feel guilty about the other two.

The tracking rule

  • Mark a simple tally (tick or X) on a calendar or notes app the moment you finish the habit — visible tracking is part of what makes it stick, not an afterthought.
  • Never miss twice in a row. Missing once is life. Missing twice is the start of the habit dying — if you miss a day, the very next anchor moment is non-negotiable, even a token version of it (2 calls instead of 5).

Troubleshooting

  • "I keep forgetting." Your anchor isn't automatic enough yet — pick one you truly never skip (brushing teeth, first coffee), not an aspirational one (a workout you're inconsistent with).
  • "I do it but it feels forced." That's normal for 2–3 weeks. Judge the system at week 3, not day 3.
  • "The habit is too big and I keep skipping it." Cut it in half. A 5-call habit you do daily beats a 15-call habit you do twice a week.

How to use it

Fill in one habit stack, run it daily for three weeks before adding a second one, and track completion visibly (a tally, a streak counter) so the habit becomes self-reinforcing rather than willpower-dependent.
