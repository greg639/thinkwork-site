Cold Call Objection Handling Cheat Sheet

One-page, word-for-word responses to the 15 objections reps hear most in the first 30 seconds of a cold call, built on a repeatable Acknowledge-Reframe-Question structure.

The 15 objections you'll hear most in the first 30 seconds of a cold call, with a response for each. Every response follows the same three-beat structure: Acknowledge (don't fight it) → Reframe (give a reason to keep listening) → Question (hand control back with something easy to answer).

Keep this on one page, next to your dialer. Don't memorize word-for-word — memorize the beat.

1. "I'm not interested."

"Totally fair, you don't know what I'm offering yet, so that makes sense. Most people I say this to aren't looking for [category] either — they're just trying to fix [specific pain]. Is that even on your radar?"

2. "We already have a vendor/solution for that."

"Good to hear you've already got something in place. Quick question — on a scale of 1 to 10, how happy are you with it?"

3. "Send me an email / send me some information."

"Happy to — I'll send it right after this call. So it's not generic, can I ask one quick question first: [key qualifying question]?"

4. "I don't have time right now."

"Understood, I'll be quick — is there a better time later today or tomorrow, or do you want the 15-second version right now?"

5. "We don't have budget."

"Makes sense, most people don't have a budget line for something they haven't evaluated yet. Setting cost aside for a second — is [specific pain] actually a problem worth solving if the numbers worked?"

6. "Just send me your info and I'll take a look."

"Will do. One thing that'll help me send the right thing instead of a generic deck — what's the biggest challenge you're dealing with around [category] right now?"

7. "How did you get this number?"

"Fair question — [source, e.g. your website / a public directory / LinkedIn]. I know that can feel intrusive, so I'll keep this short. [Continue with pattern interrupt.]"

8. "We're happy with what we're doing."

"Glad to hear it — that's rarer than you'd think. Can I ask what's working well about it? [Listen] And is there anything that isn't quite there yet?"

9. "Call me back in [6 months / next quarter]."

"Happy to — what changes between now and then that makes it the right time?"

10. "I'm not the right person."

"Appreciate you telling me straight away — who would be the right person, and would you mind if I mentioned your name when I reach out?"

11. "We tried something like this before and it didn't work."

"That's useful to know — what happened, specifically? [Listen] That's actually one of the most common reasons things stall, and it's usually fixable in how it's implemented, not the idea itself."

12. "Is this a sales call?"

"It is, yes — I won't pretend otherwise. I'm calling because [specific reason tied to their role/company]. Worth 20 seconds, or should I let you go?"

13. "I'm in a meeting" / "I'm busy right now."

"No problem — is there a better time in the next day or two I can catch you for two minutes?"

14. "We're too small/big for this."

"Understood — can I ask what would need to be different for it to make sense? [Listen and note for future timing.]"

15. "Take me off your list."

"Absolutely, done — I won't call again. Appreciate you telling me directly rather than just not answering."

The 3-Beat Framework at a Glance

BeatPurposeLength
AcknowledgeRemoves friction — you're not arguing1 sentence
ReframeGives a reason to keep listening1 sentence
QuestionHands control back, low-effort to answer1 short question

Rule of thumb: If you can't get through all three beats in under 10 seconds, you're overexplaining. Objections are reflexes, not debates — treat them that way.

How to use it

Keep it visible during live dialing, memorize the 3-beat structure rather than the exact wording, and move through all three beats in under 10 seconds per objection.
