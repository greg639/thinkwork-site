The methodologies in plain language

MEDDIC is a qualification discipline, not a conversation technique — it tells you whether a deal is real, not what to say. Built at PTC to stop reps (and forecasts) from being fooled by activity. Ideal for complex B2B where multiple stakeholders and real budget scrutiny are in play.

MEDDPICC is MEDDIC plus the two things that kill enterprise deals late: an undiscovered procurement/legal/security gauntlet (Paper Process) and an unaddressed competitor (Competition). If your deals routinely stall in legal review that nobody saw coming, this is the fix — MEDDIC alone won't catch it.

SPIN Selling is the oldest and most research-backed of the group — it's a question sequence for building a buyer's own case for change, not a qualification or forecasting system. It composes well underneath any of the others: MEDDIC tells you what to find out, SPIN gives you a technique for asking.

Challenger Sale flips the model from "align with the buyer's stated needs" to "reframe how the buyer thinks about their problem." It needs a genuine, credible insight to teach — without one, "Challenger" training just produces reps who argue with buyers. Works best in markets where every vendor sounds the same.

Sandler is built around disqualifying bad-fit deals fast and stripping out the power imbalance buyers create by withholding information ("let me think about it"). Strong for SMB/high-velocity motions where cycle speed and objection handling matter more than committee navigation.

Solution Selling predates most of the above and pioneered the idea of co-creating a buyer's vision of the solved future state before pitching capabilities. Much of its DNA is inside modern discovery frameworks; few companies run it standalone today, but its vision-processing logic is worth knowing.

Command of the Message is not a standalone methodology so much as a qualification-plus-messaging operating system, purpose-built for scaling B2B SaaS orgs that need every rep telling a consistent value story. It's usually deployed on top of MEDDPICC, not instead of it.

Common real-world blends

Most mature sales orgs don't run one methodology in isolation — they stack two layers:

  1. Qualification/process layer (is this deal real, and will it get through procurement): MEDDIC → MEDDPICC as deal complexity and procurement rigor increase.
  2. Conversation/differentiation layer (how the rep actually talks to the buyer): SPIN → Challenger → Command of the Message as market crowding and portfolio scale increase; Sandler as the alternative conversation layer when speed and disqualification matter more than insight-selling.

Pick one from each layer. Don't run three full methodologies at once — see the Sales Methodology Selection Decision Tree for how to choose.

How to use it

Use the comparison table to evaluate a training vendor pitch or pick the right foundation before you commit budget, and use the two-axis model to decide which qualification layer and which conversation layer to blend.
