Objection Handling Call Review Rubric

A 4-point scoring rubric for grading exactly how a rep handled a real objection on a real call — across acknowledge, isolate, reframe, and confirm — so coaching feedback is evidence-based instead of a vibe check.

What's inside

  • 4-point scoring scale definition (1 = Not Yet through 4 = Advanced)
  • Acknowledge dimension: behavioral descriptors per score
  • Isolate dimension: behavioral descriptors per score
  • Reframe/Respond dimension: behavioral descriptors per score
  • Confirm dimension: behavioral descriptors per score
  • Scoring sheet with total-score bands and what each band means
  • Instructions for using the rubric on a single real call
  • Calibration note for teams with more than one manager scoring calls

Score how a rep actually handled an objection on a real call — not how the objection-handling framework was trained in theory. Pick one clear objection moment per call and score it against all four dimensions below. Each dimension is scored independently; a rep can nail the reframe and still fail to isolate, and that gap only shows up if you score them separately.

The 4-Point Scale

ScoreLabelMeaning
1Not YetBehavior absent or actively counterproductive (argued, ignored, went silent, agreed and moved on)
2DevelopingBehavior attempted but incomplete, generic, or script-read rather than responsive to what the buyer actually said
3ProficientBehavior executed correctly, specific to what this buyer said, in the rep's own words
4AdvancedBehavior executed correctly AND used to advance the conversation — buyer's tone/language visibly shifts, or a new piece of information surfaces

Dimension 1 — Acknowledge

Did the rep validate the objection as legitimate before responding to it?

ScoreDescriptor
1Rep responds immediately with a counter-argument, pitch, or justification — no acknowledgment at all
2Rep uses a generic acknowledgment ("totally get that," "fair point") that could apply to any objection
3Rep reflects back the specific concern in their own words ("so the worry is X will slow your team down in month one")
4Rep acknowledges AND names the underlying stake for the buyer (why this concern matters to them specifically), which visibly lowers the buyer's guard

Dimension 2 — Isolate

Did the rep confirm this was the real (or only) objection before answering it?

ScoreDescriptor
1Rep answers the stated objection without checking if it's the only thing in the way
2Rep asks a closed, generic isolating question ("anything else on your mind?")
3Rep asks a specific isolating question tied to the objection ("if we solved X, is there anything else that would stop you moving forward?")
4Rep isolates AND surfaces a second, previously unstated objection — and the buyer confirms it

Dimension 3 — Reframe / Respond

Did the rep address the real concern with relevant, specific proof — not a generic rebuttal?

ScoreDescriptor
1Rep responds with a scripted rebuttal, feature dump, or price justification unrelated to the isolated concern
2Rep responds to the right concern but with generic proof ("lots of customers feel that way at first")
3Rep responds with specific, relevant proof (a comparable customer, a specific mechanism, a direct answer) matched to this buyer's stated concern
4Rep reframes the concern itself — buyer's own language shifts ("oh, I hadn't thought about it that way") and the objection stops being a blocker

Dimension 4 — Confirm

Did the rep check the objection was actually resolved before moving on?

ScoreDescriptor
1Rep moves on to the next topic without checking; assumes resolution
2Rep asks a closed confirming question ("does that make sense?") that invites a reflexive yes
3Rep asks a confirming question that requires a real answer ("where does that leave you on it?") and listens to the response
4Rep confirms AND secures a concrete next step that depends on the objection being resolved (buyer takes an action, not just says "okay")

Score bands:

  • 4-7 — Not Yet: Coach one dimension only, in the next 1:1, using this exact call as the example
  • 8-11 — Developing: On track; name the single highest-leverage dimension to drill next
  • 12-14 — Proficient: Reinforce what's working; stretch toward Advanced on the weakest dimension
  • 15-16 — Advanced: Use this call as a role-play example in the Role-Play Script Library for other reps

How to Use This on a Real Call

  1. Pick ONE objection moment per call — don't try to score the whole call
  2. Score all four dimensions before discussing any of them with the rep
  3. Open the coaching conversation with the lowest-scoring dimension, not the overall total
  4. Have the rep score the same clip themselves first, then compare — the gap between self-score and manager-score is itself coaching data
  5. Re-score the same rep on the same dimension within 2 weeks to check movement, not a new random call

Calibration Note

If more than one manager scores calls, calibrate quarterly: have all managers score the same 2-3 recorded calls independently, then compare. Anything more than a 1-point spread on any dimension means the descriptors are being read differently and need a joint re-read before scoring resumes.

How to use it

Pick one real objection moment from a recorded or live call, score each of the four dimensions 1-4 using the descriptors, total the score, then open the coaching conversation with the lowest-scoring dimension — not with the overall impression of the call.
