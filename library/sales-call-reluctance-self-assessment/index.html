Sales Call Reluctance Self-Assessment

A 20-question diagnostic that scores you against 12 recognized types of sales call reluctance and names which one is actually costing you dials, with a one-line remedy for each.

How to use it

Rate all 20 statements based on your last 30 days of real behavior, total your scores against the mapping key, and treat your top 1-2 scoring types as the priority to work on rather than trying to fix everything at once.

What's inside

  • 20 statements rated on a 1-5 frequency scale based on your last 30 days of real behavior
  • Full scoring key mapping every question to 1 of 12 recognized call-reluctance types
  • Separate reading thresholds for 2-question types vs. 1-question types
  • Guidance for identifying your primary and secondary constraint rather than treating all 12 equally
  • One-line remedy for each of the 12 types
  • Designed for honest self-scoring in under 10 minutes

Instructions

Rate each statement based on the last 30 days of your own prospecting behavior, not how you wish you behaved. Use this scale:

1 = Never true of me · 2 = Rarely true · 3 = Sometimes true · 4 = Often true · 5 = Almost always true

Answer honestly and quickly, your first instinct is usually the accurate one.

How to read it:

  • For 2-question types (rows 1-8): a score of 8-10 is a strong, active constraint; 5-7 is worth watching; 2-4 is not currently a driver.
  • For 1-question types (rows 9-12): a score of 4-5 is a strong, active constraint; 3 is worth watching; 1-2 is not currently a driver.
  • Most people carry a primary type and a secondary type, rarely just one. Rank your top 2 scores; those are what's actually costing you dials, not lack of effort or talent.

One-Line Remedy Per Type

  1. Doomsayer, Track actual call outcomes vs. what you predicted beforehand; the data breaks the pattern faster than willpower.
  2. Over-Preparer, Set a hard prep-time cap (e.g., 10 minutes) per account before you're required to dial.
  3. Stage Fright: Rehearse out loud in front of a peer or manager before high-stakes group calls, not just in your head.
  4. Yielder, Script one firm-but-polite follow-up line and use it verbatim until it stops feeling uncomfortable.
  5. Social Self-Consciousness: Shift your pre-call self-talk explicitly onto the prospect's problem, not your delivery.
  6. Referral Aversion: Reframe the ask as helping your contact look good to their network, not as a personal favor.
  7. Emotionally Overextended, Build a fixed recovery ritual after rejection-heavy blocks; don't stack them back to back.
  8. Role Rejection: Reconnect "sales" to the specific skill and value you deliver, not the label.
  9. Telephobia: Make the first touch in every sequence phone-first, by rule, before email/message is allowed.
  10. Oppositional Reflex: Treat the script as a skeleton to personalize (aim for ~20% your own words), not a cage.
  11. Special-Case Avoidance: Name the avoided segment explicitly and schedule those calls first in the day, not last.
  12. Cold-Start Aversion, Break new-territory prospecting into a small fixed daily quota (e.g., 5 new accounts/day) instead of "get through the list."
