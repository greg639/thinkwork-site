"Is My Quota Actually Winnable?" Self-Assessment

A 12-question, numbers-based diagnostic that scores whether your assigned quota is mathematically achievable — across territory size, ramp stage, and pipeline coverage — so quota conversations start from math, not morale.

What's inside

  • 4 territory-reality questions scoring account count, TAM-to-quota ratio, exclusivity, and tenure on the patch
  • 4 ramp-stage questions scoring months since start, ramp-schedule status, certification completion, and pipeline-generation motion
  • 4 pipeline-coverage questions with the actual coverage-ratio, late-stage-mix, cycle-vs-days-left, and win-rate-projection formulas
  • 0–36 point scoring key with three verdict bands (Winnable / Fragile / Not Winnable)
  • Section-by-section diagnosis guide identifying which leg — territory, ramp, or coverage — is actually broken
  • A scripted opening line for presenting your score in a manager 1:1

Purpose: This is a numbers-based diagnostic, not a vibe check. It scores whether an assigned quota is mathematically winnable given three factors that actually determine outcomes: territory size, ramp stage, and pipeline coverage. Answer all 12 questions honestly — pull the real numbers where asked, don't estimate.

Time to complete: 10–15 minutes. Have your pipeline report and territory account list open.

Section A — Territory Reality (max 12 points)

A1. How many accounts — named accounts plus realistic whitespace — sit inside your assigned territory today?

  • 150+ accounts → 3 pts
  • 80–149 accounts → 2 pts
  • 40–79 accounts → 1 pt
  • Fewer than 40 accounts → 0 pts

A2. What is the total addressable revenue (TAM) of your territory, compared to your annual quota?

  • TAM is 8x quota or more → 3 pts
  • TAM is 4x–7.9x quota → 2 pts
  • TAM is 2x–3.9x quota → 1 pt
  • TAM is under 2x quota, or you genuinely don't know your TAM → 0 pts

A3. How much of your territory is exclusively yours — no other rep, channel partner, or house-account carve-out competing for the same logos?

  • 90–100% exclusive → 3 pts
  • 70–89% exclusive → 2 pts
  • 50–69% exclusive → 1 pt
  • Under 50% exclusive / heavily overlapping → 0 pts

A4. How long have you owned this specific patch (not tenure at the company — tenure on this territory)?

  • 18+ months, fully mapped → 3 pts
  • 7–17 months → 2 pts
  • 2–6 months → 1 pt
  • Under 2 months, or the map was just redrawn → 0 pts

Section B — Ramp Stage (max 12 points)

B1. How many full months since your quota-bearing start date?

  • 12+ months → 3 pts
  • 7–11 months → 2 pts
  • 3–6 months → 1 pt
  • 0–2 months → 0 pts

B2. Is your quota formally ramped (e.g., 0/50/75/100%) to reflect tenure, or full quota regardless of start date?

  • Fully ramped and you're past the ramp period → 3 pts
  • Fully ramped and currently inside the ramp schedule → 2 pts
  • Partially ramped (e.g., only month 1 excluded) → 1 pt
  • No ramp at all → 0 pts

B3. Have you completed the product, competitive, and methodology certification required to sell at full capacity?

  • Fully certified → 3 pts
  • One module outstanding → 2 pts
  • Multiple modules outstanding → 1 pt
  • Minimal formal training received → 0 pts

B4. How would you describe your own pipeline-generation engine right now (not what's inherited — what you personally generate)?

  • Consistent inbound + outbound producing new pipeline weekly → 3 pts
  • A motion exists but is inconsistent → 2 pts
  • Mostly reliant on inherited/aged pipeline → 1 pt
  • Starting from a cold list, no built motion yet → 0 pts

Section B subtotal: ___ / 12

Section C — Pipeline Coverage Math (max 12 points)

C1. Calculate your coverage ratio: Total open pipeline value this period ÷ Remaining quota $ this period = ___x

  • 4x or higher → 3 pts
  • 3x–3.9x → 2 pts
  • 2x–2.9x → 1 pt
  • Under 2x → 0 pts

C2. What share of that pipeline is in late stage (verbal commit, proposal out, contract/legal, negotiation) rather than early discovery?

  • 40%+ late stage → 3 pts
  • 25–39% → 2 pts
  • 10–24% → 1 pt
  • Under 10% → 0 pts

C3. Compare your average sales-cycle length to the selling days left in the period.

  • Days remaining are 2x+ your average cycle → 3 pts
  • 1x–1.9x → 2 pts
  • Roughly equal → 1 pt
  • Days remaining are shorter than your average cycle → 0 pts

C4. Apply your trailing win rate to current pipeline value. Does projected closed-won, plus what's already closed this period, reach quota?

  • Yes, with room to spare → 3 pts
  • Yes, almost exactly → 2 pts
  • Short by 10–25% → 1 pt
  • Short by more than 25% → 0 pts

Section C subtotal: ___ / 12

Your Total Score: ___ / 36

ScoreVerdict
28–36Winnable as built. The number is fair math, not politics. Any shortfall from here is an execution issue, not a design issue.
17–27Winnable, but fragile. At least one leg — territory, ramp, or pipeline — is under real strain. Find your lowest-scoring section and act on it specifically.
0–16Not winnable as currently built. Two or more legs are structurally broken. This is a math problem, not a motivation problem.

Diagnose by section, not just total

  • Lowest score in Section A (Territory): the problem is upstream of you — bring it to your manager as a territory-design conversation.
  • Lowest score in Section B (Ramp): you may be carrying an adult quota on a rookie ramp — check your ramp schedule.
  • Lowest score in Section C (Coverage): this is fixable this week — it's a pipeline-generation problem, not a quota problem.

How to use this in a 1:1

Don't walk in saying "I don't think I can hit this." Walk in saying: "My coverage-ratio score is 0 out of 3 — I'm sitting at 1.8x with six weeks left and an average cycle of nine weeks. Here's the math." Numbers move quota conversations. Feelings don't.

How to use it

Pull your live pipeline report and territory account list, answer all 12 questions with real numbers, total your score against the 0–36 key, then use your lowest-scoring section to drive a specific 1:1 conversation with your manager.
