Sales Enablement Tech Stack Evaluation Directory

Vendor-neutral categories and hard evaluation criteria for every layer of the enablement stack — LMS, content management, conversation intelligence, coaching, practice/roleplay, certification, onboarding, and gamification — plus a weighted scoring matrix to run your own bake-off.

What's inside

  • 8 vendor-neutral stack categories with a one-line definition of what each actually does
  • 5-8 must-have evaluation criteria per category
  • A named red-flag list per category (the specific tells that a vendor demo is hiding a gap)
  • A weighted scoring matrix template for comparing up to 3 vendors head-to-head
  • Guidance on which categories can be safely combined in one vendor vs. which should stay separate
  • Deal-breaker column logic for disqualifying a vendor regardless of score
  • Notes on integration dependencies between categories (e.g., coaching platform needs conversation intelligence data)
  • A buying-sequence recommendation for orgs building a stack from zero

Eight categories, each with what it does, what to demand in a demo, and the red flags that mean a vendor is selling you a dashboard instead of a capability.

1. Learning Management / Content Delivery (LMS)

What it does: hosts and delivers structured training content and tracks completion.

Evaluation criteria:

  • Content authoring is fast enough that non-technical enablement staff can publish without a ticket to IT
  • Mobile and offline access (reps in the field, not just at a desk)
  • Completion tracking AND comprehension tracking — not just "opened the module"
  • Native integration with CRM/HRIS for auto-enrollment by role/tenure
  • Spaced-repetition or reinforcement scheduling built in, not just a one-time module
  • Certification workflow support (see category 6)

Red flags: no API; no mobile app; content is static PDF/video only with no interaction; analytics stop at "completed/not completed."

2. Sales Content Management

What it does: houses and surfaces the content reps actually use in deals (decks, case studies, one-pagers).

Evaluation criteria:

  • Search returns the right asset in under 10 seconds from within a CRM record or email client
  • Version control enforces a single source of truth — no orphaned old decks in circulation
  • Usage analytics show which content is actually opened/shared in real deals, not just uploaded
  • Content lifecycle workflow (expiry dates, owner review reminders)
  • Native integration with email and CRM so usage data flows back automatically

Red flags: tagging is manual-only with no enforcement; no usage analytics beyond upload counts; no integration with the tools reps live in daily.

3. Conversation Intelligence / Call Recording

What it does: records, transcribes, and analyzes sales conversations at scale.

Evaluation criteria:

  • Transcription accuracy holds up on accents, crosstalk, and multi-speaker calls
  • Analytics go beyond talk-ratio to keyword/moment tagging (competitor mentions, pricing discussion, objections)
  • Clips can be created and shared in under 30 seconds for coaching use
  • Searchable across the entire call corpus, not just one rep's calls
  • Integrates with dialer/video conferencing and CRM without manual upload

Red flags: analytics limited to talk-time ratio and word count; no clip-sharing workflow; no way to search across the whole team's calls for a specific moment type.

4. Coaching & Skill-Observation Platforms

What it does: structures how managers score and track rep skill development over time.

Evaluation criteria:

  • Scoring is tied to named skills with defined behaviors — not a generic 1-10 "how was the call"
  • Every score can be linked to a timestamped piece of evidence in the recording
  • Historical trend view per rep, per skill, across their tenure
  • Cross-manager calibration tooling (same call, compare scores, discuss variance)
  • Manager workflow is fast enough to actually get used weekly, not quarterly

Red flags: scores aren't tied to any specific behavior or skill; no calibration mechanism across managers; no trend view — every observation is a standalone snapshot.

5. Practice & Roleplay Platforms (including AI roleplay)

What it does: lets reps rehearse scenarios before they hit a live deal.

Evaluation criteria:

  • Scenarios are configurable to your actual competitive landscape and buyer personas, not generic templates
  • Grading rubric (AI or human) is transparent — you can see exactly why a score was given
  • Difficulty scales by rep level (a foundational scenario ≠ an advanced multi-stakeholder scenario)
  • Practice results tie back into certification and real coaching data
  • Objection and persona variability is realistic, not a scripted single path

Red flags: one fixed scenario reused for every rep and level; black-box scoring with no rubric visibility; no evidence that practice performance correlates with real-call performance.

6. Certification & Skill-Verification

What it does: formally verifies a rep has demonstrated a skill at a given level.

Evaluation criteria:

  • Scenario/behavior-based assessment, not multiple-choice trivia alone
  • Level-based progression (Foundational → Advanced), not pass/fail only
  • Audit trail sufficient for compliance or promotion decisions
  • Re-certification cadence is configurable and enforceable

Red flags: knowledge quiz only, with no tie to demonstrated skill; no re-certification cadence support at all.

7. Onboarding / Ramp Orchestration

What it does: sequences and tracks a new hire's path to full productivity.

Evaluation criteria:

  • Milestones are tracked against a real ramp benchmark, not a generic checklist
  • Tasks auto-assign by day/week without manual manager setup each cohort
  • Manager dashboard shows real-time new-hire progress, not a static spreadsheet
  • Integrates with mentor/buddy program workflows (see Resource 10)

Red flags: a static checklist with no tracking; no manager-facing dashboard at all.

8. Incentives / Gamification

What it does: motivates and reinforces desired behavior beyond comp plan mechanics.

Evaluation criteria:

  • Rewards tie to skill milestones and quality, not just raw activity volume
  • Configurable by enablement/ops without a vendor services ticket for every change
  • Fair across tenure and role — doesn't structurally favor only senior reps
  • Doesn't create incentive to game the metric instead of build the skill

Red flags: pure activity-count leaderboard; no mechanism to weight for skill quality over volume.

Buying Sequence for a Stack Built from Zero

  1. Conversation Intelligence (you need the data before you can coach or practice against it)
  2. Coaching & Skill-Observation (turns raw call data into structured development)
  3. LMS + Content Management (delivers the training the coaching data says you need)
  4. Certification (verifies the training actually took)
  5. Practice/Roleplay + Onboarding Orchestration + Gamification (accelerate and reinforce once the core loop exists)

Buying certification or gamification before you have coaching data is the most common sequencing mistake — it measures nothing real yet.

How to use it

Score each vendor against the category-specific criteria during your demo/trial period using the weighted matrix, and disqualify any vendor that trips a deal-breaker regardless of how high their total score comes in.
