Cold Call Talk-to-Listen Ratio Scorecard

A 7-criterion scoring rubric that benchmarks rep talk-time against discovery-question density on live or recorded calls, with scoring bands and a coaching script tied to the result.

How to use it

Score one live or recorded call per rep per week using the rubric, total the 7 criteria out of 35, and use the matching band to decide whether the coaching conversation is a quick note, a targeted drill, or a peer-example callout.

What's inside

  • Benchmark talk-time ratios for cold calls vs. discovery calls
  • 7-criterion scoring rubric, 1-5 scale each
  • Criteria: opening control, qualifying-question count, question density per minute, talk-time ratio, active-listening behaviors, objection handling, next-step close
  • Overall scoring bands: needs coaching / solid / elite
  • Instructions for scoring live calls vs. recorded calls
  • Manager coaching-conversation script tied to the score

Why this matters

The single most common failure pattern in cold and discovery calls is a rep who talks too much, too early, about the product instead of the prospect. This scorecard benchmarks talk time and question density so managers can coach the behavior with numbers, not impressions.

Maximum score: 35 points.

Scoring bands

Total scoreBandAction
0–17Needs coachingPair with 1:1 call-shadowing this week; drill opener + qualifying questions

27–35EliteUse as a peer-coaching example / call-library sample

How to score

Live calls: Managers listen (double-jack or listen-only line), tally question count on a notepad using the criteria above, and note the timestamp when talk-time noticeably shifts.

Recorded calls: Use built-in call-recording talk-time analytics for criterion 4 if available; otherwise time-stamp rep-speaking segments manually for a representative 5-minute sample. Count open-ended questions by listening for "what," "how," "why," "tell me about" phrasing (closed yes/no questions do not count toward criterion 2).

Coaching conversation script tied to the score

"Your talk-time ratio on this call was [X]% against a target of [Y]% for a [call type], that tells me the balance is off before we even get to what was said. Let's replay the two minutes where it tipped, and figure out where a question could have replaced a statement."
