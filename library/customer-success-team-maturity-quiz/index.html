Customer Success Team Maturity Quiz

Benchmark your CS org's maturity — reactive, proactive, or predictive — in 10 questions covering risk detection, QBRs, forecasting, escalation, expansion, and your tech stack.

What's inside

  • 10 questions covering risk detection, QBRs, forecasting, escalation, expansion, and tech stack
  • Three-option scoring per question mapped to Reactive, Proactive, or Predictive maturity
  • A total maturity score with band definitions and what each stage actually looks like day-to-day
  • Guidance on which single gap to close first when moving Reactive to Proactive
  • Guidance on the most common blocker (data integration) moving Proactive to Predictive

How this works

Answer all 10 questions about your CS org as it actually operates today — not your aspiration. Each question has three options, scored 1, 2, or 3. Add up your total.

  1. How do you find out an account is at risk?

(1) We usually hear it from the customer when they threaten to cancel or don't renew. (2) We have a health score/alerting system that flags risk before the customer says anything. (3) We predict risk before it shows up in behavior, using leading indicators and trend modeling, and intervene pre-emptively.

  1. How are QBRs run?

(1) Ad hoc, inconsistent, often reactive to a customer request or issue. (2) Every strategic account gets a scheduled QBR with a standard agenda and prep process. (3) QBR cadence and depth are tiered by account value/risk, with automated data prep feeding a consistent, insight-led format.

  1. How do renewals get forecasted?

(1) We find out an account is renewing (or not) close to the actual date. (2) We forecast renewals 60–90 days out based on CSM judgment and account health. (3) We have a rolling renewal forecast built on health-score data and reviewed weekly by leadership, with confidence bands per deal.

  1. What triggers an escalation?

(1) A customer complains loudly enough, or a CSM raises a flag informally. (2) We have a defined escalation path with clear triggers and named owners at each tier. (3) Escalation triggers are automated from health-score thresholds and route to the right owner without manual judgment calls.

  1. How is expansion revenue generated?

(1) Opportunistically, when a customer asks for more or a CSM happens to notice whitespace. (2) CSMs are trained and expected to proactively identify and pursue expansion in every account plan. (3) Expansion opportunities are systematically surfaced from usage/health data and routed to CSMs or a dedicated expansion team with playbooks per signal type.

  1. What does your CS tech stack look like?

(1) Spreadsheets, email, and whatever's in the CRM — no dedicated CS platform. (2) A dedicated CS platform (health scoring, playbooks) is in place and used consistently. (3) The CS platform is integrated with product usage, billing, and support data, feeding automated workflows and predictive models.

  1. How does CS feedback reach Product?

(1) Informally, when a CSM happens to mention something in Slack or a hallway conversation. (2) There's a structured process (e.g., quarterly feedback rollup) for CS insights to reach Product/Engineering. (3) CS data (usage, churn reasons, feature requests) is a formal, weighted input into the product roadmap process.

  1. How is individual CSM/AM performance measured?

(1) Mostly on renewal rate/GRR after the fact. (2) On a mix of leading indicators (adoption, engagement, health score trend) and lagging outcomes (renewal, expansion). (3) On a full scorecard blending leading indicators, account health trend, and business outcomes, reviewed and coached against monthly.

  1. How does leadership see the state of the book of business?

(1) Ad hoc — leadership asks when something goes wrong, or waits for the quarterly renewal number. (2) A regular (monthly) rollup report shows account health, risk, and forecast across the book. (3) Leadership has a live, always-current dashboard showing health, risk, forecast, and trend across every segment.

  1. How does onboarding hand off to ongoing CS?

(1) Informally — whenever the deal team says the account is "live," CS picks it up with whatever context they can get. (2) A defined handoff process with a checklist and context transfer exists and is generally followed. (3) Onboarding milestones feed directly into the health-scoring model, so day-1 account health already reflects onboarding quality data.

Moving up one stage

  • Reactive → Proactive: Pick ONE thing to systematize first — usually health scoring or a defined escalation path. Don't try to fix all 10 at once.
  • Proactive → Predictive: The gap is almost always data integration, not process. Connect usage, billing, and support data into one health signal before adding more manual playbooks.

How to use it

Score your team honestly against how things actually run today, identify your single lowest-scoring question, and make closing that one gap your CS ops priority for the next quarter.
