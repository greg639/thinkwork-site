LinkedIn Comment & Engagement Scripts for Social Sellers

Eight word-for-word comment templates for showing up in a prospect's or exec's comment section as a peer with something useful to say — not a rep with a pitch to hide.

What's inside

  • The Data-Point Add-On (supports or nuances their point with a number)
  • The Respectful Push-Back (disagrees without starting a fight)
  • The "Build On It" Extension (adds the next-level thought)
  • The Specific-Detail Callback (real congratulations, never generic)
  • The Open Question (invites a reply instead of ending the thread)
  • The Connector Tag (brings in a relevant person or thread)
  • The First-90-Minutes Quick Take (short, sharp, early)
  • The Own-Post Reply Continuation (keeps your own comment section alive)
  • Banned openers and phrases list
  • 4-step read-before-you-type checklist

The rule these scripts follow: a good comment adds exactly one thing — a fact, a respectful disagreement, an extension of the idea, a specific observation, a genuine question, or a useful connection. It never pivots to your product. If you can't identify which one of those five things your comment is doing, don't post it yet.

Use [brackets] as fill-in fields. Everything outside brackets is ready to use as written.

1. The Data-Point Add-On

Use when: someone posts an opinion or trend and you have a number that supports or nuances it.

  • "This tracks with what we're seeing too — [specific stat, e.g. 'reply rates drop off hard once a sequence goes past 8 touches']. Curious whether you're seeing the same drop-off point, or later, in [their space]."
  • "Adding a data point from [source/your experience]: [specific number]. Doesn't contradict your take, just puts a figure on it."
  • "The [specific stat] you cited matches almost exactly what we found in [context] — down to the percentage point. Small sample, but interesting."

2. The Respectful Push-Back

Use when: you genuinely disagree, and it's worth saying so — carefully.

  • "Pushing back gently here — in my experience, [specific counter-example], the opposite has been true. Might be a [segment/industry] difference. What are you seeing on your side?"
  • "Genuinely curious about this one. We tried [the approach they're advocating] and saw [specific different result]. Did you find a way around [specific obstacle], or was [alternate condition] what made the difference for you?"
  • "Respectfully, I'd flag one exception: [specific case]. Doesn't break your overall point — just a wrinkle worth naming."

3. The "Build On It" Extension

Use when: you agree, but you have a next-level thought that doesn't just restate theirs.

  • "Agreed — and the part that doesn't get said enough is [second-order consequence]. Once teams fix [the first thing], that's usually where the real bottleneck shows up."
  • "Yes, and the follow-on question this raises for me: [related question]. Have you seen teams solve for that once they've nailed [the original point]?"
  • "This is right, and worth adding — [related nuance]. The teams who get this right early tend to avoid [specific downstream problem]."

4. The Specific-Detail Callback

Use when: congratulating a promotion, launch, raise, or milestone. Never post a bare "Congrats!" — it reads as a like, not a comment.

  • "Congrats — [specific detail: the milestone, the timeframe, the market they're entering]. That's a hard trifecta to pull off in [timeframe/market condition]. What does the first 90 days look like?"
  • "This one's well-earned — [specific reference to their work, or a detail from the post itself]. [Specific detail] is the part that stands out to me."
  • "Congrats on [specific detail, e.g. 'closing the round in this market'] — that's not a small thing right now. Well done."

5. The Open Question

Use when: you want a reply, not just a reaction. The test: could this question be answered with a paragraph, not a shrug?

  • "Question for you — when you say [specific phrase from the post], are you seeing that hold across [segment], or mainly at [specific segment]?"
  • "How are you thinking about [related open problem] once [the thing they posted about] gets solved? Curious if that's the next fire."
  • "What changed your mind on this? Curious if it was one moment or a slow build."

6. The Connector Tag

Use when: you know someone or something genuinely relevant to the thread — this is a favor to the poster, not a networking play.

  • "This is relevant to a conversation [Name] and I were having last week about [topic] — tagging them in case it's useful. @[Name]"
  • "Worth cross-posting into [community/context] — a lot of people there are wrestling with exactly this."

7. The First-90-Minutes Quick Take

Use when: you're early on a high-visibility post. Short, sharp, no hedging.

  • "Strong take. The part most people miss: [one-line insight]."
  • "This needed to be said. [One specific reason, one line]."
  • "Saving this one. '[Specific line from the post]' is the sentence people should screenshot."

8. The Own-Post Reply Continuation

Use when: replying to comments on your own posts — this is where most social sellers go quiet after "Thanks!" and kill their own momentum.

  • "Great point, [Name] — and it pushes the idea further: [extension]. Did [specific related question] play out the same way for you?"
  • "Appreciate the pushback, [Name]. You're right that [concede the specific point]. Where I'd still hold the line is [specific nuance]."

Banned openers and phrases

Delete on sight: "Great post!", "Love this!", "So true!", "Couldn't agree more!", "This 💯", any comment under 8 words, and any comment that pivots to "We do this at [Company] — happy to chat."

The 4-step read-before-you-type checklist

  1. Can I add a fact, a disagreement, a question, or a connection — pick exactly one, not a blend.
  2. Would I be comfortable if the poster and their entire network read this and knew who I sell for?
  3. Is there a "you should try [product]" anywhere in this comment? Delete it if so.
  4. Keep it under 40 words — except the Data-Point Add-On and Respectful Push-Back, which can run 60–80 words.

How to use it

Before commenting on a prospect's or exec's post, pick the one template that matches what you actually have to offer (a fact, a disagreement, a question, or a connection), fill in the brackets with something true and specific, and run it through the 4-step checklist before you hit post.
