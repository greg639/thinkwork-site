Peak-Performance Morning Routine Checklist

The exact pre-9am sequence — body, orientation, vocal priming, visualization — that gets top performers to their first call already regulated and in state.

The pre-9am sequence that gets you to your first call already in state

The goal isn't productivity theater — it's arriving at your first call regulated, focused, and already warmed up, instead of using your first prospect as your warm-up. Adjust the exact times to your own schedule; keep the sequence and the order of categories.

Wake to 30 minutes before first call: Body before screen

  • Don't touch your phone for the first 20 minutes awake. Checking Slack/email/CRM the moment you wake up puts you in reactive mode before you've chosen anything on your own terms.
  • Move your body for at least 5–10 minutes — a walk, stretching, or a real workout if your schedule allows. This isn't about fitness, it's about shifting your physiological state before the day starts asking things of you.
  • Hydrate before caffeine — a full glass of water before the first coffee.

30 to 15 minutes before first call: Orient

  • Look at today's calendar for 2 minutes — not to plan the whole day, just to know what's coming and remove any surprise.
  • Review your top 3 accounts/calls for today — one line each: who they are, what you need from this call, what "good" looks like when it ends.
  • Write down (or say out loud) one thing you're proud of from yesterday — even a small one. This isn't optional fluff; it sets your starting emotional baseline before the day can set it for you.

15 to 5 minutes before first call: Prime the instrument

  • Vocal warm-up — literally hum or read a paragraph out loud. Your voice is your instrument on calls; most reps never warm it up and it shows in the first five minutes of every call.
  • Posture check — stand or sit the way you want to sound. Slouched bodies produce flat voices.
  • Say your opening line for the first call out loud, once, before you're live. Don't wing the first 10 seconds of your day.

5 minutes before: Visualize and enter

  • Close your eyes for 30 seconds and picture the call going well — not perfectly, just well. This primes your nervous system toward approach instead of avoidance.
  • One clean exhale, then go live.

What to protect no matter how the morning goes sideways

If the whole routine falls apart because the day starts chaotic, protect these three in order of priority:

  • No phone in the first 20 minutes (this one matters most)
  • 2 minutes reviewing your top calls for today
  • One out-loud rehearsal of your first opening line

Everything else is enhancement. These three are the floor.

How to use it

Run the full sequence for two weeks before judging it — the compounding effect (regulated start, primed voice, chosen baseline) shows up in week two, not day one.
