Cold Call Opener Swipe File

20+ word-for-word cold call openers organized by industry, buyer role, and trigger event, built to survive the first 7 seconds before a prospect decides to hang up.

How to use it

Match your opener to the buyer role, industry, or trigger event you're working, say it in your own cadence rather than reading verbatim, and always end on a question so the prospect responds instead of you filling the silence.

You have about seven seconds before a prospect decides whether you're worth listening to or worth hanging up on. That decision is made on your first sentence, not your pitch. These openers are built to survive those seven seconds: they name the interruption, respect the prospect's time, and get to a reason to keep listening before resistance kicks in.

How to use this: Pick the category that matches your situation (cold with no research, cold with a role fit, cold with an industry fit, or warm off a trigger event). Say the line in your own words, don't read it verbatim off the page. If it doesn't sound like you when you say it out loud, it won't land.

The 4 Types of Openers (and When to Use Each)

Industry-SpecificYou know the sector, not the personBorrow credibility from pattern-matching

General Pattern-Interrupt Openers (No Research Required)

  1. "Hi [Name], this is a cold call: do you want me to hang up, or do I get 10 seconds?"
  2. "[Name], I know I'm interrupting your day. I'll be quick: 20 seconds, then you decide if it's worth continuing."
  3. "Hey [Name], this is [Your Name]: you don't know me, this is a sales call. Can I have 15 seconds to tell you why I called?"
  4. "[Name], you've never heard of me, and I'm calling completely out of the blue. Is now an absolutely terrible time, or just a merely bad one?"
  5. "Hi [Name], quick one. Is this the kind of call where I should ask if it's a bad time, or should I just get to the point?"

Role-Based Openers

VP of Sales / Sales Leader

  • "Hi [Name], I work with sales leaders who are hitting quota on paper but watching ramp time creep up every time they backfill a rep. Is that anywhere close to a problem for you?"
  • "[Name], most sales leaders I talk to can tell me their win rate but not why the bottom 40% of the team keeps stalling in the same three moments of the deal. True for your team?"

CFO / Finance Leader

  • "Hi [Name], I'll keep this short, your calendar isn't built for cold calls. I talk to CFOs who've just realized [category] spend has crept up without a matching lift in output. Worth 20 seconds?"
  • "[Name], I'm not calling to sell you anything today, I'm calling to find out if [specific cost/metric] is even on your radar. Got a moment?"

CTO / VP Engineering

  • "Hi [Name], engineering leaders get pitched integrations every day, so I'll be direct: this call is about [specific technical pain]. Worth 30 seconds, or should I just email?"
  • "[Name], quick context before you hang up, I don't sell [generic category]. I help teams that are [specific technical situation]. Does that sound like anything you're dealing with?"

Head of HR / People

  • "Hi [Name], this is a cold call about something HR leaders usually only raise after it's already a problem: [specific pain, e.g. year-one attrition]. Is that on your list right now?"

CMO / Marketing Leader

  • "[Name], I'll be upfront, this is a cold call. I work with marketing leaders whose pipeline looks healthy on a dashboard but sales says the leads don't convert. Sound familiar?"

COO / Operations Leader

  • "Hi [Name], operations leaders get cold-called about efficiency constantly, so I'll skip the pitch: is [specific process] still eating more manager time than it should?"

Owner / Founder (SMB)

  • "Hi [Name], I'll be straight with you: this is a cold call, and I know you get plenty. I help [specific owner type] with [specific outcome]. Can I have 20 seconds?"

Industry-Based Openers

SaaS / Software

  • "Hi [Name], I work with SaaS companies around [ARR range] who are past product-market fit but still coaching sales the same way they did at $1M ARR. Close to home?"

Manufacturing / Industrial

  • "[Name], most plant and ops leaders I talk to are dealing with [specific pain, e.g. downtime tied to a skills gap on the floor]. On your plate?"

Healthcare / Medical

  • "Hi [Name], I know compliance means I have to be careful what I say on a cold call, so I'll just ask directly: is [specific pain] something your team is actively trying to solve?"

Financial Services / Insurance

  • "[Name], I work with agencies where producer ramp time costs more than the commission structure accounts for. Ring a bell?"

Professional Services (Legal, Accounting, Consulting)

  • "Hi [Name], partners at firms like yours usually tell me the same thing: the best people are billable, so training happens by osmosis. True at [Company]?"

Construction / Trades / Field Services

  • "[Name], I'll keep this quick: I work with contractors who lose bids not on price, but on the sales call before the estimate. Ever an issue for your team?"

Trigger-Event Openers (Highest Reply Rate: Use When You Have One)

  • Funding round: "Congrats on the [Series X] round: that's usually when [specific pain, e.g. sales hiring outpaces sales enablement] shows up. Already happening?"
  • New hire in role: "Hi [Name], saw you stepped into [Title] at [Company], congrats. The first 90 days usually means auditing [specific area]. On your list?"
  • Job posting: "[Name], saw you're hiring for [role], that usually signals [specific pain]. Is that what's driving it, or something else?"
  • Expansion / new location: "Congrats on [the new office/market], that kind of growth usually strains [specific process]. Showing up yet?"
  • Press or award: "Saw the piece on [Company] in [Publication], congrats. Quick reason I'm calling: [tie to relevant pain]."

The Universal Permission-Based Opener (Fallback for Any Situation)

"[Name], this is [Your Name] from [Company]: you don't know me, we've never spoken, and this is a cold call. Do you have 20 seconds for me to tell you why I called, and then you can tell me to get lost?"

Rules for Using This Swipe File

  • Internalize, don't read, a script read verbatim sounds like a script.
  • Cut anything over 10 seconds. If your opener needs a breath, it's too long.
  • Name the interruption before the prospect can, it disarms the reflexive "not interested."
  • Always end the opener with a question or a pause. Silence is doing work for you, don't fill it.
  • Swap the bracketed pain point for something specific to your ICP. Generic pain = generic hang-up.
