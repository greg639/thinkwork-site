IC-to-Manager Promotion Scorecard

The rubric candidates and leaders use to evaluate a rep's readiness for their first management role, weighted to catch the difference between your best seller and your best coach.

What's inside

  • 360 rating instructions (self + manager + 2 peers)
  • 1-5 behavioral rating scale definitions
  • 6 scored competency sections (coaching, talent judgment, process/rigor, cross-functional influence, composure, track record)
  • Weighted scoring formula
  • Red-flag checklist that overrides the score
  • Recommendation matrix (promote / promote with plan / hold / not yet)

For candidates and leaders evaluating a rep's first move into sales management. Fill this out as a 360: self-rating, direct manager rating, and 2 peer/cross-functional ratings, then compare notes — the gaps between raters are usually more informative than any single score.

How to use it

  1. Candidate self-rates first, in isolation, before seeing anyone else's input.
  2. Manager rates independently, using specific evidence (a call, a 1:1 the manager observed, a peer they mentored) — not vibes.
  3. Two peers who've worked closely with the candidate rate the "Influence Beyond Own Deals" and "Talent Judgment" sections only — they see things a manager doesn't.
  4. Compare scores in a live conversation. A candidate who self-rates a 5 where everyone else says 2 is itself the finding.

Rating scale (use for every row)

  • 1 — Not yet observed / actively concerning
  • 2 — Emerging, inconsistent
  • 3 — Solid, reliable in normal conditions
  • 4 — Strong, reliable under pressure
  • 5 — Exceptional, others already seek this person out for it

Section 1: Coaching & Feedback (weight: 25%)

BehaviorScore (1-5)Evidence
Gives specific, behavioral feedback (names the moment and the technique) rather than generic encouragement
Has voluntarily coached a peer or new hire without being asked to
Can identify the skill behind a deal outcome, not just the outcome itself
Adjusts coaching style to the person, not a one-size-fits-all approach
Follows up on feedback given (checks if it actually changed behavior)

Section 2: Talent Judgment (weight: 20%)

BehaviorScore (1-5)Evidence
Has an accurate, articulable read on teammates' strengths and gaps (not just "they're good/bad")
Has flagged a struggling teammate early, constructively, rather than staying silent or gossiping
Understands the difference between a skill gap (coachable) and a fit/effort problem (not)
Would be trusted by peers to give them a straight, private read on their own performance

Section 3: Process & Rigor (weight: 15%)

BehaviorScore (1-5)Evidence
Keeps their own pipeline/forecast disciplined and accurate — a proxy for whether they'll hold a team to the same standard
Documents and shares what's working (playbooks, talk tracks) rather than keeping it to themselves
Has run or contributed to a process (territory design, a deal review cadence, onboarding a peer) beyond their own deals

Section 4: Influence Beyond Own Deals (weight: 15%, peer-rated)

BehaviorScore (1-5)Evidence
Peers seek this person out for advice, unprompted
Has changed how the team operates for the better (a shared doc, a shared process, a norm) without formal authority to do so
Communicates in team settings in a way that lifts the room, not just themselves
Handles disagreement with peers/other functions (CS, marketing, product) constructively

Section 5: Composure & Emotional Regulation (weight: 15%)

BehaviorScore (1-5)Evidence
Stays level when a deal falls apart or a number is missed — doesn't spiral or deflect blame
Has delivered or received hard feedback without the relationship breaking
Can sit with someone else's bad news/bad quarter without needing to fix it immediately or take it personally

Section 6: Track Record (weight: 10% — necessary floor, not the deciding factor)

BehaviorScore (1-5)Evidence
Consistent quota attainment over a meaningful sample (2+ years, not one hot quarter)
Track record achieved without cutting corners (deal quality, customer health, team reputation intact)

Important: Section 6 is a gate, not a tiebreaker. A 5/5 here with 2/5 everywhere else is a "great IC, not ready to manage" — promoting on Section 6 alone is the single most common and most expensive promotion mistake in sales orgs.

Weighted score calculation

(Section 1 avg × 0.25) + (Section 2 avg × 0.20) + (Section 3 avg × 0.15) + (Section 4 avg × 0.15) + (Section 5 avg × 0.15) + (Section 6 avg × 0.10) = Weighted Score (out of 5)

Red flags (any one of these should pause the decision regardless of weighted score)

  • Takes credit for team/peer wins in front of leadership
  • Only "coaches" people who are already succeeding; avoids the harder conversations with strugglers
  • Track record includes a pattern of deal quality complaints from CS/onboarding
  • Cannot describe a specific time they helped someone else get better — only examples of their own deals
  • Views the move to management primarily as compensation/title progression when asked directly

Recommendation matrix

Weighted scoreRed flags present?Recommendation
4.0+NonePromote — with a 90-day onboarding plan and a mentor manager
3.0–3.9NonePromote with a development plan targeting the lowest-scoring section first
Any1+ red flagHold — address the red flag directly with the candidate before any promotion conversation continues
Below 3.0—Not yet — give the candidate this exact scorecard as their own development plan for the next 2 quarters

How to use it

Collect self, manager, and two peer ratings independently, then compare scores live — apply the red-flag checklist before any score, however high, greenlights a promotion.
