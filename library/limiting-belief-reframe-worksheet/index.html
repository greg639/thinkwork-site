Limiting-Belief Reframe Worksheet

A four-column worksheet for catching the money, rejection, and "pushy selling" stories a rep's brain runs on autopilot, and rewriting them into a specific next action before the belief costs them the next call.

How to use it

Fill in a row within an hour of catching the belief live (right after you hesitate, discount, or go quiet); review your last 5 rows with your manager once a week and pick one New Action to run before the next 1:1.

What's inside

  • The 4-column format: Belief / Evidence Against / Reframe / New Action
  • 12 fully worked examples covering money mindset, rejection, and "pushy selling" narratives
  • A trigger-spotting guide for catching the belief in the moment, not after the fact
  • Rules for what makes a reframe usable (must be sayable out loud, not an affirmation)
  • 6 blank rows formatted for a rep's own recurring beliefs
  • A weekly practice cadence and a manager debrief question set for 1:1s

Why this exists

Most mental-game coaching stops at "believe in yourself," which isn't a skill you can practice. This worksheet is a mechanical process instead: catch the exact sentence your brain is running, test it against evidence, rewrite it into something you'd actually say out loud, and attach a next action you can take in the next 24 hours. Do this enough times and the reframe becomes the default belief: not because you decided to think positively, but because you out-argued the old belief with facts often enough that it stopped showing up uncontested.

How to use it

  1. Catch it live. Fill this in within an hour of the moment it fired: right after you talked yourself out of a call, discounted before being asked, or went quiet instead of asking for the business.
  2. Write the belief exactly as it sounded in your head. Not a cleaned-up version. If it was "they're going to think I'm just chasing quota," write that.
  3. Evidence Against is facts, not affirmations. "I'm good at this" is not evidence. "Three of my last five closed deals came from a direct ask on the exact thing I just avoided" is evidence.
  4. The Reframe must be sayable out loud to a colleague without sounding delusional. If it sounds like a poster, rewrite it.
  5. The New Action must be doable in the next 24 hours and checkable: yes or no, did you do it.

Worked Examples

Money mindset

BeliefEvidence AgainstReframeNew Action
"Charging full price makes me feel like I'm taking advantage of them."Every full-price client I have is still a client 12 months later, no one has ever called it a rip-off after getting the result."Price reflects the outcome, not my worth as a person. If the outcome is real, the price is fair."State the full number on the next call without a pre-emptive apology or a "but we can look at options" tag.
"Talking about money makes me seem greedy."The client brought up budget first in 4 of my last 5 deals, they expect to talk about it."Avoiding the number doesn't make me generous, it makes the deal take longer for both of us."Ask "what's budget looking like for this" before they ask me.
"If they can't afford it, the right thing is to discount."The two deals I discounted unprompted both churned within a quarter; the two I held price on are still active."Discounting before being asked signals I don't believe in the price: that costs them confidence in the product, not just me revenue."Hold price on the next inbound objection and offer a smaller scope instead of a smaller number.
"Wanting to hit a big commission check this month is a bad reason to sell hard."My best quarter, the client got the fastest onboarding and the best results of any account I've run, the incentive and the outcome pointed the same direction."My commission being on the line doesn't make the deal worse for them. It makes me more prepared."Write down, before the next call, the specific result this deal produces for the client, not just the number I need.

Rejection

BeliefEvidence AgainstReframeNew Action
"If they say no, it means I failed."Of my last 20 "no"s, 6 came back within a quarter once timing changed: a no is a timestamp, not a verdict."A no today is data about today, not a verdict on me or the product."Log the specific reason for the no in the CRM and set a real follow-up date instead of marking it dead.
"A no on this call means they'll never buy."My current #1 account said no twice before they said yes."People change their mind when their situation changes, not when I change my pitch."Send a one-line, no-pressure check-in in 60 days instead of closing the record.
"I should already know how to handle this objection by now."Every rep on this team who's now a top performer has a note from their manager about struggling with this exact objection a year ago."Not having this handled yet is a stage, not a character flaw."Ask my manager for the exact language they use on this objection and write it on a card by my desk.
"This much rejection means I'm not cut out for this."Every closer I respect has said the volume of no's went up, not down, right before their best quarter."Volume of no's is a leading indicator of activity, not a verdict on fit."Count today's no's on a tally sheet instead of narrating a story about each one.

"Pushy" selling

BeliefEvidence AgainstReframeNew Action
"Following up again will annoy them."In my CRM, 9 of my last 15 wins took 4+ touches after the demo, the ones I let go quiet all died."Following up on something that helps them isn't pressure, it's follow-through."Send the next planned follow-up today instead of waiting for them to go quiet first.
"Asking directly for the business is pushy."The clients who've thanked me by name for being direct outnumber the ones who've ever complained about it, 20 to 0."A direct, clear ask respects their time more than a vague hint does."Use the words "are you ready to move forward" instead of "let me know what you think" on the next close attempt.
"If I sound too confident, I'll come across as arrogant."The reps I most trust as buyers are the ones who state things plainly, not the ones who hedge everything."Confidence about the product is not the same as pressure about the decision, I can hold both."Remove one "just," "sorry to bother you," or "no worries if not" from my next outbound message before sending.
"Good salespeople don't need a script or a process."The rep I most admire on this team has a note card of exact language for every stage of the call, visible on their desk."A process is what frees me up to actually listen, because I'm not improvising the hard parts."Use the exact opening line from the team script on my next 3 calls without freelancing it.

Weekly Practice Cadence

  • Daily: catch and log one belief the same day it fires. Don't batch this: memory sands off the exact wording, and the exact wording is what you're arguing with.
  • Weekly (before your 1:1): re-read your last 5 rows. Circle the belief that repeated more than once, that's the one actually running your ceiling.
  • Monthly: rewrite your top 3 reframes onto a card you keep visible at your desk or in your CRM notes pane.

Manager Debrief Questions (for 1:1s)

  • "Which belief showed up more than once this week?"
  • "What's the New Action you didn't do, and what actually stopped you?"
  • "What's the Evidence Against you're most convinced by, read it back to me."
  • "If this belief showed up on a call today, what would you do differently now versus a month ago?"
