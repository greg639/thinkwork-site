Manager's 1:1 Objection Coaching Conversation Guide

A structured question set for coaching a rep after a deal stalls or dies on an unresolved objection: built to help the rep self-diagnose the skill gap rather than receive a lecture, ending in one specific committed change.

How to use it

Work through the five parts in order in a scheduled 20-minute 1:1, letting the rep self-identify the gap in Parts 1-2 before you name it in Part 3, and always leave with one specific commitment and a dated re-check in Part 5.

What's inside

  • Pre-conversation prep steps (pull the recording, give notice, hold your own read loosely)
  • Part 1: 4 fact-finding reconstruction questions
  • Part 2: 5 rep self-assessment questions that let the rep name the gap first
  • Part 3: 5 skill-gap patterns mapped to a naming script (diagnosis/composure/reframe/closing-loop/prevention gaps)
  • Part 4: 3 joint-plan-building questions including a live role-play offer and resource pointer
  • Part 5: 3 commitment and follow-up questions with a specific re-check date
  • 'What NOT to say' list, 3 shutdown phrases to avoid
  • Closing line template

For use after a deal stalls or dies on an unresolved objection

Purpose: this is a coaching conversation, not a post-mortem interrogation. The goal is the rep leaving with one specific, practiced adjustment, not a list of everything they did wrong.

Before the Conversation

  • Pull the call recording or notes from the relevant moment. Timestamp the objection and the response.
  • Don't ambush, tell the rep in advance: "Want to grab 20 minutes to go through the [Account] call together, mostly interested in how we handle it differently next time."
  • Come with your own read on what happened, but hold it loosely, you're about to test it against the rep's own account.

Part 1: Reconstruct What Happened (2–3 minutes, fact-finding, not judgment)

  1. "Walk me through what happened on this deal, in your own words."
  2. "What was the objection, as best you remember it, in their words if you can?"
  3. "What did you say back, right in that moment?"
  4. "How do you think it landed?"

Manager's job here: listen more than talk. Resist correcting the rep's account yet, you're building a shared picture first.

Part 2, Rep Self-Assessment (5–7 minutes)

  1. "Looking back, what do you think was really behind that objection: was it what they said, or something underneath it?"
  2. "If you could rewind and say one thing differently, what would it be?"
  3. "What were you thinking in the moment right after they raised it, were you already planning your response before they finished talking?"
  4. "On a scale of 1–5, how much did you feel like you diagnosed the real issue before responding to it?"
  5. "What do you think they needed to hear that they didn't hear from you?"

Let the rep name the gap themselves wherever possible, self-identified gaps stick better than manager-assigned ones.

Part 3: Skill-Gap Diagnosis (manager-led, 3–5 minutes)

Match what surfaced in Parts 1–2 to one of these patterns and name it plainly:

  • Diagnosis gap: responded to the surface objection without checking what was underneath it
  • Composure gap: got defensive, rushed, or over-explained under pressure
  • Reframe gap: understood the real objection but didn't have the language/proof to respond to it
  • Closing-the-loop gap: responded reasonably but never confirmed it landed or secured a concrete next step
  • Prevention gap: this objection was foreseeable from earlier discovery and should have been surfaced sooner

"Based on what we just walked through, this looks like a [gap name] to me, does that match how it felt to you?"

Part 4, Build the Plan Together (5 minutes)

  1. "If this exact objection came up again next week, what would you want to say instead?" (Get them to say it out loud, in full sentences, not just describe the idea.)
  2. "What would help you practice that before the next time it comes up: want to run it as a role-play now, or with the team this week?"
  3. "Is there a resource that would help here?" (Point to the specific tool: LAER guide for diagnosis gaps, No-Budget Playbook for reframe gaps on budget objections, Role-Play Bank for composure gaps, etc.)

Part 5, Commitment & Follow-Up (2 minutes)

  1. "What's the one thing you're taking from this into your next call?"
  2. "How do you want me to check in on this: should I flag it if I hear it come up again on a call, or do you want to bring it to me?"
  3. Set a specific re-check point: "Let's revisit this in two weeks, bring me one call where this objection came up and how you handled it differently."

What NOT to Say in This Conversation

  • "You should have just said X" (shuts down self-assessment: offer it only after they've had a chance to name it themselves)
  • "This is why we lost the deal" (single-cause blame: objections are rarely the sole reason a deal dies)
  • Anything that turns this into a performance-review moment rather than a skill-development moment: if performance is genuinely a concern, that's a separate conversation with its own process.

Closing Line

"None of this is about that one deal, it's about making sure the next version of this conversation goes differently. What's the one thing you're walking out with?"
