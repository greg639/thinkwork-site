Value Proposition Canvas Worksheet

Maps your buyer's real pains and gains directly to your messaging, so every pitch answers why us and why now before a single sentence is written.

What's inside

  • Customer Profile block: Jobs, Pains, Gains (with definitions and prompts for each)
  • Value Map block: Products/Services, Pain Relievers, Gain Creators
  • Fit-testing method: mapping each Pain Reliever/Gain Creator to a specific Pain/Gain
  • Pain ranking scale (severe/moderate/minor) and Gain ranking scale (essential/expected/desired)
  • The Fit Statement formula that turns the completed canvas into your core message
  • Worked example for a full canvas
  • Red-flag checklist — signs your canvas doesn't actually fit
  • Instructions for running this as a 45-minute team workshop

Every pitch should answer “why us, why now” before it's written — this is where that answer comes from.

Part 1 — Customer Profile (fill this in first, about your buyer, with zero mention of your product)

Jobs — what is your buyer actually trying to get done?

(Functional tasks, social pressures, and emotional needs — not your product's category.)

  • Functional job: ________________________
  • Social job (how they want to be seen doing it): ________________________
  • Emotional job (how they want to feel doing it): ________________________

Pains — what gets in the way, annoys them, or risks failure?

List each pain and rank it:

PainRank (Severe / Moderate / Minor)

Gains — what outcomes or benefits would delight them?

List each gain and rank it:

GainRank (Essential / Expected / Desired)

Part 2 — Value Map (fill this in second, about your offer)

Products/Services — what do you actually offer? (list, no adjectives)

  • ________________________
  • ________________________

Pain Relievers — how specifically does each product/service kill a listed pain?

Pain RelieverWhich Pain (from Part 1) does it kill?

Gain Creators — how specifically does each product/service produce a listed gain?

Gain CreatorWhich Gain (from Part 1) does it produce?

Part 3 — Fit Test

Go line by line. For every Pain Reliever and Gain Creator, ask: does this map to a Pain or Gain ranked Severe/Essential? If a Pain Reliever only addresses a Minor pain, it belongs in your feature list, not your pitch. If a Severe pain has no Pain Reliever mapped to it, you have a gap — either build one or don't claim fit in that area.

Fit ratio = (Pain Relievers mapped to Severe pains + Gain Creators mapped to Essential gains) ÷ (Total Pain Relievers + Total Gain Creators)

A fit ratio under 50% means your messaging is currently built around things your buyer doesn't rank as urgent.

Part 4 — The Fit Statement Formula

Once the canvas is complete, assemble your core message:

“For [buyer/Job], who struggle with [top Severe Pain], [Product/Service] provides [top Pain Reliever], which means [outcome tied to top Essential Gain] — unlike [alternative/status quo], which [why the alternative fails to relieve that pain].”

Worked Example — Mid-market payroll software, sold to Finance Directors

Jobs: Functional — close payroll accurately every cycle. Social — be seen as the person finance trusts with compliance. Emotional — not dread the last week of every month.

Pains: Manual reconciliation errors (Severe). Late filings triggering penalties (Severe). Spreadsheet version confusion across sites (Moderate).

Gains: Same-day close (Essential). Audit trail with zero manual work (Essential). Nicer UI (Desired).

Value Map — Pain Relievers: Automated multi-site reconciliation → kills manual reconciliation errors. Built-in filing deadline engine → kills late filing penalties.

Value Map — Gain Creators: One-click close report → produces same-day close. Auto-generated audit log → produces zero-manual-work audit trail.

Fit Statement:

“For Finance Directors who struggle with reconciliation errors across multiple sites, [Product] provides automated multi-site reconciliation, which means a same-day close with zero manual audit prep — unlike spreadsheet-based processes, which surface errors only after the filing deadline has already passed.”

Red-Flag Checklist — your canvas doesn't actually fit if:

  • More than half your Pain Relievers map to Minor pains
  • You can't name a single Essential gain your product produces
  • Your Fit Statement reads the same with a competitor's name swapped in
  • You had to guess at Jobs/Pains/Gains rather than pull them from real buyer conversations
  • The canvas was built by marketing/product alone with zero rep or customer input

Running This as a 45-Minute Workshop

  • 0:00–0:10 — Individually, each rep lists Jobs/Pains/Gains from their last 3 real deals (no product talk allowed).
  • 0:10–0:20 — Combine and rank as a group; resolve disagreements by majority vote, not seniority.
  • 0:20–0:35 — Map Pain Relievers and Gain Creators together; challenge every mapping that looks like a stretch.
  • 0:35–0:45 — Draft the Fit Statement as a group, read it aloud, and pressure-test it against the red-flag checklist.

How to use it

Complete Part 1 using only real buyer language pulled from recent calls or win/loss interviews, complete Part 2 about your offer, run the Fit Test, then use the Fit Statement formula as the backbone of your next pitch, deck, or landing page headline.
