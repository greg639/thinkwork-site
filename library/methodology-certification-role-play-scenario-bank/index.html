Methodology Certification Role-Play Scenario Bank

Twenty realistic buyer scenarios across three difficulty tiers, each with a hidden complication designed to separate reps who can actually qualify a deal from reps who just know the acronym.

How to use it

Assign scenarios by tier to match rep tenure, run each as a 15-20 minute role-play with a manager playing the buyer, and score against the included MEDDIC or Challenger rubric to set a pass/fail certification bar.

What's inside

  • 20-scenario bank table across 3 difficulty tiers, each with a hidden complication
  • 4 fully scripted buyer briefs with in-character opening lines and grading focus
  • MEDDIC/MEDDPICC 0-2 rubric across all 6 (or 8) qualification elements
  • Challenger-adapted 0-3 Teach / Tailor / Take-Control rubric
  • Pass thresholds by tier
  • Facilitation rules for buyer actors and graders

20 buyer scenarios, three difficulty tiers, one grading rubric. Built for MEDDIC/MEDDPICC certification role-plays; a Challenger-adapted rubric is included at the end for teams running Teach-Tailor-Take-Control certifications instead.

How to run a certification role-play

  • Roles: one rep (being certified), one buyer actor (a manager or senior rep who has read the scenario brief and stays in character), one silent grader (can be the same person as the buyer actor if short-staffed, but a dedicated grader is better).
  • Time: 15–20 minutes of role-play + 10 minutes of scored debrief per scenario.
  • Buyer actor rules: stay in character for the full 15–20 minutes. Only reveal hidden information if the rep asks a question specific enough to earn it: a generic "tell me about your budget" doesn't unlock a hidden budget-process complication; a specific, well-formed question does. Don't rescue a struggling rep by volunteering information they haven't earned.
  • Grading: score in real time using the rubric below. Don't discuss the score with the rep until the role-play is fully over.

Four scenarios, fully scripted

Scenario 2, BrightPath Clinics (Tier 1)

Buyer brief (for the actor): You're the Ops Director for a 12-location healthcare clinic group. No-shows run around 18%, and you did the math last week: roughly $340,000 a year in lost revenue across all locations. You're cooperative and will share this number if the rep asks anything reasonably close to "what does this cost you today."

Opening line: "Thanks for making time: look, I'll be straight with you, we've got a no-show problem and I'm tired of guessing at how bad it actually is."

Grading focus: Does the rep get to the $340k figure, or settle for "no-shows are a problem"? Do they quantify the pain in the buyer's own numbers, and read it back to confirm?

Scenario 7: Quorum Biotech (Tier 2)

Buyer brief: You're a lab ops coordinator, two years into your career, no budget authority, and no visibility into how procurement decisions get made at your company. You genuinely love the product and will say so enthusiastically: but if asked directly "who would need to approve this" or "have you seen a deal like this get funded before," you admit you don't know and have never been part of a purchase like this.

Opening line: "Honestly this is exactly what we need, I've been telling everyone about it already."

Grading focus: Fail condition, rep treats enthusiasm as champion strength and moves the deal forward without testing for real influence. Pass condition, rep asks a validating question ("walk me through the last time your team bought something like this, who was involved?") and, on hearing the answer, explicitly starts building a plan to find a second, higher-influence contact.

Scenario 10: Trestle Energy (Tier 2)

Buyer brief: You're an Ops Manager who genuinely believes "budget won't be an issue" because your company is doing well this year: but there is no line item for this specific purchase, and you've never taken a business case of this size to your VP. If the rep asks you to quantify the cost of the current problem in dollars, you can produce a rough number with effort. If the rep just accepts "budget isn't a problem" at face value and moves to next steps, let them.

Opening line: "Budget's not really a concern for us this year, we've had a strong year."

Grading focus: Fail condition, rep takes "budget isn't a problem" as a validated Metrics/Economic Buyer signal and skips building the business case. Pass condition: rep still asks for a quantified cost of the current pain and asks who would need to see that case, recognizing that "no stated budget constraint" is not the same as "budget approved."

Scenario 16, Ferro Capital Partners (Tier 3)

Buyer brief: You're a CFO who was burned by a vendor two years ago that overpromised and underdelivered. Open cold and mildly closed-off for the first 4–5 minutes: short answers, arms-crossed tone, a comment like "we've heard this pitch before." If the rep acknowledges the skepticism directly, asks what went wrong last time, and doesn't get defensive, warm up over the next few minutes and become a normal, cooperative buyer for the rest of the call (with a real, quantifiable pain point about forecast accuracy you'll share once trust is established).

Opening line: "I'll save you some time, we've had vendors promise us the world before. So, go ahead."

Grading focus: Fail condition: rep pushes straight into discovery questions while the buyer is still guarded, or gets defensive about the implied criticism. Pass condition: rep names the skepticism out loud, asks a genuine question about the past bad experience, and earns the shift before qualifying.

Grading rubric: MEDDIC / MEDDPICC (0–2 per element)

Element012
MetricsNot attemptedAsked, but no specific/quantified answer securedSpecific, quantified figure obtained and read back to confirm
Economic BuyerNot attemptedAsked, but no name/title securedNamed, with title, and access/path to them discussed
Decision CriteriaNot attemptedAsked, generic answer accepted3+ named criteria, ranked or weighted
Decision ProcessNot attemptedAsked, vague timeline onlySpecific steps, stakeholders, and dates identified
Identify PainNot attemptedGeneric pain named ("efficiency," "growth")Specific, quantified pain tied to a business consequence
ChampionNot attemptedEnthusiastic contact assumed to be champion, untestedInfluence/access tested and confirmed, or explicitly flagged as unconfirmed

(Add Paper Process and Competition as two more 0–2 rows, scored the same way, for MEDDPICC certifications, max 16.)

Pass thresholds:

  • Tier 1: 9+ of 12, no element scored 0
  • Tier 2: 9+ of 12, no more than one element scored 0
  • Tier 3: 8+ of 12 (or 10+ of 16 for MEDDPICC), no more than one element scored 0

Grading rubric: Challenger-adapted (0–3 per pillar)

Pillar0123
TeachNo insight offered, pure feature/needs discoveryGeneric industry point madeSpecific, credible insight that reframes the buyer's thinkingInsight lands, buyer visibly reacts ("I hadn't thought about it that way")
TailorNo adaptation to buyer's role/prioritiesInsight delivered the same way regardless of who's listeningLanguage adjusted to buyer's roleInsight explicitly connected to what this buyer's function cares about
Take ControlAvoids any tension, agrees with everythingMentions a mild tension in passingDirectly raises a productive tension (cost of inaction, competing priority)Buyer engages with the tension and moves toward a next step because of it

Pass threshold: 6+ of 9, no pillar scored 0.
