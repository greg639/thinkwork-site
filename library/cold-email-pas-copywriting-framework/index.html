Cold Email PAS Copywriting Framework

A Problem-Agitate-Solve structure for cold email copy, with a fill-in-the-brackets template, three worked persona examples, subject-line formulas, and a 10-minute build process reps can run on any account.

How to use it

Fill in the bracketed master template with the persona's specific problem, cost, and mechanism, following the 10-minute build process, and pick a subject line from the five formulas.

Problem → Agitate → Solve. Three short paragraphs, one ask. Apply to any persona in under 10 minutes.

The structure

LineJobLength
ProblemName the specific problem this persona has: not a generic pain, a concrete operational one1–2 sentences
AgitateShow the cost of leaving it unsolved: time, money, risk, or reputational cost1–2 sentences
SolvePosition your solution as the resolution, tied directly back to the agitated cost: end with one specific ask1–2 sentences

Master template

Subject: [Subject line: see formulas below] Hi [Name], [PROBLEM]: [specific process/task] at companies like [Company] usually means [named problem], especially when [condition that makes it worse]. [AGITATE]: left alone, that usually shows up as [cost: hours lost / dollars / risk / missed target], particularly around [specific trigger, e.g. quarter-end, headcount growth, audit]. [SOLVE]: we help [peer company/persona] fix exactly that by [one-sentence mechanism, not a feature list]. Worth 15 minutes to see if it applies to [Company]? [Specific day/time options, or scheduling link]. [Rep name]

Worked example: CFO persona

Subject: the manual close is costing more than it looks like Hi Priya, Finance teams at ~200-person SaaS companies usually still close the books with a mix of spreadsheets and manual reconciliation, even with a modern ERP underneath. Left alone, that typically means 3–5 extra days per close cycle, and it gets worse exactly when it matters most: right before a board update or fundraise. We help finance teams like [Peer Co]'s cut close time by automating the reconciliation layer, no ERP migration required. Worth 15 minutes to see if the same gap exists at [Company]? Tuesday 10am or Thursday 2pm both work for me. [Rep name]

Worked example: VP Sales persona

Subject: pipeline coverage math for Q3 Hi Marcus, Most VPs of Sales at your stage are running 2.5–3x pipeline coverage on paper, but a chunk of that is stale or single-threaded deals that never move. Left unaddressed, that shows up as a Q3 forecast that looks fine in the CRM and misses by 15–20% in reality, usually discovered too late to course-correct. We help sales leaders get an honest read on real coverage before it becomes a miss, not after. Worth 15 minutes to walk through how [Peer Co] caught this two quarters early? [Rep name]

Worked example: Head of IT persona

Subject: shadow SaaS spend at [Company] Hi Jordan, IT teams at companies your size usually have 30–40% more SaaS tools running than what's on the official approved list: bought by individual teams, unmanaged. Left alone, that's both a security exposure and a renewal-negotiation blind spot, you can't negotiate what you can't see. We help IT leaders get full visibility into shadow SaaS without a heavy rollout. Worth 15 minutes to see what's actually running at [Company]? [Rep name]

FormulaExample

The direct question"still reconciling by hand?"

Do's and don'ts

Do

  • Name a specific, credible problem: vague pain kills credibility instantly
  • Keep total email under 100 words
  • End with one specific ask, never "let me know your thoughts"
  • Use a real peer company or persona reference if you have one

Don't

  • Don't open with "I hope this finds you well" or "My name is..."
  • Don't list product features: PAS never mentions the product until the Solve line
  • Don't stack two asks (a meeting AND a reply AND a forward)
  • Don't agitate with fear-mongering that isn't backed by a real, common outcome

10-minute build process

MinutesStep
0–2Identify the persona and their single most common operational pain (check ICP notes / battlecards)
2–4Write the Problem line: be specific, not generic
4–6Write the Agitate line: attach a real cost (time, money, risk)
6–8Write the Solve line: one mechanism, one ask, one time option
8–9Write the subject line last, using one of the 5 formulas
9–10Read aloud once, cut anything that sounds like a pitch instead of a diagnosis
