Sales Skill Gap Self-Assessment

A 12-question, 20-minute diagnostic across the Core 12 sales competencies that scores where you stand and names the single skill to work on next.

How to use it

Answer all 12 scenario questions honestly based on actual recent behavior, total your score, then take your single lowest-scoring competency as the one skill to focus on next rather than trying to fix everything at once.

What's inside

  • 12 scenario-based questions, one per core competency, each with four graded response options
  • A 12–48 point scoring scale with four bands
  • Tie-break logic for identifying your single next skill to work on
  • Cross-references to the Core 12 Framework and related resources for whichever skill comes back lowest

12 questions, about 90 seconds each, roughly 20 minutes total. For each competency, read the scenario and pick the answer closest to what you'd ACTUALLY do on a real call this week, not what you know you're supposed to do. Score: A=1, B=2, C=3, D=4 unless noted.

1. Discovery & Needs Diagnosis

A prospect says "we're just exploring options right now." You: A. Ask what products they're comparing you against and move into your pitch. B. Ask one or two questions about their current process before pitching. C. Ask a series of open questions to understand their current process, what's not working, and why now, before mentioning your product. D. Do all of C, then reflect back their situation in their own words and confirm you've understood it correctly before saying anything about your solution.

2. Active Listening & Rapport

Mid-call, the buyer goes on a tangent unrelated to your agenda. You: A. Politely redirect back to your agenda as soon as there's a pause. B. Let them finish, then move on without referencing what they said. C. Let them finish, ask one follow-up question about it, then connect it back to the call's purpose. D. Do C, and note it to reference again later in the call or in a future conversation.

3. Value / Business Case Articulation

You're asked "why should we care about this?" You: A. Describe your product's main features. B. Describe features and mention a general benefit ("saves time"). C. Translate a feature into a specific outcome relevant to something they told you earlier. D. Do C, and quantify it (time, money, risk) using numbers grounded in what they've shared.

4. Objection Handling & Reframing

Buyer says "this is more expensive than what we use now." You: A. Offer a discount. B. Defend the price by listing more features. C. Ask a clarifying question about what "expensive" is being compared to before responding. D. Ask the clarifying question, then reframe using cost-of-inaction or total value, grounded in their own numbers.

5. Negotiation & Deal Structuring

Buyer pushes for a bigger discount at the end of a call. You: A. Agree to protect the relationship. B. Say you'll "see what you can do" and follow up later. C. Trade the discount for something of value (timeline, reference, expanded scope). D. Do C, and tie the trade to a specific, named business outcome for them, not just a price concession.

6. Multi-threading & Stakeholder Navigation

Your champion says "leave it with me, I'll sell it internally." You: A. Thank them and wait to hear back. B. Ask them to keep you posted. C. Ask who else needs to be convinced and what their likely concerns are. D. Do C, then propose getting time with those stakeholders directly, or arm your champion with a specific answer to their likely objection.

7. Pipeline & Territory Management

You review your pipeline weekly. You: A. Look at total number/value and move on. B. Check which deals are closing this month. C. Re-verify stage and next-step accuracy for every deal, not just the ones closing soon. D. Do C, and actively prune or downgrade deals that don't meet your own defined criteria for their stage.

8. Forecasting Accuracy & Discipline

Asked for your forecast commit number, you: A. Give an optimistic gut-feel number. B. Add up everything that could plausibly close. C. Apply a consistent, evidence-based rule (e.g., confirmed next step and economic buyer engaged) before committing. D. Do C, and can explain the specific evidence behind every deal in your commit if asked.

9. Product & Industry Fluency

A buyer asks a question outside your standard talk track. You: A. Say you'll find out and follow up, without more. B. Give a general answer and move on. C. Give a specific, accurate answer grounded in how your product actually works or a relevant industry pattern. D. Do C, and connect it to a proof point (customer example, data) on the spot.

10. Closing & Next-Step Commitment

Ending a productive call, you: A. Say "I'll follow up" in an email afterward. B. Suggest a next step verbally, without a firm date. C. Confirm a specific next step, date, and who's involved, out loud before ending the call. D. Do C, and send a same-day recap that restates the mutual commitment (both sides, not just yours).

11. Post-Sale Handoff & Expansion Awareness

A deal you worked just closed. You: A. Move on to the next deal immediately. B. Send a brief note to whoever owns onboarding. C. Give a structured handoff (context, stakeholders, watch-outs) to whoever owns the account next. D. Do C, and flag a specific, evidence-based expansion or renewal-risk signal for later follow-up.

12. Coachability & Self-Improvement Velocity

You get feedback you don't fully agree with. You: A. Nod along and don't change anything. B. Change your behavior for that one manager's next observed call, then drift back. C. Ask a clarifying question to understand the feedback fully, then decide deliberately whether/how to apply it. D. Do C, and proactively check back with the person who gave the feedback on whether the change is landing.

Scoring (12–48)

ScoreBand
12–20Early-stage across most skills: pick one skill and go deep before spreading thin
21–32Solid in some areas, uneven overall: a real gap is likely dragging results
33–40Strong across the board: refinement, not rebuilding, is the next step
41–48Advanced, likely a candidate to mentor others on 1–2 of these

Naming your ONE skill to work on next

  1. Find your single lowest-scoring question above.
  2. If two are tied, pick the one that shows up most often in your actual deals this quarter (check your own pipeline/notes).
  3. That competency, not the one you find most interesting, is your next skill focus.
  4. Cross-reference it against The Core 12 Sales Competency Framework for what the next level up actually looks like, and the Discovery Questioning Skill-Builder Cheat Sheet or Self-Coaching Call Debrief Checklist if it's a discovery-related gap.
