Ideal Customer Profile (ICP) Worksheet

A structured worksheet for defining firmographic, technographic, and trigger-event criteria before a single dial goes out, plus a weighted scoring model to rank accounts.

How to use it

Complete it as a team using real closed-won and lost-for-fit deal data, then run the 10-deal validation check at the bottom to confirm the definition matches reality before building your outbound list.

What's inside

  • Firmographic criteria table: industry, company size, revenue, geography, growth stage, ownership type
  • Technographic criteria table: core stack, maturity signals, integration fit
  • Trigger-events checklist: funding, new leadership hire, job postings, expansion, M&A, compliance deadline
  • Anti-ICP disqualifiers section (industries, size thresholds, behavioral red flags)
  • Weighted scoring model (100-point rubric across 6 criteria)
  • 10-deal validation sanity check against closed-won and lost-for-fit deals
  • Quarterly review cadence guidance
  • Ownership-type field (VC-backed / PE-backed / family-owned / public)

A structured worksheet for defining exactly who belongs on your outbound list, before a single dial goes out. Fill this in as a team, not solo; the biggest ICP mistakes come from one person's assumptions going unchallenged.

How to use this: Complete each section, then run 10 of your best closed-won deals through the scoring model at the bottom to sanity-check your definition against reality, not just intuition.

Section 3: Trigger Events (What Makes Them Ready NOW)

List the events that historically correlate with your best deals moving fast:

  • Recent funding round (Series ___ or later)
  • New leadership hire in relevant function (title: ______________________)
  • Active job posting for a role your solution supports/replaces
  • Recent expansion (new office, new market, new product line)
  • Recent M&A activity
  • Compliance or regulatory deadline
  • Public statement about a relevant initiative (earnings call, press release, LinkedIn post)
  • Other: ______________________

Section 4: Firmographic & Behavioral Disqualifiers (Anti-ICP)

Be as specific here as in Section 1, knowing who to skip saves as much time as knowing who to call.

  • Industries to exclude: ______________________
  • Company size below/above which deals historically don't close: ______________________
  • Behavioral red flags (e.g. no dedicated budget owner, team of 1, high vendor churn): ______________________
  • Past deals that looked like ICP but weren't: what did they have in common: ______________________

Section 5: Weighted Scoring Model

Score each prospect account 1–5 on each criterion, multiply by weight, sum for a total out of 100. Anything scoring below your threshold (commonly 60) goes to a lower-priority list or gets disqualified.

Adjust weights to match what actually predicts closed-won in your historical data, don't guess. Trigger events are weighted highest here because they predict urgency, and urgency is what turns a fit account into a moving deal.

Section 6: Validation, The 10-Deal Sanity Check

Pull your 10 most recent closed-won deals and your 5 most recent lost-for-fit deals. Score all 15 against the model above.

  • Do closed-won deals cluster at 70+? If not, your weights or criteria are off.
  • Do lost-for-fit deals cluster below 50? If not, you're missing a disqualifier.
  • Is there a criterion every closed-won deal shares that isn't in your model yet? Add it.
  • Is there a criterion you assumed mattered that shows no correlation? Cut it or reweight it.

Review cadence: revisit this worksheet quarterly: trigger events and technographic signals shift faster than firmographics, and a stale ICP quietly inflates your top-of-funnel with dials that were never going to close.
