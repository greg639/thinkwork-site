Negotiation Skill-Level Rubric

Behavioral anchors for Novice through Expert negotiation, with real verbatim lines showing what each level actually sounds like on a call, not just what it's called.

How to use it

Read all 5 levels against your most recent real negotiation, score the 5 dimensions honestly, then treat your lowest-scoring dimension, not the average, as your actual level and drill only the matching practice path.

What's inside

  • 5 levels (Novice → Expert) with a real verbatim line for what each sounds like
  • A behavior-pattern description per level
  • A common scenario ("buyer asks for 20% off") answered at every level for direct comparison
  • A 5-dimension scoring table (concession discipline, frame control, multi-party negotiation, silence/anchoring, BATNA use)
  • The "gated by your weakest dimension" scoring rule
  • A specific practice path for each level transition

How to use this

Read the "what it sounds like" line for each level against a recent negotiation you ran. Score yourself where your actual behavior lands, not where you'd like to be. This rubric uses the same 5-point scale as the Sales Competency Self-Rating Scorecard.

Level 1, Novice

What it sounds like: "Let me check with my manager and get back to you." (on nearly every term, including small ones) Behavior pattern: Escalates most negotiation decisions upward. Treats any pushback as something to be resolved by someone more senior. Doesn't yet distinguish which terms are actually negotiable. On a 20%-discount ask: Either concedes on the spot to keep the deal moving, or defers entirely to a manager without first trying to understand the ask. Common tell: Negotiation "happens to" the rep rather than being run by them.

Level 2, Developing

What it sounds like: "I can probably get you 10% off if we can move forward this week." Behavior pattern: Makes concessions but ties them loosely to something in return. Doesn't yet have a clear read on what they're trading away versus what they're getting. On a 20%-discount ask: Counters with a smaller number, but the trade ("this week") is about the rep's timeline, not a real value exchange for the buyer. Common tell: Concessions are transactional but not strategic: the rep is negotiating on instinct, not on a plan.

Level 3, Competent

What it sounds like: "I can move on price if we move on term length, a 20% discount works on a 3-year commitment instead of 1." Behavior pattern: Trades value for value deliberately. Has a clear sense of levers (price, term, scope, payment schedule) and uses more than one. Holds a floor and knows it. On a 20%-discount ask: Asks what's driving the ask (budget ceiling vs. wanting a better deal) before responding, then trades a real lever, not just a number. Common tell: Every concession has a named "in exchange for."

Level 4, Advanced

What it sounds like: "Before we talk numbers, what does success need to look like for this to be an easy renewal at full price next year?" Behavior pattern: Shapes the frame of the negotiation itself: moves the conversation from "what's the discount" to "what's actually being optimized for" (risk, proof, cash flow, internal politics). Anticipates the ask before it's made. On a 20%-discount ask: Reframes the conversation toward what the buyer actually needs proven or protected, and negotiates on that axis instead of accepting price as the battlefield. Common tell: The buyer often ends up negotiating on the rep's terms without noticing the shift.

Level 5: Expert

What it sounds like: "I hear that from procurement: can we get 10 minutes with you and your CFO together, so I understand the actual constraint rather than negotiating through a relay?" Behavior pattern: Negotiates through committees and third parties (procurement, legal, multiple approvers) without losing the thread of what the actual decision-maker needs. Comfortable walking away from a bad deal and has demonstrated it. On a 20%-discount ask: Recognizes when the real negotiation isn't happening in the room, gets access to where it is, and negotiates the actual constraint rather than a proxy for it. Common tell: Other reps bring this person into their toughest negotiations to observe or co-run.

Practice path from where you are

  • Novice → Developing: Drill naming which terms are actually negotiable before every call (see Objection-Handling Swipe File, Category 7).
  • Developing → Competent: For 2 weeks, no concession without a stated "in exchange for", say it out loud even if the trade is small.
  • Competent → Advanced: Before your next 5 negotiations, write down the frame you expect the buyer to bring, and one alternative frame you could offer instead.
  • Advanced → Expert: Seek out one multi-party/committee negotiation deliberately (ask your manager to loop you in on someone else's) and observe how the constraint differs from what any single stakeholder tells you.
