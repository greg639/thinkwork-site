Objection Handling Skills Self-Assessment Quiz

A 20-question, five-category diagnostic that scores a rep's objection-handling maturity from Novice to Expert and flags the single skill gap costing them the most deals. Includes a scoring rubric, gap-priority order, and a recommended-resource map by weak area.

What's inside

  • 20 questions across 5 competency sections (Listening/Diagnosis, Composure, Reframing, Closing the Loop, Prevention)
  • 1-5 rating scale definitions (Never to Almost Always)
  • Section-by-section scoring (20 pts max each)
  • Overall score mapped to 5 maturity bands (Novice through Expert)
  • Gap-flag logic naming the priority order to work sections in when scores tie
  • Recommended-next-resource mapping by weakest section
  • Retake cadence guidance (60-90 days) to track movement over time

20 questions across 5 core competencies. Rate yourself honestly against your last 10 real objections handled, not how you think you'd perform in theory. Takes 8–10 minutes.

Scoring scale for every question: 1 = Never · 2 = Rarely · 3 = Sometimes · 4 = Usually · 5 = Almost always

Section 1 — Listening & Diagnosis

  1. When an objection comes up, I let the prospect finish completely before I start forming my response.
  2. I ask at least one diagnostic question before responding to figure out what's really behind the stated objection.
  3. I can usually tell the difference between a stall, a genuine constraint, and a polite decline.
  4. I capture specifics (a number, a name, a date, a competitor) rather than accepting a vague answer.

Section 1 total: ___ / 20

Section 2 — Emotional Composure

  1. I don't get defensive or argue when a prospect pushes back hard.
  2. I stay at the same pace and tone after an objection as I was before it — no visible rattling.
  3. I can sit in silence for a few seconds after an objection instead of rushing to fill it.
  4. I don't take objections personally or let one bad call affect my energy on the next one.

Section 2 total: ___ / 20

Section 3 — Reframing & Response Quality

  1. My response to an objection is matched to the actual root cause, not just the surface complaint.
  2. I use specific proof (a customer example, a number, a mechanism) rather than general reassurance.
  3. I can reframe a price/budget objection in terms of cost-of-inaction or comparative value, not just discount.
  4. I keep my responses proportionate — I don't over-explain a simple objection into a monologue.

Section 3 total: ___ / 20

Section 4 — Closing the Loop / Next Steps

  1. I explicitly check whether my response actually resolved the objection ("Does that address it, or is there more?").
  2. Every objection conversation ends with a concrete next step that has a date and an owner attached.
  3. I don't let "let me think about it" end a call without a specific reason and a specific follow-up trigger.
  4. I follow up on the date I committed to, without needing a reminder.

Section 4 total: ___ / 20

Section 5 — Pattern Recognition & Prevention

  1. I notice when I'm hearing the same objection repeatedly and adjust my discovery questions to surface it earlier.
  2. I ask discovery questions early in a deal that would surface likely objections (budget range, decision process, past experience) before they become late-stage surprises.
  3. I review my own calls (recordings or notes) specifically looking for objection-handling patterns, not just deal outcomes.
  4. I can name, right now, the ONE objection type that costs me the most deals.

Section 5 total: ___ / 20

Gap Flag — Find Your Priority Growth Area

Your lowest-scoring section is your priority — not the overall score. If two sections tie, work them in this order (each depends on the one before it):

  1. Listening & Diagnosis (Section 1) — if this is weak, everything downstream is guesswork
  2. Emotional Composure (Section 2) — a rattled rep can't diagnose or reframe well
  3. Reframing & Response Quality (Section 3)
  4. Closing the Loop (Section 4)
  5. Pattern Recognition & Prevention (Section 5) — the long-game skill once the rest is solid

How to use it

Score each item 1-5 against your last 10 real objection conversations, total each section, then use the gap-flag priority order (not the overall score) to pick the one skill to work on next.
