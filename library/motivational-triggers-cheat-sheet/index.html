Motivational Triggers Cheat Sheet

A one-page lookup for what actually moves each of the four personality types on your team: competitive, affiliative, achievement-driven, and autonomy-driven: so a 1:1, a shoutout, or a contest doesn't backfire.

How to use it

Print this single page and keep it next to your 1:1 notes; before praising, assigning, or incentivizing a rep, glance at their row and match the method to the type.

What's inside

  • A definition of each of the 4 types: competitive, affiliative, achievement-driven, autonomy-driven
  • 60-second diagnostic questions to identify a rep's type on the spot
  • Quick-reference table across all 4 types: what moves them, what kills it, what to say, what never to say
  • Best recognition method per type
  • Best incentive/comp structure fit per type
  • A same-team blend guide for running one team meeting that lands for all 4 types at once

One page. Keep it open during 1:1 prep, contest design, and recognition moments.

60-Second Diagnostic

Ask, or just notice the pattern over a few interactions:

  • "When you picture a great week here, is it beating a specific number, beating a specific person, mastering something hard, or doing it your own way?"
  • Do they mention rank unprompted (competitive)? Mention the team or a client unprompted (affiliative)? Mention getting better at something unprompted (achievement-driven)? Push back on being told how to do something (autonomy-driven)?

Running One Meeting That Lands for All Four

When you can't tailor 1:1 by 1:1: a team standup, a QBR, a kickoff, hit all four in under 2 minutes:

  1. Open with team purpose (affiliative), what this quarter's push means for the team as a whole.
  2. Name a rank movement (competitive): who moved up, who's closing the gap.
  3. Name a specific skill leveled up by someone (achievement): not just a number, the "how."
  4. Close by handing the room a choice (autonomy): "here's the goal, you decide how your pod gets there."
