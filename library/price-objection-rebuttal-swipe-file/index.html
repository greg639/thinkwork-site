Price Objection Rebuttal Swipe File

A focused swipe file of proven word-tracks for every flavor of budget pushback, from blunt sticker shock to 'match this competitor's quote', built to keep the conversation open instead of triggering a discount.

How to use it

Copy the word-track that matches the exact objection you just heard, adapt it to your own voice, and reach for the email versions when the pushback arrives in writing instead of on a call.

What's inside

  • 3 word-tracks for general 'it's too expensive' sticker shock
  • 3 word-tracks for 'you're pricier than [competitor]' comparisons
  • 3 word-tracks for 'we don't have budget' pushback
  • 2 word-tracks for discount / lower-price requests
  • 2 word-tracks for multi-year discount asks
  • 2 word-tracks for 'over budget by $X' gaps
  • 2 email templates for price objections that arrive in writing
  • A practice drill for building fluency instead of sounding scripted

Price objections are rarely about price: they're about value, timing, or trust in the number. Use these word-tracks as your first draft, then adjust to your own voice. Each one is written to keep the conversation open, not to defend the number.

Category A, "It's too expensive" (general sticker shock)

A1. "Too expensive compared to what: what you budgeted, or what you're seeing in value so far? Those are different conversations and I want to solve the right one."

A2. "I hear that before people see what it replaces, what's [specific cost/inefficiency] costing you today if we do nothing?"

A3. "Let's break down what's actually in that number so 'expensive' isn't doing all the work: what part feels heaviest: the price itself, or the size of the commitment?"

Category B, "You're pricier than [Competitor]"

B1. "What's included in their number: same seats, same support tier, same implementation? I want to compare the same thing, not two different offers."

B2. "You're right, we're not the cheapest option, we're priced for [specific outcome/differentiator]. Is that outcome worth the gap to you?"

B3. "If price were equal, would you still pick them? If the answer's no, then price isn't really the deciding factor here, let's talk about what is."

Category C, "We don't have budget"

C1. "Is that no budget exists, or no budget's been allocated for this yet? Those need different next steps from me."

C2. "If the ROI math works, is budget something that could get created, or is this a hard ceiling regardless of the case we build?"

C3. "What would need to be true for budget to open up: a new fiscal year, a specific event, sign-off from someone else?"

Category D, "Can you send me a lower price / discount?"

D1. "I want to get you the right price, not just a lower one, where specifically does the current number miss the mark for you?"

D2. "I have more flexibility on terms than on the number itself, would extending payment terms or adjusting scope get you where you need to be?"

Category E, "We need a multi-year discount to make this work"

E1. "I can build a multi-year structure: what length are you thinking, and what discount would make a 2- or 3-year commitment worth it to you?"

E2. "Happy to model that: just so I bring you a real number: is a multi-year commitment something you're genuinely ready to make, or a way to test if the price moves?"

Category F, "It's over budget by $X"

F1. "How far over, exactly? Sometimes the gap is closer than it feels, let's see if we can close it with scope or timing rather than just price."

F2. "If we solved that $X gap with [scaled-down starting scope / phased rollout], would that get you to yes at the number you have?"

Written / Email Versions

Use these when the price objection comes in writing or you need to follow up in text.

Email 1, "Too expensive" Subject: Re: pricing "Thanks for being upfront on this. Before I send a revised number, can I ask: is the gap about the total investment, or about the value you're seeing so far at this price? I want to fix the right thing, not just discount blindly."

Email 2: "Cheaper elsewhere" Subject: Quick comparison "Appreciate you sharing that you're comparing options: to make sure it's a fair comparison, could you tell me what's included in their quote (seats, support, onboarding)? I'll send back a like-for-like breakdown."

Practice Drill

Pick two word-tracks per category and say them out loud, in your own words, until you can deliver them without sounding like you're reading a card. Price objections land worst when they sound rehearsed: the goal is fluency, not memorization.
